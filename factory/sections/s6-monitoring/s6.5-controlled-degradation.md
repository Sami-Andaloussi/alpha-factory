# S6.5 — Stabilisation & controlled degradation

## TL;DR

Turns alerts, technical or from the markets, into immediate, proportionate and reversible actions that keep the factory in its safe zone: slow down, simplify, isolate, then stop cleanly if needed.
It owns the ladder of operating modes, from normal to a global stop; every step has hysteresis, a cooldown and a time to live, and every action is journalled with its reason.
Its control directives go to section 5 (S5.3, S5.6, S5.7, S5.9), which applies them through the controls its rulebook declares.

## Purpose

Turn the signals of alert, technical or from the markets, into immediate, proportionate and
reversible actions that keep the factory in its safe zone: slow down, simplify, isolate, then stop
cleanly if needed. This is the factory's [controlled degradation](../../glossary.md#controlled-degradation): the page owns the ladder of
[operating modes](../../glossary.md#operating-mode).

**Success looks like:** no accident (no overshoot, no oscillation), a fast return under the [SLOs](../../glossary.md#slo),
and a clear journal of everything that was done, and why.

<details>
<summary><b>What each flow carries, constraints and service levels</b></summary>

The SLO catalogue of S6.1 brings the [SLIs](../../glossary.md#sli), the SLOs and the
[baselines](../../glossary.md#baseline), with the traces, metrics and logs correlated; its
monitoring signals, and those of data integrity (S6.3) and of security (S6.6), bring the symptoms of
broken service levels, of broken integrity and of threats that call for a degraded mode or the
[kill switch](../../glossary.md#kill-switch). [Drift](../../glossary.md#drift) detection (S6.4)
sends its symptoms already filtered, with their severity and scope, and its alert-to-action table.
Section 5 exposes its surfaces of action (participation, cadence, timeouts, modules, routing): the
deployment rulebook of S5.9 declares them, with their real-time controls and who may activate what.
The incident charter of lifecycle's [incident response](../../glossary.md#incident-response) (S7.9)
says which automatic actions each level of [incident severity](../../glossary.md#incident-severity)
calls for.

The control directives carry the orders of stabilisation to section 5: to S5.7 for participation,
cadence, timeouts, the depth of its algorithms and routing; to S5.6 for the pace of the transition;
to S5.3 for the cadence of computation and the costly modules it may switch off; to S5.9 for the
local and global stops, the last rungs of the ladder, which its kill switch executes. Section 5
applies them through its own controls. Live
[execution fidelity](../../glossary.md#execution-fidelity) (S6.2) and data integrity (S6.3) send
directives within their own scope; the rulebook says who may activate what. Their decision logs
bring those directives here, so that the journal misses no entry into a mode. The journal of
actions, with every entry into and exit from a mode, is the page's decision log: it goes to
[intervention](../../glossary.md#intervention) (S6.7), to data integrity (S6.3), which keeps the
modes activated in the journal of truth, and to lifecycle's system of record (S7.2). The runbooks of
human intervention go to S6.7. The journal also goes back to S6.1 and S6.4, to reset their baselines
and thresholds.

**Constraints.**
- Consistency: decisions centred on symptoms, across strategies.
- Formats: typed events (alert, action, stop, return) with reliable timestamps and a scope
  (global, [venue](../../glossary.md#venue), strategy, instrument).
- Stability: no loop that switches on and off without an explicit cooldown.

**Service levels:**
- Integration of critical events, from detection to action, [T95](../../glossary.md#t95) per class:
  A (safety, for example a venue halted or broken data integrity) ≤ 2 s; B (strong degradation)
  ≤ 5 s; C (high noise) ≤ 30 s.

</details>

## Gates

**Entry.** Start only when:
- S6.1 is in production: SLIs, SLOs and reliable timestamps;
- the surfaces of action of section 5 are exposed and tested (idempotence, local scope);
- the sets of [replays](../../glossary.md#replay) and the scenarios of load are ready.

**Exit.** Verdict *pass* when all of these hold:
- the T95 from detection to action is met for classes A, B and C;
- oscillation = 0 in the tests;
- the journal and the runbooks are in place;
- the matrix of policies is validated.

*Retry* and *fail* follow the [gate verdict](../../glossary.md#gate-verdict) rule.

**Requalification.** Rerun the sub-section when:
- a key SLI drifts for long, beyond its [error budget](../../glossary.md#error-budget) over 2–4 weeks;
- the rules of a market or a venue change, or a new channel of execution or a new costly algorithm
  arrives;
- a postmortem reveals a hole in a policy, or an excessive cost of degradation.

Within section 6, the journal refines the detection of S6.4 and the baselines of S6.1. Lifecycle
(S7) receives proposals to update the policies, thresholds and runbooks, and the [gates](../../glossary.md#gate) for returning
to production.

## Steps

<details>
<summary><b>1 · Define the safe zone and the grammar of alerts</b> — Design task + Execution task</summary>

**Goal.** State what is tolerable, and when to act. Choosing the indicators, the service levels,
the classes and the [hysteresis](../../glossary.md#hysteresis) is design; writing the safe zone down on one page is execution.

**Actions.**
- List 12–20 vital indicators: latency at p99 and p99.9, rates of errors and rejections, drift of
  the data, [spread](../../glossary.md#spread), volatility and [liquidity](../../glossary.md#liquidity), extreme [markouts](../../glossary.md#markout), short-term [drawdown](../../glossary.md#drawdown).
- Set the SLOs and error budgets per context (venue, strategy, hour, day), with daily and weekly
  baselines, registered in S6.1's catalogue.
- Define the classes of severity (A critical, B strong, C moderate), the windows of decision (for
  example 10 s, 1 min, 5 min) and the hysteresis (the threshold to enter differs from the threshold
  to exit).
- Describe on one page the safe zone (a polygon of limit values) and the mechanics of alert:
  symptoms, not causes.

**KPI.** Precision of the alerts (≤ x% false positives over 30 days); time to detect (T95).

**Guardrail.** Baselines kept clean of crises: clean windows of reference, and a hold-out in time.

**Output.** The contract of stabilisation, v1 (SLIs, SLOs, baselines, thresholds and hysteresis) →
steps 2 to 4.

</details>

<details>
<summary><b>2 · Map the headroom and the levers of control</b> — Design task + Execution task</summary>

**Goal.** Know what to act on, within which bounds, at what cost. The levers and their bounds are
design; linking each symptom to its levers is execution.

**Actions.**
- Inventory the levers: the rate of participation, the cadence and quantum of orders, timeouts and
  retries, the depth of algorithms, the frequency of computation and sampling, routing and
  affinities to venues, modules switched on or off.
- For each lever, define a safe range, a step of adjustment, a cooldown and a marginal cost
  (latency, P&L, risk).
- Link each symptom to its primary levers. For example, when latency rises at p99, lower the
  cadence and lengthen the timeout before cutting a costly module.

**KPI.** Coverage: ≥ 90% of the symptoms have at least one primary lever; the "cost per notch" is
documented.

**Guardrail.** Monotonicity, checked by directed micro-tests: a notch of caution must never make
things worse.

**Output.** The catalogue of levers and the budget of headroom, v1 → step 3 (policies), step 4
(controllers).

</details>

<details>
<summary><b>3 · A ladder of modes and policies of action: nudges, closures, stops</b> — Design task + Execution task</summary>

**Goal.** Define how the factory degrades, by reversible steps. The modes and the policies are
design; the locality, the order of priority and the hysteresis of each policy are execution.

**Actions.**
- Set 5–6 clear modes: normal, cautious, degraded 1, degraded 2, local stop, global stop.
- For each class of severity (A, B, C) and each symptom, write the policy:
  1. soft [guardrails](../../glossary.md#guardrail): gradual nudges;
  2. closed guardrails: a local rate limit, a backoff, a local circuit;
  3. clean stops: a local or global kill switch, with the criteria to come back up.
- Define the locality (per venue, strategy, instrument) and the order of priority: first reduce
  what arrives, or simplify the service; cut only last.
- Impose hysteresis and a cooldown on each policy, against ping-pong.

**KPI.** Time to safe (from the alert to the mode applied, T95), the % of time in a degraded mode,
the rate of oscillation (< ε per hour).

**Guardrail.** A lint of the policies: no conflicting actions, and a local stop takes precedence
over a global one when it is enough.

**Output.** The matrix of symptoms × modes × actions, with the rules of entry and exit → step 4
(implementation).

</details>

<details>
<summary><b>4 · Online controllers: the safe execution of the policies</b> — Design task + Execution task</summary>

**Goal.** Execute the policies in real time, in a way that is [idempotent](../../glossary.md#idempotent), local and reversible. The
loops and their guarantees are design; the action API and its journal are execution.

**Actions.**
- Implement the loops of control that drive the actuators section 5 exposes: limiters of rate and
  cadence, a timed backoff, circuits per scope (venue, strategy), a conditional kill switch, flags
  that switch costly modules off.
- Guarantee atomic switches, priorities, idempotence, and [fail-closed](../../glossary.md#fail-closed) behaviour: in doubt, safety.
- Expose a simple action API (activate, release, state) and a journal of actions (who, when, why,
  scope).

**KPI.** Time to act (from the alert to the control in effect, T95), the p99 of the reduction of
load, the rate of successful [rollbacks](../../glossary.md#rollback).

**Guardrail.** Against thrashing: hysteresis and a cooldown are mandatory; tests of concurrency,
several alerts at once.

**Output.** Controllers in operation, the API and the journal → step 5 (tests), step 6 (audit and
learning).

</details>

<details>
<summary><b>5 · Directed validation: replay, load, targeted chaos</b> — Design task + Execution task</summary>

**Goal.** Prove that the page stabilises without breaking anything. The scenarios are design; the
measures and the tests are execution.

**Actions.**
- Replay stressed episodes (volatility, liquidity, latency), steps of load, and local failures (a
  venue down, corrupted data).
- Measure the time to safe, the overshoot (the maximum beyond the threshold), the return under the
  SLO (T95), and the cost to performance in the degraded modes (P&L and opportunities lost).
- Add tests of non-regression and of properties: no oscillation, automatic reversibility respected,
  locality preferred.

**KPI.** Time to return under the SLO; oscillation = 0 on the scenarios; coverage ≥ 90% of the
risks catalogued.

**Guardrail.** Long-tail scenarios are mandatory: fragmented markets, rejections in chain, doubtful
timestamps.

**Output.** The validation report and the list of adjustments (thresholds, steps, cooldowns) → step
6, lifecycle (S7).

</details>

<details>
<summary><b>6 · Explanation, traceability and the loop of improvement</b> — Design task + Execution task</summary>

**Goal.** Make it explainable, auditable and improvable. The journal, the dashboard and the
runbooks are execution; the loop of learning is design.

**Actions.**
- Keep a structured journal of actions: trigger, context, action, scope, duration, criteria to come
  back up.
- A dashboard of actions against results: which action brought what back under its SLO, when, at
  what cost?
- Runbooks of human intervention: who does what if the return does not happen.
- A loop of learning: adjust the baselines, the thresholds, the steps and the mapping from symptoms
  to levers, then propose a [requalification](../../glossary.md#requalification) to lifecycle (S7).

**KPI.** Rate of explanation (≥ 95% of the actions have a reason and an effect); mean time to
explain; the % of actions that led to a lasting adjustment.

**Guardrail.** Forced reversibility: a time to live on every degraded mode, with explicit criteria
to come back up.

**Output.** The journal, the dashboards, the runbooks and a quarterly plan of improvement →
intervention (S6.7), lifecycle (S7).

</details>

## Deliverables

- A clear specification: the contract of stabilisation (safe zone, severities, hysteresis).
- Catalogue of levers: safe ranges, costs, cooldowns, priorities.
- Matrix of symptoms × modes × actions (nudges, closures, stops), with the criteria to come back up.
- Controllers and the action API, documented: scopes, idempotence, fail-closed.
- [KPI](../../glossary.md#kpi) dashboard: detection to action at T95, time to return under the SLO, oscillations, time in a
  degraded mode, cost of degradation, extreme markouts, rejections.
- Log of decisions (who, when, why) and the runbooks of intervention.
- Replacement process: how to insert or remove a lever or a policy safely (tests, lints,
  validation).
