# S6.6 — Operational security & posture (factory SecOps)

## TL;DR

Keeps the factory under control against security risks: see early, decide fast, contain cleanly.
Key indicators of security, quiet alerts, safe automatic actions (block, restrict, rotate, switch over) and an indisputable trail; success is a short time to detect and contain, and limited damage.
Its signals reach stabilisation (S6.5) for degraded modes, intervention (S6.7) for incidents, and lifecycle (S7.6) to requalify models and strategies.

## Purpose

Keep the factory under control against security risks: see early, decide fast, contain cleanly.
The page provides the key indicators of security, alerts with little noise, safe automatic actions
(block, restrict, rotate, switch over) and an indisputable trail. It is the factory's [SecOps](../../glossary.md#secops), and
it measures the factory's [security posture](../../glossary.md#security-posture).

**Success looks like:** a short delay to detect and to contain, limited damage, and a clear journal
that can be replayed.

<details>
<summary><b>What each flow carries, constraints and service levels</b></summary>

The SLO catalogue of S6.1 brings the common [SLIs](../../glossary.md#sli) and [SLOs](../../glossary.md#slo), with their [baselines](../../glossary.md#baseline). Data integrity
(S6.3) keeps the write-once journal and the reliable time on which this page records its critical
events. The platform (S6.8) carries the telemetry of identities, secrets, binaries and network, and
the topology of services and strategies.

The monitoring signals ask stabilisation (S6.5) for a degraded mode or the
[kill switch](../../glossary.md#kill-switch), and [intervention](../../glossary.md#intervention)
(S6.7) to respond; they carry to lifecycle the triggers to requalify a model or a strategy (S7.6),
and every signal to the loop of missions (S7.10). The executable runbooks go to S6.7. The timeline
of each incident, with its decisions and effects, is the page's decision log, for S6.7 and for
lifecycle's system of record (S7.2). The artefacts of audit go to data integrity (S6.3), which adds
them to its audit packs. The blocks, restrictions, rotations and switches stay with this page: they
act on identities, secrets, binaries and network, not on trading, which only stabilisation (S6.5)
degrades.

**Constraints.**
- Unified formats: timestamped events, correlated by common identifiers.
- Consistency in time: the skew between clocks stays below 100 ms on the critical domains.

**Service levels** for critical events, at T90 or [T95](../../glossary.md#t95):
- An escalation of privilege: detected at T95 ≤ 2 min, contained ≤ 10 min.
- A leaked secret: detected at T95 ≤ 5 min, revoked ≤ 10 min.
- A binary not approved: blocked ≤ 2 min.
- An abnormal exfiltration: detected at T95 ≤ 2 min, switched to a restricted network mode ≤ 5 min.

</details>

## Gates

**Entry.** Start only when:
- the SecOps charter is validated (step 1);
- a minimal inventory of the assets exists;
- the synchronisation of time is proven;
- the common pipeline of events is available.

**Exit.** Verdict *pass* when all of these hold:
- the SLIs and SLOs are active;
- the alerts are tested with synthetic probes;
- the runbooks have run in simulation, plus one controlled exercise live;
- the first postmortems are closed, with their actions.

*Retry* and *fail* follow the [gate verdict](../../glossary.md#gate-verdict) rule.

**Requalification.** Rerun the sub-section when:
- a key [KPI](../../glossary.md#kpi) [drifts](../../glossary.md#drift) lastingly (more than 2 cycles of observation);
- a new threat, or a new market rule, affects the perimeter;
- the architecture changes deeply (new [venues](../../glossary.md#venue), a new chain of build);
- a costly false alert or a real incident occurs: the loop of S6.7 runs, and lifecycle's [gate](../../glossary.md#gate)
  (S7) calls for new tests, adjusted SLOs and redesigned runbooks.

Within section 6, S6.1 adjusts its baselines and its noise, and S6.3 its requirements of audit.
Stabilisation (S6.5), and through it section 5, refine their degraded modes. Lifecycle (S7)
receives the policy of versions and rotation for strategies, models and keys.

## Steps

<details>
<summary><b>1 · Set the grammar of security: perimeter, threats, SLIs, SLOs, baselines</b> — Design task</summary>

**Goal.** Without measurable objectives, everything else turns reactive and noisy.

**Actions.**
- Map the critical assets: data, secrets, binaries, pipelines, access, network.
- Define the priority scenarios of risk: an escalation of privilege, an exfiltration, the drift of
  binaries or configuration, a leaked secret.
- Set the SLIs and SLOs of security, published in S6.1's catalogue: for example the T95 to detect
  an escalation of privilege, or to contain a compromised key.
- Build the baselines (day and night, week and weekend, market [regimes](../../glossary.md#regime)) for identities, secrets,
  binaries and network.

**KPI.** The % of SLIs and SLOs defined and published (target 100%); the T95 to recompute the
baselines after a major change (< 24 h).

**Guardrail.** A quarterly review of the SLOs, and a test of noise (≤ X paging alerts per week); on
failure, the thresholds and budgets are adjusted.

**Output.** The SecOps charter: perimeter, scenarios, SLIs and SLOs, baselines, budgets of noise →
steps 2 to 6 (thresholds, alerts, tests).

</details>

<details>
<summary><b>2 · Identities, access, secrets: continuous control</b> — Design task</summary>

**Goal.** Identities and secrets are the door to every scenario.

**Actions.**
- Formalise a model of least access (roles, perimeters, lifetimes), with emergency
  ("break-glass") accounts.
- Measure continuously: unusual logins, rights too broad, old tokens, dormant accounts waking up.
- Define the rhythms of rotation, and the footprints of use: who uses what, where, when.

**KPI.** The % of accounts with multi-factor authentication and the required controls; the median
age of the secrets (target ≤ the window); the % of dormant accounts revoked per month.

**Guardrail.** No irreversible action without a double validation; decoy secrets, planted to test
the detection of leaks.

**Output.** The control board of identities and secrets (states, gaps, attached actions) → steps 3
to 6 (software integrity, drift of configuration, automatic responses).

</details>

<details>
<summary><b>3 · Software integrity and the artefacts deployed</b> — Design task</summary>

**Goal.** Even with clean access, an altered binary compromises everything.

**Actions.**
- Define the chain of approval of artefacts: build, validation, deployment.
- Give each binary and configuration a verifiable identity: its [fingerprint](../../glossary.md#fingerprint), its [provenance](../../glossary.md#provenance), a seal
  of approval.
- Journal the critical events (who, what, when, where) in [WORM storage](../../glossary.md#worm-storage).

**KPI.** Coverage of attestation: the % of deployed artefacts signed and accepted; the % of
deployments traceable end to end.

**Guardrail.** Blocked by default when a fingerprint is missing; synchronised timestamps checked;
the write-once journals verified.

**Output.** The register of integrity (authorised artefacts, fingerprints, timeline) → steps 4 to 6
(detection of drift, targeted blocking, audit of incidents).

</details>

<details>
<summary><b>4 · Configuration and attack surface: detect the drift</b> — Execution task</summary>

**Goal.** Most breaches are born from a gap of configuration that nobody noticed.

**Actions.**
- Set the desired state of the configurations: network, hosts, agents, policies.
- Compare the current state with the desired state, continuously: additions, removals,
  deactivations, open ports.
- Rank the significant gaps, and reduce their backlog.

**KPI.** The median delay to correct the critical and high drifts; the backlog of drifts older than
N days.

**Guardrail.** Detection kept apart from remediation, against loops; tests of non-regression of the
configuration.

**Output.** The view of drift and of the priority vulnerabilities, with timestamps and owners → step
5 (exfiltration, outgoing traffic), step 6 (automatic actions).

</details>

<details>
<summary><b>5 · Network and exfiltration: watch, limit, alert</b> — Design task</summary>

**Goal.** A successful exfiltration is the risk made real.

**Actions.**
- Define the legitimate outgoing paths per asset and strategy: destinations, volumes, windows of
  time.
- Baseline the volumes, the destinations and the ratios of failure to success; detect the typical
  behaviour of command-and-control traffic.
- Attach safe actions: a targeted block, a logical [quarantine](../../glossary.md#quarantine), a switch to a restricted network
  mode.

**KPI.** The rate of false positives of the alerts on outgoing traffic (target < Y%); the volume of
exfiltration blocked or aborted; the number of paths not justified.

**Guardrail.** Granular blocking (per service, per destination), and a mode of observation before
any global block; a documented [rollback](../../glossary.md#rollback).

**Output.** The map of outgoing traffic and anomalies, with the actions proposed → step 6
(containment), stabilisation (S6.5, degraded modes).

</details>

<details>
<summary><b>6 · Orchestrated response, controlled degradation and proof</b> — Design task</summary>

**Goal.** Without a clean execution, a good alert serves nothing.

**Actions.**
- Formalise the rules from symptom to manoeuvre: for example, a suspect key is rotated, and the
  unusual endpoints are blocked.
- Automate with care: safety brakes, actions tried first on a small scope, de-escalation on a false
  positive.
- Keep the timeline of each incident: reliable timestamps, decisions, effects, restoration.

**KPI.** [MTTD](../../glossary.md#mttd-mttr) and MTTR of security, per severity; the ratio of alerts to useful actions; the rate
of success of the runbooks in tests.

**Guardrail.** Brakes against oscillation (a cooldown, limits of frequency); a monthly table-top
exercise; a [blameless postmortem](../../glossary.md#blameless-postmortem), mandatory.

**Output.** Executable runbooks, an auditable timeline and the states after the incident →
intervention (S6.7, postmortem and learning), lifecycle (S7.6, the
[requalifications](../../glossary.md#requalification) of strategies and models).

</details>

## Deliverables

- SecOps specification: perimeter, scenarios, SLIs and SLOs, baselines, budgets of noise.
- Control board of identities and secrets: states, ages, uses, planned actions.
- Register of integrity: authorised artefacts, fingerprints, chain of approval.
- View of drift and vulnerabilities: ranked, with owners and delays.
- Map of outgoing traffic and anomalies: authorised paths, gaps, actions.
- Executable runbooks, model timelines of incident, and the log of decisions.
- KPI table: MTTD and MTTR, coverage, noise, false positives, delays of patching and rotation.
- Replacement process: how to disable or replace a rule, a runbook or a controller, with no debt
  and no hole in coverage.
