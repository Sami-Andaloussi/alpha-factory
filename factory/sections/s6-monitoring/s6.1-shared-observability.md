# S6.1 — Single source of truth: shared observability (SLIs, SLOs & baselines)

## TL;DR

Gives the factory one common, measurable and binding language about its operational reality: what is measured, what level is promised, and what is normal.
Every critical path, from data to P&L, gets a few SLIs, an SLO with its error budget, and baselines by hour, day and regime; alerts follow symptoms and budgets, at least 3 useful for 1 of noise.
Its SLO catalogue is what every other page of monitoring works from, and lifecycle's fitness functions, budgets and releases (S7.3 to S7.5).

## Purpose

Give the factory one common, measurable and binding language about its operational reality: what
is measured ([SLIs](../../glossary.md#sli)), what level the factory commits to
([SLOs](../../glossary.md#slo) and [error budgets](../../glossary.md#error-budget)), and what is
normal ([baselines](../../glossary.md#baseline) by hour, day and [regime](../../glossary.md#regime));
then connect those definitions to alerts and decisions. This is the factory's
[single source of truth](../../glossary.md#single-source-of-truth) for
[observability](../../glossary.md#observability).

**Success looks like:** less noise and more decisions. Everyone knows when things are fine, when
they deviate, who acts, and on what grounds.

<details>
<summary><b>What each flow carries, constraints and service levels</b></summary>

Every sub-section of the factory publishes its KPI feed here: from section 1 the
[point-in-time](../../glossary.md#point-in-time) flows, their timestamps, freshness and
completeness, and its catalogue of events; from section 2 the states of computation, the queues and
the versions of [features](../../glossary.md#feature) and [labels](../../glossary.md#label), for the
fidelity of signals; from sections 3 and 4 the figures of research and validation; from section 5
the states of the controllers ([rate limiters](../../glossary.md#rate-limiter), modes), the
rejections, fills and latencies; and from the other pages of section 6 their own health. The
certification dossier of S4.9 brings the reference versions, the ground truth for live against
simulated; the promotion record of S4.10 the specification of each strategy's instrumentation, its
metrics and alerts with their thresholds. [Drift](../../glossary.md#drift) detection (S6.4) brings
the baselines of normality of the markets and the models, which the catalogue versions beside those
of the SLIs. The trigger-action catalogue of lifecycle (S7.6) names the triggers to watch and their
windows, which the catalogue registers beside the rules of alert. The schema contracts of the
[data lifecycle](../../glossary.md#data-lifecycle) (S7.7) bring the service levels of each source
(latency, share of late data), which the catalogue registers as SLOs. The budgets of reliability and
risk (S7.4) choose the SLIs, the SLOs and the error budget of each critical flow, and send them
through their KPI feed; the catalogue registers them too.

The SLO catalogue holds the official definitions: the SLIs, the SLOs and error budgets, the
baselines, the rules of alert and escalation, and the dictionary of events with the schema of
instrumentation, which the platform (S6.8) enforces. Every other page of section 6 works from it;
lifecycle's [fitness functions](../../glossary.md#fitness-function) (S7.3) build their tests on it,
its reliability budgets (S7.4) read back from it the SLOs they chose, and its
[change management](../../glossary.md#change-management) (S7.5) the bands of guard of each release.
The monitoring signals, alerts centred on symptoms and budgets, go to stabilisation (S6.5) and to
[intervention](../../glossary.md#intervention) (S6.7); in lifecycle, the triggers of retraining,
[rollback](../../glossary.md#rollback) and withdrawal (repeated breaches, a baseline that drifts,
alerts drowned in noise) go to the lifecycle of models and strategies (S7.6), and every signal to
the loop that turns them into missions (S7.10); the [burn rates](../../glossary.md#burn-rate) and
the alerts on budgets go to lifecycle's reliability budgets (S7.4), and the alerts on costs,
[slippage](../../glossary.md#slippage) and incidents to its capital steering (S7.8). Section 4 reads
the same definitions to reproduce and judge; section 5 receives its promises of service and its
symptomatic alerts, on which [controlled degradation](../../glossary.md#controlled-degradation)
(S6.5) acts. For each steering review of lifecycle (S7.1), the page compiles the pre-read from the
KPI feeds, and delivers it 12 hours before.

**Constraints.**
- Unified formats of events: a common schema, units, time zone, correlated IDs.
- Latency of ingestion and emission: an SLO of freshness per source, with its p99 documented.

**Service levels:**
- Critical events (IPO, suspension, delisting): T90 / [T95](../../glossary.md#t95) of integration
  defined per market; an alert when exceeded.

</details>

## Gates

**Entry.** Start only when:
- the map of critical paths is validated by operations and the desk;
- reliable sources of timestamps and points of observation are identified.

**Exit.** Verdict *pass* when all of these hold:
- the SLIs, SLOs and baselines are versioned, accepted and connected to alerting;
- the acceptance report records successful [drills](../../glossary.md#drill), and the canonical
  minimal dashboard is in place.

*Retry* and *fail* follow the [gate verdict](../../glossary.md#gate-verdict) rule.

**Requalification.** Rerun the sub-section when:
- a baseline [drifts](../../glossary.md#drift) beyond its threshold;
- the attainment of an SLO falls over 2 consecutive periods;
- the ratio of useful alerts to noise falls below 2:1;
- the market changes (microstructure, regulation);
- a promising new method of estimation or timestamping appears.

Within section 6, the real burn rates refine the detectors of S6.4 and the controllers of S6.5.
Lifecycle (S7) receives the journal of changes, the triggers of retraining and
[rollback](../../glossary.md#rollback), and the debts of reliability, ranked.

## Steps

<details>
<summary><b>1 · Map the critical paths and the business view</b> — Design task</summary>

**Goal.** Before measuring, define what is vital: the paths from data to feature, signal, order,
fill and P&L, and the business views (freshness, latency, fidelity of simulated to real, costs).

**Actions.**
- List 5–10 critical paths, for example intraday: level 1, signal, marketable order, fill.
- For each path: its key events, timestamped points of attachment, dependencies (data, compute,
  network), and the windows of time that matter (the open, announcements).
- State the business questions to settle, for example "are we still within the p99 latency we can
  bear?".

**KPI.** Coverage of the critical paths ≥ 95% of the flow processed, by volume and by [alpha](../../glossary.md#alpha); open
ambiguities = 0 before step 2.

**Guardrail.** Against scope creep and blind spots: a table of scenarios validated by the desk and
production; a walkthrough of a "stress day" that must pass.

**Output.** Map of the critical paths and taxonomy of the business views → step 2.

</details>

<details>
<summary><b>2 · Define the SLIs, end to end and per segment, and their protocol of measure</b> — Design task</summary>

**Goal.** Move from paths to indicators that bind: definitions, units, windows, aggregations,
conditions.

**Actions.**
- For each path, choose 3–6 SLIs at most, mixing:
  - end to end, seen from outside: latency from signal to fill at p99 and p99.9; the gap between
    simulated and real in basis points over a daily window; freshness of data at p95 and p99;
  - per segment, seen from inside: delay of ingestion, rate of errors of computation, rejections
    of execution.
- Write for each SLI its exact formula, its unit, its window of aggregation, its dimensions of
  grouping (desk, [venue](../../glossary.md#venue), symbol, regime), its source of truth (the event that timestamps it), and
  its conditions of inclusion or exclusion (for example suspensions).
- Track dispersion by default: p95 and p99, a robust standard deviation, the interquartile range.
  Never a mean alone.

**KPI.** SLIs well defined (formula, unit, window, source, filters) = 100%; share of end-to-end
SLIs ≥ 40%; dispersion tracked (p95, p99) = 100%.

**Guardrail.** Against vanity metrics and leaks in time: point-in-time cross reviews, and a check of
each definition (no mean alone; the timestamps used are verified).

**Output.** Specification of the SLIs, one sheet per SLI → steps 3 and 4.

</details>

<details>
<summary><b>3 · Build the baselines, the profiles of normal, and their governance</b> — Design task</summary>

**Goal.** Give the context: normal on a Monday at 15:30 is not normal on an Asian night.

**Actions.**
- Segment by hour, day and regime (calm, newsy, high volatility), by observable criteria: realised
  volatility, median [spread](../../glossary.md#spread), volume.
- For each SLI and segment, extract reference profiles (robust quantiles, expected intervals) on a
  frozen window of history, so that nothing learnt is forgotten at once.
- Define a policy of update: its cadence (monthly, quarterly), its thresholds of re-learning (a
  drift detected), its validation by [replay](../../glossary.md#replay).

**KPI.** Stability of the baselines: the median gap from history below a threshold set per SLI; the
rate of drifts flagged, with few false positives (< 5% over a control month).

**Guardrail.** Against baselines that drift: frozen windows, and a [backtest](../../glossary.md#backtest)
on [out-of-sample](../../glossary.md#in-sample-out-of-sample) periods; sanity checks on extreme regimes.

**Output.** Versioned pack of baselines (per SLI and segment) and its policy of update → step 4.

</details>

<details>
<summary><b>4 · Set the SLOs, the error budgets and the logic of alert and paging</b> — Design task</summary>

**Goal.** Turn "we observe" into contracts: targets, tolerances, burn rate and escalations.

**Actions.**
- For each SLI, set a realistic SLO per path and segment, for example "end-to-end latency p99 <
  120 ms in the US session; < 180 ms overnight".
- Give each period (day, week, month) an error budget, and set thresholds of burn rate over several
  windows: a fast alert and a slow one.
- Set the severity (page or ticket) by business impact and by consumption of the budget; alerts
  are centred on symptoms, for example "end-to-end latency SLO burning ×4, US desk".

**KPI.** Attainment of the SLOs ≥ target per path; useful alerts to noise ≥ 3:1; time to
acknowledge < 5 min in active hours.

**Guardrail.** Against unrealistic SLOs: burn rates simulated on history, and a table of business
impact; a mandatory quarterly review.

**Output.** Table of SLOs and budgets, and the rules of alert and escalation → step 5, the
controllers of S6.5, the on-call of S6.7.

</details>

<details>
<summary><b>5 · Common instrumentation and correlation: logs, metrics, traces, events</b> — Execution task</summary>

**Goal.** Make every path observable, and every observation correlatable.

**Actions.**
- Set a dictionary of events: fields, units, time zone, precision, keys of correlation.
- Impose the propagation of a correlation ID end to end, from tick to fill.
- Normalise the timestamps: the source, the maximum skew, the strategy when clocks disagree.
- Define the retentions, the target cardinalities and the quotas, to control cost and volume.

**KPI.** Events correlatable end to end (the ID present throughout) ≥ 98%; skew between clocks
below its threshold, for example < 1 ms within a data centre and < X ms between sites.

**Guardrail.** Against an explosion of cardinality and cost: quotas and a controlled sampling,
validated by tests of detection.

**Output.** Schema and checklist of instrumentation, and the contracts of emission → step 6, the
platform (S6.8).

</details>

<details>
<summary><b>6 · Acceptance, the monitoring of monitoring, and governance</b> — Design task</summary>

**Goal.** Prove that monitoring sees right, and that it holds over time.

**Actions.**
- Tests of acceptance: synthetic probes on the critical paths, point-in-time replay, drills of
  latency and of load; check that the expected detections and alerts occur.
- A canonical minimal dashboard: one view per critical path, with no decorative sprawl.
- Governance: version the SLIs, SLOs and baselines; journal the changes; set the cadence of
  reviews (weekly for operations, monthly for reliability).

**KPI.** Tests of acceptance passed = 100%; a synthetic incident detected within the target
latency; versions compliant, every change traced.

**Guardrail.** Against blind monitoring: permanent synthetic probes and regular drills (monthly); a
review of the views that have become obsolete.

**Output.** The acceptance report, the cadence of review and the journal of versions → lifecycle
(S7, [requalifications](../../glossary.md#requalification)), intervention (S6.7, postmortems).

</details>

## Deliverables

- Specification of the SLIs, one sheet each: formula, unit, windows, sources, filters, dimensions.
- Table of SLOs and error budgets: targets per path and segment, burn rates and levels of alert.
- Pack of baselines: profiles per SLI and segment, the policy of update, dated versions.
- Dictionary of events and schema of instrumentation: fields, correlation IDs, rules of
  timestamps, quotas and retentions.
- Rules of alert and escalation: symptoms, severities, routing; who, when, how.
- Canonical minimal dashboard: one view per critical path, centred on SLI, SLO, baseline and burn
  rate, with nothing decorative.
- Acceptance report and plan of drills: synthetic probes, replays, recurring exercises.
- Log of choices and decisions, and the replacement process: how to propose, validate and deploy
  a new definition.
