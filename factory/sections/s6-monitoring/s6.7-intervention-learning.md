# S6.7 — Intervention & learning

## TL;DR

The control tower when things go wrong, and the living record that stops them from happening again.
It turns a business signal into coordinated action (stabilise, work around, cut off, communicate), then into lasting learning: what changes, who does what, by when.
It holds every runbook of the factory; its blameless postmortems go to lifecycle (S7.9), and its lessons back into monitoring and every section.

## Purpose

Be the control tower when things go wrong, and the living record that stops them from happening
again. The page takes a business signal (an abnormal execution, latency, doubtful data), turns it
into a coordinated action (stabilise, work around, cut off, communicate), then into lasting
learning: what was learnt, what changes, who does what, when. This is the factory's [intervention](../../glossary.md#intervention).

**Success looks like:** few alerts but right ones, a fast stabilisation, sober documentation,
actions closed, and a lower probability and severity for the next incidents.

<details>
<summary><b>What each flow carries, constraints and service levels</b></summary>

Every sub-section of the factory sends its runbooks here: the page is their one home, and tests
them. The SLO catalogue of S6.1 brings the [SLIs](../../glossary.md#sli), the
[SLOs](../../glossary.md#slo) and the [baselines](../../glossary.md#baseline). The monitoring
signals bring the symptoms: those of S6.1, of live
[execution fidelity](../../glossary.md#execution-fidelity) (S6.2), of data integrity (S6.3), of
[drift](../../glossary.md#drift) detection (S6.4) with its alert-to-action table, and of security
(S6.6). The decision logs of stabilisation (S6.5) and of security (S6.6) bring the actions already
taken and the timelines of their incidents. Section 4's execution simulator re-runs the orders of an
incident under other choices, to weigh what else could have been done. The explanation sheets of
lifecycle's system of record (S7.2) say why each trade under review was made. The incident charter
of [incident response](../../glossary.md#incident-response) (S7.9) frames every intervention: the
roles, the on-call, the one channel, the templates, and the matrix of engagement per level of
severity. The levers of section 5 (controllers, routes, sizing) are reached through the ladder of
modes of S6.5, which the incident commander activates through its action API.

The blameless postmortems go to lifecycle's incident response (S7.9). The requests to retrain, roll
back or withdraw a strategy for a time go, as monitoring signals, to the lifecycle of models and
strategies (S7.6). The timeline, the journal of decisions and the proof of the
[drills](../../glossary.md#drill) are the page's decision log, for lifecycle's system of record
(S7.2). The updates go back where they belong: SLOs and alerts to S6.1, modes and controllers to
S6.5, rules of integrity to S6.3, cases for counterfactual analysis to section 4, corrections of
integrity and [lineage](../../glossary.md#lineage) to section 1.

**Constraints.**
- Formats: timestamped and correlated (common tags); the journals of orders and executions are
  immutable.
- Consistency: one shared scale of severity, P1 to P4, and one standard classification of
  incidents.

**Service levels:**
- P1: from the symptom to the page, [T95](../../glossary.md#t95) ≤ 60 s; acknowledged (T_ack) at
  T95 ≤ 2 min; first update ≤ 5 min; back to safety (T_safe) at T95 ≤ 10 min, adapted per domain.
- Critical market events (suspensions, major halts): the rules and states integrated into the
  pipeline of alerts at T90 ≤ 2 min.

</details>

## Gates

**Entry.** Start only when:
- the baselines and SLOs of S6.1 are validated;
- the top risks are listed;
- the on-call roster and its substitutes are defined;
- at least 5 runbooks cover 80% of the probable scenarios;
- the timestamped journals are reliable.

**Exit.** Verdict *pass* when all of these hold:
- the [KPIs](../../glossary.md#kpi) of the steps are met 2 cycles in a row, a drill included;
- the catalogue of modes and the runbooks are tested;
- a template of postmortem is in place, and the backlog of actions is alive;
- the changelog and the procedure of [rollback](../../glossary.md#rollback) are active.

*Retry* and *fail* follow the [gate verdict](../../glossary.md#gate-verdict) rule.

**Requalification.** Rerun the sub-section when:
- a KPI drifts: the ratio of signal to noise falls, T_safe rises, relapses rise;
- a new strategy or model arrives, the market changes (regulation, microstructure), the
  infrastructure changes (latency, headroom), or a new security threat appears;
- automatically, when the same P1 pattern comes back, when [error budgets](../../glossary.md#error-budget) are breached again and
  again, or when a drill fails.

Within section 6, the page sends new thresholds and new signals, and adjusts the detectors.
Lifecycle (S7) receives the [gates](../../glossary.md#gate) of retraining and rollback, the criteria of release to
production, and the debt of reliability, ranked. Section 4 receives textbook cases for the
counterfactual analyses of its simulator and cost analysis; section 1 the corrections of integrity
and lineage.

## Steps

<details>
<summary><b>1 · Contract what pages: the business contracts of alert</b> — Design task</summary>

**Goal.** This is the base: without a clear contract, the rest is noise.

**Actions.**
- Define 10–15 business symptoms that deserve a page, for example "a gap of execution > X bps
  against simulated over N orders", or "a rate of rejections > Y% for Z min".
- Write the conditions of silence (recorded, with no page) and of a ticket (not urgent).
- Set the error budgets and SLOs that go with them.

**KPI.** Signal to noise (useful alerts over all alerts); the rate of business pages, as opposed to
purely technical ones; the coverage of the symptoms of the top risks.

**Guardrail.** Against storms of alerts: aggregation and hold-down, a window before re-arming,
explicit error budgets.

**Output.** The catalogue of prioritised alerts, with thresholds and severities → step 2
(governance and roles), step 3 (runbooks and modes), stabilisation (S6.5, the controllers).

</details>

<details>
<summary><b>2 · Orchestrate the intervention: roles, channel, ritual</b> — Design task</summary>

**Goal.** Alerts are only worth something if someone knows what to do, and how.

**Actions.**
- Name an incident commander (who decides), a technical operator (who acts) and a spokesperson (who
  communicates).
- One channel per incident, a timestamped timeline from T0, a journal of decisions.
- Define the scale of [incident severity](../../glossary.md#incident-severity) (P1 to P4) and the levels of escalation.

**KPI.** Time to acknowledge (T_ack) a P1 at T95 ≤ 2 min; latency of the announcement (the first
message of status) ≤ 5 min at T95.

**Guardrail.** A fair on-call rota, named substitutes, checklists to start an incident.

**Output.** The on-call of each incident, its timeline and its escalations, within the incident charter of S7.9 → step 3
(stabilisation), step 4 (diagnosis), communication inside and outside.

</details>

<details>
<summary><b>3 · Stabilise fast: runbooks and degraded modes</b> — Design task + Execution task</summary>

**Goal.** Orchestration without tested gestures stabilises nothing. Listing the modes and writing
the runbooks is design; testing them in drills is execution.

**Actions.**
- List the degraded modes ready to use, from the ladder of S6.5: execution made less aggressive,
  another [venue](../../glossary.md#venue), a data source isolated, the [kill switch](../../glossary.md#kill-switch) of one algorithm, a backoff, read-only.
- Write short runbooks per symptom: 3–7 steps at most, with the criteria to stop and to return.
- Test each runbook in a drill, and record its time.

**KPI.** Time back to the safe zone (T_safe) for a P1 at T95 ≤ 5–10 min, depending on the
perimeter; the impact avoided (bps, [drawdown](../../glossary.md#drawdown)), against the scenario with no mode.

**Guardrail.** Secured stop buttons; criteria of entry into and exit from each mode; two people
must agree before any destructive action.

**Output.** The catalogue of modes, the versioned runbooks and the proof of the drills → step 4
(factual investigation), stabilisation (S6.5, whose controllers execute), live execution fidelity
(S6.2, which observes).

</details>

<details>
<summary><b>4 · Diagnose from the facts: classification and cause</b> — Execution task</summary>

**Goal.** Diagnose once the factory is stable; before, the diagnosis makes things worse.

**Actions.**
- Classify the incident: market (volatility, [spread](../../glossary.md#spread), [liquidity](../../glossary.md#liquidity)), system (latency, load, errors),
  data (integrity, lineage), model (drift, [calibration](../../glossary.md#calibration)), security (identities, exfiltration,
  configuration).
- Correlate the logs, metrics, traces, orders and executions, on reliable timestamps.
- State the most parsimonious cause, with a degree of confidence and the options to verify it.

**KPI.** Time to a plausible cause (T_cause) for a P1 at T90 ≤ 2 h; the rate of diagnoses backed by
timestamped evidence ≥ 90%.

**Guardrail.** Synchronised timestamps required, one template of classification, a review by a
peer.

**Output.** The report of cause, with its artefacts (timestamped extracts, queries) and the standard
classification → step 5 (postmortem and actions), sections 3 and 4 (hypotheses and validation),
lifecycle (S7).

</details>

<details>
<summary><b>5 · Learn without blame: a short postmortem and a backlog of actions</b> — Execution task</summary>

**Goal.** The facts show what to change.

**Actions.**
- Write a blameless postmortem: what, when, the impact, what worked and what failed, and 3–7
  actions, each with an owner and a date.
- Tag each action: SLO or alert, controller or mode, data, model, security, process.
- Enter the actions in a prioritised backlog (impact against effort), and follow their closure.

**KPI.** Rate of actions closed by their due date ≥ 85%; rate of relapse of the same pattern at 30
days ≤ 5%.

**Guardrail.** A postmortem of 2 pages at most, no blame, and any action with no owner or no date
refused.

**Output.** A short postmortem and a timestamped plan of actions → step 6 (reintegration), S6.1 and
S6.8 ([observability](../../glossary.md#observability) and [monitoring as code](../../glossary.md#monitoring-as-code)), lifecycle (S7).

</details>

<details>
<summary><b>6 · Reintegrate the learning: rules, controllers, training, drills</b> — Design task + Execution task</summary>

**Goal.** Engrave the learning into the system. Deciding what changes is design; updating it, and
replaying the incident in a drill, is execution.

**Actions.**
- Update the SLOs and alerts, the runbooks, the modes, the controllers, the [guardrails](../../glossary.md#guardrail), the rules
  of data and the gates of deployment.
- Decide to retrain, to roll back or to withdraw for a time, through lifecycle.
- Schedule a drill that [replays](../../glossary.md#replay) the incident with the new guardrails, and measure the gain.

**KPI.** The gain measured, after against before (T_safe, signal to noise, drifts); the rate of
success of the drills ≥ 90%.

**Guardrail.** Validation in a sandbox before production, a window of rollback ready, a mandatory
changelog.

**Output.** A versioned changelog, the proof of the drill and the metrics before and after → every
section takes the update; the loop closes towards monitoring (S6) and lifecycle (S7).

</details>

## Deliverables

- Specification of intervention: contracts of alert, severities, escalations, roles, one channel.
- Catalogue of degraded modes, and short runbooks, versioned and tested.
- Templates: the timeline of an incident, the journal of decisions, the postmortem of 2 pages.
- KPI dashboard per step, and the error budgets.
- Backlog of actions with owners and dates, and the changelog of the updates.
- Replacement process (rollback), and the proof of the periodic drills.
