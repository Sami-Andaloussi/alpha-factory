# S6.4 — Drift & regime-change detection

## TL;DR

Spots early that the factory has left the world it expects, on the side of the market, the systems or the models, and puts it in the right mode before performance, costs or reliability degrade.
Its alerts are fast, right and useful: a short delay, few false positives, and a default action for each; no alert is ever raised bare.
Its signals switch the modes of stabilisation (S6.5), open incidents (S6.7) and ask lifecycle (S7.6, S7.10) to retrain, review or quarantine.

## Purpose

Spot early that the factory is no longer in the world it expects, on the side of the market, of the
systems or of the models, and put it in the right [operating mode](../../glossary.md#operating-mode) (caution, rerouting, smaller
sizes, retraining) *before* performance, costs or reliability degrade. This is the factory's watch
on [drift](../../glossary.md#drift) and on changes of [regime](../../glossary.md#regime).

**Success looks like:**
- alerts that are fast (a short delay), right (few false positives) and useful (each one triggers
  a clear action);
- a factory that stabilises without oscillation or over-reaction;
- lifecycle fed with clean signals to retrain or requalify.

<summary><b>What each flow carries, constraints and service levels</b></summary>

The SLO catalogue of S6.1 brings the normalised metrics and [SLIs](../../glossary.md#sli) of every critical path, with
traces and logs correlated by reliable timestamps and IDs, which the platform (S6.8) serves. Live
[execution fidelity](../../glossary.md#execution-fidelity) (S6.2) brings, through its KPI feed, the [slippage](../../glossary.md#slippage), the [markouts](../../glossary.md#markout) and the quality
of routing per [venue](../../glossary.md#venue) and strategy; data integrity (S6.3) the state of the [point-in-time](../../glossary.md#point-in-time) data, the
business invariants and the immutable journals. The market as it was comes through the as-of API
of S1.8. The regime adjustment of S5.5 gives the regime the factory trades under, the one source of
regime for monitoring: S6.4 does not decide it again, it watches whether the world still matches
it and its baselines. The KPI feeds of section 2 bring the drift of [features](../../glossary.md#feature) and [labels](../../glossary.md#label); those of
sections 3 and 4 the watchlists of research and validation; S5.2's the watchlist of [drawdowns](../../glossary.md#drawdown) that
coincide, S5.5's the stability of the regimes. The parameter registry of S2.1 gives the thresholds
and windows of sampling per asset and regime, whose changes it watches; the segment map of S2.6
the dated breaks that research found, a history of regimes to validate the detectors on.

The monitoring signals are the switches of mode: caution, throttling and
[circuit breakers](../../glossary.md#circuit-breaker) for stabilisation (S6.5), which owns the modes
and passes them to section 5 (normal, cautious, degraded, stopped, with their constraints);
structured alerts (symptom, severity, context, suggested action) for
[intervention](../../glossary.md#intervention) (S6.7); the regimes, as context, for live execution
fidelity (S6.2); and the events that call for retraining, review or
[quarantine](../../glossary.md#quarantine), with their reasons and artefacts, for the lifecycle of
models and strategies (S7.6) and the loop of missions (S7.10); the drifts of
[spreads](../../glossary.md#spread), volatility and [liquidity](../../glossary.md#liquidity) go to
capital steering (S7.8), which assesses [capacity](../../glossary.md#capacity) again. The
alert-to-action table goes to the controllers of S6.5 and to S6.7, with its runbooks. The baselines
of normality go to S6.1, which versions them in the SLO catalogue beside those of the SLIs.

**Constraints.**
- Format: every alert is a monitoring signal, the common schema of events (symptom, metric,
  period, severity, confidence, links to the traces).

**Service levels:**
- Detection end to end, from the first gap observed to the alert, per category, to calibrate per
  desk: critical technical, [T95](../../glossary.md#t95) ≤ a few tens of seconds; execution and
  venue, T95 ≤ 1–2 minutes; intraday market regime, T95 ≤ a few minutes.
- Integration of market events (IPO, suspension, delisting, a change of tick) until the baselines
  are updated: T90 ≤ 15 min, T95 ≤ 30 min; an alert when exceeded.

</details>

## Gates

**Entry.** Start only when:
- the SLIs and [SLOs](../../glossary.md#slo) are available and reliable (S6.1 validated);
- the live pipeline of cost analysis works (S6.2);
- the timestamps are unified, and the correlation IDs run end to end (S6.3);
- a set of historical incidents exists (a minimum per category), with a plan of synthetic stress.

**Exit.** Verdict *pass* when all of these hold:
- the reports of validation are signed: [time to detect](../../glossary.md#mttd-mttr), rate of
  false positives and [precision](../../glossary.md#precision-and-recall) within their budgets;
- the alert-to-action table is complete, and its runbooks are tested (a dry run and a role play of
  the on-call);
- the [canary release](../../glossary.md#canary-release) passed with no oscillation and no overload of alerts;
- the instrumentation is declared and versioned as [monitoring as code](../../glossary.md#monitoring-as-code).

*Retry* and *fail* follow the [gate verdict](../../glossary.md#gate-verdict) rule.

**Requalification.** Rerun the sub-section when:
- the budgets are breached again and again (false positives, delays);
- a new regime lasts, or a new venue, market rule or model enters production;
- a postmortem opens an action to change a detector or a baseline;
- at a minimum cadence: re-baselining monthly (market), quarterly (system), at each new model
  (machine learning).

Within section 6, the SLOs of S6.1 are adjusted, the controllers of S6.5 set, and the runbooks of
S6.7 enriched. Lifecycle (S7) receives the triggers of retraining, review and freeze, with the data
and the metrics to qualify them.

## Steps

<details>
<summary><b>1 · Define the scope of drift and the taxonomy of switches</b> — Design task</summary>

**Goal.** Without a common language: confusion, and alerts that serve nothing.

**Actions.**
- Map the families of drift:
  - market: [spread](../../glossary.md#spread), volatility, [liquidity](../../glossary.md#liquidity), impact, markout;
  - system: latency at p95, p99 and p99.9, errors, queues, saturation;
  - model: drift of the inputs or of the labels, a score that no longer relates to the results,
    decisions that become unstable.
- Define the target events: a switch of regime from calm to agitated, a venue that degrades, a
  critical chain that grows longer, a loss of [calibration](../../glossary.md#calibration).
- State simple invariants, for example consistency in time and the bounds of the business.

**KPI.** Coverage of the taxonomy: the % of syndromes covered, the % of metrics mapped.

**Guardrail.** Against ambiguous definitions: a cross review (trading, infrastructure, machine
learning), mandatory before the freeze.

**Output.** A dictionary of syndromes and symptoms, with formal definitions → step 2 (normality),
step 3 (detectors), S6.1 ([observability](../../glossary.md#observability)).

</details>

<details>
<summary><b>2 · Model a versioned normality: the expected profiles</b> — Design task</summary>

**Goal.** A gap can only be detected against a credible reference.

**Actions.**
- Segment normality by hour, day, asset, venue and season, and by regime: profiles on several
  scales.
- Set the expected ranges (robust quantiles, bands of tolerance, ratios without dimension) and the
  rules of exclusion (rare events, suspensions).
- Version the baselines, with their timestamp, their coverage and their justification.

**KPI.** Error of the forecast of normality (the median gap from the realised, per segment), how
much the reference moves per day, the rate of samples excluded.

**Guardrail.** Against a reference polluted by incidents: no period after an incident enters the
active baseline; strict [versioning](../../glossary.md#versioning), and a journal of the reasons.

**Output.** The reference of normality (a base of profiles) and its contract of reading (how to
interpret a gap) → step 3 (detection), the platform (S6.8), S6.1 (SLOs).

</details>

<details>
<summary><b>3 · Design the sequential detectors and the logic of aggregation</b> — Design task</summary>

**Goal.** With normality known, measure the gaps that last, not the noise.

**Actions.**
- Choose per symptom a family of detectors, on several horizons (short, medium, long): tests of a
  break in trend or in dispersion, sequential detectors with a threshold, scores of distance
  between distributions, checks of calibration.
- Set a minimum persistence (a dwell time), [hysteresis](../../glossary.md#hysteresis) and a refractory period, so that an alert
  does not flap on and off.
- Define how signals merge (by gravity and independence), and a scale of severity (info, warning,
  critical) with explicit criteria.

**KPI.** Delay of detection per category, median and at 95%; stability, the ratio of on and off
oscillations; coverage, the metrics actually watched against those planned.

**Guardrail.** Against seasonality left unhandled: hysteresis, persistence and a refractory period
are imposed; a minimum independence between the signals merged, their correlations checked.

**Output.** The specification of the detectors, the schemas of aggregation, and the initial
thresholds, documented → step 4 (actions), step 5 (validation), stabilisation (S6.5).

</details>

<details>
<summary><b>4 · Build the table of actions: from symptom to response</b> — Design task</summary>

**Goal.** An alert without a response is noise.

**Actions.**
- For each severity, define the default action and the degraded modes: a smaller size or
  frequency, another venue, the cautious mode, a partial freeze, or an escalation to a human.
- Specify who executes (an automaton or the on-call), the [guardrails](../../glossary.md#guardrail) (conditions of exit, a
  timeout, a [rollback](../../glossary.md#rollback)) and the [traceability](../../glossary.md#traceability) (a journal of actions).
- Write readable rules ("if… then… because…"), auditable and testable.

**KPI.** Rate of actionable alerts (> X%), time to the first action, delay to return to normal
after the action.

**Guardrail.** No bare alert: an alert is refused when it has no default action and no runbook.

**Output.** The alert-to-action table and its runbooks → the controllers of stabilisation (S6.5),
intervention (S6.7).

</details>

<details>
<summary><b>5 · Validate (replay, synthetic stress, shadow) and set the budgets of alert</b> — Design task + Execution task</summary>

**Goal.** Calibrate without implicit hypotheses, and prove that the alerts are useful. Setting the
budgets is design; running the [replays](../../glossary.md#replay) and the shadow is execution.

**Actions.**
- Replay annotated histories (real incidents, regimes) and synthetic scenarios (controlled stress)
  to measure the delay of detection, the false positives and the net impact of the actions on
  slippage, markout, P&L and latency.
- Run the detectors live in [shadow mode](../../glossary.md#shadow-mode), observing without acting, to check their stability on
  real flows.
- Set the budgets: false positives per week, target delays per category, a window of
  self-recovery after an action.

**KPI.** Time to detect, false positives per week, precision (the share of useful alerts), and the
net gain before and after an action on slippage, markout and P&L, on replay and in shadow.

**Guardrail.** Against doubtful annotations: a double annotation and a consensus; a synthetic
stress is required for the rare cases.

**Output.** The acceptance report (thresholds validated, budgets, known limits) → step 6
(deployment), lifecycle (the [gates](../../glossary.md#gate) of change, S7.5).

</details>

<details>
<summary><b>6 · Deploy progressively and close the loop of learning</b> — Execution task</summary>

**Goal.** Go to production without risking instability.

**Actions.**
- A canary release, then the general rollout, with a clear routing of alerts and gauges of the
  detectors' own health (coverage, frequency).
- Set the cadences: re-baselining, periodic re-evaluation, postmortems, and the triggers to
  lifecycle (retrain, review, freeze).
- Document the replacement process: disable, revise, restore.

**KPI.** Adherence to the budgets (alerts per week, delay at 95%), the rate of incidents with no
detection, the rate of successful rollbacks, the time of re-baselining.

**Guardrail.** Against alert fatigue: a budget of alerts per team, a progressive escalation that
removes the spam, and a [kill switch](../../glossary.md#kill-switch) that cuts a detector's logic over to its [fallback](../../glossary.md#fallback) when it
oscillates.

**Output.** Detectors active, budgets watched, a loop of improvement in place → lifecycle (S7.6,
[requalifications](../../glossary.md#requalification)), intervention (S6.7, postmortems), the controllers of stabilisation (S6.5).

</details>

## Deliverables

- Specification of the taxonomy, the target events and the invariants.
- Versioned reference of normality: profiles, rules of exclusion, journal of updates.
- Catalogue of detectors: families, horizons, thresholds, hysteresis, merging, severity.
- The alert-to-action table and the runbooks: who does what, in how long, with which conditions of
  exit.
- Acceptance report (replay, synthetic stress, shadow), with the budgets frozen.
- [KPI](../../glossary.md#kpi) tracking table: time to detect, false positives, precision, oscillations, return to normal
  after an action, coverage.
- Log of decisions: choices, reasons, alternatives rejected.
- Replacement process: deactivation, rollback, plan of transition.
