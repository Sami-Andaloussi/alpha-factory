# S4.4 — Multi-axis robustness & sensitivity (statistical and engineering)

## TL;DR

Checks that a signal or a strategy holds when it is shaken: dirtier data, worse costs, thinner liquidity, slower latency, imperfect labels, other regimes.
It delivers a readable sensitivity profile, tolerances in numbers, a robustness index that must stay high under weak and medium stress, and a drift watchlist.
The package goes to execution simulation and cost analysis, to section 5 through the promotion gate, and to monitoring.

## Purpose

Check that a signal or a strategy holds when it is shaken: slightly dirty data, slightly worse
costs, [liquidity](../../glossary.md#liquidity) that dries up, latency that moves, imperfect
labelling, different market [regimes](../../glossary.md#regime). This is section 4's own
measurement, independent of the [robustness](../../glossary.md#robustness) section 3 built into the strategy: S3 improves, S4
judges.

**Success looks like:** a readable profile of sensitivity, tolerances in numbers, a robustness
index and a [drift](../../glossary.md#drift) watchlist, gathered in the
[robustness report](../../glossary.md#robustness-report). It goes to the [execution simulator](../../glossary.md#execution-simulator) and
cost analysis, to test execution; to section 5, to set the exposure; and to monitoring, to watch
the fragile points live.

<details>
<summary><b>What each flow carries, constraints and service levels</b></summary>

The selection report brings the promoted candidates with their frozen parameters. The experiment
passport names the versions of the [point-in-time](../../glossary.md#point-in-time) data and the
locked [in-sample](../../glossary.md#in-sample-out-of-sample) and out-of-sample partitions, and the

constraints); the perturbations start from those versions, read as S1.8's sealed batches. The
simulation plan brings the base scenarios of costs and liquidity. The robustness report holds the
profile of sensitivity (slopes, interactions), the robustness index, the tolerances, the map of
regimes and the confidence intervals by blocks; with it go the execution hypotheses to test in the
execution simulator and cost analysis, and the recommendations of exposure and
[capacity](../../glossary.md#capacity) by regime, which section 5 receives through
[S4.10](s4.10-promotion-gates.md). Monitoring receives the drift watchlist (variables, thresholds,
windows, recipients), S6.4 for drift. Section 1's data quality (S1.5) receives the tolerances of
data and annotation as quality rules, as feedback.

**Constraints.**
- Strict [reproducibility](../../glossary.md#reproducibility): versions, seeds, partitions.
- Consistency in time: no refit after a stress.

**Service levels:**
- The complete robustness package within the same research cycle as the validated [backtest](../../glossary.md#backtest) (for
  example, one working day at most), or before the handover to execution simulation and cost
  analysis for an intraday strategy.
- Critical events (IPO, suspension, a market rule): new short stresses integrated at T90 / [T95](../../glossary.md#t95),
  for example within 24–48 h; otherwise an alert to monitoring.

</details>

## Gates

**Entry.** Start only when:
- the dataset is validated, free of [leakage](../../glossary.md#leakage);
- the partitions are frozen;
- the base performance is already proven, with credible out-of-sample values;
- the parameters are frozen.

**Exit.** Verdict *pass* when all of these hold:
- the robustness index is above the threshold set for the weak and medium stresses;
- stability per regime: the performance is acceptable in most states, and the weaknesses are
  documented;
- the tolerances on costs, liquidity, latency, noise and [labels](../../glossary.md#label) are explicit, with confidence
  intervals by blocks;
- the drift watchlist is delivered and connected at monitoring, and the package of hypotheses is
  sent to execution simulation and cost analysis.

*Retry* and *fail* follow the [gate verdict](../../glossary.md#gate-verdict) rule.

**Requalification.** Rerun the sub-section when:
- a variable of the watchlist [drifts](../../glossary.md#drift) (a red alert);
- costs, microstructure, latency or the universe change materially;
- a new module of data or labelling arrives, or the upstream pipeline changes;
- the market regime leaves the defined reference (detected by monitoring);
- an execution incident reveals a sensitivity not covered.

Monitoring (S6) receives the watchlist, its thresholds and windows, and the events that trigger
an alert. Lifecycle (S7) receives the criteria of periodic re-test, for example at each major
change of regime or update of the data. Section 5 receives the capacity steps and the conditional
rules of exposure.

## Steps

<details>
<summary><b>1 · Frame what will be stressed, and how it will be measured</b> — Design task</summary>

**Goal.** Without a clear scope and metrics that outliers cannot sway, we read luck.

**Actions.**
- List the realistic shocks: costs up, liquidity down, latency up, a shift or error in labelling,
  noise on [features](../../glossary.md#feature), gaps in quotes, suspensions.
- Define robust measures, versions of the performance and error measures that a few extreme
  points cannot break, and a common robustness index, for example the median of the out-of-sample
  results over their interquartile range.
- Fix target tolerances per type of shock (weak, medium, strong), and alert thresholds.

**KPI.** Gap between naive and robust performance: it must stay moderate; if it is huge, dominant
outliers are suspected. Tolerance to contamination: the share of extreme points needed to flip
the sign of the performance; aim for a threshold that is not trivial.

**Guardrail.** No retuning after the stresses are read: the model is evaluated, not an adjusted
cousin.

**Output.** Stress charter and robust measures → every following step, and in the end the
execution simulator, cost analysis and monitoring.

</details>

<details>
<summary><b>2 · Cut by regime and set the reference scenarios</b> — Design task</summary>

**Goal.** Sensitivity only makes sense when it is anchored in market states.

**Actions.**
- Define the states: calm or volatile, rising or falling, high or low liquidity, tight or wide
  [spreads](../../glossary.md#spread), and so on.
- Build the base scenarios per regime: no stress, the reality of the past.

**KPI.** Coverage of regimes: each state has enough observations. Stability per regime: the share
of regimes where the performance stays at or above the acceptance threshold.

**Guardrail.** Windows that are too short are classified "inconclusive": no conclusion on an
under-sampled regime.

**Output.** Map of regimes and base scenarios → step 3 (structured stresses), step 6 (synthesis);
section 5 uses it to set the exposure per state.

</details>

<details>
<summary><b>3 · Structured stress plans, from simple to combined</b> — Design task</summary>

**Goal.** A readable map of sensitivity: one variable at a time first, then realistic
combinations.

**Actions.**
- Simple grid: costs up, latency up, liquidity down, noise on features, shifted signals, one at a
  time.
- Combined grid: plausible pairs and triads, for example costs up, liquidity down and latency up
  together.
- Measure the slopes of sensitivity and the interactions; isolate the levers that really weigh.

**KPI.** Robustness index per stress (for example the median over the interquartile range): it
must stay high under weak and medium stress. Concentration of the degradation: no single factor
explains almost all of the drop; otherwise the fragility is structural. Tolerance to costs: a
shock of costs of +10% to +50% must not wipe out the performance from the first notches.

**Guardrail.** No [stress test](../../glossary.md#stress-test) for show: include unpleasant
scenarios, such as spreads widening, imbalances in the book, delays in quotes.

**Output.** Map of sensitivity (factors ranked, red, orange and green zones) → execution
simulation and cost analysis (realism of execution), section 5 (capacity, exposure).

</details>

<details>
<summary><b>4 · Gentle perturbations of the data and the pipeline</b> — Design task</summary>

**Goal.** Test the resilience to natural noise: small, realistic defects of data and labelling.

**Actions.**
- Inject micro-noise on features (a controlled jitter), a slight shift or error on labels, and
  micro-delays on signals.
- Check that the ranking of ideas and parameters does not flip at the slightest breath.

**KPI.** Rate of inversion of sign under micro-perturbations: it must stay low. Elasticity to
jitter: the levels of noise or error supported before the drop, to be recorded.

**Guardrail.** No leakage when the perturbations are created: strict respect of point-in-time
and of the partitions.

**Output.** Tolerances of data and annotation → exported as quality rules to section 1 (data
foundation), and as drift thresholds to monitoring (S6).

</details>

<details>
<summary><b>5 · Honest uncertainty through resampling in time</b> — Design task</summary>

**Goal.** Without realistic confidence intervals, we tell ourselves stories.

**Actions.**
- Resample by blocks of time, which preserves dependence, to estimate the true dispersion of the
  performance under each stress.
- Compare the base scenarios with the stresses: intervals, and the probability of a positive
  performance.

**KPI.** Width of the confidence intervals: contained relative to the level of risk targeted.
Share of resamples in which the performance stays at or above the threshold: aim for a robust
majority.

**Guardrail.** The length of block is tested over several reasonable choices; if the results are
unstable, the reading is flagged "high uncertainty".

**Output.** Honest confidence intervals and probabilities that the performance holds → S4.10
([promotion gates](../../glossary.md#promotion-gate)) and section 5 (allocation).

</details>

<details>
<summary><b>6 · Operational synthesis and the drift watchlist</b> — Execution task</summary>

**Goal.** Turn the tests into rules that can be acted on.

**Actions.**
- Compile the global robustness index, the tolerances per factor, the regimes "OK" or "fragile",
  and the five main fragilities.
- Produce the drift watchlist: variables, thresholds, alert windows, owners.
- Formalise the recommendations of capacity and exposure, and the execution hypotheses to send to
  the execution simulator and cost analysis.

**KPI.** Completeness of the package: every tolerance and regime covered.
[Traceability](../../glossary.md#traceability): each number points to a reproducible test.

**Guardrail.** A cross audit by someone not involved in the initial research, against
confirmation bias.

**Output.** Robustness report and watchlist → section 5 (through S4.10), S4.6 and S4.7 (execution
simulation, cost analysis), S6 (monitoring), S7 (lifecycle).

</details>

## Deliverables

- Stress specification: shocks, levels, regimes, robust metrics.
- Map of sensitivity, simple and combined, with clear readings.
- Global robustness index and tolerances per factor: costs, liquidity, latency, noise, labels.
- Confidence intervals by resampling in time: method, choice of blocks, diagnostics.
- Map of regimes, and a synthesis: "OK", "fragile", "to avoid".
- Drift watchlist: variables, thresholds, windows, owners, plan of escalation.
- Log of decisions: hypotheses accepted or refused, reasons, expected impacts.
- Full traceability: versions of data and partitions, seeds, scripts of tests; nothing ad hoc.
- Handover note to execution simulation, cost analysis and section 5: execution hypotheses to
  test, capacity steps, conditions of allocation.
