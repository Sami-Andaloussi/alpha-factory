# S4.6 — Execution simulator (XSIM): microstructure, calibration, verification & validation

## TL;DR

Builds an execution twin that turns a theoretical performance into an executable one: costs, fills and delays for a given style of orders, at size and by regime.
It is credible when the forecast cost stays within 10–20 bps of the measured cost in median at the target size, with a relative error of at most 15% over several days.
Its capacity-to-cost curves and execution recipes feed the operating contract (S4.5), cost analysis (S4.7), paper trading (S4.8) and the promotion gate (S4.10).

## Purpose

Build an execution twin, the [execution simulator](../../glossary.md#execution-simulator) ([XSIM](../../glossary.md#xsim)),
that turns a theoretical performance into an executable one: it predicts costs, fills and delays
for a given style of orders, at size and by [regime](../../glossary.md#regime) (the open, news,
calm).

**Success looks like:** a simulator that is credible, reproducible and can be recalibrated, whose
gaps to real costs stay within known tolerances and stable out of sample: reliable enough to set
sizes, [slippage](../../glossary.md#slippage) budgets and rules of execution before any capital is
exposed.

execution and the hypotheses to test. The cost attribution report of S4.7 brings the measured
costs that recalibrate the simulator: the two pages form the closed loop between forecast and
measure. The execution profile holds the capacity-to-cost curves, the execution recipes per
strategy and regime, the slippage budgets and the tolerances. Section 5 receives them through the
[promotion gate](../../glossary.md#promotion-gate) of [S4.10](s4.10-promotion-gates.md); its [execution chain](../../glossary.md#execution-chain) (S5.7)
and its loop from execution back to decisions (S5.8) use the calibrated simulator itself, and [intervention](../../glossary.md#intervention) (S6.7) re-runs
the orders of an incident on it, under other choices. Monitoring receives
the tables of forecast against measured (S6.2) and the [drift](../../glossary.md#drift) watchlists (S6.4); lifecycle (S7)
the log of [requalifications](../../glossary.md#requalification).

**Constraints.**
- Formats: ordered events, normalised time zones, unified identifiers of venues.
- Consistency: no future data (point-in-time), positive latencies, monotonic timestamps.

**Service levels:**
- Simulator parameters recomputed at T90 ≤ 24 h after major new stylised facts, for example a
  lasting change of [spread](../../glossary.md#spread).
- Critical events: [T95](../../glossary.md#t95) ≤ 4 h to integrate a suspension or an exceptional auction, a change of tick
  size, a new rule of a venue; an alert when exceeded.

</details>

## Gates

**Entry.** Start only when:
- replayable data is validated (section 1);
- the partitions in time are clean (S4.1);
- the base metrics of cost analysis are defined (S4.7);
- the execution intentions are documented (section 3).

**Exit.** Verdict *pass* when all of these hold:
- the [KPIs](../../glossary.md#kpi) of step 4 are within tolerance over ≥ 2 regimes (calm, volatile) and ≥ 2 target sizes;
- the capacity-to-cost curves and the execution recipes are published for section 5 (through
  S4.10), with bands of uncertainty and conditions of use;
- the loop with cost analysis is connected ([paper trading](../../glossary.md#paper-trading) at least), and its triggers are active
  (step 6).

*Retry* and *fail* follow the [gate verdict](../../glossary.md#gate-verdict) rule.

**Requalification.** Recalibrate (step 3), revalidate (step 4) and republish (step 5), with a log
kept for section 7, when:
- spread, depth or latency [drift](../../glossary.md#drift) by more than 2σ over 1 week;
- the gap in [implementation shortfall](../../glossary.md#implementation-shortfall) leaves a 15%
  band over 3 days;
- a market rule changes;
- a new style of orders appears;
- sizes double.

Monitoring (S6) receives the watchlists, the alert thresholds and the tables of forecast against
measured. Lifecycle (S7) receives the cadence of recalibration, the versions of parameters, and the
criteria of obsolescence and replacement.

## Steps

<details>
<summary><b>1 · A frame of microstructure reality: the specification</b> — Design task</summary>

**Goal.** Without explicit rules of the market, everything else floats.

**Actions.**
- List the matching rules (price-time priority, auctions, suspensions), the tick sizes, the
  visible depths and the order types allowed.
- Define the latencies (decision, network, venue), the queues (position in the queue, ageing and
  refresh) and the intraday regimes (open, midday, close, announcements).
- Choose the quantities the simulator measures: implementation shortfall, effective against
  realised cost, fill rate and delay, temporary and permanent impact kept apart.

**KPI.** Coverage of the specification (rules and metrics) ≥ 95% of the items listed; residual
ambiguities = 0.

**Guardrail.** The risk is forgetting micro-rules such as auctions and suspensions. Control: a
mandatory signed checklist and a cross review.

**Output.** Complete microstructure specification and dictionary of metrics → step 2 (the
simulator's core), step 3 ([calibration](../../glossary.md#calibration)).

</details>

<details>
<summary><b>2 · The simulator's core and its instrumentation: engine and traces</b> — Design task</summary>

**Goal.** The rules become an engine that can be traced.

**Actions.**
- Implement a replayable [order book](../../glossary.md#order-book) with timestamped events (add,
  cancel, trade) and latencies applied.
- Expose an interface for an execution agent (slicing, pace, choice of order, replace or cancel)
  and a router across venues, lit and dark.
- Add [deterministic](../../glossary.md#deterministic) logging (seeds, states of the book, decisions, timestamps), modes of
  [replay](../../glossary.md#replay), and checks of units on the metrics.

**KPI.** [Reproducibility](../../glossary.md#reproducibility): the same seed gives the same fills,
100%. Latency applied: decision → fill ≥ the declared minimum latency.

**Guardrail.** The risk is unrealistic fills, at a "magic" mid. Control: assertions on the rules of
the book, and tests of impossibility (no fill at mid without crossing).

**Output.** Working simulator engine, auditable logs and sets of unit tests → step 3
(calibration), step 4 ([verification and validation](../../glossary.md#verification-and-validation)).

</details>

<details>
<summary><b>3 · Calibrate on stylised facts, with their uncertainties</b> — Design task</summary>

**Goal.** Without an empirical anchor, a simulator "sounds right" but predicts wrong.

**Actions.**
- Record on historical data the intraday profiles (spread, depth, volumes, arrival rates of
  orders), the sizes at the best price, the times spent in the queue and the reactions of price.
- Tune the simulator to reproduce shapes and orders of magnitude; attach a band of uncertainty to
  each parameter ([calibration](../../glossary.md#calibration) = value ± margin).
- Document the regimes (calm, volatile, event) and the steps of size (as a fraction of average
  daily volume), each with its own parameters.

**KPI.** Shape and order of magnitude of the stylised facts: median relative error on spread and
depth ≤ 10%; correlation of the intraday profiles ≥ 0.8; times in the queue, simulated median over
real median ∈ [0.8; 1.25].

**Guardrail.** The risk is over-calibration. Control: keep the bands on the parameters, and
validate on periods not used for calibration.

**Output.** Calibrated sets of parameters per regime, their uncertainties, and a check of the
stylised facts → step 4 (validation), step 5 (campaigns).

</details>

<details>
<summary><b>4 · Verification and validation of credibility</b> — Design task</summary>

**Goal.** Prove that the code does what the model says (verification) and that the model matches
reality (validation): [verification and validation](../../glossary.md#verification-and-validation).

**Actions.**
- Verification: consistency tests (conservation of volumes, queues, latency applied), controlled
  cases (a frozen book, a single order), reproducibility (seeds).
- Validation: compare the simulator with real measures, from [paper trading](../../glossary.md#paper-trading)
  or a light live run, through the gaps in costs, the fill rates and delays, and the slopes of cost
  against size per regime.
- Plans of experiments: stress on several axes (tick size, depth, latency, competing churn),
  reduction of variance, and explicit bounds of acceptance.

**KPI.** Gap between forecast and measured cost (implementation shortfall): median ≤ 10–20 bps at
the target size, and relative error ≤ 15% over several days.

**Guardrail.** The risk is "it works" on one week only. Control: stability out of sample (low and
high volatility, different weeks), and tests of elasticity when the size doubles.

**Output.** Report of verification and validation (pass or fail per test) and tolerances of
validity per regime → step 5 (execution campaigns), the [gates](../../glossary.md#gate).

</details>

<details>
<summary><b>5 · Execution campaigns: capacity and cost</b> — Design task + Execution task</summary>

**Goal.** Turn credibility into decisions of size and method. Defining the scenarios is design;
running the batteries is execution.

**Actions.**
- Define the scenarios (a normal day, an announcement, a flash of volatility) × the styles of order
  (more or less urgent) × the sizes (in steps).
- Launch batteries of runs to draw the capacity-to-cost curves, the profiles of fills and delays,
  and heat maps of cost × size × moment.
- Extract the execution recipes (pace, orders, routing) and the slippage budgets per strategy and
  regime.

**KPI.** Capacity-to-cost curves that are smooth (monotonic), with intervals that do not overlap
between styles of order; uncertainty shown as intervals around each curve.

**Guardrail.** The risk is conclusions that hang on a single scenario. Control: plans of
experiments on several axes, and replications by seed and by day.

**Output.** Curves and recipes ready to integrate, with their margins of error: the
[execution profile](../../glossary.md#execution-profile) → section 5 (through S4.10), step 6 (the
loop with cost analysis).

</details>

<details>
<summary><b>6 · The loop between cost analysis, recalibration and requalification</b> — Design task + Execution task</summary>

**Goal.** Without ground truth, the simulator drifts. Running the loop is execution; revising the
structure when a KPI leaves its band is design.

**Actions.**
- Connect the cost analysis (paper trading, then a light live run): compare forecast with
  measured (costs, fills, delays) continuously.
- Define the triggers: as soon as a KPI leaves its band, recalibrate (the parameters) or revise
  (the structure), then revalidate (step 4).
- Push drift watchlists (latency, spread, depth) to section 6, and the rules of the cycle of
  [requalification](../../glossary.md#requalification) to section 7.

**KPI.** Alerts of forecast ≠ measured caught within T+1 day; time to recalibrate ≤ T+3 business
days; back within the band in ≤ 2 iterations.

**Guardrail.** The risk is letting it drift (latency, market rules). Control: automatic triggers
on the spread at T90, the median latency, the depth at the best price and the rejections of
orders.

**Output.** Tables of drift, recalibration jobs and a log of decisions → S6 (monitoring), S7
(lifecycle), the exit gate.

</details>

## Deliverables

- Versioned microstructure specification: rules, metrics, regimes.
- Simulator engine with its tests of verification (unit tests, controlled scenarios) and
  deterministic logging.
- Calibrated parameters per regime, their bands of uncertainty, and the check of stylised facts.
- Report of verification and validation (tests, results, tolerances, pass or fail) and the
  procedure of re-test.
- Library of scenarios, capacity-to-cost curves and documented execution recipes.
- KPI dashboard (forecast against measured, fills, delays, elasticity), connected to cost
  analysis.
- Log of decisions (recalibrations, [rollbacks](../../glossary.md#rollback), conditions of use) and a clear replacement process.
