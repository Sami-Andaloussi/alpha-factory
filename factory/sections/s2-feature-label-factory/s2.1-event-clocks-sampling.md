# S2.1 — Event clocks & sampling design

## TL;DR

Decides when the factory takes an observation: an event clock that samples on market activity, not on the wall clock.
Publishes, per asset and regime, a causal and stable event index with its intervals [t0, t1], its parameters and its serving contracts.
That index is the unit of work for labels, weights, validation splits and everything downstream.

## Purpose

Decide *when* to take an observation. Build an event frame, the [event index](../../glossary.md#event-index),
that captures useful activity and avoids the noise of the wall clock ([event clock](../../glossary.md#event-clock)).

**Success looks like:** for each asset and [regime](../../glossary.md#regime), a published event
index that is causal, stable and alignable across assets, with its intervals
[[t0, t1]](../../glossary.md#event-window), its parameters per regime and its integration
contracts. It becomes the shared unit of work for [labels](../../glossary.md#label) (S2.2), weights and overlaps (S2.3),
[temporal integrity](../../glossary.md#temporal-integrity) (S2.4), the strategy lab (S3), validation (S4), execution (S5) and monitoring
(S6).

<details>
<summary><b>What each flow carries, constraints and service levels</b></summary>

The sealed batches bring [point-in-time](../../glossary.md#point-in-time) prices, volumes, trades and, where available, quotes and
[order books](../../glossary.md#order-book); halt and suspension logs, and the official [trading calendars](../../glossary.md#trading-calendar); latency and quality metadata; and the normalisers
(notional value, current volatility, tick sizes). The event index includes its intervals and the
map that aligns the clocks of different assets. Sections 3 to 5 receive it through the registry
and serving contracts of [S2.10](s2.10-feature-registry.md).

**Constraints.**
- Strict formats: mandatory fields, types, units, time zones.
- Causality checked at input and at output.
- [Train/serve parity](../../glossary.md#train-serve-parity): the same logic, parameters and
  normalisations in research and in production.

**Service levels** (guides, agnostic to the asset class):
- Real-time decision class: [T95](../../glossary.md#t95) publication under half the downstream decision interval.
- Near-real-time class: T95 under one downstream decision interval.
- Batch class: T95 within the batch window.
- Critical events (IPO, suspension, delisting): T90 integration within a defined operational
  window (for example before the next session), T95 within the same window; an alert otherwise.

</details>

## Gates

**Entry.** Start only when:
- the data from S1 is validated: quality, timestamps, calendars, halts;
- the clock charter and its invariants (step 1) are approved.

**Exit.** Verdict *pass* when all of these hold:
- event index v1 is published, with its contracts, its parameters and active monitoring;
- the [KPIs](../../glossary.md#kpi) of steps 1, 3, 4 and 6 are green over test periods, and the evaluation report of step 5
  is archived;
- the [fallback](../../glossary.md#fallback) is documented and tested.

*Retry* and *fail* follow the [gate verdict](../../glossary.md#gate-verdict) rule.

**Requalification.** Rerun the exit checks when:
- event density [drifts](../../glossary.md#drift) outside its target band over *n* sessions;
- the [coefficient of variation](../../glossary.md#coefficient-of-variation) of volatility per [bar](../../glossary.md#bar) rises beyond its alert;
- the microstructure changes: new trading hours, tick size, halt rules;
- train/serve parity fails repeatedly;
- monitoring (S6) signals an inconsistency between live and simulated behaviour.

Monitoring (S6) receives the clock's metrics (density, coefficient of variation, overlaps,
latency) and their alert thresholds. Lifecycle (S7) receives the decision logs, the recalibration
cadence, the replacement criteria and the lessons learned for its update policies.

## Steps

<details>
<summary><b>1 · Frame and invariants of the clock</b> — Design task</summary>

**Goal.** Set what the clock may see (strictly causal), the microstructure boundaries (opens,
halts, auctions), and the unit of activity that makes sense for each asset family.

**Actions.**
- Formalise the invariants: causality, point-in-time, monotonic timestamps, source timestamp
  first, handling of gaps and duplicates, time zones and official calendars.
- Define the open families of triggers, without freezing their implementation:
  1. clock: pure time;
  2. activity: quantity traded, notional value, number of trades;
  3. price event: a move beyond a threshold relative to recent amplitude, or small moves
     accumulated until a break;
  4. hybrids: combinations, with guards on minimum and maximum cadence.
- State the constraints for aligning several assets: a common time reference and an alignment
  map.

**KPI.** Causal violations detected by the tests: target 0, alert above 0.

**Guardrail.** Systematic "time-travel" tests: every variable used by a trigger must exist at or
before *t0*.

**Output.** Clock charter and invariants, as a document and flow schemas → S2.2, S2.3, S2.4, S3,
S4 (shared basis), S6 (checks), S7 (governance).

</details>

<details>
<summary><b>2 · Design the triggers and their normalisations</b> — Design task</summary>

**Goal.** Triggers comparable across assets and regimes, so that an illiquid asset and a very
liquid one are treated consistently.

**Actions.**
- For each family, define the input signal (activity or price), the normalisation scale (notional
  value, recent volatility, unit cost), an anti-burst bound (minimum spacing) and a silence cap
  (maximum spacing, to cover calm periods).
- Plan adaptive thresholds per regime (typical day, session volatility, event sessions), without
  looking ahead.
- Specify the "calm zones" strategy: add low-cadence coverage points when nothing happens, so
  that models also learn what life outside the storm looks like.

**KPI.** Comparability across assets: the coefficient of variation
of intensity per bar at the normalised scale; alert above 30–40%.

**Guardrail.** Scale-fairness tests: the same normalised trigger must produce close distributions
across assets of the same class.

**Output.** Specification of the triggers and normalisations, per asset and regime segment → step
3, S2.3 (weights and overlaps), S6 (monitored thresholds).

</details>

<details>
<summary><b>3 · Generate the event index and its artefacts</b> — Execution task</summary>

**Goal.** Produce the event times *t0* and the intervals [t0, t1] per asset, and the alignment map
across assets.

**Actions.**
- Apply the triggers to the point-in-time data of S1 to produce the raw event index.
- Build the intervals [t0, t1], up to the next event or a maximum bound.
- Emit the alignment map: correspondences between the clocks of the assets, keyed by reference
  timestamp.
- Tag the metadata: source, arrival latency, estimated regime, microstructure flags.

**KPI.** Structural integrity: strictly increasing *t0*, zero duplicates, calendar coverage > 99%.

**Guardrail.** "Broken market" test sets (open, halt, resumption): no event is ever created
during a [halt](../../glossary.md#trading-halt).

**Output.** Event index v0, with intervals, alignment map and metadata → S2.2 (labels), S2.3
(overlaps), S2.4 (splits), S3 and S4 (datasets), S5 (ingestion), S6 (surveillance).

</details>

<details>
<summary><b>4 · Calibrate the parameters causally, per asset and regime</b> — Design task</summary>

**Goal.** Choose thresholds that make the intensity per event more homogeneous than in pure time,
without over-tuning to history.

**Actions.**
- Calibrate on past windows (expanding or rolling) and per type of session; never on the
  evaluation period.
- Target bar objectives (dispersion of intra-bar volatility, dispersion of activity per bar) and
  density bounds, to avoid under- and over-sampling.
- Set simple stopping rules (few parameters, minimal smoothing) to prevent
  [overfitting](../../glossary.md#overfitting).

**KPI.** Homogeneity of intensity: coefficient of variation of volatility per bar < 0.30 (alert ≥
0.40). Useful density: number of events per session within a target band, for example between
200 and 800 for a typical intraday session, adapted per asset class.

**Guardrail.** Stability across regimes: the ratio of each KPI between the extreme regimes ≤ 1.5;
alert otherwise.

**Output.** [Parameter registry](../../glossary.md#parameter-registry): tables per asset and
regime, and their update rules → step 5 (evaluation), S6 (recalibrations), S7 (governance).

</details>

<details>
<summary><b>5 · Compare the candidate clocks and choose one per asset</b> — Design task</summary>

**Goal.** Prove that the chosen clock captures information better than simple time bars, at
equal complexity.

**Actions.**
- Compare several candidate clocks on periods not used for [calibration](../../glossary.md#calibration).
- Measure:
  1. the reduction of [autocorrelation](../../glossary.md#autocorrelation) of returns (|ρ1| under
     a threshold, for example ≤ 0.1);
  2. the future overlap rate of the intervals [t0, t1] (alert above 35–40%);
  3. information efficiency: the relative gain on neutral proxies, such as the variance of short
     future returns explained, or the score of a very simple classifier of constant complexity;
  4. estimated cost and latency against marginal value.
- Rank the clocks, choose a default per asset and regime, and document the fallback conditions
  (back to pure time if performance degrades).

**KPI.** Lift over the [baseline](../../glossary.md#baseline): an improvement on at least 3 of the indicators above, all in
favour of the chosen clock.

**Guardrail.** Validation on purged time blocks, with zero [leakage](../../glossary.md#leakage) between blocks; an alert if the
ranking flips from one block to another.

**Output.** Evaluation and decision report (ranking, choice, reasons, fallback) → S3 and S4
(final datasets), S5 (contracts), S6 (alert thresholds), S7 (traceable decisions).

</details>

<details>
<summary><b>6 · Publish, contract and monitor continuously</b> — Execution task</summary>

**Goal.** Make the clock servable, measurable and replaceable.

**Actions.**
- Publish the schemas and contracts: formats of the event index, intervals and alignment map,
  mandatory fields, [versioning](../../glossary.md#versioning) rules, train/serve parity.
- Define the latency classes (real time, near-real time, batch) and their publication service
  levels (see Interfaces).
- Install monitoring of density, coefficient of variation of volatility per bar, overlaps,
  parameter drift and integrity failures, with alerting and a fallback.

**KPI.** Train/serve parity: identical hash of the pipeline and parameters; alert on any mismatch.
Latency service level met: T95 publication under target.

**Guardrail.** An automatic [kill switch](../../glossary.md#kill-switch) moves to the fallback
clock when two major KPIs stay in alert.

**Output.** Event index v1, contracts and dashboards (service levels, alerts) → directly S2.2,
S2.3, S2.4, S3, S4, S5 and S6; indirectly S7.

</details>

## Deliverables

- Specification: clock charter and invariants, design of the triggers and normalisations, and
  serving contracts (schemas, versions, service levels).
- Log of choices: parameters per asset and regime, reasons, trials and errors, fallback
  decisions.
- Ready artefacts: event index v1, intervals [t0, t1], alignment map, parameter registry.
- KPI dashboard: density, coefficient of variation of volatility per bar, autocorrelation,
  overlaps, latency, train/serve parity.
- Replacement process: kill switch, fallback clock, [requalification](../../glossary.md#requalification) procedure, activation
  criteria.
