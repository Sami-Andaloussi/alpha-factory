# S2.5 — Stationarity & memory preservation

## TL;DR

Makes series comparable over time (steadier mean, variance and dependence) without erasing the slow memory that carries the edge.
Each transformation comes from a bounded catalogue, is calibrated per asset and regime on the past only, and must prove it is stable but not amnesic.
Versioned recipes run identically in research and in production, and are watched for drift.

## Purpose

Make series comparable over time, with a steadier mean, variance and dependence, **without erasing
the useful inertia** that carries the edge: slow trends, [half-lives](../../glossary.md#half-life),
[regime](../../glossary.md#regime) effects. The result is a [stabilised series](../../glossary.md#stabilised-series), made by a
versioned [transformation recipe](../../glossary.md#transformation-recipe), that gains
[stationarity](../../glossary.md#stationarity) and keeps its [memory](../../glossary.md#memory).

**Success looks like:** steadier series, zero temporal [leakage](../../glossary.md#leakage), guaranteed
[train/serve parity](../../glossary.md#train-serve-parity), and diagnostics that show the relevant
memory is still there.

<summary><b>What each flow carries, constraints and service levels</b></summary>

The validation-ready index brings the locked event index, the purged and embargoed split schemes
and the timestamps. The sealed batches bring the asset definitions (trading hours,
[corporate actions](../../glossary.md#corporate-action) already handled in S1) and the [quality flags](../../glossary.md#quality-flag). Each stabilised series is aligned on the event
index; its recipe records the sliders, windows, bounds and dates; its diagnostics (moments,
[autocorrelation](../../glossary.md#autocorrelation) and half-life, [robustness](../../glossary.md#robustness) across regimes) travel with it. The serving contract
states parity, dependencies and the latency and cost budget. Sections 3, 4 and 6 receive all of
this through the registry of [S2.10](s2.10-feature-registry.md). When stabilisation needs a fix in
the data (S1) or in the [event clock](../../glossary.md#event-clock) (S2.1), a formal issue is opened upstream.

**Constraints.**
- Format: a common index, named columns, explicit units.
- Consistency: no retroactive recomputation without a new version.

**Service levels:**
- T90 for daily or event-driven updates ≤ one event interval.
- [T95](../../glossary.md#t95) for critical events (IPO, suspension, delisting) ≤ the end of the day concerned.
- An alert when breaches repeat more than twice a month.

</details>

## Gates

**Entry.** Start only when:
- [temporal integrity](../../glossary.md#temporal-integrity) is validated: purge and [embargo](../../glossary.md#embargo) in place (S2.4);
- the event index is frozen and shared;
- the input data is clean: splits and dividends handled upstream.

**Exit.** Verdict *pass* when all of these hold:
- the [KPIs](../../glossary.md#kpi) of step 5 are green for every asset and regime;
- train/serve parity is proven (step 6);
- recipes and diagnostics are versioned, reproducible and audited.

*Retry* and *fail* follow the [gate verdict](../../glossary.md#gate-verdict) rule.

**Requalification.** Rerun the sub-section when:
- the moments [drift](../../glossary.md#drift) persistently, or a half-life breaks;
- the microstructure changes, or a new volatility regime appears;
- a parity incident occurs;
- the universe is extended.

The affected asset or regime goes back to design work: a minimal recalibration, an A/B comparison
before and after, and the gain documented.

Monitoring (S6) receives the drift metrics and the [requalification](../../glossary.md#requalification) events, for alerts and
root-cause analysis. Lifecycle (S7) receives the target frequency of audits, the refresh calendar
and the rules for deprecating old recipes.

## Steps

<details>
<summary><b>1 · Define the target invariance and the useful memory</b> — Design task</summary>

**Goal.** Without a clear target, stabilisation is done blind and risks amnesia.

**Actions.**
- List what must stay invariant, per market: scale and variance, level, distribution.
- Set the memory horizons to preserve: a target half-life, a target autocorrelation structure, the
  granularity of regimes.
- Document the accepted trade-offs, for example a small loss of inertia for much steadier variance.

**KPI.** Charter completeness: every asset covered, every [invariance](../../glossary.md#invariance) explicit; threshold ≥ 95% of
use cases covered. A gap is a blind spot.

**Guardrail.** Against a contradictory target (flat variance with very long memory, for example):
a cross review and a worked example that shows the target is impossible.

**Output.** Invariance and useful-memory charter, per asset class and regime → step 2 (leak-free
design), step 3 (catalogue of transformations).

</details>

<details>
<summary><b>2 · Set the leak-free frame: index, windows, clocks</b> — Execution task</summary>

**Goal.** Every [calibration](../../glossary.md#calibration) must use only the past and follow the event clock validated in S2.4.

**Actions.**
- Freeze the event index: [bars](../../glossary.md#bar), events, alignments across assets.
- Choose a window scheme (expanding, or fixed and rolling), with a purge and an embargo consistent
  with the [labels](../../glossary.md#label).
- Timestamp the calibration periods and the application periods.

**KPI.** Share of valid windows, with no overlap with the future: threshold = 100%.

**Guardrail.** Against leakage through an index mistake: an automatic check that the latest
calibration timestamp comes before the earliest application timestamp.

**Output.** Leak-free calibration plan (windows, splits, timestamps) → step 3 (recipes), step 4
(calibration).

</details>

<details>
<summary><b>3 · Define the catalogue of transformations and their sliders</b> — Design task</summary>

**Goal.** With the invariance target and the leak-free frame known, the families of
transformations can be chosen.

**Actions.**
- List the families: differencing, including
  [fractional differentiation](../../glossary.md#fractional-differentiation); volatility
  normalisation; gentle detrending and centring; stabilisation of variance and distribution; light
  microstructure adjustments.
- For each family, define its sliders: bounded parameters such as a differencing intensity between
  0 and 1, a scaling horizon, a centring strength.
- Set safety bounds: a minimum and a maximum per parameter, and the expected monotonic rules (more
  stabilisation means less memory, and so on).

**KPI.** Expected monotonicity (more stabilisation, less measured memory): alert when more than 10%
of cases break the rule.

**Guardrail.** Against sliders that are too free: hard bounds, and a check of the response curves
that blocks aberrant S-shapes.

**Output.** Catalogue and parameter space, with their consistency rules → step 4 (calibration per
asset and regime).

</details>

<details>
<summary><b>4 · Calibrate per asset and regime, without leakage</b> — Design task</summary>

**Goal.** The sliders are set; their robust settings must now be found for each context.

**Actions.**
- Segment the periods by regime (volatility, structure, microstructure) with simple, traceable
  rules.
- On each segment, calibrate the sliders with only the history available to that segment,
  respecting the embargo.
- Set stopping criteria for parsimony: no retuning without a clear gain.

**KPI.** Parsimony of the settings: recalibrations per year and per asset. Target low but not zero;
alert on spikes after a change of regime.

**Guardrail.** Against [overfitting](../../glossary.md#overfitting) to the segment: a forward validation on the next segment, with
no retouching.

**Output.** Parameter table (asset × regime × period) and calibration log → step 5 (validation),
step 6 (deployment).

</details>

<details>
<summary><b>5 · Validate "stationary but not amnesic"</b> — Design task</summary>

**Goal.** Before deployment, check that the intended stability is there and that the useful memory
survives.

**Actions.**
- Stability of the moments on windows outside calibration: mean and variance steady enough.
- Residual memory: the half-life of shocks, the shape of the
  autocorrelation, a reasonable decay (neither too fast nor
  flat).
- Robustness across regimes: the properties do not collapse when the regime changes.

**KPI.** Stability of the moments: the ratio of the variance after transformation to its historical
median, within a band such as 0.8–1.25, adapted per asset. Target half-life respected: relative gap
≤ X%. Robustness across regimes: the median gap of the moments within its band.

**Guardrail.** Against amnesia (memory crushed): a sharp fall of the autocorrelation at the first
lags means *fail*.

**Output.** Validation report per asset and regime (pass or fail, with explanations) → step 6
(deployment), S3 and S4 (confidence in what they receive).

</details>

<details>
<summary><b>6 · Deploy with train/serve parity and full traceability</b> — Execution task</summary>

**Goal.** Validated means ready for reproducible production.

**Actions.**
- Freeze the recipes: the version of the parameters and of the windows.
- Apply the same chain in research and in production: the same inputs, the same sliders, the same
  calibration dates.
- Log everything: versions, dates, segments, signatures of the series before and after.

**KPI.** Train/serve parity: bit-for-bit equality on a control sample; threshold = 100%, or a zero
gap allowing for controlled rounding.

**Guardrail.** Against a diverging recipe: hashes of inputs and outputs, compared daily.

**Output.** Transformed series ready for S3, versioned recipes and complete metadata → S3
([features](../../glossary.md#feature) and signals), S4 (validation), S6 (monitoring).

</details>

<details>
<summary><b>7 · Monitor and requalify, regime by regime</b> — Design task + Execution task</summary>

**Goal.** Regimes move. Recalibrations should be few, and the right ones. The watch is routine
execution; an alert turns it into design work.

**Actions.**
- Set alert thresholds: drift of the moments, a break in half-life, degraded parity.
- On an alert, requalify: re-segment, recalibrate minimally, compare before and after.
- Close the loop with S6 and S7: audit cadence, replacement rules.

**KPI.** Share of justified alerts (alerts that lead to an accepted requalification): target high,
or the thresholds are too sensitive. [Time to repair](../../glossary.md#mttd-mttr) a requalification,
from decision to deployment: target short, without sacrificing quality.

**Guardrail.** Against permanent tuning: a cap on the frequency of recalibration, for example at
most once a month per asset outside a major event.

**Output.** Monitoring rules, requalification runbook and changelog → S6 (detection and alerts),
S7 (governance and cadence).

</details>

## Deliverables

- "Invariance and memory" specification, per asset and regime, versioned.
- Leak-free plan (windows, splits, timestamps), tested.
- Catalogue of transformations and sliders, with bounds and monotonic rules.
- Table of calibrated parameters (asset × regime × period) and decision log.
- Validation report (moments, autocorrelation and half-life, robustness across regimes), with each
  pass or fail explained.
- Train/serve parity contract, with automated tests and control hashes.
- Live KPI dashboard and requalification runbook (thresholds, time to repair, maximum frequency).
- A clear replacement process: how to add or remove a family of transformations without breaking
  S3, S4 and S6 (the same interfaces, a double run, a controlled cutover).
