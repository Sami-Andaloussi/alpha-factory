# S2.10 — Registry, lineage & feature-serving contracts

## TL;DR

Keeps the official inventory of everything the factory knows how to make (features, labels, weights), the exact story of how each was made, and the contract under which it is served.
Each object has one card, a stable ID, a fingerprinted version, an executable lineage, and a serving contract with its latency, cost and fallback.
In one click: what serves what, at what price, how fast and with what guarantees, and an exact replay of any experiment.

## Purpose

Keep the official inventory of everything the factory can produce ([features](../../glossary.md#feature), [labels](../../glossary.md#label), weights), tell
the precise story of how each was made (who, when, with what), and fix the contract under which it
is served: freshness, latency, cost, [train/serve parity](../../glossary.md#train-serve-parity).
This page is the [feature registry](../../glossary.md#feature-registry), and the single way out of
section 2 towards sections 3 to 5.

**Success looks like:** in one click, one knows what serves what, at what price, how fast and
with what guarantees, and any experiment can be replayed exactly.

<details>
<summary><b>What each flow carries, constraints and service levels</b></summary>

Section 1 brings [point-in-time](../../glossary.md#point-in-time) data, versioned schemas and
ingestion metadata through its sealed batches and its batch registry. Every other sub-section of S2
deposits its artefacts here, most with their own serving contract. Downstream, S3 draws reliable
versions from the catalogue to compose its datasets; S4 reads [lineage](../../glossary.md#lineage),
[fingerprints](../../glossary.md#fingerprint), parity and signatures for its audits; S5 receives the
serving contracts (class, time to freshness, p95 and p99 latency, cache,
[fallback](../../glossary.md#fallback)) with their costs and quotas; S6 receives health checks,
[drift](../../glossary.md#drift) signatures and service-level statuses, and data integrity (S6.3)
the journal of transformations with its correlation IDs; S7 receives owners, statuses, the review
calendar, end-of-life and migration plans, and the compute and storage needs. The system of record
for decisions (S7.2) reads the registry, to tie each decision to the features and labels it used,
with their fingerprints; [change management](../../glossary.md#change-management) (S7.5) links each
[release package](../../glossary.md#release-package) to the versions it ships. The
[data lifecycle](../../glossary.md#data-lifecycle) (S7.7) measures on the registry the coverage of
lineage. Feedback on cost, [leakage](../../glossary.md#leakage) and instability goes back to the
other sub-sections of S2 as issues.

**Constraints.**
- Stable, self-describing formats: [feature cards](../../glossary.md#feature-card), versions, signatures.
- Idempotence and [replay](../../glossary.md#replay): every versioned object can be replayed on a dated
  [snapshot](../../glossary.md#snapshot).

**Service levels:** T90 / [T95](../../glossary.md#t95) propagation targets defined for critical events (IPO, suspension,
delisting), with an alert when they are exceeded.

</details>

## Gates

**Entry.** Start only when:
- S1 guarantees point-in-time data and the event index;
- S2 provides its integrity artefacts (purge, [embargo](../../glossary.md#embargo)) and its quality flags.

**Exit.** Verdict *pass* when, for every active object:
- the feature card is complete;
- the fingerprint and the version are published;
- the lineage is complete and parity passes;
- the contract is signed;
- signatures and health checks are in place;
- an owner and a runbook are assigned.

*Retry* and *fail* follow the [gate verdict](../../glossary.md#gate-verdict) rule.

**Requalification.** Rerun the exit checks when:
- an upstream source or schema changes;
- service levels are breached repeatedly;
- drift persists according to the signatures;
- a new method brings a gain in cost, latency or quality;
- a new market rule arrives (microstructure, calendars).

Monitoring (S6) receives a continuous flow of signatures, service-level statuses and incidents.
Lifecycle (S7) receives recommendations for promotion and deprecation, and [capacity](../../glossary.md#capacity) needs.

## Steps

<details>
<summary><b>1 · Canonical map and stable IDs</b> — Design task</summary>

**Goal.** Remove ambiguity: every object (feature, label, weight) exists under one canonical name
and one immutable ID.

**Actions.**
- Define a minimal template for the feature card: name, textual
  definition, formula or recipe, units, clock and alignment, window or horizon, point-in-time
  [guardrails](../../glossary.md#guardrail), validity range, declared dependencies, owner, status (draft, active, deprecated).
- Design a naming taxonomy (for example domain.asset.window.horizon.transform), with rules against
  collisions.
- Give each object an ID at creation, independent of its name: the name may change, the ID never.

**KPI.** Coverage of the registry over actual use: target 100%.

**Guardrail.** A name-drift detector: the same ID with a name or recipe changed without a new
version blocks publication.

**Output.** Initial registry (a living catalogue) and the validated template → S3 and S4 (to draw
from), S6 (to reference metrics), S7 (governance).

</details>

<details>
<summary><b>2 · Versioning by immutable fingerprint</b> — Design task</summary>

**Goal.** Know exactly what ran.

**Actions.**
- Define the fingerprint: a [deterministic](../../glossary.md#deterministic) hash of the logical
  code, the parameters, the schema and versions of the input data, and the order of the steps.
- Policy: any change to one of these elements means a new version (major.minor.patch, or an
  equivalent).
- Store the fingerprint, date, author, reason and the associated test artefacts.

**KPI.** [Reproducibility](../../glossary.md#reproducibility) of experiments: the share of runs that
come back bit-identical with the same fingerprint (≈ 100%).

**Guardrail.** A "no shadow change" [gate](../../glossary.md#gate): publication is refused when the fingerprint changes but
the version does not.

**Output.** Table of versions and fingerprints linked to each ID → S3 (replay), S4 (audit), S6
([traceability](../../glossary.md#traceability) of alerts), S7 (promotion and deprecation).

</details>

<details>
<summary><b>3 · Lineage: an executable dependency graph</b> — Design task</summary>

**Goal.** Make the whole production chain visible and repairable.

**Actions.**
- Model a directed graph from raw source, through transformations, to the final object, with
  timestamps and versions at every node.
- Declare for each node its required inputs, preconditions, postconditions and output signatures
  (see step 7).
- Expose the usual queries: "what breaks if X changes?", "which children of Y are out of date?".

**KPI.** Lineage completeness: the share of objects with a complete
graph. The [time to repair](../../glossary.md#mttd-mttr) dependencies, which must fall over time.

**Guardrail.** A check for orphan or broken edges at every build; an alert when an active object
has an unversioned dependency.

**Output.** Executable lineage (a metadata graph) and its query interface → S4 and S6 (audit, time
to repair), S5 (pre-computation plan), S7 (impact of a deprecation).

</details>

<details>
<summary><b>4 · Train/serve parity and the reproduction harness</b> — Design task</summary>

**Goal.** No surprise between research and production.

**Actions.**
- Define a [single source of truth](../../glossary.md#single-source-of-truth) for the computation logic, called in "train" and in "serve"
  mode: the same inputs and outputs, the same parameters, the same order.
- Design automatic parity tests (on a frozen batch, compare the training and serving outputs), and
  add tests of temporal leakage and of purge and embargo at the level of representations.
- Store the parity reports, attached to the versions of step 2.

**KPI.** Share of parity tests that pass (> 99%); median train/serve skew ≈ 0.

**Guardrail.** A leakage gate: a failure refuses publication and serving.

**Output.** Signed reports of parity and leakage, with a go or no-go status → S3 (confidence), S4
(validation), S5 (going live).

</details>

<details>
<summary><b>5 · Service contracts and latency classes</b> — Design task</summary>

**Goal.** Serve on time, at a controlled cost, with degraded modes.

**Actions.**
- Define classes (for example very low latency, intraday, end of day), each with its time to
  freshness, p95 and p99 latency, recomputation window, tolerated staleness and required parity.
- Specify the policies for caching and pre-computation, replay and
  fallback (a fallback value, version N−1, a proxy), and the
  conditions for switching.
- Attach each ID and version to a target class, with numbered service level objectives.

**KPI.** Service levels met at p95 and p99 per class (> 99%); mean freshness lag below a threshold
per class.

**Guardrail.** Regular tests of the degraded mode (a controlled chaos exercise), and a
[canary release](../../glossary.md#canary-release) before scaling up.

**Output.** Service contracts per object, readable by S5 and S6 → S5 (scheduling), S6 (monitoring
of service levels), S4 (execution simulation, cost analysis).

</details>

<details>
<summary><b>6 · Costs, quotas and priorities</b> — Design task</summary>

**Goal.** No "nice to have" feature may block critical execution.

**Actions.**
- Measure the unit cost (per call or batch) and the daily cost per class; project the peak cost.
- Define budgets per queue and priority, quotas, and a pre-emption mechanism in case of contention.
- Publish the cost table and the priority rules in the registry.

**KPI.** Cost per feature within budget; a minimum share of headroom per class, as a capacity
buffer.

**Guardrail.** A [kill switch](../../glossary.md#kill-switch) on quotas, and "hidden cost" alerts
when the observed cost departs from the forecast beyond a threshold.

**Output.** Schedule of costs, priorities and quotas, aligned with the service levels → S5
(scheduler and arbitration), S7 (capacity planning).

</details>

<details>
<summary><b>7 · Health, signatures and actionable drift</b> — Design task</summary>

**Goal.** Watch quality and stability on the side of data and features, not only on the side of
models.

**Actions.**
- Define compact statistical signatures per object and version: quantiles, robust moments, binned
  density, correlation with anchors (stable references), rates of missing values and outliers.
- Specify the alert rules: adaptive thresholds, observation window, aggregation per latency class.
- Emit health checks (up or down, staleness, errors), with direct links to S6 for display and
  alerting.

**KPI.** Precision of the drift alerts (few false positives); a shorter triage time.

**Guardrail.** Monitoring in [shadow mode](../../glossary.md#shadow-mode) when a version is
introduced: it alerts without acting during an observation period.

**Output.** Packs of signatures and health, published with each version → S6 (monitoring), S4
(drift analysis), S7 ([requalification](../../glossary.md#requalification)).

</details>

<details>
<summary><b>8 · Governance, ownership and lifecycle</b> — Execution task</summary>

**Goal.** Know who decides, and how to replace cleanly.

**Actions.**
- Assign an owner and a delegate; provide an incident runbook and a calendar of periodic reviews.
- Set the policy of promotion and deprecation: overlap period, migration plan, end-of-life date,
  recommended replacement.
- Log the decisions: why, impact, alternatives set aside.

**KPI.** Share of objects with an owner and a runbook; deprecation deadlines respected.

**Guardrail.** Decommissioning is blocked while there is no migration plan.

**Output.** Policies and runbooks attached to each ID → S7 (governance), S3, S5 and S6
(communication of changes).

</details>

## Deliverables

- Feature card specification, naming policy and IDs.
- [Versioning](../../glossary.md#versioning) policy: definition of the fingerprint, rules of increment.
- Executable lineage (a metadata graph) and its standard queries.
- Harness for parity and leakage, with go or no-go reports.
- Catalogue of contracts: classes, time to freshness, p95 and p99, cache, fallback.
- Documented schedule of costs, quotas and priorities.
- Packs of signatures and health per version.
- Incident runbooks and a decision log (reasons, alternatives).
- [KPI](../../glossary.md#kpi) dashboard: coverage, parity, service levels, cost, drift, time to repair.
- Replacement process: promotion, overlap, migration, end of life.
