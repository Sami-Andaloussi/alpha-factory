# S3.7 — Robustness by construction

## TL;DR

Builds structural shock absorbers into the strategy, so that it stays steerable when the market leaves the manual: outliers, gaps, regime changes, model errors.
The robust version decides almost as the plain one does in calm markets, and limits the damage in dirty ones, without killing the alpha.
It leaves with standard resilience certificates and a robustness card, reused in validation, execution, monitoring and lifecycle.

## Purpose

Build structural shock absorbers into the strategy, so that it stays steerable when the market
leaves the manual: outliers, gaps, [regimes](../../glossary.md#regime), model errors.

**Success looks like:** the robust version takes decisions close to the plain one in calm markets,
and limits the damage in dirty conditions, without killing the [alpha](../../glossary.md#alpha). It leaves this sub-section
with standard resilience certificates, gathered in its
[robustness card](../../glossary.md#robustness-card), reusable as they are in validation (S4),
execution (S5), monitoring (S6) and lifecycle (S7).

## Gates

**Entry.** Start only when:
- the modules of section 2 are validated: interfaces and metadata;
- a minimal "classic" [backtest](../../glossary.md#backtest) is available, for comparison;
- the data of section 1 is compliant: quality and timestamps.

**Exit.** Verdict *pass* when all of these hold:
- the [KPIs](../../glossary.md#kpi) of steps A to F are green, or justified; the resilience certificates are signed;
- the gap in calm markets (robust against classic) is contained, and superiority under stress is
  demonstrated;
- fuses and fallbacks are tested in [replay](../../glossary.md#replay), and the invariances are passed;
- the robustness card and the machine-readable rules are delivered to S4, S5 and S6.

*Retry* and *fail* follow the [gate verdict](../../glossary.md#gate-verdict) rule.

**Requalification.** Rerun steps A to F when:
- a KPI [drifts](../../glossary.md#drift) beyond its threshold: maximum influence, fuse activation rate, concentration of P&L;
- market rules, microstructure or fees change;
- an extreme event occurs that the charter does not cover;
- a promising new damping method appears (after a formal review);
- the fixed cadence set by S7 comes round, for example monthly or quarterly.

Monitoring (S6) receives alert templates and the same KPIs, live. Lifecycle (S7) receives the
decision log, the [requalification](../../glossary.md#requalification) triggers and the lessons learnt.

## Steps

<details>
<summary><b>A · Define the scope of robustness and the tolerance for risk</b> — Design task</summary>

**Goal.** What has not been named cannot be damped properly.

**Actions.**
- Map the shocks to cover: outliers (prices, volatility, [spreads](../../glossary.md#spread)), breaks in correlation, pockets
  of illiquidity, latencies and order errors, changes of rules.
- Set the target tolerances: a P&L corridor, the maximum loss per event, the allowed elasticity of
  positions, the expected stability of parameters.
- Define the [invariances](../../glossary.md#invariance) to preserve (scale, neutralities, time
  translations), so as not to break the logic of the market.

**KPI.** Coverage of the key risks: ≥ 90% of the critical scenarios identified. Clarity of the
invariances: a closed, testable list.

**Guardrail.** An independent cross review (S5, S6) to find blind spots; an invariance test on a
set of toy cases.

**Output.** Robustness charter (taxonomy of risks, tolerances, invariances, priorities) → step B,
S4 (test plans), S6 (alerts).

</details>

<details>
<summary><b>B · Place the dampers in the right places: a minimal, sufficient design</b> — Design task</summary>

**Goal.** Decide where to robustify, to avoid over-armouring.

**Actions.**
- Choose the points of application:
  - internal objectives and aggregations: replace nervous means and standard deviations with stable
    statistics (bounded-influence families, weighted medians, robust scales), and objectives with a
    bounded slope;
  - constraints and caps: contextual limits on weight and leverage, depending on volatility,
    liquidity and [turnover](../../glossary.md#turnover);
  - pre- and post-filters: [winsorisation](../../glossary.md#winsorisation) or trimming targeted at
    pathological cases, 0.1–0.5% at most by design, to be justified.
- Preserve the invariances set in step A, with dedicated tests.
- Document the rationale: for each damper, the pathology targeted, the metric protected, the risk
  of over-protection.

**KPI.** Decision gap in calm markets (robust against classic) ≤ X% on sizes and weights; a gain in
stability (the variance of decisions falls).

**Guardrail.** Against over-armouring: an alert when calm performance falls by more than Y% with no
net gain under stress; an "invariances unchanged" test.

**Output.** Damper schema (a map of where, what and why; logical parameters and bounds) → step C
(dynamic logic), S5 (execution limits).

</details>

<details>
<summary><b>C · Dynamic guardrails and degraded modes</b> — Design task</summary>

**Goal.** Passive dampers are not enough in a crisis: active, reversible fuses are needed.

**Actions.**
- Define state triggers (volatility, depth, gap between mid and last price, latency) and the
  adaptive rules attached to them: dynamic caps, slower turnover, temporarily stricter thresholds.
- Specify the degraded modes: a simpler, readable [fallback](../../glossary.md#fallback) logic when
  the market sensors turn red, and more conservative entry and exit rules
  ([controlled degradation](../../glossary.md#controlled-degradation)).
- Define reversibility, with [hysteresis](../../glossary.md#hysteresis), to avoid ping-pong
  between on and off.

**KPI.** Activation rate below p% in normal markets; under stress, a clear and consistent
activation; reversal time below T.

**Guardrail.** False positives and negatives measured on replays; a hysteresis test against
flip-flop.

**Output.** Table of [fuses](../../glossary.md#fuse) and fallbacks (conditions, actions, duration,
reversal) → step D (influence measures), S5 (live management), S6 (telemetry).

</details>

<details>
<summary><b>D · Diagnostics of influence and concentration: sensitivity control</b> — Execution task</summary>

**Goal.** Check that no point, day, asset or module dictates the decision.

**Actions.**
- Set up influence measures at the level of the data point, the trade, the day, the module and the
  group of assets: leave-one-out approaches, or equivalent approximations.
- Compute the concentration of P&L: the top-k trades or days, by pocket of liquidity.
- Set influence caps: thresholds beyond which a decision is held back, reviewed or damped.

**KPI.** Maximum influence below its threshold; share of P&L from the top-10 trades or days below
its ceiling; stability of the weights when a key point is removed.

**Guardrail.** An alert when a single module or day exceeds the influence threshold; a mandatory
autopsy before promotion.

**Output.** Influence and concentration tables, and mitigation rules → step E (stress plans), S6
(influence drift alerts), S7 (postmortems).

</details>

<details>
<summary><b>E · Robust resampling and targeted stress tests</b> — Execution task</summary>

**Goal.** Prove that the performance does not rest on lucky episodes, and that the fuses hold.

**Actions.**
- Build [stress](../../glossary.md#stress-test) sets aligned with the charter of step A: historical
  regimes, synthetic shocks (spreads, gaps, latencies), removal of the dominant pockets of alpha,
  time permutations that keep the microstructure.
- Use resampling that respects dependence (blocks, by regime or [venue](../../glossary.md#venue)) to estimate robust bands of
  performance.
- Test consistency: the robust version close to the classic one in calm markets, dominant in dirty
  ones.

**KPI.** [Drawdown](../../glossary.md#drawdown), costs and [slippage](../../glossary.md#slippage) contained within target
quantiles under extreme scenarios. The gap between classic and robust: close in calm markets, robust
≥ classic under stress (a positive margin).

**Guardrail.** Diverse scenarios, with no [overfitting](../../glossary.md#overfitting) to the stress pack; a check that the bands do
not depend on one or two episodes.

**Output.** Resilience certificates (bands, quantiles of performance and costs, log of fuse
activations) → step F, S4 (the passage dossier), S6 (live references).

</details>

<details>
<summary><b>F · Traceability and governance of robustness: the robustness card</b> — Execution task</summary>

**Goal.** Make robustness inspectable, replicable and governable.

**Actions.**
- Compile the robustness card: the charter (A), the damper schema (B), the fuses (C), influence and
  concentration (D), the certificates (E), the invariance checklist and the requalification rules.
- Publish the KPI dashboard and the service levels.
- Define the replacement process: when and how to substitute a damper, a threshold, a fallback.

**KPI.** Completeness of the dossier: 100%. Freshness below Δt (the service level). Audit time
below T90.

**Guardrail.** A failed test triggers an automatic requalification.

**Output.** A robust dossier ready for validation, and artefacts → S4 (execution simulation, cost
analysis), S5 (execution rules), S6 (monitoring), S7 (governance).

</details>

## Deliverables

- A clear specification: the robustness charter and its invariances.
- Log of choices: why this damper here, that cap there (dated, signed).
- Executable artefacts: the damper schema, fuses and fallbacks, machine-readable rules, invariance
  tests.
- KPI dashboard for steps A to F, with thresholds, alerts and history.
- Resilience certificates: performance bands, scenarios, logs of activation.
- Replacement process: checklist, criteria, [rollback](../../glossary.md#rollback) plan.
