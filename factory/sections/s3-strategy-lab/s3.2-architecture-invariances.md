# S3.2 — Architecture & invariances: re-implementation on factory data

## TL;DR

Rebuilds the admitted strategy in the factory's standard architecture, on the factory's own point-in-time data, with a clear and testable way to assemble its parts.
Fixes the decision graph and its clock, the interface charter, one assembly pattern, the invariances to pass, the degraded modes, and what optimisation may touch.
If the lab's result does not reproduce on factory data, the dossier goes back to the lab.

## Purpose

Re-implement the admitted strategy in the factory's standard architecture, on the factory's own
[point-in-time](../../glossary.md#point-in-time) data: a clear, testable recipe to assemble its
parts (detectors, filters, sizing, risk) into a strategy that is coherent, stable and sober. The
result is the [reference implementation](../../glossary.md#reference-implementation).

**Success looks like:**
- a readable architecture: who does what, in what order, at what rhythm;
- a book of [invariances](../../glossary.md#invariance) that the strategy passes: scale, time,
  neutralities;
- clean interfaces (inputs and outputs, cadence, latency) and degraded modes;
- a precise optimisation contract for what follows: levers open and closed, constraints, search
  surface;
- the lab's result reproduced on factory data.

## Gates

**Entry.** Start only when:
- the dossier is admitted (S3.1);
- the modules of section 2 comply with the [input/output charter](../../glossary.md#input-output-charter),
  and their point-in-time and [leakage](../../glossary.md#leakage) tests are green;
- the [calibration](../../glossary.md#calibration) data is point-in-time and one clock is defined.

**Exit.** Verdict *pass* when all of these hold:
- the lab's result reproduces on factory data: the reference implementation, run on the dossier's
  own period and universe, gives the dossier's results within the tolerance its results field
  states (handoff contract);
- the invariant pass rate is at or above its threshold, and the brittleness score at or below its
  own;
- the [latency budget](../../glossary.md#latency-budget) is respected;
- the optimisation contract is complete.

*Retry* and *fail* follow the [gate verdict](../../glossary.md#gate-verdict) rule. A result that does
not reproduce sends the dossier back to the lab, with the gaps explained.

**Requalification.** Rerun the sub-section when:
- monitoring (S6) detects invariance [drift](../../glossary.md#drift);
- market rules change, or the cadence, a normalisation or the universe changes;
- a degraded mode fails in production, or optimisation shows abnormal multipliers (aberrant prices
  of constraints).

Monitoring (S6) receives the invariance dashboard and the online probes. Lifecycle (S7) records
the [design rationale](../../glossary.md#design-rationale) and the triggers of periodic
[requalification](../../glossary.md#requalification).

## Steps

<details>
<summary><b>1 · Map the flow and the clock</b> — Design task</summary>

**Goal.** Lay out the [decision graph](../../glossary.md#decision-graph) of the modules, with
their cadences, latencies and dependencies: who talks to whom, when, and at what step.

**Actions.**
- List every candidate part: type of input, frequency, maximum latency, state required.
- Draw a minimal sequential or parallel flow, with no cycles; assign a cadence to each node and a
  decision window to each actuator.
- Fix the strategy's [reference clock](../../glossary.md#reference-clock) (its [timebase](../../glossary.md#timebase)) and the alignment rules: the upsampling and
  downsampling allowed, and their adapters.

**KPI.** Latency budget respected: the sum of latencies ≤ the decision service level. Cycle-free
check: the graph is valid.

**Guardrail.** A "cadence mix" test: no fast decision depends on a slower signal without an
explicit adapter; an alert on any implicit downsampling.

**Output.** Architecture schema v1 (graph, clock, cadences) → S3.3 (optimisation), S4 (execution
simulation).

</details>

<details>
<summary><b>2 · Interface charter and invariant normalisations</b> — Design task</summary>

**Goal.** Define stable input and output signatures (formats, units, conventions, time zone,
calendars), and normalisations that add no information.

**Actions.**
- Specify for each part: its required inputs, its type of output (score, probability, raw size),
  scale, cadence, maximum latency and missing status.
- Set the input norms (returns, de-meaning, [winsorisation](../../glossary.md#winsorisation)…) and the point-in-time contracts (zero
  leakage).
- Golden rule: no downstream transformation can increase the information about the target (the
  data-processing inequality).

**KPI.** Zero-leak tests: rebuilt features do not correlate with future labels. Schema
conformance rate.

**Guardrail.** Automatic point-in-time and "no peek" tests; every post-processing documents the
loss of information it is expected to cause.

**Output.** Interface charter (catalogue of types and schemas) → S2 (to package the modules), S5
(execution).

</details>

<details>
<summary><b>3 · Assembly patterns and the rules of gating and voting</b> — Design task</summary>

**Goal.** Choose a parsimonious [assembly pattern](../../glossary.md#assembly-pattern) (sequential
pipeline, parallel committees, hierarchy, contextual gating) and formalise who has a say.

**Actions.**
- Select one primary pattern, not three, from a short list: sequential (A → B → C), parallel
  (A ∥ B ∥ C, with a vote), hierarchical (a master signal gating the sub-modules).
- Write the gating contracts (when the tap opens or closes, with
  [hysteresis](../../glossary.md#hysteresis) or a delay) and the policies of voting and weighting
  (bounds, caps, influence budgets).
- Document the degrees of freedom left to optimisation (coefficients, thresholds, budgets) against
  the fixed points (topology, orders).

**KPI.** Useful coverage: a non-trivial share of time "armed". Influence cap compliance: no module
above X% of influence.

**Guardrail.** A brittleness [kill switch](../../glossary.md#kill-switch): if the strategy dies when one module fails, the
architecture is refused (fragility score above its threshold).

**Output.** Assembly specification (topology and decision rules) → S3.3 (objectives and
constraints).

</details>

<details>
<summary><b>4 · Invariance book and prototype tests</b> — Design task</summary>

**Goal.** State the strategy's "laws of physics" (its invariances and symmetries), and write
mechanical tests that must pass.

**Actions.**
- Define the relevant group of actions (rescaling of prices or returns, allowed time translation,
  permutations of the universe to neutralise, sector or market-mode rotations) and the maximal
  invariant statistic: what still matters after that reduction.
- List the target invariances, at least: scale (a stable decision when everything is multiplied),
  clock (consistency under a small allowed shift), neutralities (market, sector), affine or rotation
  where relevant, sign equivariance where the logic calls for it.
- Prototype unit micro-tests: a rescaling of ±10% changes the decision by ≈ 0; a time shift within
  the allowed granularity changes it minimally; [neutralisation](../../glossary.md#neutralisation)
  (removing the market mode) gives the expected behaviour, neither mute nor panicked.

**KPI.** Invariant pass rate (≥ threshold). Largest change of decision under the actions of the
group (≤ tolerance).

**Guardrail.** An architecture that depends on absolute levels or arbitrary calendars is
rejected; the "maximal invariant" discipline avoids chasing patterns that cannot be identified.

**Output.** Invariance book and test harness → S4 (to stress), S6 (to monitor invariance drift).

</details>

<details>
<summary><b>5 · Parsimonious aggregation, without duplicates</b> — Design task</summary>

**Goal.** Go from signals to decisions while limiting double counting and keeping complexity in
check.

**Actions.**
- Define an aggregation rule (vote or weighting) with influence budgets and penalties for
  redundancy (internal correlations capped).
- Fix a complexity budget (number of active parts, gating switches, rules) and an explicit parsimony (L1 or
  L2 penalties, in the generic sense).

**KPI.** Redundancy index: mean correlation within a block ≤ threshold. Complexity score ≤ budget.
Implied [turnover](../../glossary.md#turnover) compatible with execution.

**Guardrail.** The curve of residual against parsimony is monotonic, a sign of a healthy
trade-off; a module that adds only a cloned signal is switched off.

**Output.** Aggregation block (specification, and parameters open or closed) → S3.3 (discipline of
objective and constraints).

</details>

<details>
<summary><b>6 · Degraded modes and operational robustness</b> — Design task + Execution task</summary>

**Goal.** Guarantee reasonable behaviour when a part or a data source fails, or when an outlier
occurs.

**Actions.**
- Define backup routes ([fallbacks](../../glossary.md#fallback)): substitute signals, "hold last
  size", partial neutralisation; state their triggers (latency, missing data).
- Use bounded-influence functions in the sensitive stages (robust families, in the general sense)
  to limit the effect of extreme values.
- Plan failure tests: cut a critical module, simulate missing data, inject outliers.

**KPI.** Graceful-degradation score: simulated failures without a switch of [operating mode](../../glossary.md#operating-mode). Outlier
sensitivity: a bounded change.

**Guardrail.** If one sensor controls more than X% of the expected P&L, refactor; aim for bounded
influence in the robust sense rather than fragile rules.

**Output.** Degraded-mode runbook (rules, thresholds, tests) → S5 (execution), S6 (monitoring).

</details>

<details>
<summary><b>7 · Optimisation contract and search surface</b> — Design task</summary>

**Goal.** Say what can be optimised, and what cannot, with its constraints and an economic reading
of the multipliers.

**Actions.**
- List the degrees of freedom exposed (weights, thresholds, budgets) and the constraints
  (exposures, turnover, transaction costs, neutralities).
- Document the domain (bounds, local convexity where it applies) and the dual quantities that
  optimisation must expose: the price of each constraint, through its multiplier (its
  [shadow price](../../glossary.md#shadow-price)).

**KPI.** Expected duality gap: ≈ 0 in a convex frame. Share of active constraints: a sane reading,
neither all active nor all inactive.

**Guardrail.** No topology is optimised blindly: the grammar of steps 1 to 3 stays fixed; only the
sliders move.

**Output.** Contract for S3.3 (objective, constraints, degrees of freedom), with its expected
readings: multipliers as opportunity costs → S3.3.

</details>

## Deliverables

- Reproduction report: the lab's results rerun on factory data, with every gap explained.
- Architecture sheet: graph, clock, cadences.
- Interface charter: input and output schemas, normalisations, point-in-time contracts.
- Assembly specification: the pattern chosen, gating, vote, influence budgets.
- Invariance book and test suite: scripts and thresholds.
- Degraded-mode runbook: fallbacks, triggers, failure tests.
- Optimisation contract: open degrees of freedom, constraints, readings of the multipliers.
- KPI tracking table per step, and the decision log (design rationale).
