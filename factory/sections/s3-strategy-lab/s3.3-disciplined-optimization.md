# S3.3 — Disciplined optimisation programme

## TL;DR

Turns the re-implemented strategy into a clear, solvable and governable optimisation problem: what it maximises net of costs, what it forbids, and how it stays simple.
This is fine calibration under a declared trial budget: every trial run here joins the dossier's trial registry, and section 4, not this page, judges the result.
Delivers a compact, stable formulation with zero violations, clear stopping rules and readable prices of constraints.

## Purpose

Turn a strategy already architected by S3.2 into an optimisation problem that is clear, solvable
and governable: a [disciplined optimisation](../../glossary.md#disciplined-optimisation).

- what is maximised: a genuinely useful utility, net of costs;
- what is not allowed: red lines of risk, exposure, [turnover](../../glossary.md#turnover) and [liquidity](../../glossary.md#liquidity);
- how it stays simple: penalties on complexity, parsimony.

In the factory this is fine [calibration](../../glossary.md#calibration) under a declared [trial budget](../../glossary.md#trial-budget).
Every trial run here is appended to the dossier's [trial registry](../../glossary.md#trial-registry),
and section 4 corrects its statistics for the total number of trials: S3 improves, S4 judges.

**Success looks like:** a compact and stable formulation, zero violations, clear stopping criteria,
readable prices of constraints, ready for validation, execution simulation and cost analysis.

## Gates

**Entry.** Start only when:
- the thesis and the architecture are validated (S3.1, S3.2);
- the signals of S2 are point-in-time, with a dictionary of scales;
- the risk and cost envelopes are available (step B);
- the eligible universe and the liquidity masks are up to date.

**Exit.** Verdict *pass* when all of these hold:
- violations = 0; the duality gap is at its threshold; turnover ≤ budget; the gap between ex-ante
  and ex-post costs at T90 ≤ 25%;
- the multipliers are documented, with an explanation of the binding constraints;
- the package of deliverables is complete; the trial budget declared in step A is respected, and
  every trial is in the trial registry.

*Retry* and *fail* follow the [gate verdict](../../glossary.md#gate-verdict) rule.

**Requalification.** Rerun the sub-section when:
- costs or volatility [drift](../../glossary.md#drift) by more than 30% at T90;
- the market changes: new rules, new tick sizes, microstructure;
- a new module of S2 affects the objective or the constraints;
- monitoring (S6) reports instability, turnover out of budget, or constraints binding abnormally,
  more than 80% of the time.

Monitoring (S6) receives checkers ready to run: violations, duality gap, stability, prices of
constraints. Lifecycle (S7) receives the policy for refreshing penalties and constraints, the
[requalification](../../glossary.md#requalification) triggers and the decision log.

## Steps

<details>
<summary><b>A · Pin down the useful objective</b> — Design task</summary>

**Goal.** Write the target utility function: expected return net of costs, weighted by risk
aversion and stability, consistent with the thesis and the horizon.

**Actions.**
- Declare the trial budget of this calibration, and record it in the dossier's trial registry
  before the first run.
- Choose the ingredients of the utility: net return, the chosen risk, the cost of turnover,
  smoothing or stability.
- Normalise the objective to the horizon and to the unit of decision (position, order).
- Set the preference weights ([risk tolerance](../../glossary.md#risk-tolerance), aversion to turnover) and their allowed range of
  variation, for [sensitivity analysis](../../glossary.md#sensitivity-analysis).

**KPI.** Consistency of the objective with the thesis and the horizon (a binary checklist);
[traceability](../../glossary.md#traceability) of the preference weights (a log).

**Guardrail.** A cross review: the objective must include costs and stability, or the work stops.

**Output.** Formal specification of the objective (a document and a clear mathematical expression)
→ step B (risk and costs quantified), step D (final formulation).

</details>

<details>
<summary><b>B · Quantify risks and costs: envelopes</b> — Design task</summary>

**Goal.** Make the components of risk and cost that enter the objective measurable and bounded.

**Actions.**
- Define the risk measure suited to the thesis (variance, tails, stability of weights) and its
  horizon of calculation.
- Define the relevant costs and frictions (impact, [spread](../../glossary.md#spread), fees,
  [slippage](../../glossary.md#slippage)) and their budgets.
- Set uncertainty bounds, prudent ranges, for risk and costs: they feed the tolerances of the
  optimisation.

**KPI.** Gap between ex-ante and ex-post (T90) on risk and costs ≤ 25%; coverage of at least 2
major [regimes](../../glossary.md#regime).

**Guardrail.** "Sanity bounds", minimum and maximum, per asset and overall (liquidity blocks).

**Output.** Risk and cost envelopes (table of parameters and update rules) → step C (constraints),
step D (formulation).

</details>

<details>
<summary><b>C · A few constraints, but structuring ones</b> — Design task</summary>

**Goal.** Translate the risk policy into operational red lines.

**Actions.**
- Fix 4 to 7 constraints at most, those that matter: risk budgets, maximum exposures, required
  neutralities, a turnover cap, concentration limits, liquidity rules.
- Define their formats and domains (linear where possible), and the priority rules: which
  constraints may bind first.
- Specify the aggregation windows (intraday, daily) for turnover and exposures.

**KPI.** Number of constraints ≤ 7; share of time with at least one binding constraint within
20–60%, neither decorative nor stifling.

**Guardrail.** A compatibility test: the feasible set is not empty (a simple feasibility
programme).

**Output.** Constraint register (list, bounds, reasons, frequency of evaluation) → step D
(formulation), step F (reading the prices of constraints).

</details>

<details>
<summary><b>D · Choose the form and aim for convexity</b> — Design task</summary>

**Goal.** Write the complete optimisation programme (objective A, constraints C, penalties E),
preferring convexity.

**Actions.**
- Choose the class, [convex](../../glossary.md#convex-optimisation) first. If non-convexity cannot
  be avoided, propose a disciplined relaxation and measure the cost of non-convexity (gap in the
  objective, instability).
- Fix the stopping criteria (duality gap or tolerance, no violation) and the sensitivity scheme
  (small perturbations of the inputs).
- Define the explanatory artefacts required at the output: weights, active constraints,
  multipliers, stability diagnostics.

**KPI.** Duality gap ≤ 1e-4, or its equivalent; a stable convergence time.

**Guardrail.** Tests of conditioning and of scalability over size and resolution.

**Output.** Problem specification (class, the optimality and duality conditions targeted,
tolerances) → step E (parsimony), step F (results, prices, sensitivities).

</details>

<details>
<summary><b>E · Install parsimony: simplicity that holds</b> — Design task</summary>

**Goal.** Prefer few degrees of freedom, with explicit penalties.

**Actions.**
- Choose a complexity penalty: L2 for stability, L1 for pruning, and possibly information criteria
  or minimum description length for the architecture.
- Define a range of penalty weights and the selection rule: a balance of performance, stability
  and cost.
- Plan a parsimony check: count the active parameters against their marginal gain.

**KPI.** Activation rate of the degrees of freedom ≤ 40–60%; a marginal gain per active parameter
that decreases.

**Guardrail.** A test of the stability of the weights (a variation below a set threshold) under
small perturbations.

**Output.** Penalty schedule (formula, ranges, selection rule) → step F (solving and diagnostics),
section 4 (validation).

</details>

<details>
<summary><b>F · Solve, read, stabilise</b> — Execution task</summary>

**Goal.** Produce the solution, check that it is clean, and extract what governance needs to know.

**Actions.**
- Solve the problem with the tolerances of step D, within the declared trial budget; every run
  goes into the trial registry.
- Check: zero violations, stopping criteria met, stable weights (sensitivities), ex-ante turnover
  within budget.
- Read the multipliers, the [shadow prices](../../glossary.md#shadow-price) of the constraints;
  identify the binding ones and document the implicit trade-offs.
- Short loop: if the solution is unstable or too expensive, go back to E, then C (reduce
  complexity, adjust the bounds); otherwise, freeze the parameters.

**KPI.** Violations = 0; expected turnover ≤ budget; gap between ex-ante and ex-post costs at
T90 ≤ 25%; bounded sensitivity (change of weights per change of input).

**Guardrail.** Re-runs on shifted windows, and synthetic perturbations ([labels](../../glossary.md#label), volatility, costs);
a repeated failure means requalification.

**Output.** Results package: weights or positions, active constraints, multipliers, diagnostics of
stability and costs → section 4 (execution simulation, cost analysis), section 5 (decision and
execution), section 6 (monitoring).

</details>

## Deliverables

- Objective specification: useful and net of costs, weighting of risk and stability, horizon.
- Risk and cost envelopes: values, ranges, update rules.
- Constraint register: at most 7, with formats, bounds, priorities, frequencies.
- Complete formulation: the class targeted, optimality and duality criteria, tolerances, rules of
  relaxation where needed.
- Parsimony schedule: type of penalty, ranges, selection rule.
- Results package: weights or positions, multipliers, active constraints, stability diagnostics,
  expected turnover.
- [KPI](../../glossary.md#kpi) tracking table per step, with thresholds and alerts, wired to S6.
- Log of choices (decisions, reasons, impacts) and a replacement process: what to adjust first —
  penalty, then constraint, then objective.
