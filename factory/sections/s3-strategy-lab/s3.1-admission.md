# S3.1 — Admission: dossier intake & decision frame

## TL;DR

The factory's front door: a strategy dossier from the lab is checked against the handoff contract, and its thesis is restated in factory terms.
The restatement fixes the scope, the falsifiable thesis, the objective and budgets, the rules to act and to stop, and the evidence section 4 will demand.
The dossier is admitted, returned to the lab, or rejected: the factory does not search for alpha, it takes in what the lab found.

## Purpose

Admit a strategy into the factory. The research lab hands over a
[strategy dossier](../../glossary.md#strategy-dossier); this page checks it against the
[handoff contract](../../../handoff/README.md), restates its thesis in factory terms, and decides
whether it is admitted, returned to the lab, or rejected: the [admission](../../glossary.md#admission).

The restatement is the [decision frame](../../glossary.md#decision-frame): what the strategy
believes, what it aims for, what it accepts to pay in risk and costs, and when it stops, on one
readable, falsifiable page that aligns research (S2, S3), validation and execution (S4, S5), and
supervision (S6, S7).

**Success looks like:** no ambiguity and no pointless debate: a complete dossier, and a decision
frame from which every other sub-section of S3 works.

<details>
<summary><b>What each flow carries, constraints and service levels</b></summary>

The two registries say what the factory can already supply: the universe, calendars, trading hours
and [venues](../../glossary.md#venue), summaries of microstructure, and the available modules with their interfaces. The page
also reads the cost and friction metrics and the simulation scenarios that section 4 offers, and
the alert channels of section 6 ([drifts](../../glossary.md#drift), breaks in correlation, changes of market rules). An
admitted dossier leaves with its decision frame for every other sub-section of S3; a returned or
rejected one goes back to the lab with its reasons. Monitoring (S6) receives the indicators of
activation and invalidation to watch live.

**Constraints.**
- Versioned formats, with mandatory fields: universe IDs, timestamp, owner, status, links to
  artefacts.
- Consistency: a change must preserve the dependencies between budgets, decision rules and the
  [kill switch](../../glossary.md#kill-switch); otherwise the sub-section is requalified.

**Service levels:**
- Critical events (IPO, suspension, new market rule): the documents they affect are updated at
  T90 24 h / [T95](../../glossary.md#t95) 48 h.
- Updates reach the downstream sections (S4, S5, S6) within a service level objective of 24 h,
  with an acknowledgement of integration.

</details>

## Gates

**Entry.** Start only when:
- a strategy dossier has arrived from the lab;
- the usable universe is consolidated (S1, S2);
- the cost and simulation templates are available (S4);
- the alert channel is operational (S6).

**Exit.** Verdict *pass* when all of these hold:
- the dossier is complete against the handoff contract, or it has been returned or rejected with
  its reasons;
- the five parts of the decision frame are validated, signed by their owner and cross-reviewed;
- the checklist for section 4 is filled in, with its go / no-go thresholds accepted.

*Retry* and *fail* follow the [gate verdict](../../glossary.md#gate-verdict) rule.

**Requalification.** Rerun the sub-section when:
- a [KPI](../../glossary.md#kpi) drifts, or costs inflate lastingly;
- market rules change, or the mechanism breaks;
- major new modules arrive from S2;
- the lab sends a new version of the dossier.

Monitoring (S6) receives the indicators of activation and invalidation to monitor live.
Lifecycle (S7) records the conditions for putting the strategy back in play: review dates, drift
thresholds, regulatory events.

## Steps

<details>
<summary><b>0 · Take in the dossier and check it against the handoff contract</b> — Execution task</summary>

**Goal.** Nothing is restated from a dossier with gaps: completeness comes first.

**Actions.**
- Check every field the handoff contract requires: theory, hypothesis stated before the data, code,
  logic, data used, results, validation report, [trial registry](../../glossary.md#trial-registry);
  and, in the validation report, the lab's short paper run on fresh data.
- Check that the lab's results trace back to its code and data, and that its
  trial registry holds every [variant](../../glossary.md#variant) tried.
- Record what is missing.

**KPI.** Completeness of the dossier against the contract: every field present.

**Guardrail.** No restatement starts on an incomplete dossier: it goes back to the lab with the list
of gaps.

**Output.** Intake checklist, complete or listing the gaps → step 1, the lab (when a part is
missing).

</details>

<details>
<summary><b>1 · Restate the scope and the intent</b> — Design task</summary>

**Goal.** Without a scope, every downstream decision becomes ambiguous.

**Actions.**
- Fix the universe (assets, venues, trading hours, calendars), the horizon of action (intraday,
  daily, swing) and the [regimes](../../glossary.md#regime) considered (directional, mean-reverting,
  event-driven).
- List the non-negotiable constraints: forbidden exposures, rules of compliance and latency, windows
  of illiquidity.
- State the priority intent: net stability, compound growth, or bounded opportunistic capture.

**KPI.** Rate of "out of scope" decisions detected downstream.

**Guardrail.** Against too broad a scope: an independent cross review, and checklists of the
non-negotiable constraints.

**Output.** Scope and intent sheet (one page) → S2 (compatible modules), steps 2 to 5, S4
(simulation ranges), S5 (ex-ante constraints).

</details>

<details>
<summary><b>2 · Restate the falsifiable thesis: hypothesis and mechanism</b> — Design task</summary>

**Goal.** The scope sets the frame; the thesis sets the testable bet. It is the lab's hypothesis,
stated before the data, restated in factory terms.

**Actions.**
- Write a testable sentence, "if… then… because…", with its activation conditions (when it
  applies) and what makes it obsolete: a [falsifiable thesis](../../glossary.md#falsifiable-thesis).
- Describe the expected [alpha](../../glossary.md#alpha) mechanism (economic, behavioural, microstructure) and the observation
  variables that follow from it: what should move, and in which direction.
- Specify the boundaries: what the thesis does not claim to capture.

**KPI.** Share of trades traceable to the declared mechanism.

**Guardrail.** Against an irrefutable thesis: try to refute it with opposite regimes and placebos.
A thesis that cannot be refuted is not admitted: the dossier goes back to the lab to be rewritten.

**Output.** Thesis statement (at most ten lines) and map of observation variables → S2 (targeting
of modules), S4 (tests aimed at the mechanism), S6 (monitoring of the mechanism).

</details>

<details>
<summary><b>3 · Set the objective and the budgets: utility and costs</b> — Design task</summary>

**Goal.** A thesis without rules of the game optimises the wrong thing.

**Actions.**
- Choose the decision objective, for example net return under a constraint of stability, of maximum
  loss or of sensitivity to costs: a [net-of-cost objective](../../glossary.md#net-of-cost-objective).
- Allocate the budgets: risk (admissible volatility or load, tolerated serial losses), costs
  (maximum [turnover](../../glossary.md#turnover), footprint per trade, tolerated [slippage](../../glossary.md#slippage)),
  exposures (directions, factors, concentrations).
- Set explicit priorities, for example stability before the last tenth of gross performance.

**KPI.** Cost and risk budgets respected on a mock-up.

**Guardrail.** Against budgets unrealistic for the horizon: a mock-up of costs and turnover, and a
simple stress, before any estimate of edge.

**Output.** Objective and budgets book (table of constraints and priorities) → S3 (combination of
signals), S4 (constraints in execution simulation and cost analysis), S5 (allocation and risk),
S6 (cost and risk alerts).

</details>

<details>
<summary><b>4 · Decision rules and the stop protocol</b> — Design task</summary>

**Goal.** The objective and the budgets dictate how to act, and when to stop.

**Actions.**
- Write the rules to open, add, reduce and close: gating by regime, signal quality, the price of
  the remaining risk, available [liquidity](../../glossary.md#liquidity).
- Define the conditions of invalidation: underperformance against plan, a broken mechanism,
  runaway costs, a change of market rules.
- Name the authority that can cut, and the process to pause and resume: delays, required documents,
  escalation channel.

**KPI.** Median delay from trigger to action (the kill switch).

**Guardrail.** Against a kill switch that cannot be applied: cold, table-top exercises on past
cases, with the time to execute measured.

**Output.** Decision and stop [runbook](../../glossary.md#runbook) (one page of rules and an
"if / then" matrix) → S4 (simulation of the rules), S5 (faithful execution), S6 (alert triggers),
S7 (governance).

</details>

<details>
<summary><b>5 · Evidence required and the contract with section 4</b> — Design task</summary>

**Goal.** To avoid wandering, the right level of evidence is required before the downstream
sections are mobilised.

**Actions.**
- Specify the expected order of magnitude of the edge, before and after costs, and the minimum
  [robustness](../../glossary.md#robustness): stability per regime, sensitivity to costs and latency, resilience to outliers.
- List the required tests and scenarios (placebos, permutations, alternative regimes,
  microstructure stress) and the go / no-go thresholds.
- Define the artefacts S4 expects: key curves, cost tables, risk bounds, decision log.

**KPI.** Go / no-go rate at the first pass, without framing iterations.

**Guardrail.** Against decorative evidence: accept only tests aligned with the mechanism and with
costs, with numbered thresholds.

**Output.** "Expected evidence and thresholds" dossier and a checklist for section 4 → S4
([simulation plans](../../glossary.md#simulation-plan)), S6 (monitoring references), S7 (learning).

</details>

<details>
<summary><b>6 · Decide: admit, return to the lab, or reject</b> — Design task</summary>

**Goal.** The admission ends with one decision, recorded with its reasons.

**Actions.**
- Admit the dossier when it is complete and its decision frame is signed; it then travels, with its
  decision frame and the lab's trial registry, to every other sub-section of S3.
- Return it to the lab when a part is missing, when the thesis cannot be restated in falsifiable
  form, or when the factory cannot obtain its data [point-in-time](../../glossary.md#point-in-time) (the data is named, and section 1
  is asked for a new source), with the list of what to fix.
- Reject it when its thesis falls outside the factory's non-negotiable constraints.
- Record the decision and its reasons in the [decision log](../../glossary.md#decision-log).

**KPI.** Share of dossiers admitted at their first intake.

**Guardrail.** No admission without a complete intake checklist and a signed decision frame.

**Output.** Admission decision (admitted, returned or rejected, with its reasons) → S3.2 to S3.9
(the admitted dossier), the lab (returned or rejected), S7 (decision log).

</details>

## Deliverables

- Intake checklist and admission decision, with its reasons.
- Scope and intent sheet (one page, versioned).
- Thesis statement (at most ten lines) and the map of observation variables.
- Objective and budgets book: table of constraints and priorities.
- Decision and stop runbook: "if / then" matrix, authority, delays.
- "Expected evidence and thresholds" dossier: required tests, target levels, contract with S4.
- Log of choices: decisions, alternatives rejected, reasons.
- KPI table: the KPIs tracked, thresholds, alerts.
- Replacement process: who triggers it, how to switch, the acceptance criteria for the replacement.
