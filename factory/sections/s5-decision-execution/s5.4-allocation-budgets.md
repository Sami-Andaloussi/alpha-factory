# S5.4 — Allocation layer & budgets (risk, liquidity, capacity)

## TL;DR

Gives the portfolio engine a workable envelope: how much risk is accepted, what share of liquidity is consumed, what capacity each line can carry, and which limits are non-negotiable.
The budgets are clear, measurable, updated at the right pace (the day before the open, and within 2 minutes of a shock), and hold in the real market.
Its risk budget book is what portfolio construction (S5.3), the transition planner (S5.6) and the execution chain (S5.7) apply as constraints.

## Purpose

Give the portfolio engine a workable envelope: how much risk the factory accepts, what share of
[liquidity](../../glossary.md#liquidity) it consumes, what [capacity](../../glossary.md#capacity)
each line can carry, and which limits are non-negotiable. This is the
[allocation layer](../../glossary.md#allocation-layer).

**Success looks like:** budgets that are clear, measurable and updated at the right pace,
translated into constraints and parameters that S5.3 can consume, and that hold in the real
market (costs, latency, microstructure): the [risk budget book](../../glossary.md#risk-budget-book).

<details>
<summary><b>What each flow carries, constraints and service levels</b></summary>

The entry tickets of S5.1 bring the eligible strategies, their initial weights and horizons; the
interaction map of S5.2 the correlations, the [drawdowns](../../glossary.md#drawdown) that coincide
and the overlaps, so that risk is shared out without crowding, and its interaction rules; the cost
surface of S5.8 the costs, the impact and the participation that can really be achieved, measured
before and after trading; the regime adjustment of S5.5 the multipliers that modulate the budgets
for a time; the deployment rulebook of S5.9 the limits that are never crossed. The as-of API of S1.8
gives the [point-in-time](../../glossary.md#point-in-time) data of liquidity. The steering note of
lifecycle (S7.1) brings the budgets of risk and capital as its steering review arbitrated them,
which the book applies within the day. The policy matrix of lifecycle (S7.4) brings the ceilings of
risk per horizon, and the caps on size and [turnover](../../glossary.md#turnover) that each state of
reliability and risk imposes; the book turns them into living budgets. The capital ladder of
lifecycle (S7.6) brings the bounds of the [capital tier](../../glossary.md#capital-tier) each
strategy holds, which the book applies as that strategy's budget. The capital allocation plan of
lifecycle's capital steering (S7.8) brings the target size and caps of each unit, and their plans of
ramp, which the book turns into budgets.

The risk budget book goes to S5.3 as the contract of constraints and penalties: bounds of risk,
leverage and concentration, limits of participation and turnover, expected costs, and the budget of
the [cost of change](../../glossary.md#cost-of-change), each ranked hard or soft and tagged so that
its sensitivity comes back. It goes to S5.6 as the caps of participation and turnover per instrument
and hour, the [no-trade zones](../../glossary.md#no-trade-zone) and the budget of change; to S5.7 as
the caps of scheduling per [venue](../../glossary.md#venue) and window, the hourly rules and the
allowed lists. The caps on crowding that S5.2 sets reach S5.6 and S5.7 through it. S5.1 and S5.5
read the budgets in force, S5.8 the costs and capacities in force, and S5.9 reviews each release
candidate. Data integrity (S6.3) reads the risk constraints in force, against which it checks the
journal of truth. Capital steering (S7.8) receives the budgets with their consumption and their
capital efficiency; lifecycle's reliability and risk budgets (S7.4) their consumption in real time,
which its traffic lights read. The override runbooks go to S6.7.

**Constraints.**
- Format: stable, versioned, self-describing schemas (types, units, horizons).

**Service levels:**
- Structural update: published on the day before, ahead of the open (local time), with
  [T95](../../glossary.md#t95) < 15 min between computation and publication.
- Tactical update: T95 < 2 min after the trigger, for example a shock of liquidity.
- Critical events: an IPO, suspension or delisting integrated into the scopes and budgets at
  T90 < 10 min; an alert when exceeded.

</details>

## Gates

**Entry.** Start only when:
- the strategies are accepted by S5.1, and the interaction map of S5.2 is available;
- the view before trading of costs and participation is ready, and the regulatory constraints are
  listed;
- the data of liquidity is point-in-time and replayable.

**Exit.** Verdict *pass* when all of these hold:
- the risk budget book is approved, and the contract with S5.3 delivered;
- the [stress tests](../../glossary.md#stress-test) are green, and the policy of update is validated;
- the live dashboard is connected.

*Retry* and *fail* follow the [gate verdict](../../glossary.md#gate-verdict) rule.

**Requalification.** Rerun the sub-section when:
- the gap between the view before trading and the realised persists (above its threshold for N
  periods);
- the headroom of capacity falls below its threshold, the rate of breaches rises above its own, or
  one constraint keeps dominating;
- the [regime](../../glossary.md#regime) changes (S5.5), or a market rule, a venue or the
  microstructure changes;
- a promising new method of estimating costs or liquidity appears, with no tool imposed.

Monitoring (S6) receives the [KPIs](../../glossary.md#kpi) and alerts: breaches, headroom, abnormal activation of
constraints. Lifecycle (S7) receives proposals to promote, withdraw or cap a strategy by its
capital efficiency per unit of budget, and tickets of action: raise or cut a budget, review the
interactions.

## Steps

<details>
<summary><b>1 · Map what is budgeted: axes, scopes, red lines</b> — Design task</summary>

**Goal.** Without a clean perimeter a budget floats: fix first where the rules apply.

**Actions.**
- Define the axes along which budgets are cut: the global level, families of [alphas](../../glossary.md#alpha), strategies,
  sub-portfolios, instruments and venues, horizons (intraday, daily, weekly), latency.
- List the red lines, the limits never crossed: regulatory and operational limits (concentration,
  short selling, hours, allowed venues), and the constraints of technical sovereignty.
- Build the matrix of scope: who may consume what. An intraday strategy does not draw on the same
  budget of participation as a weekly one.

**KPI.** Coverage of the scope: the % of assets, strategies and venues correctly classified;
threshold ≥ 99% of the assets under management covered.

**Guardrail.** A test of ambiguity: no asset or strategy may belong to two contradictory scopes;
a manual spot audit.

**Output.** The official taxonomy, the matrix of scope and the register of red lines → step 2
(the envelopes), S5.3 (the format of the constraints).

</details>

<details>
<summary><b>2 · Define the envelopes top-down: risk, liquidity, capacity</b> — Design task</summary>

**Goal.** The perimeter fixed the sandboxes; now each one is filled with a quota.

**Actions.**
- Risk: a global target (volatility, total risk), target contributions by family and strategy,
  [guardrails](../../glossary.md#guardrail) on the tail (tiers of [drawdown](../../glossary.md#drawdown) and
  [expected shortfall](../../glossary.md#expected-shortfall)), rules of leverage, and caps of
  concentration (names, sectors, factors).
- Liquidity: the maximum rate of participation per instrument, venue and hour, the daily and
  weekly turnover tolerated, minimum sizes, trading windows, and tiers by the depth observed.
- Capacity and impact: curves from capital to net performance by execution channel and horizon
  (the point where the alpha erodes), and budgets of impact (the cost of implementation expected
  before trading).
- Classify the constraints hard (inviolable) or soft (penalised), and rank them.

**KPI.** Target rate of use of the budgets: median within [60%, 90%].

**Guardrail.** A check of cross-consistency: a budget of participation must not make the target
of risk unreachable. A "waterbed" test: tightening one limit must not blow another beyond a
threshold set in advance.

**Output.** The risk budget book: envelopes in numbers, and the hierarchy of hard and soft →
step 3, S5.5 (the multipliers of regime).

</details>

<details>
<summary><b>3 · Translate into constraints and penalties that S5.3 can consume</b> — Design task</summary>

**Goal.** The envelopes must become mathematically actionable.

**Actions.**
- For each budget, produce the set of constraints (bounds, aggregations, caps of concentration,
  limits of participation and turnover) and the matching penalties (expected costs, cost of
  change).
- Define the hierarchy of application (the order of priority), and make each constraint
  traceable: tags, sensitivities, [shadow prices](../../glossary.md#shadow-price).
- Specify the budget of the cost of change, what S5.6 may spend to rebalance, from the turnover
  allowed and the impact tolerated.

**KPI.** Feasibility: 100% of the offline runs succeed; the rate of active constraints by type
(risk, liquidity, capacity) stays in a healthy zone, neither 0% nor 100%.

**Guardrail.** Detection of a dominating constraint: when the same constraint binds in more than
70% of the periods, the hierarchy or the penalty is reviewed. Sanity checks on the priorities of
hard and soft.

**Output.** The contract with S5.3: the file of constraints, the parameters and weights of the
penalties, the priorities → S5.3 (construction), S5.6 (no-trade zones, paths), S5.7 (caps of
participation).

</details>

<details>
<summary><b>4 · Validate by scenarios and stress, before trading</b> — Design task</summary>

**Goal.** Check that the whole holds in varied conditions before going live.

**Actions.**
- Test the feasibility of the constraints on history across regimes, and under extreme shocks:
  volatility, liquidity, venues unavailable.
- Measure the gaps between the forecast and the caps (risk, participation, turnover,
  concentration), and which constraints are active or blocking.
- Adjust budgets and penalties where there are structural bottlenecks (the waterbed effect).

**KPI.** Gap between the forecast and stress: the maximum overshoot stays below a threshold set for
risk, participation and turnover. Simulated rate of breaches < 1%, and time to return < X days.

**Guardrail.** Tests of extreme regimes (a venue unavailable, [spreads](../../glossary.md#spread) multiplied, turnover
forced); when a case is infeasible, countermeasures: dynamic caps, temporary reductions.

**Output.** Stress report, recommendations of adjustment, and a release candidate of the budgets →
step 5, governance (S5.9).

</details>

<details>
<summary><b>5 · Deploy the rhythm of update: structural and tactical</b> — Execution task</summary>

**Goal.** Validated offline, the budgets now need a rule for when and how they are updated, without
instability.

**Actions.**
- Structural (weekly or monthly): a fixed cadence to recalibrate the envelopes from the latest
  risks and costs.
- Tactical (on event or regime): rules of temporary adjustment (tighten participation in a crisis,
  cut leverage after a shock), with thresholds of activation and windows of return.
- Define freeze windows (no change) around sensitive events, and a journal of overrides: who,
  what, why, for how long.

**KPI.** Stability of the budgets: the median change per update stays in a healthy band; freezes
respected, with 0 exceptions.

**Guardrail.** A limit on overrides (their number, and their maximum duration per month); every
override reviewed afterwards.

**Output.** Policy of update, its calendar and the override runbooks → step 6, S5.5 (which applies
the multipliers of regime).

</details>

<details>
<summary><b>6 · Live loop: realised measures recalibrate the budgets</b> — Design task</summary>

**Goal.** A policy with no feedback goes stale: connect the live measures, and correct.

**Actions.**
- Collect the realised costs, the real participation, the [slippage](../../glossary.md#slippage)
  by component, the portfolio's gaps from its targets, and any breach.
- Update the capacities and budgets of impact from what was realised; adjust the caps of
  participation and turnover where needed.
- Define the triggers of [requalification](../../glossary.md#requalification), and push the
  corrections to S5.3, S5.6 and S5.7, to monitoring (S6) and to lifecycle (S7).

**KPI.** Gap between the view before trading and the realised (costs, participation, turnover);
headroom of capacity, the margin before the alpha erodes; capital efficiency, the
[information ratio](../../glossary.md#information-ratio) or the P&L per unit of budget.

**Guardrail.** An alert when the live rate of breaches exceeds its threshold, when the headroom
falls below X%, or when a constraint is active more than Y% of the time; each triggers an
automatic requalification.

**Output.** Live dashboard, versioned changes of the budgets and tickets of requalification →
S5.3, S5.6 and S5.7 in production, monitoring (S6), lifecycle (S7).

</details>

## Deliverables

- A clear specification: perimeter, axes, the hierarchy of hard and soft, priorities, rules of
  override, cadence.
- The versioned risk budget book: envelopes in numbers (risk, liquidity, capacity) per scope.
- The contract with S5.3: a library of constraints and penalties, schemas of exchange, and traced
  examples with their expected sensitivities.
- Runbooks: structural (periodic recalibration) and tactical (shocks, venues unavailable,
  abnormal spreads).
- Log of decisions: reasons, overrides, audits of the waterbed effect.
- Live KPI dashboard with alerts: use, breaches, headroom, activation.
- Replacement process: how to substitute a method of estimating costs or liquidity without
  breaking the interface; criteria of acceptance; [rollback](../../glossary.md#rollback).
