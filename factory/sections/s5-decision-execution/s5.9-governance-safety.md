# S5.9 — Governance, safety & risk-driven deployment

## TL;DR

The control tower of section 5: it authorises, frames and, when needed, stops the release of decisions into the market, each one explainable, bounded and reversible.
Novelties go live in small, controlled doses (paper, canary, gradual, full); nothing runs off undetected, a rollback takes minutes, and any trade can be explained in 5 minutes.
Its deployment rulebook sets the limits, gates and real-time controls that construction, budgets, transitions and execution (S5.3 to S5.7) obey.

## Purpose

Be the control tower that authorises, frames and, when needed, stops the release of decisions into
the market, and make each decision explainable, bounded and reversible: the factory's
[risk-driven deployment](../../glossary.md#risk-driven-deployment).

**Success looks like:** novelties released in small, controlled doses
([canary releases](../../glossary.md#canary-release)); no run-off that goes undetected; a clean
[rollback](../../glossary.md#rollback) in minutes; an audit in 5 minutes (who, what, why, which
limits, which optimality conditions).

The deployment rulebook goes to S5.3 as the contracts of constraints (soft and hard limits), to S5.4
as the limits that no budget crosses, to S5.6 and S5.7 as the [gates](../../glossary.md#gate) and
the real-time controls (throttles, stops, kill switch), and to S5.1 as the limits a new strategy
must fit. Monitoring (S6.1) receives the thresholds of trigger and the journals; stabilisation
(S6.5) the surfaces of action and who may activate them, and

releases and the decisions to promote, roll back or withdraw, through the decision log (S7.2); the
incident reports and their postmortems go to S7.9.

**Constraints.**
- Contractual formats: stable, versioned schemas; [point-in-time](../../glossary.md#point-in-time)
  guaranteed.

**Service levels:**
- Update of the limits: [T95](../../glossary.md#t95) < 1 min; of the thresholds: T95 < 5 min.
- Critical events (IPO, suspension, delisting, changes of regulation): ingested and propagated at
  T90 / T95 < 15 min; an alert when exceeded.

## Gates

**Entry.** Start only when:
- the rulebook is signed and the [RACI](../../glossary.md#raci) active;
- the matrices of limits are published, and the runbooks of gates and rollback ready;
- the controllers are tested, and [traceability](../../glossary.md#traceability) is on.

**Exit.** Verdict *pass* when all of these hold:
- the canary passed, and the tiers were crossed without alert;
- the cost analysis matches the forecast before trading;
- no hard limit breached for N days;
- an audit in 5 minutes succeeded.

*Retry* and *fail* follow the [gate verdict](../../glossary.md#gate-verdict) rule.

**Requalification.** Rerun the sub-section when:
- a [KPI](../../glossary.md#kpi) drifts persistently: the gap between live and simulated, excess costs, latency;
- new structural data arrives, or the microstructure or the regulation changes;
- a promising new method appears; it goes back through paper and canary.

Monitoring (S6) receives the adjusted thresholds and the new signals of alert. Lifecycle (S7)
receives the decisions to promote, roll back or withdraw, and the updates of the strategy's life:
clean closures, archiving, end of support.

## Steps

<details>
<summary><b>A · The rulebook and the field of play: the pre-flight checklist</b> — Design task</summary>

**Goal.** Set the rules before playing: objectives, perimeters, soft and hard limits, roles, the
criteria to move from paper to live, the triggers of stop.

**Actions.**
- Write a concise rulebook (≤ 10 pages): objectives; the perimeter of instruments and
  [venues](../../glossary.md#venue), with the [restricted list](../../glossary.md#restricted-list)
  of instruments not to trade; trading windows; soft and hard limits (risk,
  [liquidity](../../glossary.md#liquidity), costs, latency); rights and duties (who may activate
  what); the criteria of acceptance from paper to canary, gradual and full; the conditions of stop.
- Define the RACI matrix (research, approval, deployment, supervision) and the separation of
  roles.

**KPI.** Rate of ambiguous rules found in review < 5%; delay to update the rulebook after an
incident at T90 < 48 h.

**Guardrail.** Reading the rulebook is a mandatory gate for any release; a release missing a
criterion of acceptance is refused automatically.

**Output.** Rulebook v1 and the RACI → S5.3, S5.4, S5.6, S5.7, monitoring (S6), lifecycle (S7).

</details>

<details>
<summary><b>B · Living maps of risks and limits, soft and hard</b> — Design task</summary>

**Goal.** Translate the rulebook into budgets and bounds in numbers, testable in real time.

**Actions.**
- Define budgets and limits per family, strategy and venue: risk (volatility,
  [expected shortfall](../../glossary.md#expected-shortfall)), [capacity](../../glossary.md#capacity) and liquidity (average
  daily volume, depth, maximum participation), costs (budgets of [slippage](../../glossary.md#slippage) and impact), latency
  (the service level of routing), concentration (single names, sectors), leverage.
- Specify the soft thresholds (throttling) and the hard ones (stop), the windows of measurement
  and the grace times.

**KPI.** Rate of soft and hard breaches per period (< 1% hard); time out of band at T95 < 5 min a
day; share of orders refused for a limit (normal below X%).

**Guardrail.** Hard means an immediate stop; soft means a throttle, by a set factor of reduction.
Promotions freeze after more than N hard breaches in 30 days.

**Output.** The matrix of limits, as interface contracts → S5.3 (constraints), S5.4 (budgets),
S5.7 (routing), monitoring (S6).

</details>

<details>
<summary><b>C · Gates of deployment, from paper to full, and rollback</b> — Design task + Execution task</summary>

**Goal.** Push without breaking: small doses, a gradual rise, and the rollback ready before the go.
The stages and criteria are design; the runbooks and the journal are execution.

**Actions.**
- Define the stages of a [progressive rollout](../../glossary.md#progressive-rollout): paper,
  then canary (≤ 1% of capital or flow), then gradual (tiers of 1, 5, 25, 50 and 100%), then full.
- Specify the criteria of promotion (windows, stability, costs against the forecast, the gap
  between live and simulated), the criteria of rollback, and what is logged: the version of model
  and parameters, the hypotheses, who validates, why.
- Write the runbooks of rollback: neutralise positions, cancel orders, clear states.

**KPI.** Rate of success of the canaries (from 1% to 100% without alert) > 90%; time of rollback,
from alert to a safe state, at T90 < 10 min; rate of incidents per release < 5%, and their size
(P&L, [drawdown](../../glossary.md#drawdown), costs).

**Guardrail.** No big bang: skipping a tier is forbidden; rollback is the default when a key metric
stays out of its band for more than X min.

**Output.** Runbooks of gates and rollback, and the journal of releases → S5.6 and S5.7
(execution), monitoring (S6, alerts), lifecycle (S7, promotion and withdrawal).

</details>

<details>
<summary><b>D · Real-time guardrails: throttles, circuit breakers, kill switch</b> — Design task + Execution task</summary>

**Goal.** Protect in flight: slow down cleanly, isolate locally, and be able to cut everything.
Choosing the triggers is design; implementing and testing the controls is execution.

**Actions.**
- Implement throttles (reductions of participation, size or frequency), triggered by costs,
  latency, the gap between live and simulated, a drift of execution or a drift of [alpha](../../glossary.md#alpha).
- Local [circuit breakers](../../glossary.md#circuit-breaker) (per strategy and venue) and a
  global kill switch, the red button, with escalation and acknowledgement of each action.
- Tests end to end (simulation and [replay](../../glossary.md#replay)), and regular
  [drills](../../glossary.md#drill).

**KPI.** Latency of a trigger, from alert to action, at T95 < 1 min; false positives and negatives
on the triggers < 5%; coverage of tests > 95% of the critical cases.

**Guardrail.** A mandatory dry test before any activation in production; an engineering lock: no
single person can disable a hard stop.

**Output.** The real-time controllers and the table of thresholds → S5.7 (the [OMS](../../glossary.md#oms), [EMS](../../glossary.md#ems) and their
logic of steering), monitoring (S6, detection), lifecycle (S7, the evidence of tests).

</details>

<details>
<summary><b>E · Strong traceability and sovereignty, ready for audit</b> — Design task + Execution task</summary>

**Goal.** Explain and replay: which decision, when, and on what grounds? Sovereignty: the factory
holds every version it needs to replay a decision by itself. What to record is design; recording
it and keeping it replayable is execution.

**Actions.**
- Journal each decision: timestamp, versions of data, costs and constraints, the target weights,
  the sensitivities and optimality conditions of the solver, the gaps from target to executed,
  the changes after cost analysis, the events of control (throttle, stop), and who validated or
  acted.
- Keep the chain of versions: point-in-time data, parameters, releases, rules, limits; replay
  guaranteed bit for bit.

**KPI.** Coverage of traceability, the decisions with a complete journal, > 99%; delay of audit,
the time to explain a trade, < 5 min.

**Guardrail.** A promotion is blocked when an essential field is missing; a weekly cross review,
two pairs of eyes, is mandatory.

**Output.** A readable journal of audit and the artefacts to reproduce each decision → monitoring
(S6, attribution and diagnosis), lifecycle (S7, promotion and return), risk and compliance.

</details>

<details>
<summary><b>F · The loop of incident, requalification and learning</b> — Design task + Execution task</summary>

**Goal.** Close the loop: when something deviates, brake or cut, understand, and requalify. The
runbooks and rules are design; running the loop and closing its actions is execution.

**Actions.**
- Runbooks of incident response: detection, stabilisation
  (throttle, stop), diagnosis (the journals of step E, the signals of S6, the cost analysis),
  action (rollback, limits adjusted), and a postmortem review with teach-back
  ([blameless postmortem](../../glossary.md#blameless-postmortem)).
- A systematic [requalification](../../glossary.md#requalification) of a strategy on a drift of
  KPI, new data, a change of market or rule, or a promising new method.
- Feedback to S5.4 (budgets), S5.3 (constraints), step A (the rulebook), and steps C and D
  (thresholds).

**KPI.** Time to detect and time to return to a safe state ([MTTD / MTTR](../../glossary.md#mttd-mttr))
at T90 < 10 min; corrective actions closed within 7 days > 95%; repeated incidents down by more
than 50% over 90 days.

**Guardrail.** Deployments freeze while the root causes are not treated; any change of a limit by
more than X% gets an independent review.

**Output.** Incident reports and sheets of requalification → lifecycle (S7, the governance of the
cycle), S5.1, S5.2 and S5.8 (technical lessons), the risk committee.

</details>

## Deliverables

- A clear specification: the rulebook, the RACI, and the interfaces of limits and thresholds.
- Log of choices: decisions at the gates, validations, reasons, hypotheses.
- Testable modules and their documentation: throttles, circuit breakers, kill switch, runbooks of
  rollback and incident, scenarios of test.
- KPI tracking table, a minimal dashboard, connected to monitoring (S6).
- Replacement process: the criteria of a clean withdrawal, the plan of shutdown, archiving and
  audit.
