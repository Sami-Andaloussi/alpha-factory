# S7.9 — Incident response & blameless postmortems

## TL;DR

Stabilise fast, explain clearly, correct for good: who does what, when and how, as soon as a fault touches the factory (data, model, execution, latency, P&L).
Success is a short time to a safe state, a factual timeline, traceable decisions, and a blameless postmortem whose actions are verifiable and followed.
Its incident charter frames every intervention of S6.7; it takes S6.7's postmortems and turns their actions into missions and tests of non-regression.

## Purpose

Stabilise fast, explain clearly, correct for good. The page organises who does what, when and how,
as soon as a fault touches the factory: data, a model, execution, latency, P&L. Monitoring's
[intervention](../../glossary.md#intervention) (S6.7) carries out each response live; this page
frames the response before the crisis, and follows it through after. This is the factory's
[incident response](../../glossary.md#incident-response).

**Success looks like:** a short time to a safe state, a factual timeline, traceable decisions, and
a [blameless postmortem](../../glossary.md#blameless-postmortem) that leads to corrective actions, verifiable and followed. Every incident
becomes a lesson for the whole system, which strengthens the factory.

<details>
<summary><b>What each flow carries, constraints and service levels</b></summary>

Intervention (S6.7) brings the blameless postmortem of each incident, with its timeline and its
journal of decisions: the correlated alerts, the [SLOs](../../glossary.md#slo) and
[SLIs](../../glossary.md#sli), the [drifts](../../glossary.md#drift) detected, and the what-if of
section 4, since S6.7 re-runs the orders of an incident on the
[execution simulator](../../glossary.md#execution-simulator). The incidents of section 5 arrive the
same way. The deployment rulebook of S5.9 declares section 5's ability to reduce, cut and reroute,
and who may activate what. The replay API of S1.8 [replays](../../glossary.md#replay) the log, to
rebuild a timeline for audit; the provenance registry of S1.3 gives the versions and the
[lineage](../../glossary.md#lineage) of sections 1 and 2 behind each segment. The decision log of
S7.2 links each incident to the data, [features](../../glossary.md#feature), signals and orders it
touched.

The incident charter carries the frame: to intervention (S6.7), which applies it to every incident
(the roles, the on-call, the one channel, the templates, the matrix of engagement); to stabilisation
(S6.5), the automatic actions that each level of severity calls for. The drilled runbooks go to
S6.7, their one home. The journal of each incident (declaration, timeline, decisions, closure,
postmortem, actions assigned) goes to the system of record (S7.2). The corrective actions open
mission cards in the loop of S7.10, towards sections 1 to 6: fixes, refactors, new tests and alerts,
[guardrails](../../glossary.md#guardrail). Monitoring (S6.1) receives, through the KPI feed, the
times to detect, to acknowledge, to a safe state and to repair.

**Constraints.**
- Formats: unique incident IDs, timestamps in UTC, cross links from dataset to feature, signal and
  order.
- Integration: no tool imposed; clear APIs and documentation, timestamped logs, and timelines that
  can be replayed for audit.

**Service levels:**
- A safe state for a P1: from the first signal to the effective action, T90 ≤ 5 min.
- Propagation of decisions: a reduction or a cut is visible to execution and to monitoring at
  [T95](../../glossary.md#t95) ≤ 2 min.
- Critical market events (a halt, an IPO, a delisting): T95 ≤ 5 min to inform, and ≤ 10 min to a
  safe state when the impact is direct.

</details>

## Gates

**Entry.** Start only when:
- the on-call rota is published 30 days ahead, and the incident commander of the week is named;
- the matrix of severity is validated, and the [kill switch](../../glossary.md#kill-switch) and the switch-overs are tested (in
  simulation);
- ≥ 90% of the critical scenarios are covered by runbooks, and a [drill](../../glossary.md#drill) was passed this month;
- the templates (declaration, timeline, closure, postmortem) are available.

**Exit.** Per incident, verdict *pass* when all of these hold:
- the safe state is reached and verified, and the message of closure is sent;
- the postmortem is held within 72 h, and each corrective action carries a due date and a test of
  non-regression;
- the decision log is complete, with links to the data, features, signals and orders concerned.

*Retry* and *fail* follow the [gate verdict](../../glossary.md#gate-verdict) rule.

**Requalification.** Rerun the sub-section when:
- a major change arrives: a new flow of data, a new logic of scheduling, a new [venue](../../glossary.md#venue), a new type of
  order, a change of market rules;
- the [KPIs](../../glossary.md#kpi) drift 2 cycles in a row (for example, the time to a safe state or the false positives
  above their thresholds);
- the same pattern relapses, within 30 or 90 days.

Monitoring (S6) receives adjustments of its alerting (thresholds, grouping), and new SLIs and SLOs.
Lifecycle (S7) updates its [fitness functions](../../glossary.md#fitness-function) of resilience
(the time to a safe state, the rate of relapse) and its
[steering cadences](../../glossary.md#steering-cadence). Section 4 receives, as missions through
the loop of S7.10, the tests of non-regression and the cases for its execution simulator that each
incident leaves.

## Steps

<details>
<summary><b>1 · A frame ready before the crisis: roles, on-call, one channel</b> — Design task</summary>

**Goal.** Without a frame, an incident turns into cacophony: the skeleton comes first.

**Actions.**
- The roles: an incident commander (one voice, who decides), a scribe (the journal), a technical
  lead (the diagnosis), a liaison with the business and risk, and a liaison with brokers and
  providers; with the technical operator and the spokesperson that intervention (S6.7) names for
  each incident.
- On-call: a calendar, thresholds of escalation, relays by day and by night.
- One channel during an incident (text or voice), with rules of speaking: the incident commander
  first.
- Templates: the declaration of an incident, a timeline minute by minute, the message of closure.

**KPI.** Coverage of on-call (% of the time slots covered); the average delay before the incident
commander speaks, after a call.

**Guardrail.** A monthly review of the rotas; a silent call every week, to check that the chain
works.

**Output.** The frame of intervention (roles, rituals, templates), in the incident charter → every
section (S1 to S7); intervention (S6.7) applies it.

</details>

<details>
<summary><b>2 · Severity and the rules of engagement: the matrix, and the safe state</b> — Design task</summary>

**Goal.** With the roles in place, set when and how to act.

**Actions.**
- Levels that anyone can read, on the one scale of [incident severity](../../glossary.md#incident-severity)
  (P1 to P4, S6.7), by their impact on clients, capital, security or latency.
- Automatic actions for each level: kill or reduce, a switch back to the [champion](../../glossary.md#champion-challenger) model, suspect
  data isolated, orders slowed down or rerouted.
- Explicit rights: who may cut what, and on which criteria, as the deployment rulebook of S5.9
  declares them.

**KPI.** % of incidents classified in < 3 min; % of the decisions of safe state taken in < 5 min for
a P1.

**Guardrail.** A compact table of decision (10–15 lines at most); a quarterly audit of the relevance
of the thresholds.

**Output.** The matrix of engagement (level → actions, target delays, authorisations), in the
incident charter → execution (section 5, through stabilisation, S6.5), alerting (S6), the decision
log (S7.2).

</details>

<details>
<summary><b>3 · Runbooks and drills: a tested library</b> — Design task + Execution task</summary>

**Goal.** The rules of engagement demand codified gestures. Writing the runbooks is design; running
the drills is execution.

**Actions.**
- Write short runbooks for the major cases: an outage of data, drift or latency, failed orders, a
  gap of P&L between live and simulated, excess costs, a drift of [capacity](../../glossary.md#capacity).
- Regular drills (war games): replay 2–3 critical scenarios a month.
- Check reversibility: the procedures of [rollback](../../glossary.md#rollback) and of return to normal.

**KPI.** Coverage of the top risks by runbooks (%); the time to run each runbook in a drill.

**Guardrail.** Remove or simplify any runbook never used in 6 months, or failed 2 times.

**Output.** The library of runbooks, held by intervention (S6.7), and the calendar of drills →
on-call (S6 and S5), data (S1), features (S2), validation and simulation (S4).

</details>

<details>
<summary><b>4 · Detection, declaration, triage: take control</b> — Execution task</summary>

**Goal.** With the frame and the gestures in place, plug in the real flow; intervention (S6.7)
runs it live, under the charter.

**Actions.**
- The trigger: every priority gap becomes an incident, through a minimal form (what, when, symptom,
  likely impact).
- Triage by the incident commander: classify the level, open the timeline, announce the objective
  of safety.
- The scribe starts the factual journal: timestamped, with no interpretation.

**KPI.** [MTTD](../../glossary.md#mttd-mttr) (detection), the mean time for the incident commander to acknowledge, and the rate of
false positives of incidents.

**Guardrail.** A monthly review of the hygiene of alerting: grouping, backoff, dynamic thresholds.

**Output.** The incident ticket opened, the timeline active, the level assigned → the incident
commander, the scribe, monitoring (S6, the correlation of alerts), section 5 (immediate actions).

</details>

<details>
<summary><b>5 · Stabilise to a safe state, then a short diagnosis</b> — Execution task</summary>

**Goal.** Protect capital first, then understand; intervention (S6.7) runs it live, under the
charter.

**Actions.**
- Run the actions of the level, as the runbook plans them: kill, reduce, switch over.
- Check the residual exposure, the orders in queue, the data coming in, the consistency of the P&L.
- A fast diagnosis, boxed in time: short hypotheses (≤ N minutes), targeted tests, and a decision:
  fix now, or defer.

**KPI.** The time to a safe state (from detection until the fault is contained); % of incidents with a
hypothesis of cause ≤ 15 min.

**Guardrail.** When the time to a safe state exceeds its threshold twice, deployments freeze on the
perimeter until it improves.

**Output.** A safe state attested, a probable cause, and a plan of correction, immediate or deferred
→ execution (section 5), data and features (sections 1 and 2), tests (section 4), the decision log
(S7.2).

</details>

<details>
<summary><b>6 · Controlled recovery, and validation</b> — Execution task</summary>

**Goal.** Stabilised is not recovered: reopen step by step, measured; intervention (S6.7) runs it
live, under the charter.

**Actions.**
- Apply the minimum viable correction: a patch, a workaround, a setting.
- Put back online step by step: a ramp of exposure, and a closer watch.
- Validate against what is expected: P&L, latency and costs back in their ranges.
- The message of closure: what, when, the impact, the cause, the residual risk.

**KPI.** MTTR (back to nominal service); the rate of relapse within 24 h.

**Guardrail.** A progressive reopening is mandatory; a check of four lights: exposure, orders,
latency, P&L.

**Output.** The system recovered, and an internal public report → monitoring (S6, a close follow-up),
lifecycle (S7, [traceability](../../glossary.md#traceability)).

</details>

<details>
<summary><b>7 · A blameless postmortem, and actions made industrial</b> — Design task + Execution task</summary>

**Goal.** With a cool head, turn the event into lasting progress. Defining the corrective actions
and their tests is design; holding the postmortem and closing the loop is execution.

**Actions.**
- The standard postmortem, held within 72 h by intervention (S6.7) on the template of the charter:
  a factual timeline, contributing causes (technical, process, human, signal), systemic factors,
  no blame.
- Corrective actions (tests, alerts, refactors, guardrails), each assigned with a due date and a
  metric of success.
- Close the loop: publish to the decision log, push missions to sections 1 to 6, plan the test of
  non-regression.

**KPI.** % of postmortems held within 72 h; the rate of actions closed on time; the rate of relapse
at 30 and 90 days.

**Guardrail.** No action without a defined test of non-regression; a fortnightly review of the
actions that are late.

**Output.** The postmortem report, a traceable backlog of actions, and the tests of non-regression
→ monitoring (S6, alerts), section 4 (tests), sections 1 and 2 (data, features), section 5
(execution), lifecycle (S7, steering).

</details>

## Deliverables

- A clear specification: roles and on-call, the matrix of severity and the rules of engagement, the
  policies of communication.
- Log of choices and decisions: incident IDs, timelines, decisions of the incident commander,
  criteria of safe state, messages of closure.
- A library ready for use: concise runbooks (≤ 1 page each), checklists, procedures of switch-over
  and rollback.
- KPI tracking table: MTTD and MTTR, the time to acknowledge, the time to a safe state, % of
  postmortems within 72 h, the rate of relapse, false positives, the load of on-call.
- A clear replacement process: when a role is unavailable, a delegation set in advance; when a
  runbook fails, a workaround; when an alert is noisy, a controlled silencing, with a delay and a
  review.
