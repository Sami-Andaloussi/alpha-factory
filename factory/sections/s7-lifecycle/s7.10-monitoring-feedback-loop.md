# S7.10 — Monitoring-to-missions feedback loop

## TL;DR

Turns every relevant signal of monitoring into a short, reversible, measurable mission, with an owner, a priority, a test, acceptance criteria and a rollback.
Success is no orphan alert, a short delay from signal to decision, experiments run as canaries, decisions promoted or rolled back cleanly, and a written record.
It keeps the one queue of mission cards that monitoring's signals and every lifecycle page open, and routes each card to the section that does the work.

## Purpose

Turn every relevant signal of monitoring (a breached [SLO](../../glossary.md#slo), a
[drift](../../glossary.md#drift) of a [feature](../../glossary.md#feature),
[slippage](../../glossary.md#slippage), latency, a problem of data quality) into a short, reversible
and measurable mission, with an owner, a priority, a test, acceptance criteria and a
[rollback](../../glossary.md#rollback). This is the factory's
[monitoring-to-missions feedback loop](../../glossary.md#monitoring-to-missions-feedback-loop).

**Success looks like:** no orphan alert, a short delay from signal to decision, executions run as
canaries, decisions promoted or rolled back cleanly, and a written record in lifecycle's journal.

<details>
<summary><b>What each flow carries, constraints and service levels</b></summary>

Monitoring brings its qualified signals: the breaches of SLO (S6.1), the gaps of execution and their
costs (S6.2), the breaks of integrity and of data quality (S6.3), the drifts of models, features and
markets (S6.4), and the threats (S6.6). The context of section 5 (the real fills, the limits, the
incidents of execution) comes with the signals of S6.2; the health of data and features (service
levels, backfills, contracts) with those of S6.3 and with the mission cards of the
[data lifecycle](../../glossary.md#data-lifecycle) (S7.7). Every page of lifecycle opens its
missions here, as mission cards: steering (S7.1), the system of record (S7.2), fitness (S7.3), the
budgets of reliability and risk (S7.4), change (S7.5), the lifecycle of strategies (S7.6), data
(S7.7), capital (S7.8) and [incident response](../../glossary.md#incident-response) (S7.9).

The page keeps the one queue of missions, and routes each card to the section that does the work:
data (section 1), features (section 2), validation, simulation and cost analysis (section 4),
decision and execution (section 5), or the infrastructure. A mission that changes production travels
as a [release package](../../glossary.md#release-package) of
[change management](../../glossary.md#change-management) (S7.5), whose canary,
[kill switch](../../glossary.md#kill-switch) and rollback S5.9 applies. The decision of each mission
goes to the system of record (S7.2); the runbook of the loop's
[fallback](../../glossary.md#fallback) goes to S6.7. Monitoring (S6.1) receives, through the KPI
feed, the delays and the outcomes of the loop and, as feedback, new thresholds and metrics that move
from symptoms to causes.

**Constraints.**
- One common schema for the mission card: ID, source, hypothesis, bet, criteria, rollback, owner,
  due date.
- [Traceability](../../glossary.md#traceability) end to end: alert → mission → canary → decision → promotion.
- Compatibility: no mission bypasses validation and the simulator when its impact on the portfolio
  is not trivial.

**Service levels** (proposed):
- Ingestion of a signal: [T95](../../glossary.md#t95) ≤ 60 s (technical).
- A hot signal to a canary live: ≤ 6 h.
- A kill ≤ 1 min; a rollback ≤ 15 min.
- Critical events (an IPO, a suspension, a delisting, a change of ticker, a split): from the
  detection at the source to a mission card for data ≤ 15 min (T95), and an [idempotent](../../glossary.md#idempotent) backfill
  ready ≤ 2 h (T95).

</details>

## Gates

**Entry.** Start a mission only when:
- the alert is qualified (not noise), the triage is done, the data of context are available, and
  an owner is named;
- for a mission on data or features, a [schema contract](../../glossary.md#schema-contract) exists (S7.7), with a plan of idempotent
  backfill.

**Exit.** Verdict *pass* when all of these hold:
- the acceptance criteria are met, or the rollback is executed;
- the journal is complete, and the feedback is sent to monitoring and lifecycle;
- the thresholds and alerts are updated when needed.

*Retry* and *fail* follow the [gate verdict](../../glossary.md#gate-verdict) rule.

**Requalification.** Rerun the sub-section when:
- the [KPIs](../../glossary.md#kpi) of the loop drift (delays, success, reopened missions), market rules change, a new
  source of data arrives, the [regime](../../glossary.md#regime) changes, or [alpha](../../glossary.md#alpha) decays;
- a trigger fires: 3 similar hot signals within 7 days, a rate of rollback above 40% in one
  category, or noise above 60% of the alerts of one source.

Monitoring (S6) receives new thresholds, and new metrics that move from symptoms to causes.
Lifecycle (S7) receives the decisions, the [fitness functions](../../glossary.md#fitness-function) updated, and an adjustment of the
budgets of change when reliability is hit.

## Steps

<details>
<summary><b>1 · Capture and sort: a disciplined intake of signals</b> — Design task + Execution task</summary>

**Goal.** Move from a stream of alerts to one queue, without duplicates and sorted: hot, warm,
cold. Writing the schema and the rules of triage is design; running the queue is execution.

**Actions.**
- Normalise the schema of intake: timestamp, source, severity, a key that identifies the symptom,
  estimated impact, context.
- Remove the duplicates: the same key, close in time → one card.
- Rules of triage: hot (an immediate impact on P&L or risk), warm (important, not urgent), cold (to
  watch).

**KPI.** From signal to triage at T95: hot ≤ 5 min, warm ≤ 4 h; the rate of duplicates removed
≥ 60% at peak.

**Guardrail.** A threshold against flapping ([hysteresis](../../glossary.md#hysteresis)), a limit of rate per source, and a
cooldown after acknowledgement.

**Output.** A sorted list of candidate missions → step 2 (qualification).

</details>

<details>
<summary><b>2 · Qualify, and write the mission card</b> — Design task</summary>

**Goal.** Turn a signal into the smallest experiment that can act.

**Actions.**
- From the problem (the gap observed) to a hypothesis (its cause), then to the smallest bet: the
  smallest change that can be tested.
- The experiment (where and how: a sub-universe, a size, a duration), the acceptance criteria, a
  rollback ready, an owner, a due date.
- The impact expected (in basis points, the risks touched), and the domains affected: data,
  features, execution, infrastructure.

**KPI.** From triage to mission at T90: hot ≤ 30 min, warm ≤ 1 day.

**Guardrail.** A card with no criterion of success and no plan of rollback is refused; every
mission is linked to the alert it came from.

**Output.** The mission card, signed → step 3 (prioritisation), and sections 1, 2, 4 and 5 and the
infrastructure, for their technical input.

</details>

<details>
<summary><b>3 · Prioritise and sequence</b> — Design task</summary>

**Goal.** Order by impact, risk and delay, and avoid scattering.

**Actions.**
- A simple score: the net impact expected (basis points, risk avoided) × the probability of
  success ÷ the effort.
- A calendar with a maximum of work in progress: a limit on the hot and warm missions run in
  parallel.
- A reserve for the canaries: a quota of capital and orders for the tests.

**KPI.** Rate of completion of the weekly top N ≥ 80%; work in progress ≤ its threshold; a hot
mission pre-empts the queue in < 2 h.

**Guardrail.** Against local optimisations: an estimate of the impact on the portfolio is required,
not only a micro-metric.

**Output.** The prioritised backlog → step 4 (design and protections), and its execution by
sections 1, 2, 4 and 5 and the infrastructure.

</details>

<details>
<summary><b>4 · Design the experiment and its protections: a canary ready to run</b> — Design task</summary>

**Goal.** Make the test safe: small, reversible, measurable.

**Actions.**
- Define the population of the canary (a sub-universe, a size of order, windows), its minimum
  duration, and the metrics before and after.
- The kill switch, the thresholds to stop, and the plan of rollback (parameters, data).
- Checks of integrity: data, and an idempotent backfill for a mission on data.

**KPI.** Coverage of the metrics: at least 1 of cost, 1 of performance and 1 of risk; the delays of
switch-over: a kill ≤ 1 min, a rollback ≤ 15 min.

**Guardrail.** No deployment without a kill switch and a scripted rollback; a dry run on a short
history.

**Output.** The plan of the experiment, validated, and the checklist of the canary → step 5 (the
run).

</details>

<details>
<summary><b>5 · Execute and measure: an instrumented canary</b> — Execution task</summary>

**Goal.** Observe the real effect, cleanly.

**Actions.**
- Launch the canary, journal the changes, collect before, during and after.
- Compare with benchmarks: a control matched on assets and time.
- Produce a short report: the effect, its variance, side effects, incidents.

**KPI.** Canaries completed with no incident ≥ 95%; the latency of the metrics at T95 ≤ 5 min.

**Guardrail.** Automatic stops when the costs or the risks exceed their thresholds; an alarm when
data are missing.

**Output.** The canary report → step 6 (the decision).

</details>

<details>
<summary><b>6 · Decide, promote or roll back, and record</b> — Execution task</summary>

**Goal.** Settle it (promote, iterate or roll back), close, and learn.

**Actions.**
- Decide against the acceptance criteria set at step 2.
- When promoted: a gradual ramp in steps, with reviews at T+1 and T+5; when it fails: the rollback,
  a short analysis of the cause, and the next bet.
- Record what was done, the result and the decisions: publish them to lifecycle's decision log and
  to the catalogue of missions.

**KPI.** From signal to decision at T95: hot ≤ 24 h, warm ≤ 5 days; the rate of success of the
missions; the rate of rollback (expected not to be zero); reopened missions < 10%.

**Guardrail.** No promotion when the impact on the portfolio was not measured; a check of costs and
risks is mandatory at D+5.

**Output.** The decision log → lifecycle (S7), monitoring (S6), as feedback on its thresholds and
alerts.

</details>

## Deliverables

1. Specification of the loop: the schema from signal to mission and decision, the triage (hot,
   warm, cold), the service levels, the steps of the canary.
2. Log of choices (hypotheses, arbitrations, criteria), and a register of decisions linked to the
   artefacts: code, parameters, datasets.
3. Templates ready to use: the mission card, the plan of a canary, the checklist of kill and
   rollback, the report before and after.
4. KPI table, kept up to date: delays, conversion, success, rollback, net impact, reopened missions.
5. A clear replacement process: when the loop is unavailable, a clear fallback (a minimum reserve
   for canaries, who decides, how to trace).
