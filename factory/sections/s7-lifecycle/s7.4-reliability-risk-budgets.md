# S7.4 — Reliability budgets (SLO/SLI) & risk budgets

## TL;DR

An automatic, traceable frame that says when the factory may accelerate (deploy, retrain, add exposure) and when it must ease off (freeze, reduce, repair).
It couples two simple counters, the error budget left and the risk budget consumed, into traffic lights tied to mechanisms: ramp-up, freezes, kill switch.
It reads S6.1's SLO catalogue and S5.4's risk budget book; its policy matrix tells section 5 and change management what each state allows.

## Purpose

Set up an automatic, traceable frame that says *when* the factory may accelerate (deploy, retrain,
add exposure) and *when* it must ease off (freeze, reduce, repair). It couples two simple counters:
reliability ([SLOs](../../glossary.md#slo) promised against [SLIs](../../glossary.md#sli) measured, and the [error budget](../../glossary.md#error-budget) left) and risk (the budgets
consumed: [tracking error](../../glossary.md#tracking-error), [expected shortfall](../../glossary.md#expected-shortfall), stress, [liquidity](../../glossary.md#liquidity), [turnover](../../glossary.md#turnover), [capacity](../../glossary.md#capacity)).

**Success looks like:**
- promises measured continuously, with an error budget that is understood and used;
- clear risk budgets, consumed in real time;
- readable rules of coupling (traffic lights), tied to mechanisms of execution (ramp-up, freezes,
  [kill switch](../../glossary.md#kill-switch));
- one dashboard and a [decision log](../../glossary.md#decision-log) that close the loop.

imply. The steering note of S7.1 brings the budgets as arbitrated.
There is one catalogue of SLOs and one book of risk budgets: this page sets the ceilings per horizon

The policy matrix says what each state allows: to S5.4, the ceilings per horizon and the caps on

SLIs, SLOs and error budgets chosen for each critical flow, which its catalogue registers, the
states of the traffic lights and the thresholds of alert on SLOs and budgets; the decisions go to
the system of record (S7.2).

**Constraints and service levels.**
- [Point-in-time](../../glossary.md#point-in-time) timestamps for every measure.
- Integration of critical events (IPO, suspensions, delistings, changes of tick or lot, new [venues](../../glossary.md#venue)):
  at T90 / [T95](../../glossary.md#t95), with an alert when exceeded.
- A service level for the latency of update, for example "p95 < X min before the open" for the flows
  before trading.
- Stable, versioned formats for the metrics and the states, compatible with audit and [replay](../../glossary.md#replay).

</details>

## Gates

**Entry.** Start only when:
- the register of critical items and horizons is validated (step 1);
- the method of measuring the SLIs is defined: windows, quantiles, owners;
- a point-in-time method exists for risk (tracking error, expected shortfall, stress, liquidity),
  with base scenarios of stress.

**Exit.** Verdict *pass* when all of these hold:
- the SLOs and SLIs are active, and the error budgets computed (step 2);
- the risk budgets are in place, with their consumption live (step 3);
- the policy matrix and its runbooks are published, and the mechanisms of execution tested (steps
  4 and 5);
- the unified dashboard and the decision log are in operation (step 6).

*Retry* and *fail* follow the [gate verdict](../../glossary.md#gate-verdict) rule.

**Requalification.** Rerun the sub-section when:
- an SLO drifts beyond its threshold, the burn rate accelerates, or gaps between live and simulated
  persist;
- the market [regime](../../glossary.md#regime) changes (volatility, liquidity), or new market rules arrive;
- a new strategy or method changes the horizons or the capacity;
- a major incident, or a series of overrides, calls for a postmortem, and for updated thresholds
  and [hysteresis](../../glossary.md#hysteresis).

Monitoring (S6) receives new useful SLIs, adjusted thresholds, and alerts to create or remove.
Lifecycle's fitness and release (S7.3, S7.5) receive the needs of architecture (latency end to end,
coupling) and adjustments of the release cadence; the
[data lifecycle](../../glossary.md#data-lifecycle) (S7.7) receives the needs of data
([provenance](../../glossary.md#provenance), contracts), as missions through the loop of S7.10.

## Steps

<details>
<summary><b>1 · Map the perimeter and the horizons of decision</b> — Design task</summary>

**Goal.** List what is critical (flows, jobs, releases, models, strategies) and at which horizon the
factory decides (within the day, daily, weekly). Without it, no useful SLO and no relevant budget
can be defined.

**Actions.**
- Draw up the list of critical flows and artefacts: data before the open, computations end to end,
  the generation of orders, the aggregations of risk.
- For each: its owner, its window of use (before the open, after the close), its tolerance to
  latency, and its business impact when it drifts.
- Define the taxonomy of risk that matters (tracking error, expected shortfall, stress, liquidity,
  turnover, capacity), aligned with the horizon.

**KPI.** Critical coverage = critical items mapped / critical items identified ≥ 95%.

**Guardrail.** No SLO without an owner and a defined window of use; otherwise the measure misses the
real stake.

**Output.** The register of critical items and horizons: one table of item, owner, window, impact
and horizon → step 2 (SLOs and SLIs), step 3 (risk budgets).

</details>

<details>
<summary><b>2 · Define the SLIs and SLOs, the error budgets, and their measure</b> — Design task</summary>

**Goal.** Turn the critical points into promises that can be checked.

**Actions.**
- Choose 2–4 SLIs at most per critical flow, readable, for example: freshness of data before the
  open, latency end to end at p95, rate of failed releases, time to repair.
- Set the matching SLOs, for example "≥ 99.5% of data fresh < 5 min before the open", or "p95 end to
  end < 2 min", in S6.1's catalogue, the one home of every SLO.
- Define the error budget, 1 minus the rate of success of the SLO over the period, and its burn
  rate, the speed at which it is consumed.
- Describe how it is measured: window, aggregation, point-in-time timestamps.

**KPI.** Attainment of each SLO; error budget remaining (%); burn rate (per day, per week).

**Guardrail.** No moving of the goal posts: lowering an SLO to avoid a freeze is forbidden; only a
validated process of reframing may change it, outside an incident.

**Output.** The specification of the SLOs, SLIs and error budgets (definitions, windows, thresholds,
burn rate, owners) → step 4 (rules of coupling), step 6 (dashboard).

</details>

<details>
<summary><b>3 · Define the risk budgets and their consumption</b> — Design task</summary>

**Goal.** Frame the boldness of the portfolio.

**Actions.**
- Set ceilings per horizon: maximum tracking error, maximum expected shortfall (on a consistent
  horizon), maximum stress losses, minimum liquidity, maximum turnover, capacity per instrument and
  strategy.
- Describe the rules of aggregation (per strategy, book, instrument) and the frequencies of update.
- Define the metrics of consumption: % of tracking error consumed, expected shortfall realised
  against budget, stress losses realised against scenario, capacity used, slippage against budget.

**KPI.** % of tracking error consumed, % of expected shortfall consumed, the stress gap (observed
minus scenario), capacity used, turnover realised against budgeted.

**Guardrail.** Horizons aligned: a daily budget is never judged with a weekly measure; point-in-time
computations, against any [leakage](../../glossary.md#leakage); a minimum coverage of scenarios of stress (≥ N independent
scenarios).

**Output.** The risk budgets (ceilings, point-in-time methods of computation, aggregations,
frequencies), carried by the risk budget book of S5.4 → step 4 (rules of coupling), section 5
(decision and execution).

</details>

<details>
<summary><b>4 · Write the rules of coupling (reliability × risk) and the policies</b> — Design task</summary>

**Goal.** Turn the counters into mechanised decisions: traffic lights.

**Actions.**
- Define a matrix of states:
  - reliability OK (error budget > X, a low burn rate), alert, or KO (budget exhausted);
  - risk OK (consumption < Y), alert, or high (close to the ceiling).
- Attach to each cell its allowed and forbidden actions:
  - OK and OK: deployments allowed, a faster ramp-up;
  - reliability KO, whatever the risk: a freeze (on a defined scope), and corrections first;
  - risk high, whatever the reliability: caps on size and turnover, and the entry of new
    challengers delayed.
- Add hysteresis (against flapping), dampers against pro-cyclicality (acceleration limited when
  volatility climbs), and rules of override: who, when, how, and why, journalled.

**KPI.** Automatic decisions triggered over the total; time spent in a freeze; incidents after
release over releases.

**Guardrail.** A limit on overrides (for example ≤ 1 decision in 10), each reviewed afterwards;
thresholds with buffer zones, against oscillation.

**Output.** The policy matrix and its runbooks (the matrix from states to actions, thresholds,
hysteresis, overrides, examples) → step 5 (execution), section 5 (execution within the day),
monitoring (S6, for the alerts).

</details>

<details>
<summary><b>5 · Set up the mechanisms of execution: throttle, ramp-up, kill switch</b> — Design task + Execution task</summary>

**Goal.** Make the policies happen without improvisation. The calendar, the steps and the runbooks
are design; running and testing them is execution.

**Actions.**
- Define a calendar of change (safe windows) and checklists before each release.
- Standardise ramp-ups in steps (exposure, capital, universe), a throttle of deployment
  (progressive), and a kill switch and a rollback that are tested.
- Prepare runbooks from state to state (KO to alert to OK), against jolts.

**KPI.** Change failure rate, time to repair, lead time for change, throughput of releases, rate of
successful rollback.

**Guardrail.** Two pairs of eyes for the kill switch and the rollback; regular tests of restoration;
a clear separation of roles (author, operator).

**Output.** Runbooks and templates of change (generic scripts and processes, criteria of rollback,
roles, responsibilities) → operations and release, section 5 (decision and execution).

</details>

<details>
<summary><b>6 · One dashboard and one decision log</b> — Execution task</summary>

**Goal.** Give a double gauge on one line, "error budget consumed" and "risk consumed", and keep a
record of the choices.

**Actions.**
- Build one view per strategy or flow: attainment of the SLOs, error budget remaining and burn rate,
  % of tracking error and expected shortfall consumed, the states of the traffic lights, the actions
  triggered.
- Keep a decision log (who, what, when, why), tied to the measures and to the state: input, action,
  result, and the postmortem when there was an incident.

**KPI.** Freshness of the dashboard; the rate of decisions that can be traced, with links to their
measures; the delay of update after an action.

**Guardrail.** An alert on any decision with no trace, or with no measure attached; an alert when the
gap between live and simulated exceeds its threshold.

**Output.** The unified dashboard and the versioned register of decisions → lifecycle's reviews (the
[steering cadences](../../glossary.md#steering-cadence)), the loop from monitoring to missions (S7.10).

</details>

## Deliverables

1. The register of critical items and horizons: owner, windows, impact, horizon.
2. The specification of SLOs, SLIs and error budgets: definitions, windows, thresholds, burn rate,
   measure.
3. The risk budgets: tracking error, expected shortfall, stress, liquidity, turnover, capacity,
   with their point-in-time methods and frequencies.
4. The policy matrix and its runbooks: traffic lights, hysteresis, overrides, examples.
5. Runbooks of execution: windows of change, ramp-up in steps, throttle, kill switch, rollback.
6. The unified dashboard (the double gauge of reliability and risk) and the decision log, tied to the
   measures.
7. A clear replacement process: how to freeze or replace a policy or a threshold, and how to go back.
