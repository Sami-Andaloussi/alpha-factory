# S7.8 — Capital, capacity & cost steering

## TL;DR

Gives the right size to the right place at the right pace, to turn validated signals into a net P&L that lasts and scales.
Every week or fortnight it arbitrates capital, capacity and costs, smooths every move, respects the budgets of risk, and journals each choice.
It allocates within each strategy's capital tier (S7.6); its capital allocation plan goes to the allocation layer (S5.4) and the transition planner (S5.6).

## Purpose

Give the right size to the right place, at the right pace, to turn validated signals into a net P&L
that lasts and scales. In practice: arbitrate capital, [capacity](../../glossary.md#capacity) and
costs at a cadence (weekly or fortnightly), smooth the moves (ramp-up and ramp-down), respect the
budgets of risk, and journal every choice. This is the factory's steering of capital, capacity and
costs.

**Success looks like:** a net [information ratio](../../glossary.md#information-ratio) of the portfolio in line with what was expected,
costs under control, capacity respected, sizes that stay stable (no yo-yo), and full [traceability](../../glossary.md#traceability).

- Formats: normalised target sizes (the same units), with their metadata: timestamp, version,
  reason.

**Service levels:**
- The target sizes for the next day are published before the cut-off (H−x), at

- The register of capacity is adjusted within 1 business day (T95) after an IPO, a suspension, a
  delisting, or a change of market rules that monitoring signals.
- The reconciliation of realised against expected is published within 24 h (T90) after the end of
  each window of rebalancing.

## Gates

**Entry.** Start only when:
- the strategies have passed the simulator and cost analysis, with plausible costs;
- the universe, the budgets of risk, the cadence and the roles are validated by lifecycle;
- monitoring is connected, to report the costs realised and the events of liquidity.

**Exit.** Verdict *pass* when all of these hold:
- the allocation policy is published: sizes, thresholds, caps;
- the runbooks of ramp and the scheduler are active, and the operational journal is in place;
- 2 consecutive cadences show a net information ratio within the expected band, and 0 overrun of
  participation.

*Retry* and *fail* follow the [gate verdict](../../glossary.md#gate-verdict) rule.

**Requalification.** Rerun the sub-section when:
- costs drift: the error between expected and realised costs exceeds 20% over 2 or more cadences;
- the [regime](../../glossary.md#regime) changes: spreads, volatility or liquidity leave their bands, or new market rules
  arrive;
- the structure changes: units are added or removed, correlations or [breadth](../../glossary.md#breadth) change notably;
- performance slips: the net information ratio of the portfolio leaves its band over 2 rolling
  windows.

Monitoring (S6) receives new thresholds of alert on costs, slippage and turnover. Lifecycle (S7)
receives adjustments of cadences and budgets, and targeted freezes or restarts.

## Steps

<details>
<summary><b>1 · Set the frame of allocation: units, budgets, cadence</b> — Design task</summary>

**Goal.** Without a frame, allocation scatters and becomes pro-cyclical.

**Actions.**
- Define the unit of allocation (a strategy, a sleeve or a book) and its tradable universe.
- Set the objectives of performance (a target net information ratio, a tolerable [drawdown](../../glossary.md#drawdown)) and
  the budgets: risk, costs, turnover.
- Draw the cadence of decision (weekly or fortnightly), the windows of action, and the roles: this
  page against execution.

**KPI.** Rate of coverage (units of allocation defined over units active) ≥ 99%; stability of the
cadence: decisions outside the cadence = 0, except on an incident.

**Guardrail.** Any decision outside the cadence requires an incident as its reason, and the
approval of lifecycle.

**Output.** The charter of steering (frame, roles, budgets, cadence) → step 2 (the register), step
4 (the allocation policy).

</details>

<details>
<summary><b>2 · Build the living register of capacity and costs</b> — Design task</summary>

**Goal.** The frame demands real, measured limits.

**Actions.**
- For each unit: its safe capacity (maximum participation, depth, liquid hours) and its
  sustainable turnover.
- Curves of cost (commissions, spreads, impact) per size and window of the market, read per unit
  from the cost surface of S5.8.
- Rules of freeze and thaw: suspensions, special days, markets closed or volatile.

**KPI.** The error between expected and realised costs (median, %), with an alert above 20% over 2
cadences; overruns of participation (occurrences) = 0.

**Guardrail.** The ramp freezes automatically when participation exceeds its threshold, or when
abnormal liquidity is detected.

**Output.** The register of capacity and costs, versioned, and updated from what monitoring and
[execution analytics](../../glossary.md#execution-analytics) report → step 3 (net information ratio before trading), step 5 (the
scheduler).

</details>

<details>
<summary><b>3 · Estimate the net information ratio before trading, per unit and for the portfolio</b> — Design task</summary>

**Goal.** A gross information ratio, with no costs and no capacity, misleads.

**Actions.**
- Assess the quality of information (the [information coefficient](../../glossary.md#information-coefficient), its stability over time) and
  the effective breadth: how independent the opportunities really are.
- Bring in the costs and capacity of the register, to obtain the net information ratio before
  trading, per unit.
- Map the curve from capital to net information ratio, and the effect of correlation:
  diversification against cannibalisation.

**KPI.** The gap between the net information ratio before trading and the [out-of-sample](../../glossary.md#in-sample-out-of-sample) [backtests](../../glossary.md#backtest)
stays within ±X, with an alert when it breaks 2 times; effective breadth does not fall at constant
capital.

**Guardrail.** When correlations explode, the efficient points are assessed again before any
increase of size.

**Output.** The table of what can be allocated: the net information ratio expected, the margins,
the efficient points → step 4 (the allocation policy).

</details>

<details>
<summary><b>4 · State the allocation policy and the rules of elasticity</b> — Design task</summary>

**Goal.** Turn the analysis into simple, stable rules that can be executed.

**Actions.**
- Define the target sizes per unit, subject to the budgets and to diversification.
- Set rules of elasticity to the signals (thresholds, dead bands, [hysteresis](../../glossary.md#hysteresis)), against excess
  turnover.
- Set the limits of risk per unit and overall ([tracking error](../../glossary.md#tracking-error), exposure), and safety nets: a stop
  of allocation, caps.

**KPI.** Turnover induced by the policy ≤ its budget; stability of the sizes (their churn) within
the band defined.

**Guardrail.** A minimum hysteresis and dead bands are mandatory; allocating to a mere "winner of the
month" is forbidden.

**Output.** The written allocation policy (target sizes, thresholds, caps and floors) → step 5
(ramp and scheduler), step 6 (journal and control).

</details>

<details>
<summary><b>5 · Set up the ramp-up, the ramp-down, and the scheduler of rebalancing</b> — Design task + Execution task</summary>

**Goal.** The policy must live over time without breaking the instrument. Writing the steps and the
calendar is design; running them is execution.

**Actions.**
- Define the steps of rise and fall within each strategy's capital tier: maximum speeds, the size
  of each step, the criteria of freeze.
- Schedule the rebalancing: frequencies, cheap windows of the market, and the days and hours to
  avoid.
- Keep apart the order of decision (here) and the details of execution (section 5).

**KPI.** Steps respected: unjustified deviations from the plan = 0; the average cost per
rebalancing ≤ the budget of cost per unit.

**Guardrail.** The ramp stops at once when the cost per trade stays above its threshold, or when
spreads or volatility leave their bounds.

**Output.** The plans in time (plans of ramp, the calendar of rebalancing, the rules to stop), as the
capital allocation plan → decision and execution (scheduling), step 6 (control and feedback).

</details>

<details>
<summary><b>6 · Control live, reconcile, journal, and close the loop</b> — Design task + Execution task</summary>

**Goal.** Without a short loop, the net information ratio drifts and capacity is overestimated.
Adjusting the register and the windows is design; comparing and journalling is execution.

**Actions.**
- Compare realised against expected: net alpha, costs, slippage, induced turnover.
- Feed the register (step 2) with the costs realised; adjust the windows and the participations.
- Journal every decision (who, what, why), and hold reviews at the cadence, with actions.

**KPI.** The net information ratio of the portfolio against expected, within ±Y over 4–8 weeks; the
rate of closure of the actions of the reviews ≥ 90% at D+10.

**Guardrail.** A review afterwards is mandatory when 2 consecutive bands break; the register and
the cadence are then recalibrated.

**Output.** The net dashboard, the decision log, and missions to monitoring and lifecycle → every
step (the loop of improvement), lifecycle (cadences), monitoring (alerts).

</details>

## Deliverables

1. The charter of steering: units, budgets, cadence, roles.
2. The living register of capacity and costs, versioned, and fed by monitoring.
3. The table of what can be allocated: net information ratio before trading, efficient points,
   constraints.
4. The allocation policy: target sizes, thresholds, caps and floors, dead bands and hysteresis.
5. The runbooks of ramp-up and ramp-down, and the scheduler: calendar, windows, rules to stop and
   freeze.
6. The decision log, tied to the complete operational chain.
7. [KPI](../../glossary.md#kpi) dashboard (per step), and the procedure of [requalification](../../glossary.md#requalification).
8. A replacement process: how to freeze or replace a unit without breaking the portfolio.
