# S7.5 — Change management & release strategies

## TL;DR

Puts any change into production (data, features, models, logic of alpha, execution) in a way that is reversible, traced and painless.
Each change travels as one immutable release package, checked before flight, rehearsed in production-like conditions, then rolled out step by step under guard, with a rollback in seconds.
It applies the fitness gates of S7.3 and the freezes of S7.4; section 5 (S5.9) rolls each package out by its plan.

## Purpose

Put *any* change into production (data, [features](../../glossary.md#feature), models, logic of [alpha](../../glossary.md#alpha), execution) in a way that
is reversible, traced and painless. This is the factory's [change management](../../glossary.md#change-management).

**Success looks like:** frequent releases with no major incident, a [rollback](../../glossary.md#rollback) in a few seconds,
proof of conformity with what was validated upstream, and the gaps between live and canary under
control.

with their guarantees of idempotence: the [sealed batches](../../glossary.md#sealed-batch) of the

travel as release packages: announced, with a period of coexistence and a rollback.

Each release package goes, with its rollout plan, to the governance of section 5 (S5.9), which
routes the flow and the capital step by step and holds the map of the [kill switch](../../glossary.md#kill-switch); section 5
applies the change in the windows of the market the plan allows. The operational runbooks go to
S6.7; the missions of follow-up to the loop of S7.10. Monitoring (S6.1) receives, through the KPI
feed, the series after each release (gaps, errors, latency) and the events of promotion and
rollback. The register of changes, enriched at each step, and the consumption of the budgets go to
the system of record (S7.2); the calendar of the next releases reaches steering (S7.1) through the
KPI feed, which the [pre-read](../../glossary.md#pre-read) of S6.1 carries.

**Constraints.**
- Test and production are identical: the same procedures of packaging and activation.
- [Deterministic](../../glossary.md#deterministic) transformations: [seeds](../../glossary.md#seed), order and timestamps under control.
- Versioned schemas, compatible by "expand and contract".
- Windows: no promotion during the blackouts defined (the open, macro announcements, stress
  detected).

**Service levels:**
- Effective rollback, from the kill to the measured stop: [T95](../../glossary.md#t95) ≤ X s.
- Propagation of a switch: T95 ≤ Y s.
- Update of an artefact: T95 ≤ Z min.
- Critical market events (an IPO, a suspension, a regulatory change): a rule they affect is
  integrated or neutralised fast, by a switch or a safe deactivation, at T90 / T95.

</details>

## Gates

**Entry.** Start only when:
- the evidence of validation, the simulator and cost analysis is linked to the package;
- the manifest is complete: artefacts, versions, dependencies, risks, rollback;
- the contracts and smoke tests are defined, and green in a neutral environment;
- the window of the market and the rollout plan are validated, with explicit criteria of promotion.

**Exit.** Verdict *pass* when all of these hold:
- the final soak passed at the target step, and the gaps stayed within their bands for the time
  required;
- the register is updated: decisions, measures, incidents if any;
- the runbooks, tests and contracts are updated, and the time of rollback measured ≤ its objective;
- the missions of follow-up are filed, when corrective actions are needed.

*Retry* and *fail* follow the [gate verdict](../../glossary.md#gate-verdict) rule.

**Requalification.** Rerun the sub-section when:
- a significant [drift](../../glossary.md#drift) follows a release (costs, latency, errors);
- the market changes (microstructure, rules, calendars);
- data or schemas evolve in a way that breaks the contracts;
- a promising new method, or a better proof of capacity, arrives;
- an incident occurs, or a budget of reliability is breached.

Monitoring (S6) receives refined thresholds and new metrics of early warning. Lifecycle (S7)
receives adjustments of the cadences, of the budgets of risk and reliability, and of the priority of
missions. Validation (section 4) receives new tests, from the cases found in staging or live, as
missions through the loop of S7.10; data and features (sections 1 and 2) harden their contracts and
their [replay](../../glossary.md#replay).

## Steps

<details>
<summary><b>1 · Define the unit of release and the register of changes</b> — Design task</summary>

**Goal.** Wrap what changes (code or rules, artefacts of data or features, parameters or models,
configuration of execution) into one immutable package with its manifests (versions, [fingerprints](../../glossary.md#fingerprint),
dependencies, compatibility), and open a line in the decision log: who, when, what, why, the risks,
the plan of rollback.

**Actions.**
- The format of the unit of release: the package, its manifests, and the evidence of validation
  upstream.
- A mandatory link to the validations of the simulator, cost analysis and paper trading, to the
  point-in-time data, and to the hypotheses of capacity.
- The minimal schema of the register: ID of the release, perimeter, risks, plan of test, plan of
  rollback, windows of the market, criteria of acceptance.

**KPI.** Full [traceability](../../glossary.md#traceability): the % of releases with their manifests and links of evidence ≥ 99%.

**Guardrail.** Blocked automatically when a manifest is incomplete or an artefact is not versioned
(a test of presence, and of the hashes).

**Output.** One release package, and one complete entry in the register → step 2 (compatibility),
lifecycle (traceability).

</details>

<details>
<summary><b>2 · Compatibility and pre-flight: contracts, smoke tests, migrations by "expand and contract"</b> — Design task + Execution task</summary>

**Goal.** Guarantee that the package is compatible with the data, schemas, APIs and calendars, and
that it does not leak time; prepare reversible migrations (expand, migrate, contract) when the
structure evolves. Writing the contracts and the plan of migration is design; running the smoke
tests is execution.

**Actions.**
- Contracts of compatibility: schemas, ranges of time, units, maximum latency.
- Blocking smoke tests: determinism end to end, no [leakage](../../glossary.md#leakage) in time, latency within its budget, a
  valid configuration.
- A plan of migration in two stages (expand, then contract), documented in the register, with its
  point of return.

**KPI.** Rate of smoke tests passed ≥ 99%; duration of the pre-flight, P50 and P95.

**Guardrail.** No take-off when non-determinism is detected, when a gap of schema is not covered by
the expand, or when latency exceeds its budget.

**Output.** The pre-flight report, PASS or FAIL, and the artefacts of migration ready → step 3
(rehearsal), step 4 (rollout plan).

</details>

<details>
<summary><b>3 · Rehearsal in production-like conditions: a realistic staging, and a rollback rehearsed</b> — Execution task</summary>

**Goal.** Replay real flows (data, latencies, profiles of orders) to observe the behaviour, measure
the gaps against the [baseline](../../glossary.md#baseline), and rehearse the rollback against the clock.

**Actions.**
- Replay scenarios of the market: calm, stress, events.
- Measure: simulated [slippage](../../glossary.md#slippage), costs, latency, stability, the use of quotas and rate limits.
- A rehearsal of rollback against the clock: a target time, and the procedures.

**KPI.** The gap between staging and baseline (slippage, costs, latency) within the bands defined;
the time of rollback measured at P95 ≤ its objective.

**Guardrail.** When the rollback takes longer than its threshold, or the gaps leave their bands: back
to step 2, for adjustments, before going further.

**Output.** The report of rehearsal, the time of rollback measured, and a refined runbook → step 4
(a rollout plan in figures), monitoring (the thresholds of guard).

</details>

<details>
<summary><b>4 · A reversible rollout plan: pattern, windows of the market, criteria of promotion</b> — Design task</summary>

**Goal.** Choose the pattern (blue-green, canary, progressive), set the steps (of exposure or
capital), the window of the market ([liquidity](../../glossary.md#liquidity), volatility), the criteria of promotion and the
thresholds of kill; frozen in the register.

**Actions.**
- The pattern and its steps (for example 5% → 20% → 50% → 100%), with the soak of each step.
- The map of the kill switch: what to cut (a module, a function, a rule, a strategy), and in which
  order.
- The windows: the periods allowed and forbidden (blackouts around events).
- The criteria of promotion: the gaps of costs, latency and errors against the stable version, a
  minimum operational t-statistic on the canary, no critical alert.

**KPI.** Coverage of the [guardrails](../../glossary.md#guardrail): criteria defined for 100% of the critical risks; the quality of
the plan: steps consistent with capacity.

**Guardrail.** No release when the window of the market does not comply, or when the criteria of
promotion are missing or ambiguous.

**Output.** The rollout plan signed in the register, and a checklist of execution → step 5
(execution), decision and execution (section 5: the routing of flow and capital).

</details>

<details>
<summary><b>5 · Progressive execution under guard: switches, routing, observation</b> — Execution task</summary>

**Goal.** Deploy the same way everywhere (the same scripts and procedures), activate at the smallest
step, observe live, and promote step by step *only* when the criteria pass; otherwise, kill and roll
back at once.

**Actions.**
- Activate step 1; observe the gaps between live and stable (slippage, costs, latency, errors,
  quality of fills).
- Decide at the end of each soak: promote, hold or roll back, recorded in the register.
- Fine adjustments by switches that turn a module off without redeploying, when needed.

**KPI.** Gap between canary and stable within its bands; the % of capital exposed respected; the
latency of the decision of promotion.

**Guardrail.** Kill when a critical threshold is exceeded (costs, errors, latency, impact on the
market), on alerts of SLOs or SLIs, or on signs of operational [overfitting](../../glossary.md#overfitting).

**Output.** The change in production at the current step, and the log of decisions per step → step 6
(closure), monitoring (the series after release), lifecycle (the budgets of reliability and risk).

</details>

<details>
<summary><b>6 · Closure, after release, and learning</b> — Execution task</summary>

**Goal.** Confirm the stabilisation after the final soak, document the results and gaps, update the
runbooks, tests and contracts, and close the loop towards monitoring (missions) and lifecycle
(cadence, budgets).

**Actions.**
- The final validation after the soak, or the decision to stay at a step below 100% when that is
  optimal.
- A review after release (successes, gaps, causes), with corrective actions (tests, guardrails,
  contracts).
- Update the indicators of reliability and the calendar of the next releases.

**KPI.** Change failure rate (incidents after release over releases), time to repair after release,
lead time from a validated idea to production.

**Guardrail.** A postmortem is mandatory for any breach of a budget of reliability, or any rollback
that was not planned.

**Output.** The report of closure, the register updated, and a backlog of missions for S6 and S7 →
monitoring (alerts, fitness), lifecycle (steering), validation (feedback on its tests).

</details>

## Deliverables

- Specification of release: the unit of release, its perimeter, risks and rollback.
- The register of decisions, up to date: who, when, what, why, criteria, windows, results per step.
- Contracts and smoke tests: determinism, schemas, latency, configuration.
- The rollout plan: pattern, steps, soak, criteria of promotion, map of the kill switch, blackouts.
- Operational runbooks: deployment, switch-over, rollback, escalation, communication.
- [KPI](../../glossary.md#kpi) dashboard: gaps of the canary, costs, latency, errors, time to repair, change failure rate,
  lead time.
- A clear replacement process: deactivation by switches, the "contract" after the "expand", the
  restoration of artefacts.
