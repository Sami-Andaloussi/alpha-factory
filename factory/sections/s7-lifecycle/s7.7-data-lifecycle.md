# S7.7 — Data lifecycle & provenance

## TL;DR

Makes sure every value the factory uses keeps a reliable record: where it comes from, what the world looked like at instant T, and a replay of that state to the byte.
Section 1 builds these guarantees; this page keeps them true over the life of the data: living contracts, idempotent backfills, batch and stream that converge, tests that block leakage.
It reads section 1's manifests and registries; its schema contracts go to the data gate (S1.8), to monitoring and to change management.

## Purpose

Make sure that every value used in the factory keeps a reliable record: the factory knows where it
comes from, what exact state the world was in at instant T, and can
[replay](../../glossary.md#replay) that state to the byte. Section 1 builds these guarantees; this
page keeps them true over the life of the data, through revisions, backfills, and changes of
contract and of source. This is the factory's [data lifecycle](../../glossary.md#data-lifecycle),
and its [provenance](../../glossary.md#provenance).

**Success looks like:**
- [point-in-time](../../glossary.md#point-in-time) replay, with zero [leakage](../../glossary.md#leakage) in time;
- [traceability](../../glossary.md#traceability) end to end: source → transformations → [features](../../glossary.md#feature) and [labels](../../glossary.md#label) → decisions;
- living data contracts: changes announced, tested and reversible;
- [idempotent](../../glossary.md#idempotent) backfills: history recomputed without being altered.

Without them, research is unstable, validation proves nothing, execution [drifts](../../glossary.md#drift) and monitoring
cannot explain.

<details>
<summary><b>What each flow carries, constraints and service levels</b></summary>

Section 1 brings the raw facts, point-in-time, stamped with the time of the event, their identifiers
and their units, with the calendars and rules of the markets (suspensions, holidays): the manifest
of every dataset it delivers, the provenance registry of S1.3 (versions, hashes and dependencies
behind each segment, [snapshot](../../glossary.md#snapshot) or run), and the batch registry of S1.8
(the [sealed batches](../../glossary.md#sealed-batch) and their references). The feature registry of
S2.10 brings the [lineage](../../glossary.md#lineage) of every feature and label. The replay API of
S1.8 lets the page check that an experiment replays to the byte. The retention policy of S1.3 says
how long each kind of data is kept, and how it is dropped in a way that can be proven; the page
applies it over the life of the data. Monitoring keeps its own retentions (S6.8 for telemetry, S6.3
for its write-once journal) under the same rule.

The schema contracts go to the data [gate](../../glossary.md#gate) (S1.8), which refuses to publish
a batch that breaks one unannounced; to S6.1, which registers their service levels (latency, share
of late data) with the other [SLOs](../../glossary.md#slo); to the
[fitness functions](../../glossary.md#fitness-function) (S7.3), which check them before each merge;
and to [change management](../../glossary.md#change-management) (S7.5), which releases each change
of contract as a [release package](../../glossary.md#release-package). Every producer and consumer
of data signs its contracts. The snapshots stay in section 1 (S1.3, S1.8): the page gives each
experiment and each model a pointer to the snapshot it used. The backfill runbooks go to S6.7. The
page's [KPIs](../../glossary.md#kpi) and alerts, with links to the artefacts and snapshots, go to
S6.1 and to data integrity (S6.3). Mission cards in the loop of S7.10 carry the signed reports of
diff to validation (section 4), for its retests, and the breaks of service level or schema, the
needs of backfill and the contracts to redesign; the decisions go to the system of record (S7.2).

**Constraints.**
- Formats: explicit schemas, normalised units, canonical keys.
- Consistency: parity between batch and stream guaranteed; no join to the future on an as-of path.
- Traceability: every artefact referenced by the hash of its input, its timestamp and the version
  of its contract.

**Service levels:**
- Critical events (an IPO, a suspension, a delisting): until available inside the factory, T90
  ≤ 5 min and [T95](../../glossary.md#t95) ≤ 10 min.
- Regular data: an SLO of update per source; an alert when the share of late data exceeds its
  threshold over a rolling window.

</details>

## Gates

**Entry.** Start only when:
- the charter of time and identities is validated (step 1);
- the minimum process of lineage works (step 2);
- a set of reference events is defined for the tests: IPOs, halts, splits, late revisions.

**Exit.** Verdict *pass* when all of these hold:
- ≥ 95% of experiments replay to the byte through as-of snapshots (step 3);
- ≥ 98% of artefacts carry a usable lineage (step 2);
- 0 unannounced break of contract over a rolling month (step 4);
- a backfill run twice gives a diff of 0 on ≥ 99% of partitions (step 5);
- the gap between batch and stream stays within its threshold (step 6), outside the windows planned
  for late data;
- the failure rate of the critical tests stays below its threshold (step 7) over 2 cycles.

*Retry* and *fail* follow the [gate verdict](../../glossary.md#gate-verdict) rule.

**Requalification.** Rerun the sub-section when:
- a KPI drifts beyond its threshold: freshness, stability of schemas, the gap between batch and
  stream;
- new market rules arrive (calendars, official formats);
- a new provider arrives, or a unit changes;
- a new method of generating features or labels changes the dependencies.

Monitoring (S6) receives the KPIs of steps 1 to 8 and their alerts, with links to the artefacts and
snapshots. Lifecycle (S7) receives missions on breaks of service level or schema, on needs of
backfill and on contracts to redesign, and feeds its reliability budgets (S7.4), with a targeted
freeze when needed.

## Steps

<details>
<summary><b>1 · Boundaries and meaning of the facts: time and identities</b> — Design task</summary>

**Goal.** Without one grammar of time and identities, every lineage is ambiguous. The charter
restates, for the facts of data (keys, calendars, revisions), the contract of truth of S7.2, which
builds on section 1 (S1.1 to S1.3); it adds nothing that contradicts them.

**Actions.**
- Set the canonical list of the domain's facts (prices, volumes, market events, fundamental
  states), and their keys of identity: securities, [venues](../../glossary.md#venue), calendars, units.
- Specify the meaning of time, in the terms of S7.2: the [valid time](../../glossary.md#valid-time)
  of each fact against its [transaction time](../../glossary.md#transaction-time), the time zone,
  the conventions of the close, the delays expected, the late corrections.
- Set the rules of version of one fact (revision, correction), and the calendars that apply
  (holidays, suspensions).

**KPI.** Coverage of identities (% of records with a valid canonical key); ambiguities of time
(number of records with no compliant time of event).

**Guardrail.** Tests that each canonical key is unique; a test of the calendar that applies (no
point outside the valid windows).

**Output.** The charter of facts and time (identifiers, units, conventions of time, rules of
revision) → data (section 1, at ingestion), features and labels (section 2, at generation) and
validation (section 4, its as-of reference), which receive it as missions through the loop of
S7.10.

</details>

<details>
<summary><b>2 · A minimum viable lineage, and the journal of transformations</b> — Design task</summary>

**Goal.** Once the language of time and identities is fixed, the factory can tell what happens to
the facts.

**Actions.**
- Set the minimum metadata of provenance to capture for every artefact: the source and its
  version, the rules of cleaning and joins, the parameters, the timestamps, the checksums.
- Journal every important transformation (data → normalised datasets → features and labels), with
  its signature (hash and parameters) and an immutable reference to its input.

**KPI.** Coverage of lineage: % of artefacts with a complete, usable provenance.

**Guardrail.** Publication of an artefact is blocked when its provenance is incomplete; a test of
immutable references (the hash of the input stored and checked).

**Output.** The standard of lineage, which section 1's provenance registry and section 2's feature
registry carry (graphs of dependencies and metadata of process) → the strategy lab (explanation),
validation and simulation (replication), monitoring (attribution of gaps).

</details>

<details>
<summary><b>3 · As-of snapshots and the engine of replay</b> — Design task</summary>

**Goal.** With the history of the transformations known, the factory can freeze the state seen
at T.

**Actions.**
- Adopt section 1's format of snapshot (S1.3: full or delta, with an index of time), and set the
  rule "as of T": no look-ahead.
- Build the point-in-time restitution: a request "give me what the model saw at T".
- Give each experiment and each model a pointer to its snapshot, and a catalogue of the artefacts
  it used.

**KPI.** Replayability: % of experiments reproduced to the byte through as-of; the time of
restitution at P95 (the latency to replay a state).

**Guardrail.** Tests against travel in time: no datum with a time of event after T in a snapshot
as of T; tests of equal hashes between runs.

**Output.** The store of as-of snapshots and the replay API, which section 1 serves (S1.3, S1.8),
restitutions identical to the byte → validation, simulation and cost analysis (realistic tests),
the strategy lab (reproducible research), decision and execution (postmortems).

</details>

<details>
<summary><b>4 · Data contracts that evolve: schema, units, service levels</b> — Design task</summary>

**Goal.** Once the factory can replay, it can frame change without breaking replay.

**Actions.**
- Write the contracts between producers and consumers: a versioned schema, meanings, units,
  mandatory and optional fields, a service level of latency and a tolerance of late data.
- Set a gate of change: an announcement, a period of coexistence ("expand and contract"), tests
  of compatibility, a plan of [rollback](../../glossary.md#rollback).

**KPI.** Stability of schemas (rate of unannounced breaks); punctuality per source (% outside its
service level).

**Guardrail.** Tests of compatibility of schema and meaning; an alert, and a freeze, on any
unannounced break.

**Output.** The catalogue of versioned schema contracts, and a traceable register of changes →
every section; lifecycle (change management, S7.5); monitoring (alerts on service levels).

</details>

<details>
<summary><b>5 · Idempotent backfills, and a policy of recompute</b> — Design task</summary>

**Goal.** With stable contracts, history can be corrected without drifting.

**Actions.**
- Define the procedures of backfill: keys of idempotence, rules of deduplication, and oracles of
  comparison (a diff tolerated, for example).
- Specify when to redo (an error, a new source, a change of unit) and how to lock downstream
  (label, then re-tag the snapshots).

**KPI.** Idempotence: the ratio of backfills with no gap after a rerun; the time to repair data.

**Guardrail.** Run in an isolated environment, publish atomically, and pass the test "run twice,
same result".

**Output.** The backfill runbooks, and signed reports of diff (before and after) → validation
(retests, as missions through the loop of S7.10), monitoring (gaps), lifecycle (decisions of
maintenance).

</details>

<details>
<summary><b>6 · Batch and stream that converge: policies for late data and redo</b> — Design task</summary>

**Goal.** Once recompute is governed, align live production with research.

**Actions.**
- Keep a journal of the raw facts (logically append-only), and policies for late data: windows of
  waiting, [watermarks](../../glossary.md#watermark), corrections.
- Guarantee that the batch views (rebuilt) and the stream views (online) converge to the same
  as-of state.

**KPI.** The gap between batch and stream (the distance between the views at T); the rate of late
corrections.

**Guardrail.** Tests of bounded convergence (the gap stays within its threshold after Δt); an alert
when it is exceeded.

**Output.** The policy that unifies batch and stream, and its rules of convergence → decision and
execution (consistency live), the strategy lab and validation (parity between research and
production).

</details>

<details>
<summary><b>7 · Built-in quality, and no leakage in time</b> — Design task + Execution task</summary>

**Goal.** With the flows stable, build in the tests that protect the chain. Choosing the tests and
the reactions is design; running them is execution.

**Actions.**
- Define the critical tests: completeness, freshness, keys that are unique, bounds and monotonicity,
  consistency across tables, no leakage as of, the statistical drift that matters for trading.
- Choose the reactions: [fail-closed](../../glossary.md#fail-closed) (block), or fail-open (mark and degrade), by the impact
  downstream.

**KPI.** Failure rate of the critical tests; false alarms (% of tests that call for no action);
coverage of the tests.

**Guardrail.** Tests of leakage as of (no join to the future); thresholds of drift with a
documented period of grace.

**Output.** The catalogue of tests, the policies of reaction, and the journal of data incidents →
monitoring (alerts, attribution), lifecycle (reliability budgets, targeted freezes).

</details>

<details>
<summary><b>8 · Continuous observation, and the loop of maintenance</b> — Execution task</summary>

**Goal.** Once instrumented, the page is steered by its gauges.

**Actions.**
- Publish a dashboard of this page's KPIs, and tie each drift to a mission, with its owner, due date
  and acceptance criterion.
- Keep the decision log (what, why, when, impact), linked to the snapshots and the contracts.

**KPI.** Time from detection to correction; recurrence of incidents (the rate of reopening).

**Guardrail.** A [blameless postmortem](../../glossary.md#blameless-postmortem) is mandatory for any incident of severity N or above; a check
of non-regression over 2 cycles.

**Output.** A living book of operations (KPIs, alerts, decisions, corrective actions) → lifecycle
(the [steering cadences](../../glossary.md#steering-cadence)), every section, for its arbitrations.

</details>

## Deliverables

1. A clear specification: the charter of facts, identities, units and the meaning of time; the rules
   of version and revision, and the calendars that apply.
2. Log of choices and decisions: why this contract, this tolerance of late data, this policy of
   backfill; with links to the snapshots and incidents concerned.
3. Modules and documentation ready to test: a usable lineage, an as-of engine of queries, the
   procedures of idempotent backfill; the catalogue of quality tests and the policies of fail-open
   and fail-closed.
4. KPI tracking table: the KPIs of steps 1 to 8 with their thresholds, windows, owners and flows of
   alert; the history of drifts, and of the times from detection to correction.
5. A clear replacement process: how to substitute a source, a contract or a method of generation
   without breaking anything ("expand and contract", coexistence, rollback), with the criteria of
   switch-over and the plan to go back.
