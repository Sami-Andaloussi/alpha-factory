# S7.3 — System-wide fitness & architectural evolution

## TL;DR

Writes down what "good" means for the factory (latency, drift, costs, coupling, resilience), then encodes it as living tests that run before each merge, at a cadence and on events.
The tests become automatic gates that let a change through, slow it or stop it, and triggers of action: canary, rollback, refactor, migration.
Success is to move fast without breaking the chain from data to features, alpha and order; each change's gate goes to change management (S7.5).

## Purpose

Write down what "good" means for the factory (latency, [drift](../../glossary.md#drift), costs, coupling, resilience), then
encode it as living tests that run before each merge, at a cadence, and on events. The tests become
automatic [gates](../../glossary.md#gate) that allow, slow down or block the changes, and triggers of actions: canary,
[rollback](../../glossary.md#rollback), refactor, migration by "expand and contract". These are the factory's [fitness functions](../../glossary.md#fitness-function).

**Success looks like:** the factory moves fast *without* breaking the chain from data to [features](../../glossary.md#feature),
[alpha](../../glossary.md#alpha) and order.

<details>
<summary><b>What each flow carries, constraints and service levels</b></summary>

The SLO catalogue of S6.1 brings the measures the tests build on. The tests read the whole chain,
through the platform (S6.8): from sections 1 and 2, the versioned schemas, freshness and
completeness, checked against the schema contracts of the
[data lifecycle](../../glossary.md#data-lifecycle) (S7.7); from sections 3 and 4, the results of
[backtests](../../glossary.md#backtest), of the simulator and of cost analysis, costs and
[turnover](../../glossary.md#turnover); from section 5, the telemetry of execution (latency, fills,
[slippage](../../glossary.md#slippage)) and the states of release; from section 6, the series of
alerts, drifts and incidents. Sections 1 to 6 sign the catalogue of properties for their perimeter.

The fitness gate gives each change its state (go, slow, stop), which [change management](../../glossary.md#change-management) (S7.5)
applies. The missions of refactor and migration, ranked by impact, go to the loop of S7.10; the
runbooks, one per state, go to S6.7. The dials reach steering (S7.1) through the
[pre-read](../../glossary.md#pre-read), which monitoring compiles from the KPI feed; the decisions go to the system of record
(S7.2). The metrics of each property are registered in S6.1's catalogue, and section 4 takes from
the catalogue of properties its thresholds before trading.

**Constraints.**
- Formats: a stable ID per property and per test, a timestamp, a window, a level (green, amber,
  red), a link to the runbook.
- Consistency: every measure that bears on alpha is [point-in-time](../../glossary.md#point-in-time).

**Service levels:**
- Critical events (a new schema, a suspension or a delisting, a change of market rules): from
  detection to the gate at [T95](../../glossary.md#t95) ≤ 5 min, adjusted to the load.
- The aggregated dials are updated at a period ≤ 1 h.
- In continuous integration, before merge: the total time of the fitness tests stays within the
  budget of build set at step 2.
- An alert when the T90 or T95 are exceeded; the runbook "degradation of control" then applies.

</details>

## Gates

**Entry.** Start only when:
- sections 1 to 6 have signed the catalogue of properties for the target perimeter;
- the [baseline](../../glossary.md#baseline) is measured, and the initial thresholds and budgets are set;
- stable test data (point-in-time fixtures) are available.

**Exit.** Verdict *pass* when all of these hold:
- the library of priority tests is green on the HIGH perimeter;
- the policy of gating and release applies: at least one reversible release exercised end to end;
- the dashboard and the runbooks are in place, and a simulated incident was handled successfully.

*Retry* and *fail* follow the [gate verdict](../../glossary.md#gate-verdict) rule.

**Requalification.** Rerun the sub-section when:
- a new source or schema contract arrives;
- a key metric drifts lastingly (from amber to red);
- a new strategy or family of features, a new market rule, or a new version of a critical component
  arrives;
- a major incident or a postmortem occurs.

Monitoring (S6) receives alerts added or changed, new dials, and thresholds revised. Lifecycle
(S7) receives the missions of refactor and migration, adjusted [steering cadences](../../glossary.md#steering-cadence), and updates to
the decision log.

## Steps

<details>
<summary><b>1 · Catalogue the expected properties</b> — Design task</summary>

**Goal.** List the non-functional and economic properties to protect: latency end to end, freshness
and completeness of the data, stability of the features and of the
[information coefficient](../../glossary.md#information-coefficient), cost per alpha, coupling and
debt, the rate of rollback.

**Actions.**
- List the properties per section (S1 to S7) and across them (end to end).
- For each property: its unit, perimeter, window of time, tolerance, criticality and cost of
  failure.
- Rank them (high, medium, low) by their impact on net P&L and on safety.

**KPI.** Coverage of the critical properties: the % covered, against the target list.

**Guardrail.** A cross review between sections; a definition with no unit, no window or no explicit
cost of failure is rejected.

**Output.** The versioned catalogue of properties (ID, definition, criticality, tolerance, window)
→ steps 2 and 3; monitoring (S6); validation (section 4), for the thresholds before trading, as
missions through the loop of S7.10.

</details>

<details>
<summary><b>2 · Design the metrics, thresholds and budgets</b> — Design task</summary>

**Goal.** Turn each property into observable metrics, with levels of service or budgets: for
example a budget of latency, of errors, of the risk of coupling.

**Actions.**
- Define the metric or metrics of each property: formula, rolling window, targets at P90, P95 or
  P99, seasonality.
- Set the thresholds of alert and the budgets (what the factory accepts to spend before a freeze or
  a rollback), calibrated at first by measuring the current baseline plus a margin of safety.
- Decide the automatic actions per level (green, amber, red).

**KPI.** The rate of metrics with their thresholds, budgets and actions defined; the gap from
baseline to target (P95, P99).

**Guardrail.** A test of the realism of the thresholds, with false positives and negatives
simulated; a freeze when the theoretical budgets fall below the load observed.

**Output.** Metric sheets (formula, thresholds, budget, associated actions) → step 3 (the tests),
step 4 (gating).

</details>

<details>
<summary><b>3 · Build the library of fitness functions</b> — Execution task</summary>

**Goal.** Write living tests per property: before merge (fast, [deterministic](../../glossary.md#deterministic)), at a cadence (hourly,
daily, weekly), and on an event (a new schema, a new version, a drift detected, a new strategy).

**Actions.**
- For each priority property: a test in continuous integration (a binary status and its logs), a
  test at a cadence (a time series and its thresholds), a test on an event (a webhook or a logical
  trigger).
- Make the tests stable: frozen test data, controlled clocks, clear tolerances; isolate the
  dependencies.
- Add checks of the schema contracts that the data lifecycle (S7.7) keeps, with the "expand and
  contract" strategy: a new field is accepted, and the old one deprecated after a period of
  observation.

**KPI.** Stability of the tests (flaky < X%), running time before merge (≤ Y), coverage of the HIGH
properties.

**Guardrail.** Deterministic tests ([seeds](../../glossary.md#seed), fixtures); flapping tests are ejected until they are
fixed; schema contracts are mandatory for the critical sources.

**Output.** The library of tests, ranked by property, with the checks of the schema contracts
(schemas and compatibility) → step 4 (gates), sections 1 and 2 (data, features), section 5
(execution).

</details>

<details>
<summary><b>4 · Set the gates of integration and the policies of release</b> — Design task</summary>

**Goal.** Link the tests to the gates of promotion and to the strategies of evolution: canary,
[progressive rollout](../../glossary.md#progressive-rollout), [kill switch](../../glossary.md#kill-switch), rollback, migrations by "expand and contract".

**Actions.**
- Define the rules of gating by criticality: a hard fail blocks; a soft fail slows (a canary,
  quotas); a warning is only watched.
- Standardise reversible paths of release: a pre-production close to production, a progressive
  promotion, criteria of stop and go.
- Frame every change of schema or API with "expand and contract" and delays of observation, then a
  clean withdrawal.

**KPI.** Change failure rate, % of reversible releases, % of rollbacks in < Z min, % of migrations
by "expand and contract" with no break.

**Guardrail.** A hard gate on the vital properties (for example point-in-time integrity, the
maximum latency end to end); a canary is imposed when a soft fail repeats.

**Output.** The policy of gating and release (diagrams of decision, criteria to stop, procedures of
rollback) → section 3 (strategy lab, when new signals come in), section 4 (simulator, cost
analysis), section 5 (production).

</details>

<details>
<summary><b>5 · Dashboard and runbooks of action</b> — Design task + Execution task</summary>

**Goal.** Offer a readable dial, green, amber or red, per property, and runbooks that say what to
do now. Aggregating the dials is execution; tying each state to its runbook is design.

**Actions.**
- Aggregate the metrics into dials, with the budgets consumed.
- Tie each state to a runbook: ramp-down, rollback, targeted freeze, recalibration of a threshold,
  a ticket of refactor, a mission of lifecycle.

**KPI.** Delay from decision to action (p95), % of alerts with their runbook executed, time back to
green.

**Guardrail.** No silent alert: every alert points to a runbook; a monthly audit of the orphan
alerts.

**Output.** The dashboard and the versioned runbooks → lifecycle (the steering cadences, S7.1),
monitoring (S6), the runbooks of incidents (S6.7).

</details>

<details>
<summary><b>6 · The loop of architectural evolution, with the debt under control</b> — Design task + Execution task</summary>

**Goal.** Keep the architecture fit through controlled migrations, refactors at a cadence, and a
tracked [half-life](../../glossary.md#half-life) of the debt. Planning the migrations and their
criteria of exit is design; keeping the backlog and holding the reviews is execution.

**Actions.**
- Keep a backlog of debt, tied to the fitness functions that fail (coupling, slowness, fragility).
- Plan the migrations (data, API) by "expand and contract", with criteria of exit measured by the
  tests.
- Hold reviews of architecture driven by metrics: no opinion without a measure.

**KPI.** Half-life of the debt (in days), the share of refactors that close a red dial, the
throughput of successful migrations.

**Guardrail.** Breaking migrations, with no period of observation, are rejected; a measure after
each migration is mandatory.

**Output.** The ranked backlog of debt and migrations, and the journal of improvements → sections 1,
2 and 5 (targeted work), change management (S7.5).

</details>

## Deliverables

1. Catalogue of properties: IDs, definitions, criticality, windows, costs of failure.
2. Metric sheets and budgets: formulas, thresholds, windows, actions.
3. Library of fitness functions (before merge, at a cadence, on an event), with the checks of the
   schema contracts documented.
4. Policy of gating and release: diagrams of decision, canary, rollback, "expand and contract".
5. Dashboard, with a runbook tied to each alert and each state.
6. Ranked backlog of debt and migrations, and the decision log (system of record).
7. Procedure of [requalification](../../glossary.md#requalification): triggers, scope, owners, delays.
