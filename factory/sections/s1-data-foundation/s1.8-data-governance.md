# S1.8 — Governance: provenance, versioning & anti-leakage gates

## TL;DR

The single gate, impossible to bypass, between the data foundation and the rest of the factory.
Every dataset leaves as a sealed, versioned batch with its manifest and gate report; a failed critical test means quarantine, not publication.
Six months later, a backtest rerun on the same batch gives bit-identical results.

## Purpose

Set up one [gate](../../glossary.md#gate), traceable and impossible to bypass, between the data foundation and everything
downstream: [features](../../glossary.md#feature) and [labels](../../glossary.md#label), the strategy lab, validation. It seals each dataset into a
versioned [sealed batch](../../glossary.md#sealed-batch), guarantees its complete
[provenance](../../glossary.md#provenance), and blocks any temporal
[leakage](../../glossary.md#leakage), [survivorship](../../glossary.md#survivorship-bias) or
inconsistency.

**Success looks like:** six months later, a [backtest](../../glossary.md#backtest) replayed on the same batch gives bit-for-bit
the same results, with no leakage and no alteration detected.

<details>
<summary><b>What each flow carries, constraints and service levels</b></summary>

A sealed batch always travels with its [manifest](../../glossary.md#dataset-manifest). The
downstream consumers named here are the sub-sections of section 2 that read section-1 data, and the
first data readers of sections 3 to 5: the strategy lab, validation, execution simulation, cost
analysis and risk. The system of record for decisions (S7.2) reads the evidence locker, and
[replays](../../glossary.md#replay) past decisions through the replay API, and
[incident response](../../glossary.md#incident-response) (S7.9) the log, to rebuild the timeline of
an incident. [Change management](../../glossary.md#change-management) (S7.5) links each
[release package](../../glossary.md#release-package) to the sealed batches of the batch registry.
The [data lifecycle](../../glossary.md#data-lifecycle) (S7.7) checks through the replay API that
experiments replay to the byte; its schema contracts are enforced here: a batch that breaks one
unannounced is not published.

**Constraints.**
- Formats: a machine-readable manifest with a stable schema and mandatory fields.
- Consistency: an explicit time zone; raw and adjusted columns never mixed; the as-of universe
  proven.

**Service levels:**
- T90 / [T95](../../glossary.md#t95) integration of critical events (IPO, suspension, delisting, major splits). Default
  recommendation: T90 ≤ 15 min and T95 ≤ 60 min, adjusted per market.
- A stable p95 target for as-of access, set per use.
- An alert when T95 is exceeded; publication blocked while the as-of universe is not up to date.

</details>

## Gates

**Entry.** A batch enters the gate only when:
- its manifest is compliant (step 1);
- its [fingerprints](../../glossary.md#fingerprint) are computed and it is sealed (step 2);
- as-of access to it is active (step 3);
- its test suite is defined (step 4).

**Exit.** Verdict *pass* when all of these hold:
- every critical test is green;
- the [evidence locker](../../glossary.md#evidence-locker) is complete;
- [pinning](../../glossary.md#pinning) is ready and the batch is entered in the registry;
- the release, [rollback](../../glossary.md#rollback) and [quarantine](../../glossary.md#quarantine) runbooks are validated.

*Retry* and *fail* follow the [gate verdict](../../glossary.md#gate-verdict) rule; a batch that
fails a critical test goes to [quarantine](../../glossary.md#quarantine) automatically.

**Requalification.** Rerun the acceptance tests when:
- a schema or an adjustment method changes upstream;
- a market rule changes;
- monitoring (S6) detects [drift](../../glossary.md#drift), for example a rising share of non-identical [replays](../../glossary.md#replay) or any
  leakage incident;
- a new, stricter control method is adopted.

Monitoring (S6) receives the gate [KPIs](../../glossary.md#kpi) and logs, for drift detection, with their alerts and
service levels. Lifecycle (S7) records the changelog of batches, the triggers of re-tests and the
re-certification period.

## Steps

<details>
<summary><b>1 · Define the batch contract and the minimal manifest</b> — Design task</summary>

**Goal.** Without a clear identity for a batch, it can be neither versioned, sealed nor audited.

**Actions.**
- Describe the mandatory content of a manifest, the identity of the batch: hashes of files and
  objects, schema and meaning of the columns, time zone, as-of periods covered, universe at date,
  adjustment policy, upstream dependencies, quality rules already applied, known exclusions.
- Add the naming policy and the version semantics, for example MAJOR.MINOR.PATCH: a batch is not
  "the latest revision".

**KPI.** Manifest coverage, the share of batches delivered with a complete manifest: threshold
100%.

**Guardrail.** A semantic lint of the manifest, itself an [execution task](../../glossary.md#execution-task): refused when a critical
field is missing; a test that every batch name is unique.

**Output.** Manifest template and batch contract, as a document and a machine-readable schema →
the gatekeeper (step 4), the evidence locker (step 5), downstream sections 2 to 4.

</details>

<details>
<summary><b>2 · Sealing and immutable versioning: a batch is a frozen snapshot</b> — Design task</summary>

**Goal.** Once the manifest is defined, the fingerprint can be generated and the batch sealed.

**Actions.**
- Compute the fingerprint (hash) of everything that defines the batch: data, schema, manifest,
  dependencies, rules applied.
- Give the batch an immutable ID, and version it with announced semantics.
- Store it append-only, never overwritten, with cross-references to the sources and their as-of
  state.

**KPI.** [Immutability](../../glossary.md#immutability): changes after sealing = 0. Fingerprint collisions = 0.

**Guardrail.** Publication refused when the fingerprint does not cover data, manifest and
dependencies together.

**Output.** The sealed batch (ID, fingerprints, index) and its entry in the
[batch registry](../../glossary.md#batch-registry) → as-of access (step 3), the gatekeeper (step
4), downstream.

</details>

<details>
<summary><b>3 · As-of access contract: the time gate</b> — Design task</summary>

**Goal.** The batches exist; now every request must declare its observation date, so that it gets
exactly the historical state.

**Actions.**
- Define the interface: every request includes its as-of point or points and an observation
  window; no direct access to raw data outside this interface.
- Formalise the bitemporal rules (visible versus known) and the order of arbitration when
  corrections arrive late.

**KPI.** Share of requests compliant with as-of access, towards 100%; rejections for a missing
date; p95 response latency.

**Guardrail.** Any request without a date is blocked; throttling under load; control of forbidden
windows, such as an overlap after an event.

**Output.** The [as-of API](../../glossary.md#as-of-api) specification (the contract), the
authorisation and refusal rules, and request logging → every data reader: features and labels,
strategy, validation, execution simulation and cost analysis.

</details>

<details>
<summary><b>4 · Gatekeeper: anti-leakage and consistency test batteries</b> — Design task</summary>

**Goal.** With access constrained, every batch can be tested systematically before publication.

**Actions.** Build an automated test suite, the [anti-leakage gate](../../glossary.md#anti-leakage-gate),
covering at least:
- leakage: sequential [cross-validation](../../glossary.md#cross-validation) with [purging](../../glossary.md#purging) and an
  [embargo](../../glossary.md#embargo) window; strict separation of event time and publication
  time;
- survivorship: the universe at date contains no asset introduced after the observation date;
  tests of inclusions and exclusions (IPO, suspension, delisting);
- adjustments: raw and adjusted columns consistent, methods traceable, no implicit mixing;
- time alignment: monotonic and consistent timestamps, no trade and quote swapped, documented
  time zones.

**KPI.** Rejection rate by reason (leakage, survivorship, adjustment, alignment); false-positive
rate, kept low; p90 acceptance time.

**Guardrail.** Automatic quarantine when a critical test fails; publishing without green is
technically impossible.

**Output.** A batch acceptance report, the [gate report](../../glossary.md#gate-report) (tests,
verdicts, measures), with the status *accepted* or *quarantined* → the batch registry (step 2),
downstream sections 2 to 4, monitoring (S6).

</details>

<details>
<summary><b>5 · Evidence locker and audit log: the chain of custody</b> — Execution task</summary>

**Goal.** As soon as verdicts exist, keep the proof.

**Actions.**
- Archive the manifest, fingerprints, test logs, timestamps, requests, signatories and links to
  sources.
- Link every publication to its justification.

**KPI.** End-to-end [traceability](../../glossary.md#traceability), the share of batches with a complete path; mean investigation
time during an incident.

**Guardrail.** A periodic integrity check of the archives by re-hashing; an alert when any piece
is missing.

**Output.** A searchable audit file per batch and a provenance graph of dependencies → auditors,
monitoring (S6), lifecycle (S7), downstream teams.

</details>

<details>
<summary><b>6 · Release, rollback and quarantine policy, and re-tests</b> — Design task</summary>

**Goal.** With the proofs in place, manage the lifecycle of versions.

**Actions.**
- Define the release rules (when a batch becomes the reference), the
  [rollback](../../glossary.md#rollback) procedures (back to version N−1, triggerable within
  minutes) and the length of quarantine.
- Define the re-test triggers: an upstream schema change, new market rules, drift detected
  downstream, a corrected [corporate action](../../glossary.md#corporate-action), an alert from monitoring (S6).

**KPI.** p90 rollback time; leakage incidents downstream, towards 0; share of [requalifications](../../glossary.md#requalification)
done on time.

**Guardrail.** Publication blocked when a runbook is missing; automatic return to the last green
batch on a critical incident.

**Output.** [Runbooks](../../glossary.md#runbook) for release, rollback and quarantine, and a
requalification plan (when to retest what) → downstream sections 2 to 5, monitoring (S6),
lifecycle (S7).

</details>

<details>
<summary><b>7 · Publication and pinning downstream, with guidance for users</b> — Execution task</summary>

**Goal.** The gate works; now distribute cleanly and train the users.

**Actions.**
- Publish the canonical references (batch address, version, manifest) and require explicit
  pinning in every job and experiment.
- Train the consumers: how to specify as-of, read a manifest, interpret a gate report.

**KPI.** Share of downstream jobs that pin their batches; mean time to reproduce an experiment.

**Guardrail.** Jobs without pinning are rejected; an alert when a job consumes a batch that is not
in the registry.

**Output.** The published references in the batch registry, a short usage guide and a downstream integration checklist →
features and labels, strategy, validation, execution simulation and cost analysis, risk.

</details>

## Deliverables

- Batch contract and manifest template: document and machine-readable schema.
- Batch registry with fingerprints and versions, and the [versioning](../../glossary.md#versioning) policy.
- As-of access specification (the contract) and the authorisation policy.
- Gatekeeper test suite: the tests, their critical / major / minor classes, the verdict rules.
- Operational evidence locker: archiving procedure and audit access.
- Runbooks for release, rollback and quarantine, and the requalification plan.
- Published references in the batch registry, for pinning, and the downstream usage guide.
- KPI dashboard and alerts, with thresholds and recipients.
