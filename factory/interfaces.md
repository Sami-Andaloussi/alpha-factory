# Interfaces

## TL;DR

Every flow between the 64 sub-sections, generated from their Interfaces tables so that it cannot drift from them.
Who produces and reads each artefact, what crosses between sections, and which pages feed each other.
Below the tables: artefacts with several producers, the flows the pages carry in their text, and the mismatches resolved.

| Part | What it answers |
|---|---|
| [Between sections](#between-sections) | How many design flows each section sends to each other section. |
| [The standard streams](#the-standard-streams) | The four streams every page may send, and the page that gathers each. |
| [Every artefact](#every-artefact) | For each artefact, who produces it and who reads it. |

| [Mismatches resolved](#mismatches-resolved) | What building this page found, and how each finding was settled. |

The first four parts are generated by `tools/build_interfaces.py` from the Interfaces tables of the
64 pages, and `tools/check_factory.py` fails when they no longer match; the last three are written
by hand. An ID names a sub-section; the [index](sections/README.md) links each one to its page. Each
Interfaces table lists what a page reads (in) and sends (out); a flow between two pages is declared
at both ends, and the check script enforces it.

## Mismatches resolved

Building this page read every flow at once; each finding below was settled in the pages named.

1. **The replay API had a second producer outside section 1**: S6.3 read it from S1.3, every other
   reader from S1.8. S6.3 now reads it from S1.8, and S1.3 hands it to S1.8 only.
2. **S4.4 perturbed data it did not declare.** It now reads S1.8's sealed batches, the versions
   named in S4.1's experiment passport. S4.3 works on results, not on data, and keeps no data row.
3. **The restricted list had no owner.** It belongs to the perimeter of S5.9's deployment rulebook,
   and the [pre-trade checks](glossary.md#pre-trade-check) of S5.7 apply it; S5.9 and the glossary
   say so.
4. **"Playbook" had three meanings in section 5.** It is now a runbook in S5.3, S5.4, S5.6, S5.7 and
   S5.9, the matrix of allocation by [regime](glossary.md#regime) in S5.5, and the plan of
   deployment in S5.8. S5.6's runbooks of stress go to S6.7, the one home of runbooks, instead of to
   lifecycle.
5. **The ramp-up plan is one plan in several hands**, with no conflict: S4.10 proposes it in the
   promotion record; the capital ladder of S7.6 sets the tiers and the rules of ramp-up and
   ramp-down; S7.8 schedules the moves within them; S5.4 turns them into budgets, and S5.9 gates
   each step. The [architecture](architecture.md) draws the chain.
6. **Intervention and [incident response](glossary.md#incident-response) are two roles**:
   intervention (S6.7) runs the live response under the incident charter that incident response
   (S7.9) keeps. The glossary says so.
7. **Cost of change and value at risk of change both covered risk.** The
   [cost of change](glossary.md#cost-of-change) is now what a move costs (S5.4's budget, S5.6's
   spend), and the [value at risk of change](glossary.md#value-at-risk-of-change) the risk of the
   move.
8. **The costs of serving features and the [showback](glossary.md#showback) of monitoring are two
   costs**: the first reaches capital steering (S2.10 to S7.8); the second is shown back to each
   team or strategy by S6.8. The source of S7.8 names only the first, so no flow was added.
9. **The glossary's lists of readers are not exhaustive; this page is.** Every sub-section that a
   glossary entry names was checked against the tables, and all match.
10. **The declaration of conflicts of interest**, which principle 4 required and no page carried, is
    on S4.10: its three signers each declare any conflict of interest before signing.
11. **What section 3's [variants](glossary.md#variant) teach does not flow back to the research
    lab** in any page: a dossier returns to the lab only when S3.1 refuses it or S3.2 cannot
    reproduce it. Lessons for the lab's own bank of theories are the lab's to organise, under its
    handoff contract.
