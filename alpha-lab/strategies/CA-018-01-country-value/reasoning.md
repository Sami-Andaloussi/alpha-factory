# CA-018-01 — Country value and stock–country parallels: reasoning, not testable

## The theory, and the forms its sources give it

CA-018 holds that the characteristics that predict individual stocks' returns — value, size and
momentum — predict national markets' too, taken as "super-assets": a market that is cheap relative
to another, small, or whose past reveals a regime earns a relative premium afterwards, because
investors under- or overreact to what a national market aggregates, and benchmarked flows, home
bias, regional mandates and regulatory fragmentation keep the gaps open. It refutes itself if
national indices' relative valuation, size and past returns do not predict their later relative
returns; if valuation gaps close at once; or if the country-level premium is entirely the countries'
momentum, with no separate role for valuation. Its references are Asness, Liew and Stevens (1997)
and Fama and French (1998). The library holds neither paper. It names the first in Ilmanen's notes
and bibliography (*Expected Returns*, 2011, chapter 14's notes, among studies of momentum "in
broader contexts"); it describes the second in the Zacks handbook (*The Handbook of Equity Market
Anomalies*, 2011), where "the value premium is pervasive" — value stocks beating growth stocks in 11
of 12 countries of Europe, Australasia and the Far East — and in Thaler's *Advances in Behavioral
Finance* (volume II, 2005): stocks within each market, not national indices. What the library says
of them, and of the claim, gives four forms.

- **Value across national indices.** Ilmanen (*Expected Returns*, chapter 12): "Value strategies
  analogous to value-oriented stock selection also work in equity country allocation", after Asness,
  Moskowitz and Pedersen (2009); where no valuation is at hand, "multi-year average return is often
  a reasonable substitute". Among carry's "cousins" he counts "stock or country selection based on
  dividend yields or other valuation ratios" (chapter 13), and his later book (*Investing Amid Low
  Expected Returns*, 2022, chapter 6) reads "equity country allocation (based on the spread between
  dividend yield and cash rate)" as a carry strategy. Faber (*Global Asset Allocation*, 2015, Figure
  46, country rotations from 1976 to 2013): "You want the countries with the highest yield". De
  Bondt, in Keim and Ziemba's *Security Market Imperfections in Worldwide Equity Markets* (2000),
  reports studies — Asness and others (1997) among them — that "sort the countries included in the
  Morgan Stanley Capital International indexes on the basis of past returns, capitalization
  measures, and so on", and reports that "countries that lag the world index over periods of three
  to five years tend to outperform the index during later years".
- **Emerging against developed markets on their relative valuation.** Ilmanen, §16.4.1, beside
  Figure 16.2: "historically, emerging markets have outperformed developed markets when starting
  from cheap valuations", their relative price-earnings ratios read as the valuation. CA-016-01
  handed this timed form to CA-018 and to CA-019.
- **Size across national indices.** The "capitalization measures" De Bondt lists; the library gives
  no sign or size for a country-level size effect.
- **Value against growth within each country.** De Bondt's Table 2 summarises three studies over
  thirteen countries in which "the value portfolio strongly outperforms the growth portfolio" — Fama
  and French's among them: portfolios of stocks within each market, not national indices.

## Why the lab cannot test it

**The theory ranks national markets, and the lab holds three, one fund each.** Its equity funds are
SPY (the United States' large caps), QQQ and IWM (two baskets inside the United States), EFA (the
developed markets outside North America, in one fund) and EEM (the emerging markets, in one fund):
the United States, the developed world outside it taken as one, and the emerging world taken as one.
The four-asset rule bounds a card's universe, not a theory; but a card holding the three regions
alone is refused, since with three assets one of them carries more than 30% of the profit and gate 6
fails whatever the edge. The theory names no other market the lab holds to rank beside them but QQQ
and IWM, which are not national markets but baskets inside the United States; with them the group is
the five equity funds that CA-001-01, CA-024-01 and TM-040-01 have ranked. Funds held at a fixed
weight only to dilute the shares would pass gate 6's measure, which reads each asset's share of the
whole portfolio's profit, while adding no decision of CA-018's; the ranking's own profit would still
rest on three funds, one of them carrying at least a third of it, which gate 6 would not see. The
lab does not draw a card whose extra assets exist only to get under the four-asset rule (its
decision of 2026-09-27, after CA-017's reader): that is a judgement, and no code checks it.

**The regions can be carried as one group in another theory's card.** The same decision says that a
universe whose other members the theory itself ranks or times is not padding, as CA-024-01 ranked
GLD, SLV and DBC as one group. The three regions ranked by their yield, as one group among others
each ranked by its own carry or value, belong to **CA-021**, value across sectors, countries and
asset classes, whose references are Ilmanen and Asness, Moskowitz and Pedersen, the sources of this
judgement's first form, and to **CA-025**, carry, among whose "cousins" Ilmanen counts country
selection on dividend yields. Faber's and Ilmanen's texts above give that form its sign, the
high-yield markets preferred. It is not lost: it is theirs to draw, and CA-018, which ranks national
markets alone, has no other group to set beside them.

**Value by past returns is published on the lab's funds.** Ilmanen's substitute for a valuation, the
multi-year return, is CA-024-01's signal: the cheapest third of each group by its past five years,
the five equity funds among the groups, stopped at gate 2, the cheapest third earning 0.63% a year
less than the groups held in equal parts from 2010 to 2022, the equity markets carrying the loss.
CA-024-01 set country value aside on those five funds as "three overlapping US baskets and two
foreign ones, not a set of countries". De Bondt's three-to-five-year laggards are the same signal,
and reversal across national markets is **CA-019**'s, and each geographic index's own long-horizon
reversal **MR-021**'s.

**Valuation ratios are not held, and yields are not yet read.** The lab holds no book values,
earnings or price-earnings ratios, by country or by fund. The funds' distributions are encoded in
the frozen bars, and the lab's decision of 2026-09-26 requires the first theory that reads them to
build a point-in-time reading, tested, before any strategy reads them. CA-016-01's judgement named
CA-018 first among the theories whose pick would build it — "whichever of those is picked first
builds it". CA-018 declines: no card of its own can carry the regions' yields, and on the five
equity funds a yield ranking would rank QQQ's and IWM's baskets, which are not national markets,
beside the regions (how their yields order has not been read); with no card of its own to read it,
the reading would be built for nothing — as AS-013's judgement declined it, on another ground. The
reading falls to the first of MR-044, CA-025, EF-030 and EF-008 named in the decision of 2026-09-26,
or to CA-021, which the lab's decisions record with this judgement.

**Emerging against developed markets, timed on their relative valuation.** Ilmanen's form sets two
regions against each other on their relative price-earnings ratio, which the lab does not hold; a
relative dividend yield would need the reading above. A card holding EEM against EFA, or against EFA
and SPY, holds two or three funds and is refused by the four-asset rule; CA-018 times no fourth
market, so no member beside them would be other than padding, the card the lab does not draw.

**The third refutation cannot be graded.** Separating the countries' value from their momentum needs
both measured on the same national markets; with three regions neither can be carried by a card of
CA-018's own, and the five funds' momentum and price-only value are CA-001-01's and CA-024-01's,
published, their combination TM-040-01's.

**The hand-offs.** Three earlier files name CA-018. CA-016-01 left it country value by valuation
ratios, dividend yields among them, and the emerging-against-developed form, with CA-021 and CA-019,
and named it among the theories whose pick would build the yield reading; CA-024-01 set country
value aside on the five equity funds; CA-017-01 lists it, untouched, among the theories its
judgement touches. None hands a form the lab can hold in a card of CA-018's own.

**What was considered, and why it does not rescue the test:**

- **Size across the regions**: the lab holds no market capitalisation by country; SPY against IWM is
  size within the United States, not across countries.
- **Value against growth within each country**: stocks by their valuation within each market, which
  the lab does not hold.
- **Country momentum**: **CA-017**'s, recorded not testable on the same three regions, and
  CA-001-01's, run.
- **The regions' value by their past three to five years**: CA-024-01's, published, CA-019's
  reversal and MR-021's.
- **A fixed tilt chosen once**, one region over another: one decision, as AS-009-01's and
  AS-010-01's tilts were counted, and a card of two or three funds, refused.

## The bank's other theories this judgement touches

- **CA-024**, value and momentum everywhere, **CA-001**, momentum within groups, and **TM-040**,
  their combination, tested inconclusive: the five equity funds ranked as baskets and regions
  together.
- **CA-016**, equity country allocation, and **CA-017**, international momentum, recorded not

  under the four-asset rule.
- **CA-021**, value across sectors, countries and asset classes, and **CA-025**, carry, untouched:
  the regions' yields as one group in their cards.
- **CA-019**, reversion across national markets; **MR-021**, long-term overreaction and reversal;
  **EF-008**, the value premium; **MR-044**, countercyclical expected returns; **EF-030**, net
  payout yield; untouched.
- **AS-013**, recorded not testable, judged without the reading of the funds' distributions, as this
  one is, on another ground.

## Status

CA-018 is recorded `not-testable`: it ranks national markets by value, size and momentum, and the
lab holds three regions, one fund each, too few to carry a card of their own, and no valuation ratio
of any; the five equity funds' value by past returns is CA-024-01's, published, and their momentum
CA-001-01's; the regions' yields, ranked as one group among others, are CA-021's and CA-025's to
draw, and the point-in-time reading of the funds' distributions falls to the first theory with a
card to carry them; emerging against developed markets on their valuation holds two regions. No card
is drawn, and no trial is spent. The theory would become testable on single-country equity funds,
four at least as the four-asset rule asks, as CA-016-01 wrote, with their valuations read point in
time.
