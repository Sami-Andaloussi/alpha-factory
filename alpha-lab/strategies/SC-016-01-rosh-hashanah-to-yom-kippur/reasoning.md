# SC-016-01 — The equity funds out of the market from Rosh Hashanah to Yom Kippur: reasoning

## Why this theory now

SC-016 holds that some calendar anomalies are rooted in religious and cultural calendars rather than
in taxes or pay days: sequences of the calendar charged with social meaning change attention,
participation, mood or flows. It names three: "a fall or weakness around Rosh Hashanah followed by
an improvement until Yom Kippur"; outperformance during Ramadan in several Muslim markets; and a
Golden Week effect in Japan. It calls them heterogeneous and geographically concentrated. Its
refutations: none of the three patterns; effects no different from those of ordinary market
holidays; the same patterns where the calendar carries no social weight; no change in attention,
participation, mood or flows.

**The sources, as the lab read them in the library's books.**

- **Rosh Hashanah to Yom Kippur.** The Zacks *Handbook of Equity Market Anomalies* (2011, chapter 9)
  and Ziemba's *Calendar Anomalies and Arbitrage* (2012, §1.5.1) carry the same text, "the sell on
  Rosh Hashanah and buy on Yom Kippur anomaly": its origin seems to be the belief that Jewish
  investors liquidate their portfolios over the holiday to attend to worship, or, more likely today,
  do not trade. Going back to 1915, "the performance of the DJIA is −0.62% from the last close
  before Rosh Hashanah until eight days later with the last close before Yom Kippur", and from then
  to 31 December "averaged" 1.99%, figures the books take from TheStreet.com and the Kirk Report,
  with no end year, no count and no dispersion. The books date Yom Kippur by its eve ("Yom Kippur
  began on Friday, September 17, 2010"), and the years they quote on the S&P 500 all end eight days
  after the last close before Rosh Hashanah, on the last close before the eve of Yom Kippur: 2009,
  from the close of 18 September to that of 25 September, −2.24%, "the anomaly worked"; 2010, from 8
  to 16 September, +2.35%, "did not work"; and, in Ziemba's book alone, 2011, from 28 September to
  the close of 6 October, +1.2%, "failed again". The books do not otherwise test it. The bank's
  "improvement until Yom Kippur" is not the source's: the source sells on Rosh Hashanah and buys on
  Yom Kippur, the weakness lasting through the window.
- **Ramadan.** Białkowski, Etebari and Wisniewski, in the same two books (Zacks, chapter 9; Ziemba,
  §1.5.2), on fourteen predominantly Muslim markets from 1989 to 2007: SC-015's claim, which
  [SC-015-01](../SC-015-01-ramadan/reasoning.md) records as not testable on the lab's data.
- **Golden Week.** Ziemba (1991), reprinted as chapter 11 of *Calendar Anomalies and Arbitrage*, on
  the Tokyo Stock Exchange's NSA index from 1949 to 1988: three holidays in a week of late April and
  early May, the sessions before them strong (0.215% on 28 April, 0.536% on 2 May, 0.326% on 4 May,
  and 0.431% on 1 May between them), interconnected, he writes, with the holiday effect; Comolli and
  Ziemba, in Keim and Ziemba's *Security Market Imperfections in Worldwide Equity Markets* (2000),
  on 1990–94: 0.111% a day over the 98 sessions from 20 April to 21 May (a t statistic of 0.92
  against zero), against −0.055% over all sessions. Ilmanen (*Expected Returns*, 2011, §25.3)
  counts Japan's Golden Week and Chinese New Year among holidays with high returns, most such
  patterns too weak to exploit after costs, and elsewhere (§6.2) names moods as their most plausible
  explanation.
- **Cultural against state holidays.** Keim and Ziemba's volume reports Chan and others (1996): in
  India, Malaysia and Singapore the pre-holiday effect appeared only before cultural holidays —
  religious or ethnic, Chinese New Year, Deepavali, Aidilfitri, other Islamic holidays and Christmas
  among them — and not before secular state holidays; and Ho (1990): before 1983 a "reverse" turn of
  the lunar year in Hong Kong, Taiwan and Malaysia, a turn-of-the-lunar-year effect after it in
  Malaysia and Singapore. A cultural new year, then, with either sign, in markets the lab does not
  hold.

**What the lab can test.** Of the three, only Rosh Hashanah's falls on a market the lab holds: the
US equity funds, whose investors the mechanism names. Ramadan's is SC-015's, not testable. Golden
Week's is a Tokyo effect: the lab holds no Japanese fund, only EFA, of which Japan is a minority,
and a theory about one market is tested across several or is `not-testable`; its pre-holiday
sessions are SC-014's claim, which SC-014-01 read on the New York exchange's holidays. This card
reads the first pattern in the source's form; the bank's form is reported.

**What the lab already knows, and is not blind to.** Of this card's 105 in-sample sessions out of
the market, 76 fall in September and 29 in October, against about 8% and 9% of all sessions.
SC-017-01 published September's and October's means on the same five funds: September −0.020% a day
and October +0.044%, against +0.039% for the months other than September; the two months' gap to the
others carried by 2008, whose two months fell 28.5%; without 2008 they earned more than the others.
At those means, which are the five funds', the window's mix of months leans toward the claim by
about −0.035% a day, about a quarter of the three US funds' standard error in the clause. The other
published spans barely move it: 39 of the 105 sessions (37%, against 33% of all sessions) lie in
SC-008-01's span from −5 to +2 around the turn of the month, which beat the other sessions by
+0.094% a day on the five funds; 21 (20%, against 24%) in SC-009-01's window from −10 to −6, which
the three US funds trailed the mid-month by 0.065% a day; 28 (27%, against 24%) in SC-010-02's
second week of the month, weaker than the second half; and the whole window lies in the half of the
year from May to October that SC-018-01 read. None falls on a pre-holiday session of SC-014-01. And
the 2008 window, from the close of 29 September to that of 7 October, holds the first week of the
October crash. The published figures lean toward the claim, by an amount carried by 2008: with no
effect of Rosh Hashanah the clause's chance of refuting is about 27% at that lean rather than 36%,
and the gap without 2008 is the reading that counts. The card's expectation is taken from the
sources, not from these figures.

**The bank's neighbours.** **SC-017**, the month of the year, whose September this window mostly
sits in; **SC-018**, the Halloween effect; **SC-014**, the pre-holiday effect; **SC-015**, Ramadan.
No other theory of the bank names a Jewish holiday.

## From the claim to a signal

- **The market**: the lab's five equity funds, SPY, QQQ, IWM, EFA and EEM, in equal parts, as the
  lab's calendar cards took them. The clause grades the three US funds' average, the market the
  source measured and the one whose investors the mechanism names. EFA and EEM, whose home markets
  close hours before New York opens and whose investors mostly do not keep the holiday, are the
  bank's third refutation's control, reported, with a limit: they are listed in New York and priced
  at its close, so a weakness of US prices reaches them, in part or a session late, and their gap
  is also reported net of their beta to the US funds. The battery holds the five, as SC-014-01 held
  them while grading the three.
- **The dates**: Rosh Hashanah's first day, 1 Tishri, from the rules of the Hebrew calendar alone —
  the mean conjunction and its four postponements — computed in the code and read from no price;
  Yom Kippur falls nine days later, on 10 Tishri. From 2005 to 2025 Rosh Hashanah's first day falls
  between 5 September and 4 October. Each holiday begins at sunset on the day before, after New
  York's close.
- **The window**: the source's, out of the market from the close of the last scheduled session
  before Rosh Hashanah's first day to the close of the last scheduled session before the day eight
  days after it, the eve of Yom Kippur — "eight days later with the last close before Yom Kippur",
  Yom Kippur dated by its eve, as all three of the source's years are. From 2005 to 2022: 18
  windows, fifteen of six sessions and three of five, 105 in all, 5.8 a year; the first from the
  close of 3 October 2005.
- **The rule**: the five funds in equal parts on every session but the window's, and the bill over
  them; the target falls at the close of the last session before Rosh Hashanah and rises at the
  close of the last session before the eve of Yom Kippur.
- **A parameter for gate 6**: `days`, the calendar days after Rosh Hashanah's first day of the day
  before which the window ends, 8 for the base, the eve of Yom Kippur; its neighbours end the window
  earlier (4 and 6) or later (10 and 12). No variant: the source gives one window, and a second
  would only spend a trial.

The windows were counted before the card by scratch scripts outside the repository (`count8.py` and
`overlap.py`, in the session's scratch folder) that read the calendar alone; their Rosh Hashanah
dates match the published dates of 2005 to 2025, and their windows the source's 2009, 2010 and 2011.

## What the battery judges, and what to expect

**The size predicted — a judgment, from the sources.** The source's −0.62% over about six sessions,
against about +0.15 to +0.2% for any six sessions of the Dow's price at its long-run drift, is a gap
of about −0.8% a window, about −0.13% a day. It comes without a count, a dispersion or a test, from
a secondary retelling, and two of the three later years it quotes went the other way. The card
judges the three US funds' window at about −0.13% to 0% a day against their other sessions from 2005
to 2022; EFA and EEM at about 0, the mechanism being American, a little below if US weakness reaches
them; the five funds' average at about −0.08% to 0. The rule's alpha over the five funds held always
is then about 0 to +0.45% a year before costs (5.8 sessions a year times the five funds' gap), costs
of about 0.1% a year (two switches of the whole portfolio a year at 5 basis points a side), about
−0.1 to +0.35% after; out of the market 5.8 sessions a year at about 1.38% a day, a tracking error
of about 3.3% a year, an appraisal ratio of about −0.03 to +0.11 after costs. The battery's alpha
uses the regression beta, not the share of sessions held (0.977): out of the market over a volatile
week, such as 2008's, the beta falls and the alpha rises by the difference times the funds' premium,
whatever the window's mean, as it did in SC-017-01; the alpha at a beta equal to the share held is
reported. Out of the market 2.3% of the time, the rule's Sharpe ratio is about the benchmark's,
about 0.39 to 0.42: the card expects the base to fail gate 2, or gate 3 if it passes it — to beat
90% of its placebos, its own weights shifted in time, the window's gap would have to lie about 1.3
standard errors below zero, beyond the source's own size — and gate 6 on one fund's share of the
profit: the battery splits the rule's gross profit by the funds it holds, and a rule in the market
98% of the time splits it about as the benchmark does over 2005–2022, which put 33% on QQQ in
SC-017-01, in the market 83% of the time.

**The clause.** Judged before costs: over the in-sample sessions of the market from the first, the
three US funds' (SPY, QQQ, IWM) average daily return less the bill's rate, each from the market's
previous close; the mean over the sessions after the last scheduled session before Rosh Hashanah's
first day up to and including the last scheduled session before the eve of Yom Kippur, less the
mean over the other sessions, with the standard error of that difference, the square root of the
sum of each group's variance over its count, and its t statistic, computed apart from the battery.
The theory is refuted in this form if that t statistic is +0.35 or above. Below it, short of passing
gates 1 to 7, it is not proven, not refuted; a base that passes gates 1 to 7 while the clause
refutes leaves the theory refuted in this form.

Of SC-016's refutation criteria, the first is graded in the source's form, weakness through the
window, and the bank's form, a fall around Rosh Hashanah and an improvement until Yom Kippur, is
reported through the window's two parts; its Ramadan and Golden Week patterns are out of reach. The
second, an effect no different from ordinary market holidays', does not apply as the bank states it
— New York stays open on both holidays — and is read two ways: through Chan and others' cultural
holidays, the two eves, which as cultural holidays should show SC-014-01's pre-holiday strength;
and through the season, an effect fixed in the solar calendar, September's, not the holiday's,
showing as much on the season's other sessions. The third is reported through EFA and EEM, within
the limit above. The fourth, a change in participation, is read crudely through the US funds'
traded volume, their own shares changing hands, which the snapshot holds beside the closes.

**The test's power.** At a daily standard deviation of about 1.33% for the three US funds' average
over all in-sample sessions, and 105 window sessions against about 4,430 others, the standard error
of the difference is about 0.131% a day; at the five funds' volatility in September and October that
SC-017-01 published, 1.65%, about 0.16%. The clause then refutes about 36% of the time with no
effect, about 21% at −0.06% a day and about 9% at −0.13%, the source's size (about 24% and 12% at
the higher volatility); at the published figures' lean, about 27% with no effect. It cannot tell an
effect of the predicted size from none, and 2008's window alone, the first week of the October
crash, can move the t statistic by more than half a unit.

**Measures stated before the run**, reported, not graded, each gap with the standard error from the
two groups' variances, for the three US funds' average unless stated:

- the same gap for each of the five funds, for the five funds' average, and for EFA and EEM
  together, the unexposed control, also net of their beta to the US funds' average over all
  in-sample sessions;
- the season: the gap of the window's sessions against the other sessions from 1 September to 15
  October (455 of them), and the gap of those other sessions against the sessions outside the
  season — the holiday's weakness against September's;
- the two eves: the last session before Rosh Hashanah (18 in-sample), and the session after the
  window when it falls on the eve of Yom Kippur, in the fifteen years whose eve is a weekday (in
  2006, 2009 and 2020 the eve is a Sunday and the session after the window is Yom Kippur's own),
  each against the sessions outside the window and the two eves;
- the bank's form: the window's sessions that fall on Rosh Hashanah's two days, and its later
  sessions, each against the sessions outside the window;
- window by window: each year's return over the window less the average of the other sessions over
  as many sessions, and the number of the 18 below zero;
- without 2008, and over 2005–2011 and 2012–2022;
- from the session after the window to the year's last session, against the other sessions: the
  source's +1.99%, about the index's drift over that span;
- volume: for each year, the mean of the log of SPY's, QQQ's and IWM's traded volume over the
  window's sessions less that over the year's other sessions from 1 September to 15 October, the
  third Friday of September, a quarterly expiry, and September's last session, a quarter's end, left
  out of both groups, and the mean of the 18 with its standard error;
- the base's alpha before and after costs, and at a beta equal to the share of sessions held; the
  neighbours' and the one-day-late rule's alphas and appraisal ratios.

**Risks named before the run.**

- **Few windows**: 18, one of them in October 2008.
- **The lab's own figures** lean toward the claim through September, by about a quarter of a
  standard error, carried by 2008.
- **Gate 6**: the neighbours end the window from four days before the eve of Yom Kippur to four
  after; a real effect confined to Rosh Hashanah's days would keep most of its gap at every end, one
  confined to the days before Yom Kippur would not. The five funds are held together, and the
  battery's share of the profit is each fund's share of the rule's gross profit, about the
  benchmark's own split at 98% of sessions held: one fund, QQQ as in SC-017-01, is likely to carry
  more than 30% of it, and the rule to fail gate 6 on that limit whatever its edge.

## Choices, and the options rejected

- **Out of the market over the window, rather than into it**: the claim is weakness; the rule avoids
  it, as SC-017-01 and SC-009-01 avoided theirs.
- **The three US funds as the market graded, the five held**: a universe of three equal funds cannot
  pass gate 6, and the five are likely to fail it on QQQ's share of the funds' gross profit over
  2005–2022; the US sector funds, a market in which no fund would carry a third, would hold the same
  US equities as SPY, two of them only from 2015 and 2018, and would leave the card unlike the lab's
  other calendar cards, run on the five. The five are held and the three graded, and gate 6's
  failure on one fund's share is written here before the run.
- **Yom Kippur's own eve as the end, `days` at 9**: the last close before the holiday read with its
  first day in the calendar's sense; the source counts eight days and dates Yom Kippur by its eve,
  and its 2010 and 2011 end a session earlier, 2009 fitting both readings, the eve of Yom Kippur a
  Sunday. `days` at 8 is the source's; 9 is not among its neighbours, and the one-day-late rule ends
  its window on the eve.
- **Holding from Yom Kippur to 31 December as a variant**: that is the benchmark's holding over the
  same sessions; the source's +1.99% is reported, not traded.
- **Hebrew dates from a published table**: the calendar's rules give the same dates and read nothing
  outside the code.
- **EFA as a Golden Week test**: Japan is a minority of EFA, the effect's sessions are Tokyo's, and
  one fund is one asset.

## Implementation

Long only, the five equity funds, each with a European (UCITS) fund that tracks it, or cash; two
orders a year on dates known years ahead. The targets are filled at the close of the session on
which they are set; gate 6 adds a day's delay, which leaves on the session after the last before
Rosh Hashanah and returns on the session after the last before the eve of Yom Kippur — Rosh
Hashanah's first day and the eve of Yom Kippur when they fall on weekdays.
