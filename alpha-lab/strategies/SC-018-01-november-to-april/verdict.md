# SC-018-01 — Equities from November to April, cash from May to October: verdict

**Stops at gate 2, economic edge. Not refuted, not proven — and the in-sample clause was decided
before the run: the winter half earned about the long record's gap over the summer half, within
noise, as SC-017-01's published table had shown.** Holding the five equity funds in equal parts from
November to April and cash from May to October earns an alpha of 1.61% a year over the five held
always, 1.72% before its costs of 0.10% a year, an appraisal ratio of 0.15: at the top of the
predicted 0.8 to 1.6% after costs and 0.08 to 0.16, below the 1.9% before costs that SC-017-01's
table implied at a beta of one half, which the alpha at the share of sessions held recovers (below).
Its Sharpe ratio, 0.39, falls just short of the 0.4 required and of the benchmark's 0.40, and it
beats 89.0% of its placebos where 90% are needed. Before costs, the winter sessions averaged 0.030%
a day more than the summer sessions, with a standard error of 0.041%: a refutation was excluded
before the run, and the reading is not evidence. What was unread — the gates, Ziemba's timing and
the split by fund — shows a variant a little stronger than the base through 2008 alone, and the gap
larger in the foreign funds than in the US ones. Thresholds, version 2; the battery's figures are
the notebook's, [report.ipynb](report.ipynb), and the clause's differences, the alphas before costs
and at the share of sessions held, the betas, the months shifted, the funds, the years, the blocks,
the holdout's difference and the figures without 2008 are computed apart from it, on the same
sessions and costs. The theory is [SC-018](../../bank/SC-018-halloween-effect-sell-in-may.md).

## Gate by gate

1. **Hygiene: passes.** No look-ahead on 174 dates; 37 decisions, once clustered, over 18.0 years
   in-sample, from the first holding on 2005-01-03.
2. **Economic edge: fails.** A Sharpe ratio of 0.388, below the 0.4 required and the benchmark's
   0.398, and an alpha of 1.61% a year; at twice the costs, 0.38 and 1.51%. Costs are 0.10% a year,
   0.007 of a Sharpe unit.
3. **Significance: fails.** The probabilistic Sharpe ratio is 0.96; the rule beats 89.0% of 1,000
   placebos, its own weights shifted in time by a year or more, where 90% are needed.
4. **Multiple testing: fails.** The alpha, as an appraisal ratio, is 0.15, against 0.47 expected
   from the best of twenty-five effective trials by luck: a deflated Sharpe ratio of 0.10.
5. **Stability: fails.** The blend of the two variants passes gates 2 and 3 but fails gate 4 (a
   deflated Sharpe ratio of 0.13). The base's alpha is positive in three of the five blocks —
   2008–09 (6.1% a year), 2010–14 (5.0%) and 2015–19 (2.4%) — and negative in 2005–07 (−1.4%) and
   2020–22 (−2.6%); without its best year, 2008, it is 0.45% a year.
6. **Robustness: passes.** The neighbours, five, seven, three and nine months out, keep a median of
   83% of the base's Sharpe ratio and at least 84% at ±25%; the largest share of the profit is
   SPY's, 22%; a day's delay keeps 103%. The five funds form one cluster, so none is left out.
7. **Sealed holdout: passes.** Over 2023 to 2025, a Sharpe ratio of 0.56 and an alpha of −2.71% a
   year, both above the tenth percentile of the in-sample paths (−0.26 and −6.05%).

## Verdict

The strategy stops at gate 2, and fails gates 3 to 5 as well.

## The card's refutation, clause by clause

Computed apart from the battery, from the in-sample sessions after 2005-01-03, the first having no
previous close: the five funds' average daily return less the bill's rate was 0.0304% a day higher
over the 2,222 sessions from November to April than over the 2,308 from May to October, with a
standard error of 0.0410% from the daily returns (t 0.74) — the +0.030% SC-017-01's table had given.
The base beats 89.0% of its placebos.

- *The difference zero or less, and half of the placebos beaten or fewer*: neither is met, the first
  as known before the run. The theory is **not refuted**, a refutation having been excluded before
  the run; short of gates 1 to 7, it is **not proven**. The reading is a confirmation of what was
  known, not evidence.

Reported, not graded, as the card stated them:

- **The variant**, Ziemba's timing, out from the close of May's first session to the close of
  October's sixth-last: a difference of +0.0430% a day over its 2,328 sessions held against 2,202 (a
  standard error of 0.0409%, t 1.05); an alpha of 2.23% a year after costs, 2.33% before, an
  appraisal ratio of 0.21. Its timing was not known before the run.
- **Without 2008**: the base's difference is +0.0179% a day (0.0375%, t 0.48), its alpha 0.45% a
  year, an appraisal ratio of 0.05.
- **The months shifted**, whose differences were known: cash from April to September, an alpha of
  −0.49% a year; from June to November, +0.53%.
- **The holdout's difference**, 2023 to 2025, new for this rule only: −0.0077% a day over 368 winter
  and 384 summer sessions (0.0715%, t −0.11); as the card said, three years cannot tell the effect
  from none.
- **The base's alpha before costs**: 1.72% a year.

**The test's power.** A blind test would have refuted a vanished effect about 47% of the time and
the long record's whole gap about 23%: eighteen years could not tell the long record's gap from
none, and the standard error came in at 0.041% a day, as estimated. The run measured a gap about the
size of the long record's (0.030% against 0.028% a day), 0.74 standard errors from zero.

## What was learned

- **About the theory.** On the lab's five equity funds from 2005 to 2022, the winter half earned
  about the long record's gap over the summer half, 0.030% a day against Ilmanen's 0.028%, but
  within noise (t 0.74), positive in ten years of eighteen, with 2008 carrying some 40% of it. The
  in-sample reading was known before the card from SC-017-01's table, so it confirms a disclosed
  number rather than testing a prediction. The part the run added: the rule beat 89.0% of its
  placebos, within a point of gate 3 and within the placebo count's own noise, fell 0.012 of a
  Sharpe unit short of gate 2, and stayed far from gate 4 (a deflated Sharpe ratio of 0.10);
  Ziemba's timing did a little better than the calendar halves, through 2008; the gap was larger in
  the foreign funds; and over the holdout, 2023 to 2025, the gap was about nil, within its wide
  noise.
- **About the variant, found after the result.** Ziemba's timing holds May's first session and
  October's last five, which SC-008-01 found among the turn of the month's sessions. Without 2008,
  the variant's difference is +0.0143% a day (0.0373%), below the base's +0.0179%: its gain over the
  base rests on 2008, when entering on 24 October caught the rebound at the month's end. Read after
  the result, it grades nothing.
- **About the alpha, found after the result.** The regression gives the base a beta of 0.51, near
  its 0.49 share of sessions held; at a beta equal to that share, the alpha is 1.80% a year after
  costs, 1.90% before, the 1.9% the table implied. Unlike SC-017-01's, this alpha does not rest on a
  beta lowered by 2008, though 2008 lowers it a little (0.54 without it).
- **About the market.** The rule's best years against the benchmark were 2008 (+16.0 points of
  hedged return), 2019 (+9.8) and 2011 (+7.9); its worst 2007 (−7.8), 2020 (−4.9) and 2005 (−4.7).
  Over the holdout it lost 2.71% a year of alpha, within the in-sample range.
- **About the lab.** A result published for one card can decide another card's test: SC-017-01's
  table of calendar months fixed this card's in-sample clause, its neighbours and its months
  shifted, and every card on the same funds had opened the same holdout. The card disclosed it
  before the lock, the independent reading before the lock completed the list, and the clause was
  read as known. A theory whose test the lab's own publications have already read is still run when
  the data can test it, with the disclosure written into the card; recording it as not testable
  would hide the reading rather than state it.

SC-018 is `tested-inconclusive`: on the lab's five equity funds from 2005 to 2022, the winter half
earned about the long record's gap over the summer half, within noise and known before the card; the
rule fell just short of gates 2 and 3 and far from gate 4, and its holdout gap was about nil. The
mechanisms the bank names — a cycle of optimism, the New Year's risk appetite, summer's lower
participation — are not tested.
