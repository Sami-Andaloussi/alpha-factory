# CA-014-02 — Rotation on the dollar's trend: reasoning

## Why this theory again

CA-014, intermarket analysis, holds that the major markets absorb turns of growth and inflation at
different speeds and through different channels — bonds through rates, equities through earnings,
commodities through demand and supply, currencies through growth and yields — so that one market's
trend tells another's. CA-014-01 tested the relation the lab could hold whole at the time, the
commodity/bond ratio and the sector rotation it is said to drive, and its verdict named the currency
relations as the form left untested. Its reasoning said the lab held no currency. That was untrue:
two exchange rates, the dollar and the euro in Swiss francs (USDCHF and EURCHF), were frozen beside
the lab's prices two days before its card, to read returns in francs, and were not passed to
strategies. The lab's decisions of 2026-09-27 record the correction, leave CA-014-01 as it ran,
reopen CA-014 for its dollar relations, and have the rates passed to strategies first, read as the
close of the last day before each session, those older than a card's memory moved by gate 1 as the
prices are. They now are. This card is not a refinement of CA-014-01 (`parent: null`): it reads
another market.

**The order this card tests is Murphy's, not the bank's.** The bank's theory orders the markets'
reactions bonds first, then equities, then commodities, then exchange rates: currencies last.
Murphy, its reference, reads the dollar as the first link of a chain — the dollar's turn moves
commodities, commodities move rates, rates move stocks (chapters 1 and 6) — and this card follows
him. A refutation here says that the dollar's trend, read as Murphy reads it, did not tell which
side of the markets would lead, on these funds and years; it does not say that currencies turn last,
and a pass would not show that they turn first. The bank's first refutation, markets reacting
together or in no stable order, is what the card grades in this narrow form.

**CA-014-01's results, published, which this card has seen.** Rotating each week between the
inflation side — energy (XLE), materials (XLB), gold (GLD) and commodities (DBC) — while DBC over
IEF stood above its 200-session mean, and the rate-sensitive side — staples (XLP), health care
(XLV), financials (XLF), utilities (XLU) and Treasury notes (IEF) — otherwise, stopped at gate 3: a
Sharpe ratio of 0.52 against 0.51 for the nine funds held in equal parts, an alpha of 0.98% a year,
an appraisal ratio of 0.14, 87.4% of its placebos beaten. Its average mix held fixed earned −0.16% a
year, so its alpha was timing; but its sector funds alone earned an appraisal ratio of 0.04 against
the six sectors, and "timing the non-sector legs alone earns an alpha of 0.87% a year", nearly all
of it the commodity fund (+0.85%), gold and the notes adding nothing — the ratio's own trend. Its
blocks: 2005–07 −0.7% a year, 2008–09 −2.5%, 2010–14 +1.9%, 2015–19 −0.7%, 2020–22 +5.5%; its edge
by year in 2008 +5.9%, 2009 −10.8%, 2012 −5.6%, 2014 +11.1%, 2015 +9.0%, 2016 −10.2%, 2022 +10.2%;
over the ordinary monetary regimes its appraisal ratio was −0.03, over the zero-rate years 0.29; in
the holdout an alpha of −5.17% a year. Its verdict also published returns of the funds this card
holds (2008 financials −55%, 2009 materials +48%, 2014 commodities −28%, utilities +29%, health care
+25%; over 2023–25 gold +134%, energy +13%, materials +24%, commodities +4%), and the registry
carries its monthly returns hedged of its benchmark.

**Where this card is CA-014-01, and what it grades instead.** This card holds the same nine funds on
the same two sides, in equal parts, reset on the same first session of each week. Wherever the
dollar's side and the ratio's agree, its holdings are CA-014-01's exactly, and their outcome is
published: counted from the signals alone, from CA-014-01's first holding, 2006-11-20, to December
2022, 85 of 194 months hold the same side on every session, 39 the opposite side, and 70 are mixed;
the two agree on every session of 2008 but two in a hundred, and disagree on every session of 2022,
the dollar on the rate-sensitive side and the ratio on the inflation side nine sessions in ten, the
reverse in the tenth. So the clause grades only the **new sessions**: those into which the base
holds a side, set by its last target before the session, that differs from the side CA-014-01's
rule, rebuilt over the same market (its base, `average` 200, `legs` "all"), holds into it, or into
which that rule holds nothing, before 2006-11-20 — 1,814 of the 4,347 sessions from the first
target, 291 of them before that rule's first holding, 7.2 years, a session's return being earned by
the side held into it, all of them counted from the signals, reading no return. On those sessions
the base holds the side CA-014-01 did not, so CA-014-01's published results bear on them as a near
mirror: its registry series approximates, with the sign reversed, the base's hedged returns in the
39 months wholly opposed, and nothing was computed from it before this card; its edge of 2022,
published for the whole year, is the mirror of the base on all of 2022's 251 new sessions, so 2022
is left out of the graded clause and the clause with it is reported apart. The other published years
(2008, 2009, 2012, 2014, 2015, 2016) fall across new and agreeing sessions, between 2% and 52% new,
359 new sessions in all, and stay in the graded sample, disclosed, the clause without them reported
apart. The whole sample, which the battery judges, is reported apart too. What the clause grades is
what the dollar says where the ratio said otherwise, or before the ratio's rule held anything: 1,563
sessions, 6.2 years, the dollar on the inflation side in 57.5% of them.

## The source

Murphy's *Intermarket Analysis* (2004), the theory's reference, gives the dollar four statements.

- **The principle.** His recap of the relations that ruled the last thirty years of the twentieth
  century (chapter 6) lists the market relationships, the first being "the dollar and commodities
  trend in opposite directions", with bonds and commodities in opposite directions and bonds and
  stocks normally together, and adds that "a rising dollar is good for U.S. bonds and stocks". A
  falling currency, he writes in the same chapter, "usually gives a boost to commodity prices quoted
  in that currency", which "reawakens inflation fears" and pushes rates up.
- **The channel.** "The influence of the dollar on bonds and stocks is usually filtered through its
  influence on commodity markets" (chapter 6): the dollar moves commodities, commodities move rates,
  rates move stocks. A falling dollar is bearish for bonds and stocks "only if it coincides with
  rising commodity prices" (chapter 1).
- **The beneficiaries.** "Historically, the primary beneficiary of a falling dollar is the gold
  market and gold mining shares" (chapter 8); in 2002 the commodity index "turned up at the exact
  point that the dollar peaked", which he calls "consistent with the intermarket principle that a
  falling dollar usually results in higher commodity prices" (chapter 9); in 1997 and 1998 gold
  "mirrored dollar moves almost perfectly" (chapter 4).
- **The lag, and the warning.** "Of the four financial markets used in intermarket work, the dollar
  is probably the most difficult to fit into a consistent intermarket model"; long delays between
  the dollar's trend changes and other markets' "are part of the reason" (chapter 1): after the
  dollar's peak of 1985, "it took a year or two" for commodities to rally. In 1995 commodities rose
  with a rising dollar, "with a time lag" before the normal pattern returned (chapter 4); in 2002
  there was none.

He also writes, against the sides this card uses, of a channel through foreign earnings (chapter 6):
a weak dollar helps multinationals — Procter & Gamble, "a multinational consumer products firm", a
staples company — drug stocks, and service companies, "which include insurance"; and a rising dollar
favours small stocks over large ones. Three of the four sector funds on the rate-sensitive side —
staples, health care and financials — hold such companies, which the base holds when the dollar
rises. The card follows the channel his principles name — through commodities to the inflation-type
and rate-sensitive sides — and names the other as a risk; it is one reason the variant holds no
sector.

## From the principle to a signal

- **The dollar.** The lab holds no dollar index. The dollar's price in euros is USDCHF over EURCHF,
  each read from `market.signal_rates`, the close of the last day before the session (a currency's
  daily close comes after the US close). By the dollar index's definition, not Murphy's text, the
  euro weighs about 58% of it; the yen, the pound, the Canadian dollar, the krona and the franc make
  the rest. The euro is a proxy, and the card says so: a move of the dollar against the yen alone is
  not seen.
- **Its trend.** The dollar is falling when its reading stands below the mean of the readings among
  the last 200 sessions, up to and including the session's own; rising at or above. Murphy reads the
  major trend of markets from their 200-day average on daily charts and their 40-week average on a
  Friday close, and reads the dollar's own turns with trendlines and patterns (the
  head-and-shoulders top of 2002, a seven-year support line), which are drawn by eye and have no
  rule to lock; CA-014-01 read its ratio with the average, for the same reason.
- **The gaps.** EURCHF has no close from 2008-08-01 to 2008-08-25. The lab reads a rate up to seven
  calendar days old, so the close of 31 July is read through 7 August, and none from 8 to 26 August:
  thirteen sessions without a reading. The mean is taken over the readings that exist among the last
  200 sessions, at least nine tenths of them (180), so that the gap does not blank the trend for 200
  sessions. A week whose first session has no reading sets no target and holds what it held: the
  weeks of 11, 18 and 25 August 2008. The week of 4 August sets its target on USDCHF of 1 August
  over EURCHF of 31 July, and the readings of 5, 6 and 7 August, which enter the mean, are likewise
  read from different days. The two rates are also read from different days on 2007-11-27,
  2007-12-19, 2012-12-05, 2013-10-09 and 2019-05-23, none of them the first session of its week; and
  EURCHF repeats the close of 30 April 2010 on three days from 2010-05-03, a hole in the vendor's
  series that the lab's data record names.
- **The two sides**, CA-014-01's. A falling dollar holds the inflation side, gold and commodities
  first among it, with energy and materials, the sectors of Murphy's commodity/bond list; a rising
  dollar the rate-sensitive side, the notes and the sectors that follow rates. The rotation is
  Murphy's chain read from its first link: the dollar's turn, then commodities', then rates'.
- **The variant "classes"**: gold and commodities (GLD, DBC) while the dollar falls, the notes (IEF)
  while it rises — the dollar relations alone, without the stocks through which Murphy says they are
  filtered and the foreign-earnings channel runs against the sides: "the dollar and commodities
  trend in opposite directions", "the primary beneficiary of a falling dollar is the gold market",
  and "a rising dollar is good for U.S. bonds and stocks", the notes being the bonds the lab holds.
  It is the purer test of the currency relation, as the sector funds alone were the purer test of
  CA-014-01's ratio. It is not CA-014-01's variant: the sectors alone read on the dollar would test
  the filtered channel with the other against it, and CA-014-01's sectors alone showed nothing on
  the ratio. Two variants, not three.
- **The pace.** Each week, on its first session: Murphy reads daily charts and Friday closes, as for
  CA-014-01.
- **The start.** 200 readings exist from 2005-10-18, but the mean first exists on 2005-09-20, on 180
  readings, under the nine-tenths rule written for August 2008's gap, which brings the start forward
  by four weeks; the first target is on 2005-09-26, when DBC does not yet trade (from February
  2006): the inflation side is then XLE, XLB and GLD, and under "classes" GLD alone. The funds of
  the side held that trade share it equally.

**The signal counted before the card.** As the lab's decisions allow when the pace is in doubt, and
as CA-014-01 did, the signal was counted on the lab's rates before this card, reading no return.
Read weekly with a 200-session mean, the dollar changes side 53 times from September 2005 to
December 2022, every year but 2005 and 2007 (8 times in 2013 and in 2016), and 20 times in the
holdout; the neighbours make 63 (150), 45 (250), 95 (100) and 40 (300). It stands below its mean in
49.6% of the weeks. Over the 838 weeks both rules read, the dollar's side agrees with CA-014-01's
ratio's in 62.4% of them: 315 weeks disagree, the dollar choosing the inflation side in 156 of them.
The new sessions the clause grades were counted the same way. The pace was not chosen by these
counts.

**A departure.** Before the card, the dollar's weekly side was also compared with DBC's and GLD's
own prices against their 200-session means: the dollar's falling agrees with DBC above its mean in
64.7% of the weeks, and with GLD above its mean in 64.1%. These read no return, but they read the
in-sample prices of two funds the card holds, and measure, before the lock, how often the
mechanism's first link — the dollar against the commodities' trend — held on the graded data: more
than the decision on counts covers, which is a signal's changes when the pace is in doubt.
Rebuilding CA-014-01's rule to count the weeks the two rules agree, and the new sessions the clause
needs, reads DBC's and IEF's in-sample prices too, and the 62.4% agreement measures the chain on the
graded data as the counts against DBC and GLD do; the clause cannot be defined without it. It is
written here, and the verdict records it.

## What the battery judges, and what to expect

The benchmark holds the nine funds in equal parts, both sides at once: the alpha is what choosing
the side on the dollar adds. The battery judges the whole sample, of which three fifths is
CA-014-01's published outcome; the card's clause judges the new sessions.

**The size predicted.** Murphy reports charts and episodes — 1985 to 1987, 1994 to 1998, 2002 — not
statistics, and no study in the lab's library measures this rule. The card assumes a small edge, an
appraisal ratio of about 0.1 to 0.25 against the nine funds, whole sample and new sessions alike,
below the 0.2 to 0.3 CA-014-01 assumed for the ratio, because Murphy calls the dollar the hardest
market to fit and its delays long: a rule that acts on the dollar's trend within weeks is early
whenever the delay is a year. The band was set after CA-014-01's published 0.14, which it brackets,
and on a rule that holds CA-014-01's positions three weeks in five: it is informed by those results
and cannot be separated from them, and it is no evidence. CA-014-01's published alpha of 0.98% at an
appraisal ratio of 0.14 implies a tracking error of about 7% a year on the same two sides, so the
assumed ratio is an alpha of about 0.7 to 1.75% a year. The variant's tracking error against its
three funds held in equal parts is larger, perhaps 10 to 12%, gold and commodities against notes.
Costs: the rule changes side a little less often than CA-014-01's and resets drifted weights each
week, so about 0.4 to 0.5% a year.

**The regimes the theory excludes.** As in CA-014-01, the card dates dominant monetary policy by the
bill rate set the session before: below 0.25% a year, the top of the Federal Reserve's target range
at its zero bound. From the first target, 46.4% of the sessions, 8.0 years, are ordinary regimes,
and 9.2 years below the bound; of the graded new sessions, 829, 3.3 years. The clause asks that the
rule fail over the ordinary regimes too before refuting, as the theory's own refutation asks; the
split can turn a refutation into not proven, never a failure into a pass.

**No placebo condition.** CA-014-01's clause also asked for a rank among the placebos at half or
below. The placebos shift the whole sample's weights, three fifths of which are CA-014-01's
published positions, so their rank cannot judge the new sessions; the battery reports it. The base's
average mix is near the benchmark's — about 12.4% in each inflation fund and 10.1% in each
rate-sensitive one against 11.1% — and CA-014-01's, the same funds, earned −0.16% a year held fixed:
the appraisal ratio alone carries the claim.

**The test's power.** Over the 6.2 graded years an appraisal ratio is measured to about ±0.40; over
their 3.3 ordinary years to about ±0.55. By simulation of the two figures, correlated as their
overlap makes them, the clause refutes a rule with no edge about one time in three, and one with the
assumed 0.2 about one time in six: it tells the two apart poorly, because what the dollar says
beyond the ratio is a third of the sample. Over the whole sample, reported apart, an appraisal ratio
is measured to about ±0.24. The lab now counts about seventy effective trials: the best of them by
luck is expected to reach about 0.63, and an appraisal ratio of about 0.9 would be needed to pass
gate 4.

**Risks named before the run.**

- **The delay.** Murphy's own warning: the dollar's effect on commodities came after a year or two
  in 1985–87 and after months in 1995, at once in 2002. A rule that acts on the dollar's current
  trend loses whenever the effect is delayed, and a card that waited a year would lock a lag the
  source does not give.
- **The euro is not the dollar index.** Moves of the dollar against the yen, which carried the
  dollar in some years, are missed.
- **The other channel.** A weak dollar helps multinational staples, drug makers and insurers through
  foreign earnings, which the base holds when the dollar rises.
- **Gold since 2019.** Gold has often moved with bonds, through real yields, as CA-014-01 named; and
  in 2022 the dollar and commodities rose together, the relation reversed — the year the graded
  clause leaves out.
- **The commodities' own trend.** Where the dollar agrees with DBC's trend the rule holds DBC while
  it rises; CA-014-01 found its edge there. On the new sessions the dollar and the ratio disagree,
  which is where the dollar is tested apart from it.
- **One fund above 30% of the profit.** Gold rose 134% in the holdout, and energy carried 29% of
  CA-014-01's profit.
- **Gate 6.** The clusters are the sectors, metals, commodities and bonds. Without the sectors, the
  base keeps three funds; gate 6 moves the base only.

## Choices, and the options rejected

- **A lag of a year**, the dollar's trend read twelve months back: the source gives a year or two
  once, months once and none once; a lag would be chosen, not read.
- **The dollar's rate of change over a year**: Murphy reads trends, not changes; the average is his
  device.
- **Foreign equities against US equities on the dollar**: Murphy's weak dollar raises foreign
  assets' appeal for an American investor, but that is currency translation, CA-016's rival, judged
  there; the lab's two foreign funds are two regions.
- **Large against small stocks on the dollar**: a multinational channel, and SPY against IWM is two
  funds.
- **The sectors alone as the variant**: the filtered channel, with the foreign-earnings channel
  against it; CA-014-01's sectors showed nothing on the ratio.
- **The dollar and the ratio together**, the inflation side only when both agree: a combination of
  two signals, one of them already run, chosen after seeing that it ran.
- **Grading the whole sample**: three fifths of it is CA-014-01's published outcome, and a clause
  over it would grade that outcome again.
- **Leaving out every published year**: 2008, 2009, 2012, 2014, 2015 and 2016 fall across new and
  agreeing sessions and were published pooled, over the whole year; left out, they would take 1.4 of
  the 6.2 graded years, leaving 1,204 sessions, 4.8 years; kept, they are disclosed, and the clause
  without them is reported, as the lab's rules ask of a state published only pooled.
- **Long bonds (TLT)**: IEF, as CA-014-01 read.

## Implementation

Long only, fully invested whenever a fund of the side held trades, nine funds, each with a European
(UCITS) fund or an exchange-traded commodity that tracks it, rebalanced weekly on closes. The
exchange rates of a session are the currencies' close of the day before, which comes after the US
close of that day and before the close of the session at which the targets trade; gate 6 adds a day
of delay.
