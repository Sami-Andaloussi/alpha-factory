# TM-007-01 — Underreaction to public information: reasoning, not testable as a theory of its own

## The theory, and the forms its sources give it

TM-007 holds that investors update too little on public news — earnings, dividends, repurchases and
other fundamental signals — through conservatism, anchoring, inattention and the slow diffusion of
information, so that prices drift in the direction of the news over the following weeks and months,
stale public information predicts returns, and the drift gives way to reversal over two to five
years. Its refutations: prices that incorporate public news fully at release, with no drift, and
stale public information with no predictive power; no stronger drift among stocks with little
analyst coverage or when information arrives gradually; investors who update too strongly on news
with persistent content, producing reversals rather than drifts; no momentum-then-reversal sequence,
or long-run reversals with no earlier underreaction; drifts that vanish once costs and short-sale
constraints are removed.

**Its sources, as the lab read them in the library.** Its core is Barberis, Shleifer and Vishny's
conservatism, in Shleifer's *Inefficient Markets* (2000, chapter 5) and in *Advances in Behavioral
Finance, Volume II* (Thaler, ed., 2005, chapter 12), which TM-005-01 read: the drift after earnings
announcements (Bernard and Thomas; Bernard's survey), after repurchases, dividend initiations and
omissions, splits and seasoned offerings, and momentum in stock prices (Jegadeesh and Titman, and
Chan, Jegadeesh and Lakonishok, who tie it to earnings surprises); in aggregate returns, a weaker
positive autocorrelation of index returns over one month to a year (Cutler and others), to which
the authors give "only minor attention". Hong and Stein's gradual diffusion and Hong, Lim and
Stein's coverage, Daniel, Hirshleifer and Subrahmanyam's underreaction to public signals, and
Hirshleifer, Lim and Teoh's distraction — earnings news underreacted on days crowded with other
announcements — complete it. Ilmanen, in *Expected Returns* (2011, chapter 6), gives Barberis,
Shleifer and Vishny's conservatism as making investors underreact to economic announcements and
other public news; in *Investing Amid Low Expected Returns* (2022) he names underreaction to public
information, "whether company-specific, sector-relevant, or macroeconomic", as one of momentum's two
behavioural explanations, and describes **macro momentum**: markets underreacting to macro news, so
that a strategy favours "an equity market with a benign backdrop — recently strong growth
surprises, soft inflation news, easy central bank policies", and follows "tightening credit
conditions and rising risk aversion" as bearish signals; the idea extends to bonds, currencies and
commodities, for which he gives no sign.

## What the lab can read of it, and where each form already stands

The lab holds the daily closes of the funds and of bitcoin, and the Treasury bill's yield, which a
strategy reads as its cash rate; two franc exchange rates are frozen for reading returns in francs
but are not passed to a strategy. It holds no announcement dates, earnings, dividends, repurchases,
analysts' forecasts or coverage, nor a calendar of macroeconomic releases (SC-011-01). On those
data, each of TM-007's forms is either already read by a verdict of the lab, is the claim of another
theory of the bank, or fails gate 1 at the one window that reads it, clearing it at another only
on noise:

- **Stale public information predicting returns, as price momentum**: read on the lab's funds at the
  six- and twelve-month windows — **TM-017-01**, **CA-001-01**, **TM-003-01**, **TM-001-01** — and
  at one month the claim of **TM-017**, still in progress, and **TM-043**.
- **The drift after an announcement, and the full reaction at release**: announcement dates, which
  the lab does not hold; the earnings drift is **EF-035**'s, analysts' revisions **EF-034**'s,
  repurchases **EF-029**'s, seasoned offerings **AS-005**'s; the drifts after dividend initiations
  and omissions and after splits need dates the lab does not hold, and no other theory claims them.
  Distraction, earnings news on crowded days, is **EF-035**'s too.
- **A stronger drift with little coverage**: coverage, which the lab does not hold; **TM-025**'s,
  bad news travels slowly, and **TM-008**'s. **When information arrives gradually**: the
  frog-in-the-pan form, read by **LL-023-01** (the more continuous half of each group's leaders
  2.04% a year below the other, standard error 1.44%, stopped at gate 2, refuted in its form).
- **Anchoring on a reference price**: the 52-week high, **TM-002**'s, read by **TM-002-01** on the
  sector funds (stopped at gate 3, not proven, not refuted: an alpha of 0.96% a year, 0.21% below
  TM-003-01's, standard error 1.01%), which set the other groups aside — across asset classes the
  ratio ranks volatility, and five equity markets are too few; the library signs the high only on
  stocks, and its time-series breakout form is TM-017's.
- **Macroeconomic news**: the bank names drifts after macroeconomic shocks in futures markets, which
  need release dates the lab does not hold. Without a calendar, Ilmanen (2022) gives the form a
  sign: macro momentum favours an equity market whose central bank is easing (his "easy central bank
  policies"), as underreaction to macro news, and follows "tightening credit conditions" as bearish.
  The lab holds one such input, the bill's yield. No verdict has read it: CA-012-01 set it aside for
  sector rotation, recorded not testable, the policy having turned four times, and LL-042-01 read
  the Treasury market's past year for the equity funds (its pure lead and lag −0.37% a year, refuted
  in that form). **CA-015** claims the bill rate's rational reading as a forecaster. Counted on the
  bill's yield alone, as the lab passes it (the yield of the session before), reading no fund
  return, the rule "the equity funds while the yield is below its level a year earlier, cash
  otherwise" changes state 20 times on the in-sample first sessions from 2006 to 2022, 18 of them
  with the yield below 0.25%: about twenty decisions, short of gate 1's thirty whatever the prices.
  The library gives the change no window; a six-month window changes 36 times, enough for the count,
  but 31 of those changes come with the yield below 0.25%, in years when the policy rate did not
  move: its decisions turn on hundredths of a point, not on easy or tight policy, as CA-012-01 found
  of such a signal. A rule that reads policy, turning only on a change of 0.10 or 0.25 point,
  changes 4 to 27 times at either window. The six-month form without a band is set aside as not
  reading the theory's signal — a judgement, not a certainty: a window with no source, chosen
  because it clears gate 1, would clear it only on the zero bound's noise. No card is drawn.
- **Macro news between equities and bonds**: Ilmanen (2011, the safe-haven note) finds past equity
  returns predicting bond returns negatively over a quarter, perhaps partly underreaction to growth
  news, and calls the evidence weak; that is **LL-042**'s claim, cross-asset momentum spillovers,
  whose card's bond legs read it at a year.
- **The reversal over two to five years, and the momentum-then-reversal sequence**: **MR-021**'s
  claim, long-term overreaction and reversal, read as value within groups by **CA-024-01** (the
  cheapest third by the past five years, 0.63% a year below the groups from 2010 to 2022, stopped at
  gate 2, not refuted, not proven), as TM-005-01 recorded.
- **Drifts that vanish with costs and short-sale constraints removed**: no price form; **MR-025**'s,
  limits to arbitrage.

**The bank's siblings.** **TM-005**, conservatism and representativeness, recorded not testable as a
theory of its own, whose source is this theory's core; **TM-006**, overconfidence, drawn as momentum
after market gains (TM-006-01, stopped at gate 2, not refuted, the market's state outside the panic
months leaving momentum unchanged); **TM-008**, gradual information diffusion; **LL-042**,
cross-asset spillovers; **CA-015**, tactical forecasting with the short rate; **CA-012**, sector
rotation by policy, recorded not testable.

**What was considered, and why it does not rescue a card of TM-007's own:**

- **The index funds' drift after their own largest daily moves**, a public shock read in prices: the
  library's one table of returns after jumps, in Chevallier and Ielpo's *The Economics of Commodity
  Markets* (2013), gives mixed signs by market and reads them through volatility, not underreaction;
  Barberis, Shleifer and Vishny have strong single news overreacting; the reversal after extreme
  moves is **MR-002**'s claim, and the lab's fund-level weekly reversal was read by MR-032-01.
- **Commodity and bond funds' momentum as underreaction to supply and macroeconomic news**: a
  mechanism for the momentum already read on the same funds — by TM-017-01 and TM-001-01 (IEF, TLT,
  GLD, DBC) and, among the commodities, by CA-001-01 — not a form with a rule of its own.
- **Other candidates, none open**: currencies underreacting to yield changes (Ilmanen 2011, carry) —
  the lab trades no currency and passes a strategy no franc rate; underreaction to inflation — it
  needs inflation or long yields; the trend in the week before payroll reports (Ilmanen 2011,
  chapter 14) — explained there by risk aversion, and TM-017's.

What would make it testable as its own: announcement dates and fundamentals for the funds' holdings,
or a calendar of macroeconomic releases frozen in the snapshot, or a longer history of policy turns.

## Status

TM-007 is recorded `not-testable` as a theory of its own: on the lab's data its stale-information
form is price momentum, read by TM-017-01, CA-001-01, TM-003-01 and TM-001-01; its announcement
drifts need dates the lab does not hold and are EF-035's, EF-034's, EF-029's and AS-005's; its
coverage form is TM-025's; its gradual form is read by LL-023-01; its anchoring by TM-002-01; its
reversal is MR-021's; and its macro-momentum form, on the bill rate, is certain to fail gate 1 at a
year, at about twenty decisions, and a shorter window clears the count only on the zero bound's
noise. No card is drawn, and no trial is spent.
