# TM-019-01 — Earnings momentum: reasoning, not testable as a theory of its own

## The theory, and the forms its sources give it

TM-019 holds that stocks sorted on their earnings surprises (standardised unexpected earnings), on
analysts' revisions or on their abnormal returns around the announcement earn abnormal returns in
the direction of the surprise for weeks to months, about four quarters, and that this earnings
momentum is distinct from price momentum and adds to it (Chan, Jegadeesh and Lakonishok, 1996, a
correlation of 0.29 between the two signals). Its refutations: surprises that do not persist from
one quarter to the next; stocks sorted on surprises or revisions earning no abnormal returns
afterwards; price momentum unchanged once earnings momentum is controlled for, and earnings momentum
adding nothing to price momentum; analysts' forecast errors with no persistence and no anchoring.
The bank names stocks, equity indices and sectors, and as data daily prices, announcement dates,
reported earnings and analysts' forecasts.

**Its sources, as the lab read them in the library.** The library holds none of the papers the bank
cites, and reads them through others' books.

- **Shleifer** (*Inefficient Markets*, 2000, chapter 5): Bernard's survey of the drift after
  earnings announcements, 84,792 announcements from 1974 to 1986 sorted on the surprise, and Chan,
  Jegadeesh and Lakonishok's momentum portfolios, read as underreaction through conservatism.
- **Barberis and Thaler** and **Jegadeesh and Titman**, in *Advances in Behavioral Finance, Volume
  II* (Thaler, ed., 2005, chapters 1 and 10): Bernard and Thomas sort NYSE and AMEX stocks each
  quarter from 1974 to 1986 on their latest surprise, and the best decile beats the worst by about
  4% over the sixty days after the announcement, not explained by beta; Chan, Jegadeesh and
  Lakonishok measure the surprise against analysts' forecasts and by the price reaction and find the
  same; the momentum chapter's section on earnings momentum tabulates the returns of portfolios
  sorted on the surprise and on analysts' revisions (tables 10.10 to 10.13), all on single stocks;
  and **Barberis, Shleifer and Vishny** (chapter 12): the highest-SUE stocks beat the lowest by 4.2%
  risk-adjusted over sixty trading days (Bernard's survey), and earnings changes are autocorrelated
  0.34, 0.19, 0.06 and −0.24 at one to four quarters (Bernard and Thomas, 2,626 firms, 1974-1986) —
  the persistence the theory's first refutation names, on firms; for underreaction in aggregate
  returns the authors give only Cutler and others' positive autocorrelation of index returns over
  one month to a year, price momentum, which the lab has read on the funds.
- **Gray and Vogel** (*Quantitative Momentum*, 2016, appendix A): Novy-Marx finds that price
  momentum earns no alpha once earnings momentum — the surprise and the abnormal return over the
  three days around the announcement — is controlled for, against Chan, Jegadeesh and Lakonishok,
  for whom both matter; on their own mid- and large-cap US stocks from 1975 to 2014 they compare the
  three long-short portfolios.
- **Ilmanen** (*Expected Returns*, 2011) ties momentum and the drift after announcements to
  underreaction and the disposition effect (chapter 6), and reports price momentum closely related
  to earnings momentum from analysts' revisions, both weakened since Regulation FD in October 2000
  (chapter 14), on stocks. **Campbell, Lo and MacKinlay** (1997, chapter 4) illustrate the event
  study on the quarterly announcements of the thirty Dow Jones firms from 1988 to 1993: prices move
  on the day and drift before it, but after it the cumulative abnormal return stays relatively
  stable — no drift among the largest stocks. **Lo and MacKinlay** (1999) list the drift among the
  anomalies without a measure. **O'Shaughnessy** (2011, chapter 17) finds the stocks with the
  largest one-year gains in earnings per share earning 11.88% a year, just above all stocks, from
  1964 to 2009, and among large stocks less than the universe, useful only beside valuation and
  price momentum (chapters 20-21). **Bali, Engle and Murray** (2016, chapter 18) give the drift as
  underreaction, on stocks.

Every size the library gives for a return that follows earnings news is on single stocks, with each
firm's reported earnings, analysts' forecasts or announcement dates.

## What the lab can read of it, and where each form already stands

The lab holds the daily closes of funds and of bitcoin, and the Treasury bill's yield; it holds no
reported earnings, analysts' forecasts or announcement dates, for firms or for the funds' holdings.
Every one of TM-019's signals — the surprise, the revision, the abnormal return around an
announcement — reads data the lab does not hold:

- **The drift after the surprise, and its persistence over four quarters**: earnings and
  announcement dates; **EF-035**'s claim, the drift after earnings announcements.
- **Analysts' revisions and the anchoring of their forecasts**: forecasts, unreadable; the drift
  after revisions is **EF-034**'s claim.
- **Earnings momentum against price momentum**: a horse race between two signals across many stocks,
  which needs the surprise; price momentum alone is read on the lab's funds — TM-017-01, CA-001-01,
  TM-001-01 and, among sector funds, TM-003-01.
- **Equity indices and sectors**, which the bank names: the aggregate surprise of a sector's or a
  country's firms, or their aggregated revisions, which the lab does not hold; by analogy, the
  aggregated surprises of a sector index's constituents are **EF-035**'s, and revisions aggregated
  over an index's constituents **EF-034**'s; **CA-017**, international momentum, names earnings
  revisions among its data but claims the persistence of country indices' price ranking. The one
  sign the library gives at the level of the market is contemporaneous, not a forecast: Ilmanen
  (*Expected Returns*, 2011, chapter 16) reports the relation between realised earnings news and
  stock market returns negative at the aggregate level, where it is positive for firms, and positive
  when analysts' forecasts replace realised earnings; Gliner (*Global Macro Trading*, 2014, chapter
  7) reads an equity market's earnings forecast revisions among the measures of its performance, as
  a guide to its currency. Both read earnings or forecasts the lab does not hold, and neither
  predicts a return; no source in the library signs a drift after a sector's or an index's aggregate
  surprise.

**The bank's siblings.** **EF-035**, the drift after announcements; **EF-034**, revisions;
**TM-007**, underreaction to public information, recorded not testable; **TM-005**, conservatism,
recorded not testable; **TM-016**, the disposition effect, recorded not testable; **TM-001**,
**TM-003** and **TM-017**, price momentum; **CA-017**, international momentum, which lists earnings
revisions among its data.

**What was considered, and why it does not rescue a card of TM-019's own:**

- **The abnormal return of a sector fund over the weeks of an earnings season as its surprise**: the
  season's dates are known only roughly, a fund holds many firms reporting on different days, and
  the library gives no sign for a fund's return over a season; drawn on the funds' returns alone, it
  is price momentum over a window chosen without a source; SC-022-01 weighed the same seasons on SPY
  and the sector funds and found no source signing an index-level announcement premium and no theory
  claiming one, and Ilmanen's run-up before announcements (2011, chapter 25) is on stocks.
- **Price momentum as fundamental momentum** (Novy-Marx): a reading of why price momentum works,
  already read on the funds.

What would make it testable as its own: firms' reported earnings, analysts' forecasts and
announcement dates, frozen in the snapshot with a stock universe.

## Status

TM-019 is recorded `not-testable` as a theory of its own: every one of its signals reads earnings,
forecasts or announcement dates the lab does not hold; its drift is EF-035's and its revisions
EF-034's; the price momentum it is set against is read by TM-017-01, CA-001-01, TM-001-01 and
TM-003-01. No card is drawn, and no trial is spent.
