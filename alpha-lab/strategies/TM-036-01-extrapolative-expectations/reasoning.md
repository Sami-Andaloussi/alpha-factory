# TM-036-01 — Extrapolative expectations: reasoning, not testable as a theory of its own

## The theory, and the forms its sources give it

TM-036 is the X-CAPM of Barberis, Greenwood, Jin and Shleifer (2015): some investors project past
returns into the future, buying after rises and selling after falls, and in an equilibrium with
rational investors, who find trading against them risky over an uncertain horizon, persistent trends
coexist with incomplete arbitrage, the extrapolators' beliefs sometimes self-fulfilling. Its
refutations: demand that does not follow past price moves, with no buying after rises and no selling
after falls; extrapolative demand fully offset by rational investors, leaving no continuation of
prices. The bank names equity indices and stocks, and daily prices only.

**Its sources, as the lab read them in the library.** The library holds the X-CAPM in others'
reports and bibliographies, not the paper.

- **Campbell** (*Financial Decisions and Markets*, 2018): investors with extrapolative beliefs,
  becoming more optimistic after rises in dividends or prices, "may create short-run momentum and
  longer-run reversal in stock returns", after De Long and others (1990), Barberis, Shleifer and
  Vishny (1998) and Barberis and others (2015) (section 11.4.1, "Noise Traders", p. 356); households
  extrapolate from their own experience of returns (Malmendier and Nagel), and extrapolation
  amplifies the price effects of undiversified holdings (section 10.4, "Portfolio Inertia and Return
  Extrapolation", pp. 331–32). The model's sign on prices is a pair: continuation, then reversal.
- **Shiller** (*Irrational Exuberance*, 3rd edition, 2015): speculative feedback loops, rises
  rewarding investors in turn as in naturally occurring Ponzi processes and drawing more in, with
  the reversal to follow (chapter 5); extreme moves of countries' indices usually came back — after
  large five-year rises 68% fell over the next five years (−14.7% on average), after large five-year
  falls 80% rose (+119.7%), and over twelve months 60% fell after large rises and 72% rose after
  large falls (chapter 8, "What Went Up (Down) Usually Came Back Down (Up)"); Greenwood and Shleifer
  (2013) relate survey measures of investors' expectations to stock prices (appendix, note 16),
  where the X-CAPM is cited among the resale models.
- **Bouchaud, Bonart, Donier and Gould** (*Trades, Quotes and Prices*, 2018, sections 20.1.1 and
  20.2.1): the trend-following puzzle, returns positively autocorrelated over weeks to months in
  every asset class for at least two centuries, which they tie to agents forecasting one another's
  behaviour; excess volatility, the other puzzle; the X-CAPM in the chapter's further reading on
  trend following.
- **Ilmanen** (*Expected Returns*, 2011): Box 6.1 offers as "perhaps a broader lesson" that greater
  uncertainty about the true process — "new assets, limited data, lack of valuation anchors" — makes
  investors more prone to extrapolation and markets more susceptible to trending; chapter 20, figure
  20.1, after Cutler, Poterba and Summers (1991): across asset classes and strategies, adjacent
  months' returns correlate at +0.20 and the latest six months' return with the six months' 18
  months earlier at −0.11; section 8.5, investors' one-year forecasts in surveys correlating at
  about 0.9 with the past year's return. **Ilmanen** (*Investing Amid Low Expected Returns*, 2022,
  section 4.2): subjective expectations inversely related to objective ones, optimistic after bull
  markets, when valuations are high.
- **Shefrin** (*A Behavioral Approach to Asset Pricing*, 2nd edition, 2008, section 6.1.4): the
  UBS/Gallup survey's expectations following the past year's return, while the S&P 500's annual
  autocorrelation over 1926 to 2002 was 0.05 (t 0.45) — a sign against continuation at a year.
  **Shleifer** (*Inefficient Markets*, 2000): index autocorrelation of about 0.1 over a month for
  stocks and slightly negative over three to five years (chapter 5); positive feedback traders, and
  Frankel and Froot's survey of currency forecasters extrapolating over a month and reverting within
  a year, as beliefs (chapter 6). **Pedersen** (*Efficiently Inefficient*, 2015, chapter 12): the
  life cycle of a trend, continuation then moves partly reversed over three to five years. Barberis
  and Thaler's survey and Durell's survey data in Thaler's *Advances in Behavioral Finance, Volume
  II* (2005), and Ilmanen (2022, section 6.2), add no sign beyond these: positive feedback,
  FP-024's; survey material; trend, TM-017's, TM-018's and CA-003's.

## What the lab can read of it, and where each form already stands

The lab holds the daily closes and traded volumes of funds and of bitcoin, and the Treasury bill's
yield; its closes are adjusted for dividends, and hold no valuations; the adjusted bars encode the
funds' distributions, which this judgement first took to be absent (a correction recorded at
AS-008), and which bear on MR-044's forms below. On those data:

- **Demand following past price moves**: investors' trades or expectations, which the lab does not
  hold. Survey expectations of returns — Greenwood and Shleifer's, cited by Shiller (appendix, note
  16) and Ilmanen (2022, section 4.2); the UBS/Gallup survey in Shefrin (2008, section 6.1.4) and
  Ilmanen (2011, section 8.5), whose one-year forecasts correlate at about 0.9 with the past year's
  return — are claimed by no other theory of the bank: they are TM-036's own evidence, and
  unreadable here. The sentiment proxies (IPO volumes, closed-end fund premiums, turnover, fund
  inflows) are **TM-037**'s; demand that does not respond to past returns, the first refutation word
  for word, is **FP-024**'s.
- **Continuation of prices after rises and falls**, the short-run momentum: read on the lab's funds
  as time-series trend by TM-017-01 and TM-047-01, across asset classes by TM-001-01, within groups
  by CA-001-01 and among sectors by TM-003-01; the momentum within the groups after their markets'
  gains is TM-006-01's (stopped at gate 2; momentum no weaker after two-year falls outside
  TM-024-01's panic states), and no source in the library gives the X-CAPM a sign conditioned on the
  market's own state. The autocorrelation over weeks is **TM-043**'s claim; over months and across
  asset classes, Bouchaud's puzzle, **TM-017**'s, read at six and twelve months by TM-017-01; the
  one-month autocorrelation, Ilmanen's +0.20 and Shleifer's 0.1, TM-017's too, still unread; the
  momentum of strategies, **CA-003**'s.
- **The longer-run reversal**: **MR-021**'s claim (the reversal of indices over one to three years,
  the recovery after extreme falls, the momentum-then-reversal sequence), which Shiller's country
  extremes and Ilmanen's −0.11 sign; within groups over five years read as value by CA-024-01; read
  through valuations, and excess volatility, **MR-044**'s (countercyclical expected returns), which
  reads dividends the lab's adjusted bars encode (the correction above); the bubble and its
  correction, **MR-023**'s.
- **Assets without valuation anchors trending more** (Ilmanen, Box 6.1): a conjecture, offered as
  "perhaps" a lesson, with no evidence, horizon or size. On the lab's funds such assets are GLD,
  SLV, DBC and bitcoin, outside the equity indices and stocks the bank names for TM-036; their
  continuation is TM-017's claim, read on GLD and DBC by TM-017-01 and within the commodity group by
  CA-001-01; bitcoin alone is one asset. Its relative claim, anchorless against anchored, would be
  judged against TM-017-01's published in-sample figures.
- **Beliefs that over-weight what is representative of recent experience**: **MR-039**'s
  (diagnostic expectations) and **TM-005**'s, recorded not testable.

**The bank's siblings.** **TM-037**, investor sentiment; **FP-024**, positive feedback trading;

**What was considered, and why it does not rescue a card of TM-036's own:**

- **An index held after its rises and left after its falls**: TM-017-01's rule on the same funds;
  the X-CAPM gives no window or threshold of its own, so a card would repeat that rule on a cause
  the rule does not read.
- **Continuation then reversal on one index**: the continuation read above, the reversal MR-021's.
- **Anchorless assets against anchored ones**: above; a conjecture, outside TM-036's classes, whose
  readable part is TM-017's and whose contrast would be judged against TM-017-01's published
  in-sample figures, a card chosen knowing them.
- **The extrapolators' share of demand, or their expectations**: surveys and flows the lab does not
  hold.
- **Excess volatility and price-dividend dynamics**: no timing sign; MR-044's, whose dividends the
  adjusted bars encode.

What would make it testable as its own: survey expectations of returns, or investors' flows, frozen
in the snapshot, against which the continuation could be conditioned.

## Status

TM-036 is recorded `not-testable` as a theory of its own: its first refutation reads investors'
demand, which the lab does not hold, and is FP-024's; its survey evidence is its own and not held;
the continuation its second names is read by TM-017-01, TM-047-01, TM-001-01, CA-001-01, TM-003-01
and TM-006-01, over weeks is TM-043's, and at one month TM-017's, unread; its longer-run reversal is
MR-021's, MR-044's and MR-023's; the one sign in the library that could be its own, Ilmanen's
anchorless assets, is a conjecture outside its classes whose readable part is TM-017's. No card is
drawn, and no trial is spent.
