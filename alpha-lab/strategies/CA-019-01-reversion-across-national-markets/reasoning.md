# CA-019-01 — Mean reversion across national stock markets: reasoning, not testable

## The theory, and the forms its sources give it

CA-019 holds that relative prices between national stock markets revert slowly: when one country
outperforms another too strongly for long, part of the move is a temporary change in risk premia,
sentiment, flows or reaction rather than a permanent divergence of fundamentals, so the long-horizon
relative losers earn more afterwards, a geographic catch-up held back by the macroeconomic,
political, currency, liquidity and governance risks arbitrageurs carry. It refutes itself if
long-horizon relative losers do not later outperform long-horizon relative winners; if divergences
prove permanent, matched by lasting divergences in fundamentals such as relative profitability; or
if losers whose underperformance came from falling multiples and outflows revert no more than those
whose fundamentals deteriorated. Its references are Balvers, Wu and Gilliland (2000) and Bhojraj and
Swaminathan (2006); the library holds neither paper, and names the first only in citations (Leung
and Li, *Optimal Mean Reversion Trading*, 2016, chapter 1, among studies of mean reversion "for US
and global equities"); it names the second in Ilmanen's notes to *Expected Returns* (2011, chapter
14), among studies of momentum "in broader contexts", and Thaler's *Advances in Behavioral Finance*
(volume II, 2005, chapter 10) cites its 2003 version as momentum in international indexes —
momentum, not reversal. What the library says of the claim gives four forms.

- **Long-horizon losers among national indices.** De Bondt, in Keim and Ziemba's *Security Market
  Imperfections in Worldwide Equity Markets* (2000), reports studies that "sort the countries
  included in the Morgan Stanley Capital International indexes on the basis of past returns,
  capitalization measures, and so on", and reports that "countries that lag the world index over
  periods of three to five years tend to outperform the index during later years". Ilmanen
  (*Expected Returns*, 2011, chapter 12) takes the "multi-year average return" as "a reasonable
  substitute" for value where no valuation is at hand, country allocation among its applications.
- **Each market's own extremes reversing.** Shiller (*Irrational Exuberance*, third edition, 2015,
  chapter 8) finds data that "confirm for countries the result first discovered by Werner De Bondt
  and Richard Thaler": of twenty-five countries after large five-year rises in real stock prices,
  seventeen fell over the next five years, and of twenty-five after large falls, twenty rose — each
  market against its own past, in real prices, at its extremes.
- **A parametric contrarian form**, as the title of Balvers, Wu and Gilliland's paper names it — the
  library gives its title only, with no construction, rule, speed or size.
- **Reversion of valuations and risk premia against fundamentals**, the theory's third refutation:
  which losers revert, by whether their fall came from multiples and flows or from profitability,
  needs countries' valuations and profitability.

## Why the lab cannot test it

**The theory ranks national markets, and the lab holds three, one fund each.** Its broad equity
funds are SPY (the United States' large caps), QQQ and IWM (two baskets inside the United States),
EFA (the developed markets outside North America, in one fund) and EEM (the emerging markets, in one
fund): the United States, the developed world outside it taken as one, and the emerging world taken
as one. The four-asset rule bounds a card's universe, not a theory; but a card holding the three
regions alone is refused, since with three assets one of them carries more than 30% of the profit
and gate 6 fails whatever the edge. Funds held at a fixed weight only to dilute the shares would
pass gate 6's measure, which reads each asset's share of the whole portfolio's profit, while adding
no decision of CA-019's, and the lab does not draw such a card (its decision of 2026-09-27, after
CA-017's reader): that is a judgement, and no code checks it. The theory names no other market the
lab holds but QQQ and IWM, which are not national markets but baskets inside the United States; with
them the group is the five equity funds that CA-001-01, CA-024-01 and TM-040-01 have ranked.

**The regions can be carried as one group in another theory's card.** The same decision says that a
universe whose other members the theory itself ranks or times is not padding, as CA-024-01 ranked
GLD, SLV and DBC as one group. The three regions ranked by their past three to five years, as one
group among others each ranked the same way, is long-horizon reversal within groups of indices:
**MR-021**'s, long-term overreaction and reversal, whose bank file says that in futures "the
reversal applies to sector or geographic indices", and CA-024-01's value within groups, run on the
five equity funds. CA-019 ranks national markets alone, and has no other group to set beside them.
The hand-off is recorded in the lab's decisions with this judgement: when MR-021 is picked, its
reasoning reads or judges the three regions ranked by their past three to five years as one group
among its others.

**The five equity funds' long-horizon reversal is published.** CA-024-01's value is the cheapest
third of each group by its past five years — De Bondt's long-horizon laggards and Ilmanen's
substitute — and its equity group is SPY, QQQ, IWM, EFA and EEM: it stopped at gate 2, the cheapest
third earning 0.63% a year less than the groups held in equal parts from 2010 to 2022, the equity
markets carrying the loss, and it set CA-019 aside on those five funds as "three overlapping US
baskets and two foreign ones, not a set of countries". Its verdict published the closest reading of
CA-019's first refutation on the lab's funds: the cheapest members of the equity group were the
foreign funds, EFA held in 97% of the months and EEM in 79%, the equity group's tilt earning −3.24%
a year (a standard error of 1.67%), 1.9 standard errors below zero, as the foreign funds "fell
further behind the US through the decade". The long-horizon laggard regions kept lagging, a contrast
between countries more than between styles. A CA-019 card on the five funds by their past three to
five years would grade that published quantity again.

**Balvers, Wu and Gilliland's form holds two or three prices.** Each region's price relative to the
others' — EFA's and EEM's against SPY's, or each against the three together — reverting towards its
mean is a rule on two or three funds, refused by the four-asset rule and, padded, the card the lab
does not draw; the library gives it no speed, window or size to lock, and each index's reversion in
its own price, without the others, is **MR-021**'s, long-term overreaction and reversal, untouched.

**The third refutation cannot be graded.** Whether losers whose fall came from multiples and flows
revert more than those whose profitability fell needs valuations and profitability by country, which
the lab does not hold; the funds' yields are not yet read point in time (the lab's decision of
2026-09-26), and would not give profitability.

**The currency the returns are measured in.** The bank's data name exchange rates; EFA's and EEM's
returns are in dollars over baskets of many currencies, and the lab holds two rates, the dollar and
the euro in francs.

**The hand-offs.** Five earlier files name CA-019: AS-010-01 handed it national markets' reversion;
CA-016-01 handed it, with CA-018, emerging against developed markets on their relative valuation;
CA-017-01 and CA-018-01 left it reversal across countries at long horizons; CA-024-01 set it aside
on the five equity funds. None hands a form the lab can hold: the timed form on two regions is
refused by the four-asset rule, and needs the price-earnings ratios Ilmanen reads, which the lab
does not hold.

**What was considered, and why it does not rescue the test:**

- **Emerging against developed markets on their relative long-horizon return**: two or three funds,
  refused before any count.
- **The laggard region held while its gap stays wide, a bond fund otherwise**: a fourth leg the
  four-asset rule would not refuse, but timed by no claim of CA-019's, so padding, and the library
  gives the rule no form.
- **A contrarian tilt held always**, the region that lagged most over the lab's first years: one
  decision.
- **Each index's own long-horizon reversal, Shiller's countries at their extremes among them**:
  **MR-021**'s, whose card reads TM-045's years form by the lab's decision.
- **Country value by valuation ratios**: **CA-018**'s, recorded not testable; **CA-021**'s across
  asset classes.

## The bank's other theories this judgement touches

- **CA-024**, value and momentum everywhere, tested inconclusive: the five equity funds' five-year
  reversal, published; **CA-001** and **TM-040**, the same group's momentum and combination.
- **CA-016**, **CA-017** and **CA-018**, recorded not testable on the same three regions.
- **MR-021**, long-term overreaction and reversal, and **CA-021**, value across sectors, countries

- **AS-010**, recorded not testable, which handed national markets' reversion here.

## Status

CA-019 is recorded `not-testable`: it ranks national markets by their long-horizon relative returns,
and the lab holds three regions, one fund each, too few to carry a card of their own; padded with
funds held at a fixed weight, a card would hide their concentration, and the lab does not draw it.
The five equity funds' five-year reversal is CA-024-01's, published: the foreign funds held as the
cheapest kept lagging the US; the parametric form, a title in the library with no rule, set on the
three regions' relative prices, holds two or three prices; the third refutation needs countries'
valuations and profitability. No card is drawn, and no trial is spent. The theory would become
testable on single-country equity funds, four at least as the four-asset rule asks, as CA-016-01
wrote, with their valuations and currencies.
