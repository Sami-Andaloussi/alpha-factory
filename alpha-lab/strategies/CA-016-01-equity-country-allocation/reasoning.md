# CA-016-01 — Equity country allocation and home bias: reasoning, not testable

## The theory, and the forms its sources give it

CA-016 holds that national equity markets are neither perfectly integrated nor valued alike: home
bias, local clienteles, regulation, accounting, coverage, sector structure and domestic flows open
persistent gaps between countries, and the markets that are relatively cheap, less crowded or
improving on the macroeconomic front outperform those that concentrate optimism, passive flows and
familiarity. It refutes itself if cheap, uncrowded or improving markets do not outperform; if the
gaps are entirely sector composition, currency or institutional breaks; or if valuation gaps close
as soon as they open. Its sources give four forms.

- **Value across countries.** Ilmanen (*Expected Returns*, 2011, chapter 12) writes that "value
  strategies analogous to value-oriented stock selection also work in equity country allocation",
  citing Asness, Moskowitz and Pedersen (2009): stock selection by price to book in four regions and
  country allocation in four asset classes, with Sharpe ratios between 0.1 and 0.9, working
  "especially well ... for equity country allocation"; where no ex ante value indicator is
  available, "multi-year average return is often a reasonable substitute". Weighting countries by
  GDP rather than market capitalization is "one way to infuse a value bias"; Arnott, Hsu and West's
  fundamental index weights countries by their firms' fundamentals (*The Fundamental Index*, 2008,
  chapter 8), one of the bank's references.
- **Yield across countries.** Ilmanen's chapter 13 counts "stock or country selection based on
  dividend yields or other valuation ratios" among the "cousins" of the carry strategy; Faber
  (*Global Asset Allocation*, 2015, Figure 46, "Rotation Strategies in Developed Markets", captioned
  as developing markets, 1976 to 2013) finds that country rotations favoured the countries with the
  highest yield, the worst trailing currency returns and the worst trailing growth. Ilmanen's §16.4
  adds that emerging markets "outperformed developed markets when starting from cheap valuations".
- **Home bias.** Ang (*Asset Management*, 2014, chapter 3, §2.6) documents that investors hold far
  less abroad than the world portfolio (US investors about 12% foreign equities against about 50%
  in the world market; the United Kingdom 30% against 92%; Japan under 10% against over 90%), and
  weighs the explanations — among them correlations that rise in global crashes, currency risk and
  transaction costs — as a puzzle for portfolio choice: a static prescription to diversify, with no
  timed rule.
- **Global markets turning together.** Murphy (*Intermarket Analysis*, 2004, chapter 15) associates
  firmer commodities with stronger emerging markets, and a weaker dollar with foreign markets'
  higher profits for American investors — an association illustrated by 2003.

## Why the lab cannot test it

**The theory's unit is the national market, and the lab holds three.** Its equity funds are SPY
(the United States' large caps), QQQ and IWM (two baskets inside the United States, the Nasdaq-100
and the small caps), EFA (the developed markets outside North America, in one fund) and EEM (the
emerging markets, in one fund): the United States, the rest of the developed world taken as one,
and the emerging world taken as one. The runbook's four-asset rule bounds a card's universe, not a
theory's, and a ranking of three inside a larger universe has been run (CA-024-01 ranked GLD, SLV
and DBC as one group). CA-016's walls are elsewhere: that the lab's three regions are one fund
each; that CA-024-01 already set country value aside on the five equity funds as "three overlapping
US baskets and two foreign ones, not a set of countries"; and that each rule the three regions can
carry is owned elsewhere, already published, or a static tilt, as below.

**The five equity funds are not five countries.** CA-001-01 and CA-024-01 ranked SPY, QQQ, IWM,
EFA and EEM as one group, because their theories — momentum and value across "sectors, countries
and asset classes", "value and momentum everywhere" — name baskets as well as countries; TM-040-01
ran value with momentum on the same five. Drawn under CA-016, that group would set QQQ's technology
and growth weight and IWM's small caps against two foreign aggregates: a gap of sector and style
composition, which the bank's second refutation names as the explanation that would refute the
theory, not as its test.

**The value form has been run on that group.** Ilmanen's substitute for value, the multi-year
return, is CA-024-01's signal: the cheapest third of each group by its past five years, the equity
markets among them, stopped at gate 2, the cheapest third earning 0.63% a year less than the groups
held in equal parts from 2010 to 2022, and the equity markets carrying the loss. A CA-016 card on
the same five funds by the same signal would grade a quantity already published.

**The valuation and yield forms have their owners.** Countries or regions ranked by valuation
ratios, their dividend yields among them, are **CA-018**'s (country value and stock–country
parallels) and **CA-021**'s (value across sectors, countries and asset classes, whose references are
this theory's Ilmanen and Asness, Moskowitz and Pedersen), both untouched, as much as this theory's;
yield as carry is **CA-025**'s; an index's own yield over time is **EF-008**'s and **MR-044**'s. The
funds' dividend yields can be recovered from the adjusted bars, and the lab's decision of
2026-09-26 requires the first theory that reads them to build a point-in-time reading, tested,
before any strategy reads them: whichever of those is picked first builds it. Among the lab's
equity funds such a rule would rank styles before countries, QQQ's technology below the rest.

**What was considered, and why it does not rescue the test:**

- **Home bias as a tilt**, the foreign funds over SPY held always: one decision, as AS-009-01's
  EEM against EFA and AS-010-01's protected markets over EEM found — short of gate 1's thirty
  whatever the prices; Ang's prescription is static.
- **Emerging against developed markets on their relative valuation** (Ilmanen, §16.4): a timed form
  on two regions, owned by **CA-018**, country value, and **CA-019**, reversion across national
  markets, and needing the price-earnings ratios Ilmanen reads, which the lab does not hold; a yield
  reading would be the valuation siblings' to build.
- **GDP weights across countries**: the lab holds no GDP, and fundamental weighting is **CA-023**'s.
- **Macroeconomic improvement**: the lab holds no country's growth, inflation or earnings; Ilmanen
  reports (§16.4, after Dimson, Marsh and Staunton) no reliable relation between a country's past
  growth and its later market returns.
- **Crowding and passive flows**: the lab holds no fund flows by country; ETF flows are
  **FP-014**'s.
- **Murphy's dollar and commodities**: the dollar against the euro can be read from the lab's two
  Swiss franc rates and commodities from DBC, and a rotation of the foreign funds against the US
  ones on the dollar would span five funds. But Murphy states an association with the dollar, not a
  lead, and a foreign-over-US rotation on the dollar would grade the currency channel the theory's
  second refutation names as its rival. Commodities into equities are **CA-028**'s; the dollar
  relations **CA-014**'s, whose reasoning's "the lab holds no currency" is corrected in the lab's
  decisions (2026-09-27): the franc rates were frozen on 2026-09-23, two days before its card, and
  CA-014 is reopened for its dollar relations.
- **Momentum across countries**: **CA-017**'s, international momentum, untouched, and
  CA-001-01's, run.
- **Reversion across countries**: **CA-019**'s, untouched, to which AS-010-01 handed national
  markets' reversion.

## The bank's other theories this judgement touches

- **CA-024**, value and momentum everywhere, **CA-001**, momentum across groups, and **TM-040**, the
  value and momentum combination, tested inconclusive: the five equity funds ranked as baskets and
  countries together.
- **CA-018**, country value; **CA-021**, value across sectors, countries and asset classes;

- **AS-009**, recorded not testable, handed "country allocation and home bias" to CA-016, and

  stocks, not portfolio home bias.
- **CA-014**, intermarket analysis, reopened for its dollar relations (in-progress), after CA-014-01
  tested inconclusive.

## Status

CA-016 is recorded `not-testable`: its unit is the national market, and the lab holds three — the
United States, the developed world outside it and the emerging world, each as one fund. The five
equity funds rank American styles and sectors against two foreign aggregates, the confound the
theory's second refutation names, and their value by past returns is CA-024-01's, published; the
valuation and yield forms are CA-018's and CA-021's, and the dollar's is the currency channel the
theory names as its rival; home bias as a tilt makes one decision. No card is drawn, and no trial is
spent. The theory would become testable on single-country equity funds, four at least, with their
valuations or their distributions read point in time.
