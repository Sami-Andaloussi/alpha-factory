# AS-012-01 — Signalling theory of dividends: reasoning, not testable as a theory of its own

## The theory, and the forms its sources give it

AS-012 holds that changes in payout carry information about the robustness of profits, management's
confidence and the sustainability of distributions, and that the market reads them as signals: a
dividend raised or kept as a sign of robust future cash flows, a cut as the opposite, mostly in
mature firms and around payout changes, with an information content that varies across regimes and
sub-universes. Its refutations: increases and cuts carrying no information about later profits and
met with no price reaction; firms with weak cash flows raising or keeping dividends as readily as
firms with strong ones, later cuts costing them nothing; price reactions fully explained by taxes,
agency or clienteles. The bank names stocks, days and months, and dividend announcements, earnings
and daily prices.

**Its sources, as the lab read them in the library.**

- **Baker, ed.** (*Dividends and Dividend Policy*, 2009, chapter 10, "Asymmetric Information and
  Signaling Theory"): announcement returns on single firms — about 3.7% at initiations (Asquith and
  Mullins, 1983); about 4% at initiations (Richardson, Sefcik and Thompson, 1986); 4.3% at
  initiations and resumptions, 1.4% at large increases, −7.7% at omissions and −9.6% at reductions,
  the bad news moving prices more (Benesh, Keown and Pinkerton, 1984); returns persisting about a
  month after changes of 25% or more except omissions (Dielman and Oppenheimer, 1984). On future
  earnings: trivial content (Watts, 1973), earnings positive around initiations (Healy and Palepu,
  1988), a link to concurrent but not future earnings (Benartzi, Michaely and Thaler, 1997),
  disputed (Nissim and Ziv, 2001; Grullon and others, 2005), and earnings recovering within one or
  two years after cuts in several studies.
- **The drift after initiations and omissions**, on single stocks: market-adjusted returns of 7.5%
  one year and 24.8% three years after initiations, −11% one year after omissions (Michaely, Thaler
  and Womack, 1995, in Cohen, Lys and Zach, chapter 6 of Zacks, ed., *The Handbook of Equity Market
  Anomalies*, 2011); stocks raising their dividends beating their benchmark by 4.5% the next year,
  initiators by 9.2%, while cuts of more than half trailed it by 3.6% (O'Shaughnessy, *What Works on
  Wall Street*, fourth edition, chapter 11).
- **Tirole** (*The Theory of Corporate Finance*, 2006, chapter 6, "Corporate Financing under
  Asymmetric Information", and chapter 7, section 7.2.2.3, "Income and Dividend Smoothing"): payout
  as a costly signal under adverse selection, prices rising at increases and falling at cuts in
  single firms, Lintner's smoothing — nothing for funds or indices.
- **Poterba and Summers** (1988, in Lo, ed., *Market Efficiency*, 1997, section 5): monthly
  innovations of the value-weighted NYSE portfolio's real dividends, 1926 to 1985, followed if
  anything by lower returns, negative and insignificant — which they read as required returns
  falling after good dividend news, not as a signal.

## What the lab can read of it

The lab holds the daily bars and volumes of twenty-two funds and bitcoin — the funds from 2005 or
their later start, bitcoin from September 2014 — adjusted for dividends and splits, which encode the
funds' distributions, though no strategy reads them until a point-in-time reading with its own test
is built; the Treasury bill's yield from 2005 and two franc exchange rates; the funds' prices, never
their holdings' prices; and no single stock, dividend announcement or earnings; its snapshot is
closed, an asset or a series being added only before the first card. On those data:

- **The price reaction to a firm's dividend change**, the claim and every refutation: single firms'
  announcements, dates and earnings, not held.
- **The drift after initiations and omissions**, which TM-007-01 recorded that no other theory
  claims: this theory's, and on single firms' event dates the lab does not hold.
- **An index's returns after its aggregate dividend news** (Poterba and Summers): the one
  index-level test in the library, readable in principle from the equity funds' distributions; but
  its sign is insignificant, it reads required returns rather than a management's signal, and it is
  not this theory's claim, which is the price's reaction when a signal arrives. It belongs with
  **MR-044**, countercyclical expected returns, which must build the reading of the funds'
  distributions before any theory reads them; a recorded decision binds MR-044's pick to read or
  judge it and report it. A fund's distribution also sums its holdings' dividends on the fund's own
  calendar, and is not a management's choice.
- **Payout as value, agency or clientele**: **EF-030**'s, **EF-029**'s, AS-011's and AS-008's; the
  payout ratio predicting earnings growth was set aside in AS-011-01; the maturity reading of
  dividend changes (Grullon and others) is **AS-013**'s.

**The bank's siblings.** **MR-044**, countercyclical expected returns, with the aggregate dividend
forms; **CA-025**, carry, and **EF-008**, value, which may read the funds' yields; **AS-013**, the
life cycle of payout; **EF-029**, repurchases; **EF-030**, net payout yield; **EF-035**, the
earnings drift; all untouched; **AS-008** and **AS-011**, recorded not testable; **TM-007**,
underreaction to public information, recorded not testable.

What would make it testable: single firms' dividend announcements with their dates and earnings —
outside the lab's funds.

## Status

AS-012 is recorded `not-testable`: its claim, its refutations and its drift read single firms'
dividend changes, announcement dates and earnings, which the lab does not hold; the library's one
index-level test of dividend news, Poterba and Summers', is insignificant, reads required returns
rather than signals, and is left to MR-044 by a recorded decision. No card is drawn, and no trial is
spent.
