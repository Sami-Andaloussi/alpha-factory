# CA-013-01 — Sector rotation across the business cycle: reasoning, not testable

## The theory, and the form its sources give it

CA-013 holds that the real economy, not money, shifts leadership between sectors: through recovery,
expansion, slowdown and recession, the composition of demand, investment, margins and bankruptcies
changes, so that sectors tied to investment, energy, materials and discretionary spending fare
differently from utilities, health care and consumer staples; and that the market adjusts to the
phases with a delay. Its phases are the economy's: the bank lists business cycle dates and
macroeconomic releases among its data, and its references read them so — Jacobsen, Stangl and
Visaltanachoti (2009) rotate sectors on the cycle's dates, as the bank's data and its "perfect
dating" put it, and Hamilton (1989) infers expansion and contraction from the growth of output. None
of the three is in the library; Hamilton's textbook (1994, chapter 22, "Modeling Time Series with
Changes in Regime") restates his model: a two-state Markov chain fitted to quarterly US real GNP
growth from 1952 to 1984, whose contraction state lasts about four quarters and whose inferred
phases lie close to the Bureau's dates.

The library describes the rotation on the same phases. Tvede (*Business Cycles*, 2006, chapter 27)
orders the sectors through the cycle — financials first, then consumer discretionary and
information technology, industrials, resources, and consumer staples and utilities at the end — and
finds that equities turn up on average some five months before the economic trough, and lead by
longer at peaks. Gliner (*Global Macro Trading*, 2014, chapter 8) asks the trader to know whether
the economy is in contraction, recovery, expansion or slowing growth, and to hold staples in
recession and industrials in growth. Ang (*Asset Management*, 2014, chapter 7, Table 7.2) reports,
over 1952 to 2011, large stocks returning 5.6% a year in the Bureau's recessions against 12.4% in
expansions, and government bonds 12.3% against 5.9%: assets differ in their sensitivity to the
cycle, as the theory's first proposition says.

## Why the lab cannot test it

**The lab holds no reading of the economy.** Its data are the daily closes and volumes of 22 funds
and bitcoin, the Treasury bill's yield and two exchange rates, frozen in dated snapshots. No output,
production, employment or purchasing-managers series, no leading indicator and no business cycle
date is among them, and a frozen snapshot does not change. Every phase CA-013 names is a state of
the economy, and none can be read from what the lab holds.

**Declaring the dates in the card would not rescue the test.** Written into a card by hand, the
dates of the National Bureau of Economic Research would be data with no hash, which the rule of a
frozen snapshot exists to prevent (SC-011-01, SC-020-01). And even were they held, they would not
give a test. Hamilton notes that they are set informally, from many series, "usually made some time
after the event"; Ilmanen (*Expected Returns*, 2011, chapter 26) that the Bureau dates the turning
points "often after a significant delay". The in-sample years, 2005 to 2022, hold two
recessions and four turning points: a peak in December 2007 and a trough in June 2009; a peak in
February 2020 and a trough in April 2020. The holdout, 2023 to 2025, holds none. Two readings are
open, and both fail before any run:

- **The dates as they were announced**, the only form that reads nothing of the future. By the
  Bureau's own announcements, which the library does not hold, the peak of 2007 was announced in
  December 2008, the trough of 2009 in September 2010, the peak of 2020 in June 2020 — after its
  trough — and the trough of 2020 in July 2021. A rotation on them changes its holdings four times
  in eighteen years: five decisions counting its first, seven at most if XLRE and XLC, which start
  in 2016 and 2018, joined a group, where gate 1 requires 30 before any figure is believed. Its
  Sharpe ratio, placebos and alpha by block would be decided by which sectors happened to lead in
  two recessions against three expansions: five draws, as CA-012's four policy shifts gave five.
  It would also hold the recession's sectors late: fifteen of its twenty-one months of recession
  holdings in 2008–10, and all of them in 2020–21, came after the trough.
- **The dates as known today**, the form of the bank's second refutation ("even when it is dated
  perfectly"). A signal that knows in December 2007 that a recession has begun reads the future,
  which gate 1 exists to refuse; the lab does not run a rule it knows to look ahead, and the same
  four turning points would give it the same five decisions.

**The four phases need more than dates.** Recovery, expansion, slowdown and recession split each
expansion by the pace of growth, which only a growth series can tell; the Bureau dates recessions
and expansions alone. Within the lab's years, the phases would be cut from two recessions and three
expansions, the first of them begun before the data.

**What the refutations read.** The first refutation compares the relative returns of the cyclical
and defensive sectors between the phases; with the phases dated after the fact, it is a
description of two recessions, not a rule, and the lab's battery judges rules. The second reads
the perfect dating the lab refuses. Neither can be graded on what the lab holds.

**What was considered, and why it does not rescue the test:**

- **The markets' own reading of the cycle** — the trends of bonds, stocks and commodities that
  Pring's six stages read, as Murphy gives them, or the commodity/bond ratio — is testable, and has
  been tested as other theories' forms: CA-011-01 reads Pring's stages from the 200-session trends
  of IEF, SPY and DBC and holds the sectors named for each, and stopped at gate 3; CA-014-01 rotated
  the sector funds on the commodity/bond ratio, and stopped at gate 3; both theories are recorded
  tested inconclusive. Tvede's own lead of about five months at the trough says why the two are
  different theories: the markets turn before the economy, and a rotation on prices times the
  market's anticipation, which the bank's second refutation says may be complete, not the economy's
  phase. Drawing that form again under CA-013 would spend a second trial on CA-011's question.
- **A regime inferred from the lab's own returns** by Hamilton's switching model: its states would
  be the market's, not the economy's; the method is MR-043's, Markov switching and
  regime-conditional reversion, which shares that method and Hamilton's paper with CA-013, not the
  economy's phases.
- **A reading of the economy itself** — Gliner's four phases read from the ISM purchasing managers'
  index, above or below 50 and rising or falling (his chapter 8 finds its slope changes and its
  crossings of 50 "provide strong signals", and his chapter 12 grades it by direction and rate of
  change), or a composite of activity such as Ilmanen's (chapter 26) — changes monthly, would give
  enough decisions, and, read from its release, looks nothing ahead. It is CA-013's own testable
  form. The lab holds no such series, a series copied into a card by hand is refused (SC-011-01),
  and adding one to the lab's data is not a card's decision. Leading indicators and output, unlike
  the purchasing managers' index, are revised after the fact, so that a test on them would need
  their values as first published.
- **The yield curve and the short rate**, which Tvede, Murphy and Ilmanen tie to the phases: the lab
  holds the bill's yield, not a long yield, and the curve's turns in the lab's years are few. The
  bill's own direction, which Murphy's chapter 13 has rising in recoveries and falling in
  contractions, is CA-012's reading, recorded not testable on four policy shifts; its reasoning
  rejected the bill's shorter moves as noise around the same five turns.
- **Countries or bitcoin in place of sectors**, as the bank's asset classes allow: the lab holds no
  country's cycle dates; bitcoin, traded from 2014, has lived through one recession of two months,
  and a rotation between bitcoin and two or three equity funds would also break the rule of at
  least four assets.
- **A longer history.** The lab's snapshot starts in 2005; Ang's evidence rests on sixty years of
  the Bureau's dates, Hamilton's on thirty-two of output growth, neither of which the lab holds.

## The bank's other theories this judgement touches

- **CA-011**, sector rotation, tested inconclusive: the same sectors, the phase read from the
  markets; CA-011-01's reasoning handed CA-013 the economy's dates, which the lab does not hold.
- **CA-012**, monetary sector rotation, recorded not testable on four policy shifts: the same
  arithmetic, the monetary channel.
- **CA-014**, intermarket analysis, tested inconclusive: the commodity/bond ratio.
- **CA-028**, commodity-to-equity transmission and oil-shock sector rotation, untouched: one channel
  of the cycle.
- **MR-043**, Markov switching and regime-conditional reversion, and **MR-044**, countercyclical
  expected returns, untouched.
- **TM-002-01** published each sector's hedged alpha and beta over the sector funds in equal parts,
  2006 to 2022 — staples 0.61, utilities 0.73, health care 0.74, materials 1.14, energy 1.29,
  financials 1.40: the sectors differ in their sensitivity to the market, which the theory's first
  proposition expects, though the market is not the cycle.
- **AS-008**, recorded not testable, handed "the sectors' differences across rates and the cycle"
  jointly to CA-011, CA-012 and CA-013: on the cycle, that is this theory's first refutation, which
  cannot be read without the economy's phases. **AS-011**, **AS-013**, **AS-014** and **AS-016** to
  **AS-020**, all recorded not testable, named CA-013 among their siblings, the sector funds read on
  time-varying signals, and handed it no form.

## Status

CA-013 is recorded `not-testable`: its phases are the economy's, which the lab does not read. Its
own testable form, a monthly reading of activity such as the purchasing managers' index, needs a
series the lab does not hold and a card may not copy in by hand; the Bureau's dates, even were they
held, would change its holdings four times in the in-sample years, far too few decisions for the
battery to judge, and read perfectly, as the bank's second refutation asks, they look ahead. No
card is drawn, and no trial is spent. The markets' reading of the cycle is CA-011's and CA-014's,
both tested. The theory would become testable on a monthly reading of the economy as first
published, frozen in the lab's data, over the lab's own years, or on decades of sector returns with
the economy's phases as known at the time.
