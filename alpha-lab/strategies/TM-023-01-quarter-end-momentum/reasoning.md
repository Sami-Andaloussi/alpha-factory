# TM-023-01 — Momentum in the months that end a quarter: reasoning

## Why this theory now

TM-023 is next in the trend-and-momentum family, taken in number order. It holds that momentum is
not uniform over the calendar: tax-loss selling before the year's end and fund managers dressing
their reports before quarter-ends and the year-end push the period's winners up and its losers down,
so that momentum is reinforced in the months that end a quarter and weakened or reversed in January,
when the pressure lifts and the losers rebound. Its refutations: momentum returns in January no
different from the other months, and no rebound of past losers at the turn of the year; no
reinforcement of winners before quarter-ends and the year-end; seasonal patterns that persist in
markets or periods without tax incentives or reporting dates; commodity momentum unaffected by
production and consumption cycles.

Four judgements of the lab have handed this theory its forms on prices: TM-012-01 (funds buying
winners before their reporting dates, Sias's quarter-end momentum and Ilmanen's December), TM-015-01
(institutional rebalancing at quarter-ends and the year-end), TM-016-01 (the overhang's December
strength and January reversal) and TM-001-01's verdict, which reported its January gap between asset
classes "in TM-023's direction, on too few months to read" and left it here.

**Its sources, as the lab read them in the library's books.**

- **Jegadeesh and Titman**, "Momentum", chapter 10 of *Advances in Behavioral Finance, Volume II*
  (Thaler, ed., 2005), table 10.3: their six-month/six-month momentum on NYSE, AMEX and NASDAQ
  stocks, the smallest decile left out, loses 1.55% a month in January and earns 1.48% a month
  outside it, positive in every calendar month but January, after Jegadeesh and Titman (2001); their
  1993 study, the smallest firms included, lost 6.86% in January. "The momentum effect is entirely a
  non-January phenomenon." Table 10.6 reproduces Grundy and Martin (2001) on **industries**: twenty
  industries of NYSE and AMEX stocks from July 1963 to July 1995, the top three by their past six
  months held against the bottom three, earn 0.47% a month value-weighted, −0.34% in January (t
  −0.38) and 0.55% outside it (t 2.57); equal-weighted 0.78%, −0.42% (t −0.49) and 0.89% (t 4.92);
  with a month skipped between ranking and holding, 0.16% value-weighted, −0.90% in January and
  0.26% outside it.
- **Gray and Vogel**, *Quantitative Momentum* (2016, chapter 7, "Momentum Investors Need to Know
  Their Seasons"): Sias (2007), long the decile of stocks with the strongest past six months and
  short the weakest, from 1984 to 2004, earns 0.45% a month in all months, 1.50% outside January,
  3.10% in the months that end a quarter against 0.59% in the other months outside January, 5.52% in
  December, and more where institutions trade most; Sias: investors exploiting momentum "should
  focus their efforts on quarter-ending months". Their own replication on mid- and large-cap US
  stocks from 1927 to 2014 (table 7.1), value-weighted momentum deciles, gives the spread −1.72% a
  month in January, 1.73% in March, 1.55% in June, 1.44% in September and 2.76% in December, against
  −0.62% to 1.89% in the other months — about 1.9% a month in the months that end a quarter against
  0.7% in the others outside January. A momentum portfolio rebalanced quarterly before the months
  that end a quarter (at the close of February, May, August and November) compounded 15.97% a year
  against 15.49% for one rebalanced every month and held three, and 15.06% for one rebalanced at the
  close of the months that end a quarter (table 7.2): "at the margin". They read window dressing
  before quarter-ends and tax selling before the year's end as the causes.
- **Ilmanen**, *Expected Returns* (2011): momentum and trend-following strategies "tend to
  outperform in December and underperform (reverse) in January" (§25.2), against most risk-seeking
  strategies, strongest in January; his active commodity momentum strategies earned 1.6% on average
  in December against 0.4% in January (chapter 14); institutions buy "recently-outperforming assets
  in November and December" to show them at the year's end.
- **Shefrin** (*A Behavioral Approach to Asset Pricing*, 2008, chapter 29) reads the turn of the
  year through the disposition effect and tax-loss selling in December; **Fabozzi, Füss and Kaiser**
  (*The Handbook of Commodity Investing*, 2008, chapter 3) report seasonality in the basis of
  agricultural commodities and none in that of metals (Fama and French), and find strong seasonality
  in their own seasonal allocations, silver and copper among them: seasons of the assets' returns,
  not of momentum.

**The form this card tests.** The library signs both halves of TM-023 on cross-sectional momentum,
among stocks and among industries: stronger in the months that end a quarter (Sias, Gray and Vogel,
on stocks; Ilmanen's December, among asset classes and commodities), weaker in January (Jegadeesh
and Titman on stocks, Grundy and Martin on industries, Ilmanen among commodities and trend
strategies). The lab's reading of cross-sectional momentum among its funds is CA-001-01's: within
each of three groups — the eleven sector funds, the five equity markets, the three commodity funds —
the top third by the past year's return, the last month left out, which is Gray and Vogel's ranking,
twelve months less the last. The card keeps that ranking unchanged and grades the leaders' gap to
their group by calendar month: the calendar is the only change.

**What the lab already knows, and is not blind to** — estimates, not the sample's figures:

- **TM-001-01**, between asset classes (SPY, EFA, EEM, IEF, TLT, GLD, DBC, the two best by the past
  year): its gap to the seven in equal parts was −1.84% a year in January (7.24%, 16 months) against
  +2.09% (3.37%) in the other months. That is a different ranking — across classes, not within
  groups — and a different gap, and it is not part of this card's graded sample; but it leans the
  January half's way on the same years, and the card was drawn knowing it.
- **SC-006-01**, on the sector funds: the three nearest their year's high, less the other sector
  funds and adjusted for their beta, over December's last sessions but one, −0.03% a year (0.14
  standard errors from zero); over January's first five sessions −0.40% (0.50%), reported. The
  ranking differs (nearness to the year's high, not the past year's return) but the quantity — the
  sector winners less the other sector funds — is this card's on a part of the same months. The
  sector group's December and January group-months are therefore left out of the graded clause and
  reported apart.
- **SC-002-01** published IWM less SPY and IWM less QQQ over late December and to January's tenth
  session, and **SC-007-01** each equity fund's return over December's last five and January's first
  two sessions (EEM and EFA the strongest): returns of named funds, not the gap of the leaders
  ranked by their past year, which those verdicts do not identify. **SC-019-01** (the January
  barometer) and **SC-023-01** (assets ranked across the universe on their past residual returns in
  the same calendar month) read the calendar on the same funds and years, not the momentum gap;
  **SC-009-01** found the months that end a quarter no weaker before their end, for the market, not
  for momentum. No verdict has read momentum by calendar month within the groups.
- **CA-001-01**'s leaders, this ranking, earned an alpha of 0.20% a year over the groups in equal
  parts, a tenth of its prediction; its variant without the skip a third. TM-006-01 found the gap no
  weaker after the market's losses (+0.19% a year, standard error 5.08% over 84 group-months against
  447), and in TM-024-01's 41 panic group-months −6.86% a year against the others (7.17%); TM-024-01
  published its base's episodes, among them −1.4 points in January and February 2009, TM-039-01 the
  gap in its dispersed months, and CA-001-01 its funds' returns from March to December 2009. These
  are published by state, not by calendar month, and their states fall across the three sets of the
  clause, so they stay in the graded sample: RUNBOOK step 3 leaves out a part whose outcome a
  verdict has published on the graded quantity (TM-006-01 left out the panic months because they
  were part of its own graded contrast); these states' gaps are published pooled, not by calendar
  month, and fall across the three sets, so they stay — as do TM-006-01's down set and TM-039-01's
  dispersed months, for the same reason. The clause without TM-024-01's panic group-months is
  reported. The audit of this card counted their calendar split before the lock — 11 in the months
  that end a quarter, 1 January, 27 others, of 39 in the graded sample — from the months TM-024-01's
  verdict lists; no return was read.

**The form was chosen knowing these results.** TM-001-01's January, SC-006-01's December and
January, CA-001-01's weak edge and the states above, on the same years; the card grades a quantity
no verdict has published by calendar month, and leaves out the part of it SC-006-01 has.

**The bank's siblings.** **FP-018**, window dressing, untouched, whose pressure on recent winners
builds "over several days or weeks" before a reporting date and reverses after it: TM-012-01 and
TM-015-01 gave Sias's quarter-end momentum to both TM-023 and FP-018, and this card spends that
shared bet — a later FP-018 card discloses this result and leaves its months out; **FP-023**,
mandated rebalancing of balanced and target-date funds, untouched, which trades against the
preceding move at quarter-ends and would fit a quarter-end half refuted; **FP-026**, tax-loss
selling, the losers' fall before the year's end and rebound in January, untouched; **SC-006**,
December strength in the year's winners, read by SC-006-01; **SC-002**, the turn of the year, read
by SC-002-01; **SC-012** and **SC-013**, rebalancing windows, recorded not testable; **TM-012**,
**TM-015** and **TM-016**, recorded not testable, which handed their calendar forms here;
**CA-001**, momentum within groups, read by CA-001-01.

## From the claim to a signal

- **The momentum**: CA-001-01's, unchanged — within each group, the funds ranked by their return
  from `lookback` to `skip` sessions before the session before the target, 252 and 21, ranked when
  trading on the target's session and on the window's first session; the top third of each group's
  ranked funds, rounded half to even, one at least, share the group's n/N; a fund trading but not
  yet ranked holds 1/N. The tilt is those weights less the group in equal parts, 1/N for each fund
  trading.
- **The calendar**: a target month *ends a quarter* when it is March, June, September or December.
  The rule trades at the close of each month's first session, so its month runs to the next month's
  first session's close and carries that session — January's first session in the December month,
  each quarter's first session in the month that ends the one before, the sessions on which the turn
  of the year and the end of dressing reverse — which works against both halves. The clause
  therefore grades the calendar month the sources measure, from the close of the session before its
  first session to the close of its last, the leaders ranked for the month's target, which reads
  nothing beyond the session before; the rule's months are reported. The rule holds each group in
  equal parts plus k times its tilt, k = 1 in the months that end a quarter and 0 in the others: the
  tilt taken where the library puts momentum's profits, and left where it puts little or a loss,
  January among them. Targets on the first session of each month on which a fund is ranked, fully
  invested, read up to the session before.
- **The variant**: k = 0 in January only, 1 in every other month — Jegadeesh and Titman's "entirely
  a non-January phenomenon", and the half of the theory the library signs among industries.
- **Parameters**: `lookback` and `skip`, CA-001-01's, with its neighbours for gate 6 (189 and 315,
  126 and 378; 16 and 26, 10 and 32). The months are a text parameter naming the variant, with no
  neighbours: the sources fix them.
- **Memory**: 252 sessions, the ranking's window.

Gray and Vogel's own rule, a portfolio rebalanced quarterly at the close of February, May, August
and November and held three months, is set aside as the base: it holds the tilt in January too, and
differs from CA-001-01 only in its rebalancing dates, a difference they measured at half a point a
year on stocks.

## What the battery judges, and what to expect

**The size predicted — a judgment.** On stocks, the months that end a quarter beat the other months
outside January by about 1.2 (Gray and Vogel, 1927–2014) to 2.5 (Sias, 1984–2004) points a month in
a long-short decile spread; among industries the library gives January, not the quarter-ends: about
0.9 point a month below the other months, value-weighted, top three of twenty against the bottom
three. The industries' January contrast is against every other month, the quarter-ends included;
against the other months outside them, as the clause reads it, it would be smaller under the theory.
CA-001-01's long-only top third against its group takes about half of a long-short spread, among a
few funds whose momentum was a tenth of its prediction in this ranking and a third without the skip.
The card judges the leaders' gap to their group's ranked funds about +0.5 to +5% a year higher in
the months that end a quarter than in the other months outside January, and about −0.5 to −6% a year
lower in January, since a seasonal contrast need not scale with the average edge. November, when
Ilmanen has institutions buying winners, and October, the funds' tax year, stay among the other
months, as in Sias; they dilute the contrast. For the rule, which drops the tilt in two months of
three, CA-001-01's weak edge leaves an alpha over the groups in equal parts of about −0.3 to +0.6% a
year, and a Sharpe ratio close to theirs.

**The gates.** The rule holds the groups in equal parts in two months of three, and the card expects
it to fail gate 2, gate 3 (CA-001-01 beat 47.6% of its placebos), gate 4 (an appraisal ratio of
about 0.63 or more is expected by luck from the best of the lab's effective trials) and gate 5 with
them. Gate 4's clusters may count it with CA-001-01, TM-024-01 and TM-006-01.

**The clause.** Judged before costs, on the market's closes, over the in-sample calendar months from
February 2006, the month of the first target on which a fund is ranked, to December 2022, each
group-month from the close of the session before the month's first session to the close of its last
session, the last ending at the close of 2022-12-30, over the group-months with two ranked funds or
more, the sector group's December and January group-months left out: the return of the group's
leaders — CA-001-01's top third of the group's funds ranked for the month's target, bought in equal
parts at the close of the session before the month's first session and held — less the return of all
the group's ranked funds bought and held the same way. An ordinary least-squares regression of the
gaps on two indicators, the months that end a quarter and January, and one constant for each group,
the standard errors clustered by month, the groups' gaps in each of the 203 months one cluster,
since they move together; the coefficients' t statistics computed apart from the battery. The
quarter-end half is refuted in this form if its t statistic is −0.35 or below: momentum no stronger
in the months that end a quarter than in the other months outside January. The January half is
refuted in this form if its t statistic is +0.35 or above: momentum no weaker in January. Between,
short of passing gates 1 to 7, each half is not proven, not refuted; a base that passes gates 1 to 7
while a half refutes leaves that half refuted in this form.

**The test's power.** CA-001-01's first holding is on 2006-02-01, 203 months to December 2022; the
commodity group has two ranked funds only from March 2007, a year after DBC began trading: 596
group-months, and without the sector group's 17 Decembers and 16 Januarys 563 — 183 in the months
that end a quarter, 31 Januarys (16 of the equity markets, 15 of the commodities) and 349 others,
counted from the calendar and the funds' first sessions, which read no return. TM-006-01 published a
standard error of 5.08% a year on 84 group-months against 447, clustered by month; scaled by the
square roots of the counts, that gives about 3.9% a year on the quarter-end coefficient and about 8%
on January's, more if the clustering bites harder; TM-006-01's down set was mostly the commodities'
noisy group-months, which makes these errors, if anything, a little large. January's coefficient
rests on 16 clusters, too few for a clustered error to be trusted closely. At those errors the
quarter-end half refutes about 36% of the time with no effect and about 15% at +2.75% a year, the
middle of its range; the January half about 36% with no effect and about 22% at −3.25% a year. The
test can barely tell the quarter-end prediction from none, and cannot tell the January prediction
from none.

**TM-023's refutations, and what the card can grade.**

1. *January momentum no different from the other months, and no rebound of past losers at the turn
   of the year*: its first part graded by the January half, against the other months outside the
   quarter-ends, and reported against every other month; the losers' rebound is left to FP-026,
   tax-loss selling, whose graded quantity it is, so as not to publish it here on the same funds and
   sessions.
2. *No reinforcement of winners before quarter-ends and the year-end*: graded by the quarter-end
   half.
3. *Seasonal patterns that persist without tax incentives or reporting dates*: every market the lab
   trades has both; the patterns cannot be read apart from them.
4. *Commodity momentum unaffected by production and consumption cycles*: the library signs
   production cycles on single commodities' bases and returns, agricultural ones above all; the
   lab's commodities are gold, silver and a broad basket whose agricultural part cannot be read
   apart, and a cycle of the assets' returns is not a cycle of momentum.

**Measures stated before the run**, reported, not graded:

- the sector group's December and January group-months, apart, and their gap against the others;
- the same regression on the rule's months, from the close of each month's first session to the
  close of the next month's first session;
- the same regression without TM-024-01's panic group-months;
- January against every other month, one indicator and the group constants;
- the gap by month of the year, pooled and by group, and the number of group-months in each;
- December alone against the other months that end a quarter, over the graded group-months (the
  equity markets and the commodities);
- the same regression with the gaps hedged of their group's market, one beta for each set, to tell
  leaders that are defensive in a falling month from a momentum that is weaker;
- the rule and its variant against CA-001-01, by `lab.compare`: the alpha's difference and its
  standard error on the monthly differences;
- the clause over the months of 2006 to 2013, over those of 2014 to 2022, over the in-sample months
  without 2008 and 2009, and over the holdout, 2023 to 2025;
- the alpha before and after costs, the neighbours' and the one-day-late rule's alphas and appraisal
  ratios.

## What was considered, and why each is set aside

- **Recording TM-023 not testable**: its data list names institutional holdings, a tax calendar and
  funds' reporting dates, but the library signs its prediction on prices alone, and the calendar of
  quarter-ends and of the year's end needs no data the lab does not hold.
- **Between asset classes**, TM-001-01's leaders by calendar month: its January split is published;
  the card would be chosen from it.
- **Time-series trend by calendar month** (Ilmanen's December and January for trend followers):
  TM-017-01's rule, whose months no verdict has split; the library's figures for it are a
  strategy's, not a fund's, and the cross-sectional form carries both of the sources' halves. A
  trend card by month would be a second trial on the same claim; it is left for a later strategy of
  this theory if this one's result calls for it.
- **Grading every group-month**: the sector group's December and January, a part SC-006-01 has
  published for a related ranking, would enter the clause; they are reported apart.
- **Gray and Vogel's quarterly rebalancing as the base**: above.
- **A six-month ranking**, Sias's and Jegadeesh and Titman's: CA-001-01's twelve months less the
  last is Gray and Vogel's, whose table 7.1 carries the same seasonal pattern; gate 6 moves the
  window to six and eighteen months.
- **The losers' January rebound**: FP-026's graded quantity, left to it.
- **The pressure before a reporting date, in its days**: FP-018's form of the last days and of the
  reversal after the date; the monthly form this card grades is shared with it (above).
- **Treasuries**: the lab holds SHY, IEF and TLT, three maturities of one issuer, whose ranking by
  the past year orders duration; the library gives momentum among them no seasonal sign (Ilmanen's
  autumn for government bonds is a season of their return, SC-023's kind), and CA-001-01 formed no
  Treasury group. **Currencies**: the lab trades none.
- **Commodity seasonality from production cycles**: agricultural, which the lab does not hold as
  single funds (above).
- **Crypto-assets**: the bank does not name them for TM-023, and a card of one asset is not drawn.

## Implementation

Long only, the nineteen funds in their groups, with European (UCITS) funds that track them; one
decision a month, the signal read up to the session before and traded at the close, a day's delay;
the rule departs from the groups in equal parts only in the months that end a quarter.
