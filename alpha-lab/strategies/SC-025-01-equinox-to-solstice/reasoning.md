# SC-025-01 — Out of the northern equity funds from the equinox to the solstice: reasoning

## Why this theory now

SC-025, the seasonal affective disorder effect, links the seasonality of returns to the season's
light: where the length of the day varies strongly over the year, enough investors become more
risk-averse as the days shorten in autumn and winter to raise the premia they require and weigh on
prices. Its signature is a difference between the hemispheres, and its refutations: no seasonal
pattern related to the length of the day; the same timing in both hemispheres; an effect no stronger
where the day's length varies more; a pattern accounted for by the other seasonal forces of the
calendar; no seasonal variation in investors' own risk aversion, or a pattern that does not survive
outside its first samples.

**Its sources, as the lab read them in the library's books.** Kamstra, Kramer and Levi's paper, and
Kramer and Weber's, Garrett, Kamstra and Kramer's and Hirshleifer, Jiang and Meng DiGiovanni's, are
not in the library; what it holds of them is second-hand, and gives no size, no window and no
southern contrast.

- **Dzhabarov and Ziemba**, in the Zacks *Handbook of Equity Market Anomalies* (2011, chapter 9) and
  in Ziemba's *Calendar Anomalies and Arbitrage* (2012, §1.4), within their account of Sell in May:
  Kamstra, Kramer and Levi (2003) and Garrett, Kamstra and Kramer (2004) link "the shorter,
  relatively sunless days of fall and winter" to depression and to an unwillingness to take risk;
  Kamstra concludes that the explanation "does not lead to a profitable trading strategy" because
  the risk premium varies with the seasons; and Doeswijk finds it insufficient, SAD starting as
  early as September, so that it cannot explain the historically high November returns. The same
  pages report Bouman and Jacobsen finding Sell in May in 36 of 37 countries, their
  southern-hemisphere subset among them.
- **Harris** (*Trading and Exchanges*, 2002, "Why October?") gives a SAD-type account of October's
  weakness and volatility — people notice the approach of winter and the loss of daylight most in
  October, and the darkness makes them more risk-averse, which "may cause prices to fall in
  October" — and notes that its prediction for southern markets, in April, would probably be tested
  inconclusively, northern and southern markets moving together through northern ownership.
- **Lo** (*Adaptive Markets*, 2017, chapter 4) cites the paper for "the lack of sunlight during
  winter months tends to depress stock market prices"; Tulchinsky and others (*Finding Alphas*,
  2020) report that returns "vary seasonally with the length of the day", with no figure.
- **Ilmanen** (*Expected Returns*, 2011): in §25.2, Sell in May's months out cover "the autumn gloom
  (sometimes linked to seasonal affective disorder, SAD)"; in his section on behavioural finance,
  some calendar seasonalities appear consistent with it, most such mood patterns are weak, likely
  partly data-mined, and too small to exploit after costs. His Figure 25.1, the US equity premium by
  calendar month from 1927 to 2009, as the lab read it by eye for SC-017-01, in percent a month:
  January 1.3, February 0.15, March 0.35, April 1.0, May 0.35, June 0.65, July 1.15, August 1.1,
  September −1.15, October −0.2, November 1.2, December 1.45.
- **Chincarini and Kim** (*Quantitative Equity Portfolio Management*) list Kamstra and others among
  the calendar effects of the literature, without figures.

**The season the card takes, and how it was chosen.** The bank's mechanism has risk aversion rise
"as the days shorten" and the premia it raises "weigh on prices": read as a risk premium that varies
with the seasons, as the books report Kamstra, the lab reads its timing so: prices fall while the
premium rises, up to the longest night, and once it is high the returns that follow are high, not
low. Harris places the effect in October, the month the loss of daylight is most felt; Ilmanen's
"autumn gloom" and Doeswijk's reading, a SAD that starts in September, place it in the autumn too.
The books' "fall and winter" describe the mood, and Lo's winter the price level, not low returns
through the winter. The card therefore takes the autumn, from the September equinox to the December
solstice, the days shortening with the night longer than the day. Its drafting went back and forth,
and the lab states it: a first reading took the autumn; a second, reading the sources' "fall and
winter" as a sign for both seasons, moved it to the half year from equinox to equinox; a third,
reading the risk premium's timing and Harris's October, found the autumn the better reading, and the
card returned to it. The lab's published figures (below) put the autumn form's in-sample gap on the
refutation's side of the line and the half year's near zero: the card returns to the form those
figures lean against, and reports the other.

**What the lab already knows, and is not blind to.** SC-017-01 published the five equity funds'
average excess return by calendar month from 2005 to 2022, in percent a month: January −0.34,
February 0.20, March 1.31, April 2.26, May 0.05, June −0.26, July 2.41, August −0.23, September
−0.41, October 0.93, November 1.64, December 1.13. Weighted by the days of each month in the window,
22 September to 20 December, they average about +1.08% a month, against about +0.61 over the rest of
the year: a difference of about +0.022% a day, which for five funds, at the standard error below,
is a t statistic of about +0.43 to +0.50, past the clause's +0.35. That is this sample's own figure,
not a prediction: the in-sample clause is largely known before the run, likely to refute, and only
leaving EEM out, the exact days and the standard error can move it. The same table puts the half
year from equinox to equinox at about +0.72 against +0.73, and the winter alone, 21 December to 20
March, at about +0.37 against +0.73 for the spring and summer; Ilmanen's long record puts the winter
at about +0.73 against +0.59. SC-018-01 published the November-to-April half against the other;
SC-016-01 the weeks from Rosh Hashanah to Yom Kippur, which overlap this window in about half the
years, 2008's among them. The holdout, 2023 to 2025, is the only new reading, and is reported.

**How this form differs from the lab's earlier cards.** SC-017-01 held cash in September and
October, the months Ilmanen's long record names weak and in which he places the autumn gloom, and
was not refuted, through 2008; SC-018-01 held equities from November to April. This window is fixed
by the astronomy, not by a table: it drops September's first three weeks, keeps its last days and
October, and holds cash through November and most of December, which SC-018-01 held.

**The bank's neighbours.** **SC-017**, the month of the year, and **SC-018**, Sell in May, read the
same year by calendar months; **SC-026**, the daylight-saving anomaly, a weekend of the same light
inside this window; **SC-028**, sunshine, the weather's daily mood.

## From the claim to a signal

- **The markets**: the four equity funds of northern markets the lab holds, SPY, QQQ and IWM, whose
  market lies at about 41 degrees north, and EFA, whose developed markets are about three fifths
  Europe's, from about 45 to 60 degrees north, a fifth Japan's, at about 35, and about a fifteenth
  Australia's and New Zealand's, in the south. EEM is left out: its markets were mostly northern
  over these years — China and Hong Kong, Taiwan, Korea, India, Russia, Mexico — but at lower
  latitudes on average, several near the tropics, with a southern minority, Brazil, South Africa and
  Chile; it is reported apart.
- **The window**: from 22 September, the September equinox's date to within a day, for `days` 90
  days, to 20 December, the day before the solstice's date. The rule holds cash over it and the four
  funds in equal parts over the rest of the year, the long-only trade of a claim that the rising
  premia weigh on prices while the days shorten.
- **The parameter**: `days`, the window's length from 22 September, 90 for the base; its neighbours
  68 and 112, to 28 November and 11 January, and 45 and 135, to 5 November and 3 February. No
  variant. The neighbours at 112 and 135 hold cash over the turn of the year and part of January
  (SC-002, SC-007), so they also judge other calendar effects.
- **The calendar**: as SC-017-01 did, the rule leaves the funds at the close of the last scheduled
  session before the window and returns at the close of its last scheduled session, read from the
  New York Stock Exchange's calendar of scheduled sessions, derived from its holiday rules alone.
  The unscheduled closures of 29–30 October 2012 and 5 December 2018 fall inside the window but on
  none of its ends. The first session, 3 January 2005, is outside the window, and the rule holds
  from it: in-sample, the first entry, 18 exits and 18 returns, 37 decisions, above gate 1's 30.

## What the battery judges, and what to expect

**The size predicted — a judgment.** No source in the library gives the effect a size. Ilmanen's
long US record, read by eye and weighted by the window's days, puts the window at about +0.54% a
month against about +0.62 for the rest of the year, a gap of about −0.08, near nothing, November's
and December's strength against September's weakness, which Doeswijk's objection names. The card
judges the window's excess return at about 0 to 0.5% a month below the rest of the year, the
mechanism's sign, up to about 0.5% a month; 0 to −0.024% a day. Out of the funds a quarter of the
year, the rule's alpha over the four held always is about (1/4) × (3/4) × twelve times the monthly
gap: 0 to +1.1% a year before costs of about 0.1% a year. Its hedged return moves by the funds'
volatility, about 20.6% a year, times √(1/4 × 3/4), about 8.9% a year: an appraisal ratio after
costs of about −0.01 to +0.11. The window holds October and November 2008: the regression beta will
fall below the three quarters of the sessions held and lift the alpha mechanically, by as much as
the whole predicted range, as SC-017-01's did; the alpha at a beta equal to the share held is
reported. The card expects the clause to refute, the base to fail gate 2 or 3 and gate 4, which asks
about 0.65; gate 3's 90% is hard for a yearly rule, whose placebos shifted by about a whole number
of years hold near copies of the window (SC-018-01 beat 89.0% of them); and gate 6 on QQQ's share of
the profit is likely, QQQ having carried 32 to 33% among five funds (SC-016-01, SC-017-01) and more
among four.

**The clause.** Judged before costs, over the in-sample sessions from 4 January 2005, each session's
return taken from the market's previous close: the four funds' average daily return less the bill's
rate over the sessions whose date is in the base's window, less the same over the other sessions;
the standard error of that difference from the two groups' daily variances, and its t statistic,
computed apart from the battery. The theory is refuted in this form if that t statistic is +0.35 or
above: the autumn no weaker than the rest of the year. Below it, short of passing gates 1 to 7, it
is not proven, not refuted. The in-sample result is largely known, likely a refutation (above), and
is read as such, not as fresh evidence.

**The test's power.** About 1,135 sessions in the window and 3,400 outside, at a daily volatility of
about 1.3%, and more in the window through October and November 2008, as SC-017-01's September and
October were: a standard error of about 0.045 to 0.052% a day. The clause refutes about 36% of the
time with no effect and about 19 to 21% at −0.024% a day, the top of the range. It cannot tell an
effect of the predicted size from none.

**SC-025's refutations, and what the card can grade.**

1. *No seasonal pattern related to the length of the day*: graded, on the northern markets the lab
   holds. The years 2005 to 2022 lie wholly after Kamstra, Kramer and Levi's sample, so the clause
   also reads the fifth refutation's "does not survive outside the samples in which it was first
   found"; investors' own risk aversion is out of reach.
2. *The same timing in both hemispheres*: out of reach. The lab holds no southern market alone, and
   Harris expects such a test inconclusive for the markets' common ownership; Bouman and Jacobsen's
   southern Sell in May, in the library, leans toward the calendar causes this refutation names.
3. *An effect no stronger where the day varies more*: reported, EFA less the US funds, which barely
   tests it: EFA's Europe lies farther north than New York, its Japan farther south, its Australia
   in the south, and it is priced at New York's close.
4. *A pattern accounted for by the calendar's other forces*: not graded. The window holds the
   Halloween turn (SC-018), the Thanksgiving pre-holiday (SC-014), the daylight-saving change of
   November (SC-026), part of SC-016's window, the tax-loss selling Ilmanen describes and Doeswijk's
   fourth-quarter optimism.

**Measures stated before the run**, reported, not graded:

- the gap by fund, the four funds' average, and EEM's;
- the half year from equinox to equinox, 22 September to 20 March, against the rest of the year; and
  the winter alone, 21 December to 20 March, against the spring and summer, 21 March to 21
  September;
- EFA less the US funds' average, in the window against outside;
- by month of the window: its days of September, October, November and its days of December;
- over 2005–2011 and 2012–2022, and without 2008;
- the same gap over the holdout, 2023 to 2025;
- the alpha before and after costs, at the regression beta and at a beta equal to the share of
  sessions held; the neighbours' and the one-day-late rule's alphas and appraisal ratios.

## Choices, and the options rejected

- **The half year from equinox to equinox**, the books' "fall and winter" read as a sign for returns
  through both: the risk premium's reading makes the winter's returns high once the premium is, and
  Harris and Ilmanen place the effect in the autumn; it is reported.
- **The hemispheres**, the theory's signature: out of reach, as above.
- **The five funds**, as SC-017-01 and SC-018-01 held: EEM's lower latitudes and southern minority
  mix the claim's markets with markets it signs weakly or the other way.
- **A regression on the night's length**, the sources' measure: it needs the effect's size by hours
  of night, which the library does not give; the window is the claim's season, fixed by the
  astronomy.

## Implementation

Long only, four funds in equal parts or cash, with European (UCITS) funds that track them; one exit
and one return a year, on dates known ahead.
