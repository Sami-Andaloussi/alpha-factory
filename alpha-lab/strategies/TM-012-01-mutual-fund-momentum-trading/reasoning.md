# TM-012-01 — Momentum trading by mutual funds: reasoning, not testable as a theory of its own

## The theory, and the forms its sources give it

TM-012 holds that mutual funds buy past winners more readily than they sell past losers, and that
their performance-chasing flows, which they invest in the stocks they hold, prolong the winners'
rise: the stocks funds buy after a rise keep rising, over a few weeks and, the effect being
cumulative, beyond. Its refutations: funds that sell past losers as readily as they buy past
winners, or whose purchases show no relation to past returns; stocks bought by performance-chasing
funds showing no continuation over the following weeks; inflows that follow past performance
leaving no trace in the returns of the funds' holdings.

**Its source, as the lab read it in the library.** Grinblatt, Titman and Wermers (1995), which the
library holds in others' reports: the Zacks *Handbook of Equity Market Anomalies* (2011) reports
that, in the quarterly holdings of 155 mutual funds from 1975 to 1984, most funds bought stocks on
their past returns and herded a little, both statistically significant "but not considerably
large", and reports Badrinath and Wahal (2002), who find in institutions' holdings from 1987 to 1995
momentum trading on entry and exit, none in the adjustment of continuing holdings, and none when the
institutions are aggregated; Jegadeesh and Titman's chapter in *Advances in Behavioral Finance,
Volume II* (Thaler, ed., 2005) cites them, with Chan, Jegadeesh and Wermers (2000), as finding that
mutual funds tend to buy past winners and sell past losers — practitioners screening on momentum,
with no asymmetry between buying winners and selling losers, on which TM-012's first refutation
turns; Hirshleifer and Teoh's chapter in the *Handbook of Financial Markets: Dynamics and Evolution*
(2009) cites them among the studies of correlated institutional trades.

## What the lab can read of it, and where each form already stands

The lab holds the daily closes of funds and of bitcoin, and the Treasury bill's yield; it holds no
fund holdings, no flows and no creations or redemptions of its own funds, and passes a strategy no
volume. Every refutation TM-012 names reads funds' purchases, sales or flows:

- **Funds buying winners more readily than they sell losers, and purchases related to past
  returns**: holdings, which the lab does not hold; the correlated trades of institutions are
  **FP-009**'s claim, institutional herding.
- **Stocks bought by performance-chasing funds continuing to rise**: the holdings that identify
  those stocks; on prices alone the continuation is momentum, read on the lab's funds by TM-017-01,
  CA-001-01, TM-003-01 and TM-001-01 — and among sector funds, O'Neal's momentum of sector mutual
  funds in the Zacks handbook, by TM-003-01. Over a few weeks, the continuation is **TM-043**'s at
  the index level, and within the lab's groups MR-032-01 found a fund's past five sessions against
  its group predicting its next week with a rank correlation of +0.0106 (standard error 0.0114).
  Beyond a year the library signs it the other way — investors chase multiyear winners at the
  horizon where markets "tend to exhibit more mean reversion than continuation" (Ilmanen, *Investing
  Amid Low Expected Returns*, 2022, §17.1), and flows into funds with high past returns are followed
  by low returns (Frazzini and Lamont's "dumb money", in Ang's *Asset Management*, 2014); the
  reversal is **MR-021**'s, read as value within groups by CA-024-01, and the flows' own reversal
  **FP-020**'s.
- **Inflows following performance and leaving a trace in the holdings' returns**: flows; the price
  pressure of open-end fund flows is **FP-004**'s claim, the convex relation of flows to performance
  **FP-019**'s, momentum and reversal from fund switching **FP-020**'s, and ETF creations and
  redemptions **FP-014**'s, all needing flows, holdings or creations.
- **Crypto-assets**, which the bank names for TM-012: the lab holds no flows into bitcoin vehicles;
  the library gives bitcoin's trend no sign (TM-005-01), and a card of one asset is not drawn.

**The bank's siblings.** **TM-015**, slow-moving capital and delegated management; **FP-009**,
**TM-031** and **TM-029**, herding; **FP-004**, **FP-019**, **FP-020** and **FP-014**, flows;
**TM-023** and **FP-018**, the calendar of funds' buying.

**What was considered, and why it does not rescue a card of TM-012's own:**

- **Funds buying winners before their reporting dates, read on prices through the calendar**: the
  one form of funds' buying of past winners that the library signs on prices alone. Sias (2007), as
  Gray and Vogel (*Quantitative Momentum*, 2016, chapter 7) report him, finds stock momentum earning
  3.10% a month from 1984 to 2004 in the months that end a quarter against 0.59% in the others,
  January aside, and more where institutions trade most, and reads it as window dressing; Ilmanen
  (*Expected Returns*, 2011, §25.2) finds momentum and trend strategies doing better in December and
  reversing in January, his commodity momentum earning 1.6% in December against 0.4% in January, and
  names institutions buying recently outperforming assets before the year's end. On the lab's funds
  it reads as a momentum tilt stronger in the months that end a quarter. It is **TM-023**'s claim,
  momentum's seasonality, whose refutations name the reinforcement of winners before quarter-ends
  and the year-end, and **FP-018**'s, window dressing, both untouched: drawn here, it would spend a
  trial their cards must count for the same bet. The lab has published TM-001-01's January gap
  between asset classes, −1.84% a year (7.24%, 16 months) against +2.09% (3.37%) in the other
  months, left to TM-023, and SC-006-01's year's winning sector funds over December's last sessions,
  −0.03% a year (0.22%); no verdict has read the months that end a quarter.
- **Other forms in the library**: mutual funds herding into analysts' upgrades (Brown, Wei and
  Wermers, in Hirshleifer and Teoh) need analysts' revisions, and are FP-009's and TM-029's; winning
  funds indexing toward the year's end (Chevalier and Ellison) gives no sign on prices; the funds'
  October tax year (Singal) is tax selling, **FP-026**'s and **SC-002**'s; country flows forecasting
  returns (Froot, O'Connell and Seasholes) need flows.

What would make it testable as its own: the funds' holdings or flows — the quarterly holdings of
mutual funds, or the creations and redemptions of the ETFs the lab holds — frozen in the snapshot.

## Status

TM-012 is recorded `not-testable`: every one of its refutations reads funds' purchases, sales or
flows, which the lab does not hold; the continuation it would add to is price momentum, read by
TM-017-01, CA-001-01, TM-003-01 and TM-001-01, and over a few weeks TM-043's; its flows and herding
are FP-004's, FP-019's, FP-020's, FP-014's and FP-009's; its calendar form, funds buying winners
before quarter-ends and the year-end, is TM-023's and FP-018's. No card is drawn, and no trial is
spent.
