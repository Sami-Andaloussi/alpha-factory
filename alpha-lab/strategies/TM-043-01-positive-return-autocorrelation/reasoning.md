# TM-043-01 — Positive return autocorrelation: reasoning, not testable as a theory of its own

## The theory, and the forms its sources give it

TM-043 is Lo and MacKinlay's variance-ratio evidence (1988): the variance of returns over q periods
exceeds q times the one-period variance when returns are positively autocorrelated, and the
autocorrelation comes from underreaction, positive feedback trading and lead-lag relations, with
bid-ask bounce and nonsynchronous trading adding a spurious part at short horizons. Its prediction:
variance ratios of US stock indices significantly above one at 2 to 16 weeks, weekly and monthly
returns positively autocorrelated, especially for equally weighted indices, which small
capitalisations dominate. Its refutations: variance ratios not different from one at 2 to 16 weeks,
or a positive autocorrelation that vanishes once bid-ask bounce and nonsynchronous trading are
accounted for; no difference between equally weighted and value-weighted indices. The bank names
equity indices and stocks, and daily prices.

**Its sources, as the lab read them in the library.**

- **Lo and MacKinlay** (*A Non-Random Walk Down Wall Street*, 1999): the CRSP equal-weighted index
  rejects the random walk at weekly horizons from 1962 to 1985, the value-weighted index much less;
  the size quintiles' weekly autocorrelations run from 42% for the smallest to 14% for the largest
  (chapter 2, table 2.2); the index's autocorrelation "completely attributable to cross effects",
  large stocks leading small ones (chapter 5); in the preface, an update of the weekly indexes on
  1986 to 1996 "conforms more closely to the random walk".
- **Campbell, Lo and MacKinlay** (*The Econometrics of Financial Markets*, 1997, sections 2.4 to
  2.8): table 2.4, the first-order autocorrelation of the CRSP indexes from 1962 to 1994 — the
  equal-weighted index 35.0% daily, 20.3% weekly and 17.1% monthly; the value-weighted index 1.5%
  weekly (5.6% to October 1978, −2.0% after) and 4.3% monthly (6.4% to 1978, 1.3% after); table 2.5,
  the equal-weighted index's variance ratios significantly above one at every q and in both halves,
  the value-weighted index's 1.02 to 1.04, their test statistics "all statistically insignificant";
  table 2.6, the size quintiles, the largest's evidence "limited only to the first half of the
  sample period".
- **Foerster and Keim** (in Keim and Ziemba, *Security Market Imperfections in Worldwide Equity
  Markets*, 2000): nontrading explains a small part of the smallest portfolio's weekly
  autocorrelation; the daily autocorrelations of the size quintiles and of the S&P 500 declined
  through the 1970s and 1980s.
- **Lo** (*Adaptive Markets*, 2017, figure 8.8): the daily autocorrelation of the value- and
  equal-weighted CRSP indexes over 750-day windows waxes and wanes, and is significantly negative
  after 2008. **Lo** (*Hedge Funds: An Analytic Perspective*, updated edition, table 2.2): the S&P
  500's monthly first-order autocorrelation −0.6%, January 1994 to July 2007.
- **Shleifer** (*Inefficient Markets*, 2000, chapter 5), after Cutler, Poterba and Summers: index
  returns autocorrelated at about 0.1 over a month, across the world's markets and in the US alone,
  1960 to 1988. **Poterba and Summers** (1988, in Lo, ed., *Market Efficiency*, 1997): positive
  serial correlation at short horizons "pervasive" across fifteen national indices, monthly, 1957 to
  1986, some of the index data time-averaged.
- **Ilmanen** (*Expected Returns*, 2011, figure 20.1): adjacent months' returns correlated at +0.20
  on average across asset classes and strategies, 1990 to 2009, a part of it "illiquidity and return
  smoothing"; its equity-market bars, as read off the figure, about +0.21 for emerging markets and
  +0.13 for developed ones, all positive.
- **Baker and Kiymaz** (*Market Microstructure in Emerging and Developed Markets*, 2013, chapter
  21): weekly returns of Asian emerging markets mean-reverting from 1990 to 2004 (Hoque, Kim and
  Pyun), Latin American markets a random walk (Ojah and Karemera), emerging markets "at least as
  efficient as developed markets" (Griffin, Kelly and Nardari).

## What the lab can read of it, and where each form already stands

The lab holds the daily closes of funds — the S&P 500, the Nasdaq-100, the Russell 2000, developed
and emerging markets outside the US and the eleven US sectors, all weighted by capitalisation — and
no equal-weighted index. On those data:

- **Weekly continuation and variance ratios above one at 2 to 16 weeks on the indices the lab
  holds**: the sources' strong sign is on the equal-weighted index; for the value-weighted index,
  the nearest to the lab's funds, the variance ratios are 1.02 to 1.04 and insignificant, the weekly
  autocorrelation −2.0% from 1978 to 1994, and Lo's daily autocorrelation turns negative after 2008.
  TM-041-01's rule was the plain trade of this weekly claim on the fourteen US equity funds, and it
  stopped at gate 2, a Sharpe ratio of 0.14 against the funds' 0.49 held always; its verdict
  published their weekly lag-one slopes, slightly negative for eleven of fourteen (SPY −0.011, IWM
  −0.020). For EFA and EEM, whose weekly slopes no verdict has published, Baker and Kiymaz's weekly
  signs on emerging markets are null or negative, and the library gives no weekly sign for developed
  markets outside the US: no sign to grade.
- **The equal-weighted index against the value-weighted one**: an equal-weighted fund, RSP, is not
  in the snapshot; the size side of the contrast, small capitalisations against large, is TM-041's,
  read by TM-041-01 (IWM's weekly slope less SPY's −0.009, not refuted, not proven).
- **An autocorrelation that vanishes once microstructure is accounted for**: bid-ask bounce is
  **MR-004**'s claim, nonsynchronous trading **LL-004**'s; the index autocorrelation made by large
  stocks leading small ones is **LL-001**'s (Lo and MacKinlay, chapter 5). A fund's price carries
  little of the first two (TM-041-01's reasoning, after Madhavan).
- **Monthly continuation of capitalisation-weighted indices**: signed positive by Ilmanen's equity
  bars for 1990 to 2009, by Cutler, Poterba and Summers and by Poterba and Summers across national
  indices to 1988; faded to 1.3% for the US value-weighted index from 1978 to 1994 and −0.6% for the
  S&P 500 from 1994 to 2007. This is the one-month form of **TM-017**'s claim — the sign of past
  returns over 1 to 12 months predicting the next, across equity indices, bonds, currencies and
  commodities — which TM-017-01, reading six and twelve months, left unread; earlier judgements
  placed it with TM-017 (TM-005-01, TM-036-01), some with TM-043 too (TM-007-01, TM-015-01). It is
  drawn whole, as **TM-017-02**, next: the one-month continuation of the lab's funds across TM-017's
  asset classes, the equity index funds among them, TM-043's monthly form judged through it.
  TM-041-01's twenty-session slopes of SPY and IWM, +0.019 and −0.031, are already published and
  that card must treat them so.

**The bank's siblings.** **TM-017**, time-series momentum, in progress, which draws the monthly
form; **TM-041**, small against large, read by TM-041-01; **LL-001**, large capitalisations leading
small; **LL-004**, nonsynchronous trading; **MR-004**, bid-ask bounce; **FP-024**, positive feedback
trading; **MR-021**, long-term reversal, the sources' negative autocorrelation at three to five
years, all untouched; **TM-036**, extrapolative expectations, recorded not testable.

**What was considered, and why it does not rescue a card of TM-043's own:**

- **Weekly continuation on the equity funds**: the sources' sign for capitalisation-weighted indices
  is insignificant or negative after 1978, the plain trade is TM-041-01's rule, stopped at gate 2,
  and the fourteen US funds' weekly slopes are published; EEM's weekly sign in the library is null
  or negative, and EFA has none.
- **Variance ratios at 4, 8 and 16 weeks on the funds**: the same indices, the value-weighted ratios
  1.02 to 1.04 and insignificant in the source; the multi-week continuation is TM-017's trend at its
  shortest lookbacks.
- **Monthly continuation on the equity funds**: a part of TM-017's one-month form across asset
  classes, drawn whole as TM-017-02 rather than in part here, so that one trial, not two, reads it.
- **An equal-weighted index built from the sector funds**: eleven large-capitalisation sectors in
  equal parts are not the small-capitalisation-dominated index the sources test.

What would make it testable as its own: an equal-weighted index of US stocks, or its fund, frozen in
the snapshot with its capitalisation-weighted counterpart.

## Status

TM-043 is recorded `not-testable` as a theory of its own: its strong positive sign lives in
equal-weighted indices, which the lab does not hold; for capitalisation-weighted indices its sources
find weekly variance ratios near one and autocorrelations insignificant or negative after 1978, the
plain weekly trade was TM-041-01's, stopped at gate 2, and the US funds' weekly slopes are
published; the size side of its equal-against-value contrast is TM-041's, read; its microstructure
refutation is MR-004's, LL-004's and LL-001's; its monthly form, which Ilmanen signs positive on
developed and emerging equity markets, is the one-month form of TM-017's claim, drawn next as
TM-017-02. No card is drawn under TM-043, and no trial is spent.
