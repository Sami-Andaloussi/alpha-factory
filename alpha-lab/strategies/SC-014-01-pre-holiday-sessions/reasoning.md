# SC-014-01 — The equity funds over the session before each market holiday: reasoning

## Why this theory now

SC-014, the pre-holiday effect, holds that the trading session before a market-closing holiday earns
abnormally high returns compared with ordinary days. The explanations named are short covering and
the unwinding of positions before a closure, a build-up of individuals' buy orders, market makers'
inventories, and holiday optimism. The books document it over a long history and in many markets,
weakened or shifted in recent samples, sometimes to the third session before the holiday, and more
and more a small-cap effect.

**The sources.** The lab read them in the library's books.

- Lakonishok and Smidt (1988), on the Dow Jones Industrials from 1897 to 1986 (their Table 3, 915
  holidays in ninety years): the preholiday rate of return averaged 0.220% a day against 0.009% on
  regular days, 23 times more, 63.9% of preholidays up, and holidays accounted for about half of the
  index's price rise; preholiday returns were generally two to five times the preweekend ones, so
  that the closure alone does not explain them. By subperiod, still 0.178% from 1964 to 1975, but
  0.011% against 0.023% on regular days from 1976 to June 1986 (103 holidays). Ariel (1985), as they
  report him, found 0.53% and 0.36% on CRSP's equally and value weighted indices from 1963 to 1982,
  the holidays carrying 38% of the value-weighted index's annual return.
- Ziemba (*Calendar Anomalies and Arbitrage*, 2012, §1.5), who retells the Dow's figure as 51.5% of
  its gain on about eight preholidays a year, on S&P 500 and Russell 2000 futures from 1993 to 2011
  (his Table 1.6): the session before the holiday (−1) averaged +0.03% for the S&P 500 (z 0.40) and
  +0.06% for the Russell 2000 (z 0.58), against +0.01% and 0.00% on the other days; the third before
  (−3) averaged +0.19% (z 1.95) and +0.31% (z 2.47); the second before, −0.03% and 0.00%; the two
  after, +0.06 to +0.09%; the sessions before holidays calmer than the others (a standard deviation
  of 1.07% against 1.27% for the S&P 500). A regression he presents as the cash market's, which the
  Keim and Ziemba volume gives as his own on the Nikkei from 1949 to 1988 (Ziemba, 1991), put −1 at
  +0.19% (t 3.71) and −3 at +0.08% (t 1.49). He concludes that the holiday effect "has diminished
  greatly" in the 1990s and 2000s and survives, to some extent, on −3. The Zacks *Handbook of Equity
  Market Anomalies* (2011, chapter 9) carries the same text with the table to 2010 (its Table 9.3):
  −1 +0.05% (z 0.54) and +0.14% (z 1.40), −3 +0.20% and +0.33%, the other days +0.01%.
- Cervera and Keim, in Keim and Ziemba's *Security Market Imperfections in Worldwide Equity Markets*
  (2000), on seventeen markets outside the United States from 1980 to 1994: pooled, the pre-holiday
  session earned 0.131% a day more than the others (t 3.91), about five and a half times their
  return, and was less volatile; the difference was significant in eight of the seventeen, not in
  most European markets (France, Germany, Italy, the Netherlands, Spain, Switzerland and the United
  Kingdom), and they report Cadsby and Ratner (1992) finding the markets' holiday effects unrelated,
  their holidays falling on different dates. The same volume's chapter on the Japanese market finds
  it faded in 1990–94: +0.046% over 61 pre-holidays (t 0.29) against −0.046% on the other days, the
  weakest of its calendar effects that period.
- Ilmanen (*Expected Returns*, 2011, chapter 25): returns tend to be high on the day before major US
  holidays, with similar patterns in most other markets; he reports Grimbacher, Swinkels and Van
  Vliet (2010), on the US market from 1963 to 2008, finding the Halloween and turn-of-the-month
  effects the strongest, "even subsuming the holiday effect".
- Kaufman (*Trading Systems and Methods*): Merrill found 67.9% of the sessions before holidays up
  from 1897 to 1964; Fosback's rule, buying two sessions before a major holiday and selling on the
  session before it, as Kaufman describes it, which reads as this card's base, was profitable over
  1928 to 1975, and Freeburg's update found it "still profitable, but not as impressive"; Kaeppel
  buys at the close of −3 and sells at the close of −1.

None of the bank's other references — Zweig (1986), Ariel (1990), Cadsby and Ratner (1992), Chong,
Hudson, Keasey and Littler (2005), Ko and Yang (2024), whose title holds that the premium has become
a small-firm effect out of sample — is in the library, and nothing in it measures the effect after
2011. Ziemba's futures cover 2005 to 2011 of the lab's years; 2012 to 2022 lie outside all the
sources.

**What the lab already knows, and is not blind to.** Three verdicts on the same five funds hold most
of this card's sessions. SC-001-01 published, over the 122 long weekends, the week's last session
less the next week's first: +0.149% for the five funds' average and +0.176% for IWM (a standard
error of 0.177%), against +0.036% and +0.059% over the other weekends; 121 of this card's 161
sessions are those last sessions, and 91 are Fridays. SC-008-01 published the five funds' means by
session of the month, from −4 to +4, all months together, and its span from −5 to +2 beat the other
sessions by +0.094% a day; 97 of the 161 lie in that span, 56 in its base's −1 to +3, and 27 are a
month's last session, whose mean over all months was −0.059%. SC-007-01 published its window's means
by session, December's last among them (−0.081%, the eve of New Year's Day in sixteen of eighteen
years), and its window holds 32 of the 161, Christmas Eve's in sixteen years; SC-002-01's December
window and SC-017-01's monthly means bear on a few. No verdict reads the pre-holiday sessions as a
set, nor any fund's; but about three quarters of them sit inside published figures that lean
positive, and a refutation, as the lab stands, has less than the 36% chance computed with no effect,
by an amount those figures do not fix. The card's prediction is taken from the sources, not from
these figures.

**The bank's neighbours.** **SC-007**, the Santa Claus rally, which holds the eves of Christmas and
New Year's Day; **SC-008**, the turn of the month; **SC-001**, the weekend effect, the closure of
every week. This card reads the sessions before the exchange's holidays.

## From the claim to a signal

- **The market**: the lab's five equity funds, SPY, QQQ, IWM, EFA and EEM, in equal parts, as the
  lab's calendar cards took them. The clause grades the three US funds' average, the market the
  sources measured and its small caps, where the effect is said to last: EFA's and EEM's home
  markets stay open on most US holidays, and Cervera and Keim find no pre-holiday effect in most
  European markets. The five funds' average is reported, and the battery holds the five, as
  SC-001-01 held them while grading IWM.
- **The pre-holiday session**: the last scheduled session before one of the calendar's scheduled
  holidays that falls on a weekday, on the New York Stock Exchange's calendar of scheduled sessions
  derived from its holiday rules alone, as SC-008-01 built it — about nine a year in-sample (eight
  in 2005 and 2011, when New Year's Day fell on a Saturday; Juneteenth from 2022); a half session,
  as on 3 July, counts as a session. A session's return is taken from the previous session's close.
- **The base**: the five funds in equal parts over each pre-holiday session's return, and the bill
  otherwise: the target rises at the close of the second session before the holiday and falls at the
  close of the first, Fosback's rule as Kaufman's words read. **The variant**: the same over the
  third session before the holiday, where Ziemba finds what remains of the effect.
- **A parameter for gate 6**: the offset of the session held before the holiday, 1 for the base,
  with its neighbour 2 at both steps, the second session before.

The sessions were counted before the card by a scratch script outside the repository (`pace.py`, in
the session's scratch folder) that reads the calendar and the market's dates alone, and the logic
audit confirmed them: 161 pre-holiday sessions in-sample, the first held on 2005-01-14, 322
switches; as many for the neighbour and the variant; 31 sessions before Columbus Day and Veterans
Day, on which the exchange stays open. The in-sample closures outside the rules (2007-01-02,
2012-10-29 and 30, 2018-12-05) fall on none of the sessions −3 to −1.

## What the battery judges, and what to expect

**The size predicted — a judgment, from the sources.** The pre-holiday session earned about twenty
times an ordinary day on the Dow from 1897 to the mid-1970s, and no more than an ordinary day from
1976 to 1986; about +0.02 to +0.13% above the other days in futures from 1993 to 2010 and 2011;
nothing in most European markets. The card judges the three US funds' pre-holiday session at about 0
to +0.08% a day above the other sessions from 2005 to 2022, more in IWM than in SPY; EFA and EEM at
about 0, Christmas, New Year's Day and Good Friday, which their markets share, aside; the five
funds' average at about 0 to +0.05%. The third session before: about 0 to +0.20% above the other
sessions for the US funds — Ziemba's −3 is the best of five days scanned in one sample (z 1.95 and
2.47, about p 0.25 and 0.07 once five are counted), seven of his nineteen years among the lab's. The
base's alpha is about nine sessions a year times the five funds' gap, about 0 to 0.45% a year before
costs of about 0.9% a year (eighteen switches of the whole portfolio at 5 basis points a side):
about −0.9 to −0.45% after costs. Nine sessions a year at about 1.4% a day give a volatility of
about 4.1% a year, and an appraisal ratio of about 0 to 0.11 before costs. The card expects the base
and the variant to fail gate 2: the variant would reach a Sharpe ratio of 0.4 only at a gap near
+0.25% a day for the five funds, near the top of the sources' figures, and would fall short at twice
the costs up to about +0.30%.

**The clause.** Judged before costs: over the in-sample sessions from the base's first held, the
three US funds' (SPY, QQQ, IWM) average daily return less the bill's rate, each from the market's
previous close; the mean over the sessions whose return spans the last scheduled session before one
of the signal's scheduled holidays that falls on a weekday, less the mean over the other sessions,
with the standard error of that difference, the square root of the sum of each group's variance over
its count, and its t statistic. The theory is refuted in this form if that t statistic is −0.35 or
below. Above it, short of passing gates 1 to 7, it is not proven, not refuted; a base that passes
gates 1 to 7 while the clause refutes leaves the theory refuted in this form.

Of SC-014's refutation criteria, the first, pre-holiday returns no higher than ordinary days', is
graded: it is the bank's first, and every mechanism named acts at the close before the closure; the
second, an effect as strong before holidays on which the exchange stays open, is reported through
the sessions before Columbus Day and Veterans Day; the third, an excess that comes only from the
closing print, is partly read through the session after the holiday — a close at the ask reverses
on the next session (Keim, 1989, as Cervera and Keim report him), and the funds' one-cent spreads
leave little of that component; the fourth, the effect as strong in large caps as in small once
known, is reported through IWM against SPY; the fifth, the trading of short sellers, individuals and
market makers, is beyond the lab's data of daily closes.

**The test's power.** At a daily standard deviation of about 1.33% for the three US funds' average
over all in-sample sessions, and 161 pre-holiday sessions against about 4,370 others, the standard
error of the difference is about 0.107% a day. The clause then refutes about 36% of the time with no
effect, about 21% at +0.05% and about 10% at +0.10%; the logic audit's simulations with fat tails,
the funds' own returns resampled and their volatility path kept these rates. Sessions before
holidays are calmer in every source: at their volatility the standard error would be nearer 0.09 to
0.10%, and the chances about 18 to 20% at +0.05% and 7 to 9% at +0.10%. The clause cannot tell an
effect of the predicted size from none.

**Measures stated before the run**, reported, not graded, each gap with the standard error from the
two groups' variances, for the three US funds' average unless stated:

- the same gap for each of the five funds, for the five funds' average, and for IWM less SPY;
- the gap of the sessions −3, −2, +1 and +2 around each holiday, Ziemba's table, each placed by the
  market session whose return spans the scheduled session and set against all the sessions outside
  its group, a session in two groups counted in both (Christmas's +2 is New Year's Day's −3 in some
  years), for the US funds' average, the five funds' and IWM; and the −3 gap over 2012–2022, outside
  Ziemba's sample, for the same three;
- the gap of the sessions before Columbus Day (the second Monday of October) and Veterans Day (11
  November when it falls on a weekday), 31 sessions, on which the exchange stays open, all of the
  first on Fridays; and the pre-holiday gap less it, with its standard error;
- the pre-holiday gap inside SC-008-01's span from −5 to +2 (the pre-holiday sessions against the
  span's other sessions) and outside it (against the other sessions outside), and the pre-holiday
  Fridays against the other Fridays: Grimbacher, Swinkels and Van Vliet's subsumption, and
  Lakonishok and Smidt's comparison with the weekend;
- the pre-holiday gap without the pre-holiday sessions of Christmas and New Year's Day (127 sessions
  remain), which SC-007-01's window holds, and by holiday, Juneteenth's single in-sample session
  (2022-06-17) apart;
- the gap over 2005–2011, inside Ziemba's sample, and 2012–2022, outside it;
- the base's and the variant's alphas before and after costs, and the neighbour's and the
  one-day-late rule's alphas and appraisal ratios.

**Risks named before the run.**

- **Costs**: eighteen switches a year, about 0.9% a year, twice the top of the predicted alpha.
- **Few sessions**: 161 sessions; a crash on the eve of a holiday, as before Thanksgiving in 2008 or
  on Christmas Eve in 2018, weighs much.
- **Gate 6**: the neighbour holds −2 and the day's delay +1, where the sources find little; a real
  effect confined to −1 may fail gate 6 on them. The five funds are held together, each fund's
  share of the profit its own gain on those sessions; with EFA and EEM near nil, one US fund, IWM
  most likely, may carry more than 30% of it, as EEM carried 42% of SC-007-01's and 41% of
  SC-008-01's.

## Choices, and the options rejected

- **A tilt rather than in or out**: the claim is about the market's level on those sessions.
- **The three US funds as the market**: a universe of three equal funds gives each a third of the
  profit and cannot pass gate 6; the five are held and the three graded.
- **The third session before as the base**: Ziemba finds the remaining effect there, but the bank's
  claim, and its first refutation criterion, are about the session before the holiday, where every
  mechanism named acts; −3 has no mechanism and is the best of five days scanned in one sample (z
  1.95 and 2.47, about p 0.25 and 0.07 once five are counted). It is the variant.
- **Weekends as holidays**: every Friday closes the market for two days; SC-001 holds that claim.
- **Unscheduled closures**: outside the exchange's holiday rules, some announced days ahead and one,
  Hurricane Sandy's, not at all. The sources differ: Lakonishok and Smidt counted every day on which
  trading would normally have occurred but did not, the 1968 Wednesday closings aside; Cervera and
  Keim counted legal holidays that closed the market. The card keeps the rules' holidays, known a
  year ahead.

## Implementation

Long only, the five equity funds, each with a European (UCITS) fund that tracks it, or cash;
eighteen orders a year on sessions known ahead. A weekly or monthly portfolio cannot hold it: a
calendar effect enters a portfolio only if it passes the battery. The targets are filled at the
close of the session on which they are set; gate 6 adds a day's delay, which holds the holiday's
first session after instead, and, for New Year's Day 2007, the session of 3 January, the exchange
having closed on the 2nd.
