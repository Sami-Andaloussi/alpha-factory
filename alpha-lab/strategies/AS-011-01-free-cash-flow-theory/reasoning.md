# AS-011-01 — Free cash flow theory: reasoning, not testable as a theory of its own

## The theory, and the forms its sources give it

AS-011 is Jensen's free cash flow hypothesis: mature, cash-rich firms with low organic growth and
few disciplining investment opportunities are prone to overinvestment, to value-destroying
acquisitions and to managers' private consumption of rents, and dividends, debt service or
repurchases limit it by taking the discretionary cash out of management's hands. Its refutations:
cash-rich firms with few opportunities overinvesting no more than comparable firms; higher payouts
in such firms not followed by a better use of the remaining capital; the cash of mature, low-growth
firms later spent, on average, on profitable investments. The bank names stocks, years, and free
cash flow, capital expenditure, dividends and repurchases, acquisitions and daily prices.

**Its sources, as the lab read them in the library.**

- **Baker, ed.** (*Dividends and Dividend Policy*, 2009), chapter 9, "Agency Costs and the Free Cash
  Flow Hypothesis": Jensen (1986), managers expanding the firm past the size that serves its owners;
  Richardson (2006), overinvestment concentrated where free cash flow is highest. Its return tests,
  all on single firms but one: a stronger price reaction to dividend changes of more than 10% either
  way in firms with Tobin's Q below one (Lang and Litzenberger, 1989, 429 changes from 1979 to
  1984), contested (Howe and others, 1992; Denis and others, 1994; Yoon and Starks, 1995);
  announcement returns of special dividends and self-tenders rising with cash in low-Q firms (Lie,
  2000), which the chapter calls strong support; and a positive market reaction to Roosevelt's
  surprise announcement of a 1936 tax on retained profits, larger where excess cash was a risk
  (Christie and Nanda, 1994). Chapter 14 expects repurchase announcements to be welcomed more for
  likely overinvestors. Chapter 7, "Residual Dividend Policy": at the level of the market, the
  payout ratio predicting higher five-year earnings growth from 1871 to 2001 (Arnott and Asness,
  2003) — a sign on earnings, not returns.
- **Cohen, Lys and Zach** (in Zacks, ed., *The Handbook of Equity Market Anomalies*, 2011, chapter
  6): firms with high abnormal capital investment underperform (Titman, Wei and Xie, 2004), which
  the authors read as investors underreacting to empire building — on single stocks.
- **Tirole** (*The Theory of Corporate Finance*, 2006): chapter 5, the agency cost of free cash and
  the payout and debt that curb it; chapter 1, extravagant investment, with Jensen's (1988) example
  of oil companies that kept exploring heavily in the late 1970s though reserves could be bought
  more cheaply on the stock market, and return signs only on single firms' acquisition
  announcements; chapter 2, section 2.5.2, cash windfalls from lawsuits followed by more
  acquisitions (Blanchard and others, 1994), and oil price shocks moving oil-linked firms' other
  investment (Lamont, 1997) — investment, not returns.
- **Campbell** (*Financial Decisions and Markets*, 2018, section 5.4.3): the aggregate investment
  rate predicting market returns negatively (Cochrane, 1991), grounded in the q theory of investment
  — the rational rival to the agency reading.

## What the lab can read of it

The lab holds the daily bars and volumes of twenty-two funds and bitcoin — the funds from 2005 or
their later start, bitcoin from September 2014 — adjusted for dividends and splits, which encode the
funds' distributions; the Treasury bill's yield from 2005 and two franc exchange rates; the funds'
prices, never their holdings' prices; and no single stock, free cash flow, capital expenditure,
Tobin's Q, acquisition, payout announcement or earnings; its snapshot is closed, an asset or a
series being added only before the first card. On those data:

- **Overinvestment by cash-rich firms with few opportunities**, the claim and its three refutations:
  firms' cash flows, investment and acquisitions, not held.
- **The price reactions to payouts by firms' cash and Q** (Lang and Litzenberger, Lie, chapter 14):
  single firms' announcements, not held; an equity fund's distribution passes on its holdings'
  dividends, net of expenses, on its own calendar, and is not a management's choice.
- **The market's reaction to a tax on retained profits** (Christie and Nanda): one event in 1936,
  before the lab's years, and one decision against gate 1's thirty.
- **Low returns after heavy investment**: single stocks, the forms of **EF-018** (abnormal capital
  investment, whose reference is Titman, Wei and Xie), **EF-017** (asset growth) and **EF-014**
  (profitability and investment), no more readable here.
- **Aggregate forms**: the payout ratio's sign is on earnings growth and needs the index's earnings,
  which the lab does not hold; the aggregate investment rate is not held, a q-theory predictor with
  **EF-014** and among **MR-044**'s countercyclical expected returns.
- **The energy fund (XLE) as Jensen's oil industry**: his episode is the late 1970s, the library
  signs no return for the sector after its cash booms, and a static underweight would make about one
  decision, certain to fail gate 1, as AS-009-01's static tilt; oil-shock sector forms are
  **CA-028**'s. **The commodity capital cycle**, supply rising as the investment projects of booms
  come to fruition (Fabozzi, Füss and Kaiser, eds., *The Handbook of Commodity Investing*, 2008): an
  industry supply cycle, not the agency theory, needing producers' investment the lab does not hold.
  No source in the library treats cash-rich sectors, leverage discipline on funds, or crypto
  treasuries.

**The bank's siblings.** **EF-019**, capital allocation discipline, the nearest mechanism;
**EF-011**, free cash flow yield; **EF-016**, value-creating growth; **EF-002**, Tobin's q;
**EF-014**, **EF-017** and **EF-018**, investment; **EF-027**, net share issuance; **EF-029**,
repurchases; **EF-030**, net payout yield; **AS-012**, dividend signalling; **AS-013**, the life
cycle of payout; **MR-044**, countercyclical expected returns; **CA-013**, business-cycle sector
rotation; **CA-028**, oil-shock sector rotation; all untouched; **AS-010**, separation of ownership
and control, the agency conflict it specialises, recorded not testable.

What would make it testable: firms' free cash flows, investment, acquisitions and payout
announcements — outside the lab's funds.

## Status

AS-011 is recorded `not-testable`: its claim and every refutation read firms' free cash flow,
investment and acquisitions, which the lab does not hold; its return tests in the library are single
firms' announcements and investment, with one market-wide event in 1936, before the lab's years; its
aggregate forms need earnings or investment series the lab does not hold; the overinvestment
anomalies are EF-018's, EF-017's and EF-014's, on single stocks. No card is drawn, and no trial is
spent.
