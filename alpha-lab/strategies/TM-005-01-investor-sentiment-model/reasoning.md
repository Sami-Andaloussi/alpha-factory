# TM-005-01 — Conservatism and representativeness: reasoning, not testable as a theory of its own

## The theory, and the forms its sources give it

TM-005 is Barberis, Shleifer and Vishny's model of investor sentiment: investors who update too
little on an isolated piece of news, through conservatism, and extrapolate a series of news of the
same sign, through representativeness, make prices underreact over 1 to 12 months and overreact
over several years, so that returns continue first and reverse later. Its refutations: no positive
autocorrelation over 1 to 12 months after news, and no reversal over 2 to 5 years; prices that move
by the full amount of an isolated announcement on the announcement; stocks with long series of good
news performing no worse over the following years than stocks with mixed news, or no better in the
short run; arbitrage capital that closes the gap between price and value quickly.

**Its source, as the lab read it in the library.** Barberis, Shleifer and Vishny, "A model of
investor sentiment", in *Advances in Behavioral Finance, Volume II* (Thaler, ed., 2005, chapter 12).
They define underreaction as a higher average return after good news than after bad, and
overreaction as a lower average return after a series of good news announcements than after a
series of bad. They give "only minor attention" to aggregate stock and bond returns, which
"generally do not provide enough information" to reject efficient markets; most of their evidence
is the cross-section of stocks:

- **Underreaction**, over 1 to 12 months: the drift after earnings announcements (Bernard and
  Thomas), after repurchases, dividend initiations and omissions, splits and seasoned offerings, and
  momentum in stock prices (Jegadeesh and Titman); and, weaker by their own account, positive
  autocorrelation of excess index returns over one month to a year (Cutler and others), about 0.1 a
  month for stocks and around zero for US bonds.
- **Overreaction**, over 3 to 5 years: in index returns, a slight negative autocorrelation over
  three to five years in many markets (Fama and French, Poterba and Summers, Cutler and others),
  and high past returns or low dividend yields predicting low returns (Campbell and Shiller); in the
  cross-section, De Bondt and Thaler's five-year losers beating the winners, Zarowin's firms with a
  sequence of bad earnings beating those with a sequence of good, and the value and glamour
  evidence of Lakonishok, Shleifer and Vishny and of La Porta.
- **The series and the single piece of news**: stocks with a consistent record of good news, "and
  hence extremely high past returns", are overvalued; "securities with strings of good performance,
  however measured, receive extremely high valuations", and those valuations return to the mean. In
  simulations of their model, the gap after a string of same-sign earnings changes falls from
  positive after one year to negative after three and four (their Table 12.2), and so does the gap
  between the deciles of highest and lowest cumulative return over one to four years (Table 12.3).
  In their conclusion, one-time strong news should also cause an overreaction, as prices rebounded
  in the weeks after the 1987 crash; "a real test" of it, they write, must await an objective
  measure of the strength of news.

## What the lab can read of it, and where each form already stands

The lab holds the daily closes of funds and of bitcoin, and no announcement dates, earnings,
analysts' forecasts, dividend yields or book values. On those closes, each of TM-005's forms is
either already read by a verdict of the lab or is the claim of another theory of the bank, still
untouched or in progress:

- **The continuation over 1 to 12 months**: read on the lab's funds at the six- and twelve-month
  windows the sources favour — **TM-017-01**, each asset's own trend against cash, stopped at gate
  3; **CA-001-01**, the leaders within the sector funds, the equity markets and the commodities,
  stopped at gate 2, refuted in its base form; **TM-003-01**, the sector funds, stopped at gate 3,
  not proven; **TM-001-01**, the leaders between asset classes, stopped at gate 2, not refuted,
  1.78% a year above the seven funds before costs, 0.57 standard errors from zero. The one-month
  autocorrelation of index returns is unread: it belongs to **TM-017**, still in progress, whose
  refutation is the sign of the past 1 to 12 months, and in its weekly form to **TM-043**, positive
  return autocorrelation; for the lab's US Treasury funds the source reports none.
- **The drift after an isolated announcement**, and prices that move by the whole announcement:
  announcement dates, which the lab does not hold; the claim of **TM-007**, underreaction to public
  information, which names the same drift and the full reaction as its refutation, and of
  **EF-035** (the earnings drift), **EF-029** (repurchases) and **AS-005** (seasoned offerings).
- **A series against mixed news, in the short run**: TM-005's references sign it both ways.
  Barberis, Shleifer and Vishny put a string of same-sign news on the overreaction side, with a
  lower return after it (their Table 12.2); Gray and Vogel (*Quantitative Momentum*, 2016) read the
  frog-in-the-pan result, continuous small gains beating discrete ones, as underreaction in the same
  model. **LL-023-01** tested the second sign among each group's leaders and found the more
  continuous half 2.04% a year lower (standard error 1.44%), on the first sign's side and within
  chance, most of it in months when the pool held funds that had lost. The comparison is read, and
  it is no test of TM-005.
- **The reversal over 3 to 5 years**, and the series against mixed news over the following years:
  on prices, a long record of good news implies a high return over the past three to five years, by
  the source's own inference; that is De Bondt and Thaler's reversal, the claim of **MR-021**,
  long-term overreaction and reversal, untouched, whose refutations name the transitory component of
  index returns and the momentum-then-reversal sequence as well. Within groups, **CA-024-01** read
  it as value: the cheapest third of each group by its past five years earned 0.63% a year less
  than the groups from 2010 to 2022, stopped at gate 2, not refuted, not proven; its reasoning left
  MR-021's own forms — the transitory component of each index's price and the reversal on the
  extremes of past performance — to MR-021. The series of earnings, Zarowin's form, and the value
  and glamour evidence read fundamentals the lab does not hold; the latter is **EF-008**'s claim,
  and La Porta's analysts' forecasts are **MR-039**'s, diagnostic expectations. Campbell and
  Shiller's dividend yield needs yields the lab does not hold.
- **Arbitrage capital closing the gap quickly**: no price form with a sign; it would need the
  capital's positions, and it is the claim of **MR-025**, limits to arbitrage.
- **Crypto-assets**, which the bank names for TM-005: the source's evidence is on stocks and
  indices; the library gives bitcoin's continuation or reversal no sign — one of its books on
  crypto-assets, Burniske and Tatar's, describes moving averages as practitioners' tools, with no
  evidence of either, and their chapter on bitcoin's bubbles measures falls from peaks known only
  afterwards, all six peaking by December 2013, before the lab's prices start. Bitcoin's prices,
  from September 2014, hold in-sample a single span of three to five years and the span after it,
  and a card of one asset is not drawn in any case.

**The bank's siblings.** **TM-006**, overconfidence and biased self-attribution, predicts the same
sequence from another bias and names crypto too, and **TM-036**, extrapolative expectations, the
continuation from extrapolating returns; any bitcoin form with a sign would be theirs as much as
this theory's. TM-006 and TM-007 carry the refutation "no momentum-then-reversal sequence" as well.

**What was considered, and why it does not rescue a card of TM-005's own:**

- **Barberis, Shleifer and Vishny's own sort on past returns** (their Table 12.3, simulated from
  their model), where the gap between winners and losers falls from positive at one year of
  formation to negative at three and four. Its one-year cell is read (TM-001-01, CA-001-01). Its
  three- and four-year cells are De Bondt and Thaler's reversal: MR-021's claim, and read within
  groups by CA-024-01 at three and five years, where the cheapest lost to the groups. Its two-year
  cell is a simulated point between the two, with no evidence behind it in the source.
- **The whole sequence on one ranking** — TM-001-01's leaders between asset classes, held over 1 to
  12 months and then read over 13 to 60: the second half is MR-021's reversal, and its refutation
  "no momentum-then-reversal sequence" is MR-021's too; drawn here, it would spend a trial that
  MR-021's card would then have to count, for the same bet.
- **A long series against a single large move over the same span**, holding the past return fixed:
  the source sorts strings only on earnings; on prices it sorts on the cumulative return (Table
  12.3), and it has single strong events overreacting as well, so it gives no sign to a string
  against a single move of the same size.
- **A single extreme move followed by a rebound**, the conclusion's strong news: the source leaves
  it untested until the strength of news can be measured objectively, and the reversal after
  extreme short-run moves is **MR-002**'s claim, short-term overreaction, and **MR-012**'s after
  liquidity shocks.
- **Bitcoin's own trend**: no sign in the library, and TM-005's source does not read it.

What would make it testable as its own: announcement dates and earnings for the funds' holdings, on
which the single piece of news and the series could be told apart, as Bernard and Thomas and
Zarowin did; or a stock universe, which the lab's scope provides for theories that need one.

## Status

TM-005 is recorded `not-testable` as a theory of its own: on the lab's closes, its continuation is
read by TM-017-01, CA-001-01, TM-003-01 and TM-001-01, and its one-month form belongs to TM-017 and
TM-043; its series in the short run is read by LL-023-01, whose result is no test of it; its
reversal, and the series over the following years, are MR-021's claim, read within groups by
CA-024-01; its announcement drift is TM-007's and needs dates the lab does not hold; its strong
single news is MR-002's; and the library gives its crypto form no sign. No card is drawn, and no
trial is spent.
