# TM-008-01 — Gradual information diffusion: reasoning, not testable as a theory of its own

## The theory, and the forms its sources give it

TM-008 is Hong and Stein's model: newswatchers, who observe private signals that spread slowly among
them and do not infer each other's information from prices, make prices underreact; momentum
traders, who read only past price changes, add to the move and push prices beyond their value, from
which a later correction brings them back. Returns are positively correlated over about 1 to 12
months and partly reversed at longer horizons; momentum is stronger where diffusion is slowest, in
small stocks and stocks with low analyst coverage, and where attention is limited. Its refutations:
no drift after news in stocks with slow diffusion, or momentum no stronger there than elsewhere;
momentum not followed by a partial reversal, or reversal with no prior underreaction.

**Its sources, as the lab read them in the library.** Hong and Stein, "A unified theory of
underreaction, momentum trading, and overreaction in asset markets", in *Advances in Behavioral
Finance, Volume II* (Thaler, ed., 2005, chapter 14). Beyond the first-order predictions —
"positive correlations at short horizons, and negative correlations at longer horizons", which the
model is "designed to do" — they list its "testable auxiliary implications": "(1) both short-run
continuation and long-run reversals should be more pronounced in those (small, low-analyst-coverage)
stocks where information diffuses more slowly; (2) There may be more long-run overreaction to
information that is initially private than to public news announcements; and (3) there should be a
relationship between momentum traders' horizons and the pattern of return autocorrelations", which
they conjecture turns on trading costs: stocks with high bid-ask spreads should have
autocorrelations that stay positive for longer. Hong, Lim and Stein (2000), reproduced there, find
six-month momentum declining sharply with market capitalisation past the smallest stocks, and
stronger and longer-lived in stocks with low residual analyst coverage. The survey in the same
volume (chapter 1) and Ilmanen (*Expected Returns*, 2011, chapter 6) summarise the model; Gray and
Vogel (*Quantitative Momentum*, 2016) name it (chapter 3) and cite Hong, Lim and Stein's size and
coverage as proxies for limited attention beside the frog in the pan (chapter 6).

## What the lab can read of it, and where each form already stands

The lab holds the daily closes of funds and of bitcoin, and the Treasury bill's yield, and no market
capitalisations of single firms, analyst coverage, bid-ask spreads or news dates. On those data,
each of TM-008's forms is either already read by a verdict of the lab or is the claim of another
theory of the bank:

- **The continuation over 1 to 12 months**: read on the lab's funds at the six- and twelve-month
  windows — TM-017-01 (each asset's own trend, stopped at gate 3), CA-001-01 (within groups, stopped
  at gate 2, refuted in its base form), TM-003-01 (sector funds, stopped at gate 3, not proven),
  TM-001-01 (between asset classes, stopped at gate 2, 1.78% a year above the seven funds in equal
  parts before costs, 0.57 standard errors from zero) — and in its weekly form, 2 to 16 weeks, the
  claim of **TM-043**, and at one month of **TM-017**, still in progress. Its bonds and commodities,
  the bank's futures analogy, are read on IEF, TLT, GLD and DBC by TM-017-01 and TM-001-01, and
  among GLD, SLV and DBC by CA-001-01; its macro news, on the bill rate, by TM-007-01 (certain to
  fail gate 1 at a year); the lab trades no currency and passes a strategy no franc rate.
- **The partial reversal at longer horizons, and the sequence**: **MR-021**'s claim, read as value
  within groups by CA-024-01 (0.63% a year below the groups from 2010 to 2022, stopped at gate 2,
  not refuted, not proven), as TM-005-01 recorded.
- **Momentum stronger in small stocks**: firms' sizes, which the lab does not hold. Its one
  fund-level reading, US small caps (IWM) against the large (SPY, QQQ), is **TM-041**'s claim, which
  names Russell 2000 futures by analogy and the less liquid commodity markets; the higher
  autocorrelation of indices dominated by small caps is **TM-043**'s, and **MR-025** names momentum
  in small, illiquid securities among its refutations. The lab holds one small-cap fund, so a
  comparison by size rests on one asset, which the lab tests across several or not at all. No
  verdict has read IWM's own trend against the large caps' — TM-017-01 set IWM aside as repeating
  SPY — but CA-001-01, LL-023-01, TM-024-01, TM-039-01, TM-040-01, TM-006-01, CA-024-01 and
  LL-042-01 held it on the same sessions, which a card of TM-041's discloses.
- **Reversals stronger where diffusion is slow**: the same small, little-covered stocks should be
  most reversal-prone, a prediction Hong and Stein call not yet investigated. On the funds it is
  IWM's long-run reversal against the large caps' — one small-cap fund; each index's reversal is
  **MR-021**'s, and the reversal within the equity funds, IWM among them, is published on the same
  sessions by CA-024-01. No theory of the bank claims the size refinement; it needs a stock
  universe.
- **Momentum stronger and longer with low analyst coverage**: coverage; **TM-025**'s claim, bad news
  travels slowly, where, as the survey reports, momentum among little-covered firms comes almost
  entirely from losers — firm-level, and on the short side the lab does not hold.
- **More overreaction to private information than to public news**: news dates, which the lab does
  not hold; **MR-003** cites Hong and Stein for the overshoot after ambiguous announcements, which
  needs release dates too.
- **Momentum traders' horizon and trading costs**: Hong and Stein conjecture that where trading
  costs are higher momentum traders hold longer and autocorrelations stay positive longer — stocks
  with high bid-ask spreads and, across asset classes, houses and collectibles, positive up to three
  and two years in Cutler, Poterba and Summers — and warn that the stale prices of costly assets
  make autocorrelations spuriously positive. The lab holds no spreads and none of those assets, and
  charges every fund the same 5 bps a side; bitcoin, the one asset it charges more, is one asset,
  and the library gives its trend no sign (TM-005-01). The momentum traders are **FP-024**'s claim,
  positive feedback trading, which names Hong and Stein's; the positive autocorrelation of illiquid
  assets is **MR-007**'s.
- **Where attention is limited**: the frog-in-the-pan form, read by **LL-023-01** (the more
  continuous half of each group's leaders 2.04% a year below the other, standard error 1.44%,
  stopped at gate 2, refuted in its form), as TM-007-01 recorded; **LL-024** (media and limited
  diffusion) and the turnover forms of **TM-027** and **TM-028**.
- **Gradual diffusion between markets** — industries leading the market, one country leading
  another, styles, the price delay of slow assets — the bank's lead-lag family, which reads the same
  slow diffusion between markets (CA-006, LL-009 and LL-017 cite Hong, Torous and Valkanov, 2007;
  **LL-011**, vertical industry links, cites Hong and Stein): **CA-006**, industries leading the
  market, read by **CA-006-01** (stopped at gate 2, not refuted, not proven); **LL-001**, price
  delay; **LL-009**, industry diffusion; **LL-011**; **LL-015**, style links; **LL-017**,
  cross-country diffusion; **LL-042**, cross-asset spillovers, read by **LL-042-01** (stopped at
  gate 2, its pure lead and lag refuted in its narrow form); and the others of the family,
  untouched.

**The bank's siblings.** **TM-005**, **TM-006** and **TM-007**, the other behavioural accounts of
the same continuation: TM-005 and TM-007 recorded not testable as theories of their own, TM-006
drawn as momentum after market gains (TM-006-01, stopped at gate 2, not refuted). **TM-001**'s bank
file claims Hong, Lim and Stein's coverage result too; TM-001-01 left it to TM-025. **FP-024**
claims the momentum traders.

**What was considered, and why it does not rescue a card of TM-008's own:**

- **Momentum stronger or longer-lived in IWM than in SPY and QQQ**: the library signs it on stocks
  sorted by size; on the funds it rests on one small-cap asset, and it is TM-041's claim (above).
- **Autocorrelations positive for longer where trading costs are higher**: signed across asset
  classes only on houses and collectibles, which the lab does not hold; every fund pays the same
  costs, and bitcoin is one asset (above).
- **Reversals stronger in the small-cap fund**: an untested prediction of the source, one asset, and
  a reversal within the equity funds already published by CA-024-01 on the same sessions.
- **The momentum traders' horizon of a year or so**: the first-order continuation and reversal, read
  and MR-021's.
- **The losers' drift under low coverage**: firm-level, TM-025's, and on the short side.

What would make it testable as its own: a stock universe with sizes and analyst coverage, which the
lab's scope provides for theories that need one.

## Status

TM-008 is recorded `not-testable` as a theory of its own: on the lab's data its continuation is read
by TM-017-01, CA-001-01, TM-003-01 and TM-001-01; its reversal is MR-021's; its size form, whose one
fund-level reading is IWM against the large caps, is TM-041's and rests on one asset; its coverage
form is TM-025's; its momentum traders are FP-024's and its trading costs MR-007's and FP-024's,
signed only on assets the lab does not hold; its attention form is read by LL-023-01; its diffusion
between markets is the lead-lag family's. No card is drawn, and no trial is spent.
