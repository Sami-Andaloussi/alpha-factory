# CA-003-01 — Style momentum within sectors and equity markets: reasoning

## Why this theory now

CA-003, factor momentum, holds that factors themselves — value, momentum, low risk and others — show
a persistence of performance of their own: capital moves slowly towards the styles that have done
well, managers are organised by style, smart-beta products receive flows, and investors learn late
which premium is being paid, so that a style that has just earned tends to earn again. Its
prediction: factors with high recent returns go on to earn more than factors with low recent
returns, a persistence distinct from the momentum of single stocks and of industries. Its
refutations: factors' past returns not predicting their later returns; that persistence fully
accounted for by stock or industry momentum. The bank names stocks, sectors, equity indices and
crypto, over months; its references are Gupta and Kelly's *Factor Momentum Everywhere* (2019), which
the library does not hold, and Asness, Moskowitz and Pedersen's *Value and Momentum Everywhere*
(2013), CA-024's reference, known to the lab through the library's books.

A first judgement drew CA-003 as not testable, reading factors as long/short portfolios of single
stocks sorted on accounting data, which the lab does not hold. Its reader showed that the library
reads factors more broadly — Ilmanen calls "factor investing" or "style investing" both long-only
tilts and long/short style premia, rewarded "in multiple asset classes", and Asness, Moskowitz and
Pedersen build value and momentum from index-level assets — that the lab already builds two such
styles from its own funds (CA-001-01, momentum within groups; CA-024-01, value within groups), that
the library gives the persistence a sign and a window, and that four earlier judgements (TM-015-01,
TM-031-01, TM-036-01, TM-038-01) had handed "the momentum of strategies and styles" to CA-003, so
that closing it would leave the form tested nowhere. That judgement was withdrawn before any
commit; this card draws the form.

## The sources

The lab read them in the library's books.

- **Ilmanen** (*Expected Returns*, 2011):
  - §12.3, "Tweaks including style timing": momentum is "the first indicator in many firms' style
    rotation models"; when value works, "it is reasonable to predict further near-term success", and
    value's performance persists mainly over a one-month horizon (table 12.1); style timing is
    fraught with overfitting and at best a modest improvement on a static strategy.
  - §20.2, "Feedback loops on less directional positions": across the major asset classes and
    strategies of the book, 1990 to 2009, the correlation between adjacent months' returns averages
    +0.20 and the long-term reversal, the latest six months against the six months eighteen months
    earlier, −0.11 (figure 20.1); the evidence on short-term momentum is "especially compelling",
    though part of it may reflect illiquidity and smoothing; the size effect in US equities is
    mildly negative in that sample and mildly positive over longer ones. A strategy's success draws
    inflows through extrapolation or learning, then crowding and a later exit (figure 20.2).
- **Ilmanen** (*Investing Amid Low Expected Returns*, 2022):
  - Chapter 6, "Style Premia": factor or style investing covers long-only smart beta and long/short
    style premia; four styles — value, momentum, carry and defensive — rewarded in multiple asset
    classes; smart beta's popularity in the 2010s, then disappointment cooling investors' interest.
    Section 6.2: trend following rewarded on equity long/short factors ("factor momentum"), and
    Gupta and Kelly (2018) and Ehsani and Linnainmaa (2019) finding time-series and cross-sectional
    momentum among stock-selection factors. Section 6.4: defensive, low-risk strategies within
    equity markets, and betting-against-beta strategies working in stock selection and "also in
    equity country allocation".
  - Section 16.2: timing value, momentum, carry and defensive styles with valuations, factor
    momentum and other predictors gave only moderately positive Sharpe ratios over nearly a century;
    the best out-of-sample timing added less than a tenth to a diversified blend's; "some areas of
    supportive evidence" for momentum-based factor timing, none highly robust across subperiods and
    asset classes.
- **Dunham** (chapter 8, "Momentum: The Technical Analysis Anomaly", in Zacks, ed., *Handbook of
  Equity Market Anomalies*, 2011, section "Momentum at Industry Levels"): Lewellen (2002) finds that
  portfolios formed on size and on book to market show momentum as strong as single stocks and
  industries, 0.46% to 0.53% a month for size portfolios and 0.38% to 0.50% for size and
  book-to-market portfolios over a five-month holding period, 1941 to 1999, and concludes that
  momentum is not only firm-specific; Moskowitz and Grinblatt's industry momentum, and O'Neal's
  (2000) in sector funds, in the same section.
- **Campbell** (*Financial Decisions and Markets*, 2018, problem 3.5): shifting sentiment about
  quantitative value strategies may give value short-term positive autocorrelation, "style
  momentum", and longer-term reversal, set as an exercise on a one-year moving average of small
  value against small growth, and the quarterly autocorrelation out to twelve quarters.
- **Christopherson, Carino and Ferson** (*Portfolio Performance Measurement and Benchmarking*, 2009,
  chapter 24, "Styles, Factors, and Equity Benchmarks"): styles leading in turn over rolling
  five-year windows, and the number of managers in each style moving in a lagged fashion with that
  style's excess returns — the flows of the mechanism.
- **Ang** (*Asset Management*, 2014, chapter 14): factors predictable only to a very small degree,
  and investors giving up on factor strategies after losses — the contrarian side, timing by
  valuation.
- **Lo** (*Hedge Funds*, updated edition, 2010, chapter 6): the long-only constraint limits a
  portfolio's factor timing — the lab's own constraint, which this card meets by moving a style's
  share between its long leg and the funds held in equal parts.

## From the claim to a signal

- **The styles.** Three, each built by the rule from the funds' closes, within each of the two
  equity groups the lab already uses — the eleven sector funds, and the five equity markets SPY,
  QQQ, IWM, EFA and EEM — each ranking the group's members that trade and have the prices it needs:
  - **momentum**: the return from 252 sessions to 21 sessions before the session before, CA-001-01's
    base; the long leg is the top third;
  - **value**: the return over the 1,260 sessions to the session before, reversed, CA-024-01's base;
    the long leg is the bottom third, the cheapest by their price then against their price now;
  - **defensive**: the volatility of the daily returns over the 252 sessions to the session before;
    the long leg is the least volatile third, Ilmanen's defensive, low-risk style, which he reports
    in equity country allocation as in stock selection, measured by volatility, the simpler of the
    two statistical low-risk measures he names, low beta and low volatility; a beta against the
    group's own market would add an estimation window and a shrinkage the sources set for stocks.
  Each style's short leg is the opposite third of the same size: the top third by past return for
  value, the bottom third for momentum, the most volatile third for defensive. A third is the number
  of ranked members over three, rounded half to even, one at least, ties to the order of the
  universe; a group with fewer than two ranked members has no legs for the style. The legs are set
  on the first session of each month and kept to the next.
- **The styles, not the carry.** Carry needs yields or futures curves the lab's funds do not give;
  trend, a fund's own past against cash, is TM-017's and TM-038's; the size and growth spreads of
  IWM and QQQ against SPY are inside the equity-markets group's rankings, and on their own would
  make a universe of three funds, which the battery refuses.
- **Each style's own return**, the style's track record: on each session, the mean daily return of
  its long leg less the mean daily return of its short leg, in each group, the groups weighted by
  their numbers of ranked members — a long/short book, computed by the rule from the closes, never
  held. A style has a return on a session only once both its legs are set in at least one group.
- **The timing, the theory's claim**: on the first session of each month, a style whose returns
  summed over the last `window` sessions to the session before are above zero has earned; the card
  predicts that it earns again over the next month. The base's window is 21 sessions, about a month:
  Ilmanen's adjacent months, whose continuation he finds "especially compelling", and his
  one-month persistence of value. The variant's is 252, about a year: Campbell's one-year average,
  a choice of the lab's.
- **Long only.** Each style holds a third of the portfolio. A style that has earned holds its third
  in its long leg, CA-001-01's way: of the N funds trading, a group of n ranked members holds n/N of
  the third, split equally among its long leg, and a fund trading but not ranked holds 1/N of the
  third, its benchmark weight. A style that has not earned, or whose record is shorter than the
  window, holds its third in the N funds in equal parts, the benchmark. The portfolio is the three
  thirds added; it is always fully invested once targets begin.
- **The start**: the targets begin on the first month in which any style's record covers the
  window — momentum and defensive rank their first members in early 2006 — and a style joins when
  its own does; value ranks nothing before 2010. The in-sample period is the lab's, 2005 to 2022.
- **The memory**: the value style reads 1,260 sessions back from the session before a formation,
  and the signal reads the styles' returns up to 252 sessions back, through legs formed up to a
  month earlier: about 1,536 sessions at most. The card declares 1,600.

## What the battery judges, and what to expect

**The size predicted.** The graded quantity is the style's next-month long/short return after it
earned, against after it did not. Ilmanen's +0.20, across asset classes and strategies from 1990 to
2009, is the sources' figure; the lab's own published books point lower — TM-038-01's trend book
autocorrelated at −0.073 at one month, and MR-032-01's members at +0.01 over a week — and styles
built from five or eleven funds are coarse. The card predicts a correlation of about 0 to +0.2 at
one month, at or below Ilmanen's. The styles' monthly volatility, from published figures only:
CA-001-01's long-only tilt tracked its funds at about 6% a year, and a long/short book between the
top and bottom thirds is about twice a top-third tilt, about 12% a year, 3.5% a month. For normal
returns, a correlation ρ makes the mean after a positive month exceed the mean after a negative one
by about 1.6ρ standard deviations: at +0.2, about 1.1% a month; at +0.1, about 0.55%; at 0, nothing.

**The rule's alpha.** Each style's third moves between its long leg and the funds in equal parts.
If a style earns about half its months, the timing adds to that third about a quarter of the
difference above, on a long-only leg that carries about half of the long/short return: at +0.2,
about 1.6% a year for the portfolio from the timing; at 0, nothing. The styles' unconditional
premia add little — momentum's 0.20% a year and value's −0.63% in CA-001-01 and CA-024-01, on a
third each when earned. The card predicts an alpha of about 0 to 1.5% a year over the sixteen funds
held in equal parts, on a tracking error of about 2 to 3% a year: an appraisal ratio of about 0 to
0.7. Gate 4, over some sixty-four effective trials and seventeen years, needs about 1.0: the card
expects to fail gates 3, 4 and 5 — gate 5 fails whenever the blend of the variants fails gate 3 or
4 — possibly gate 2, since the predicted alpha reaches zero, and grades its own clause apart from
them. Costs: at a window of one month, a style's
sign changes about every other month, moving about a third of the portfolio's third each time:
about 0.2 to 0.4% a year at the lab's costs.

**The clause.** Judged before costs, apart from the battery, on the base's styles over the in-sample
months, each style from its own first formation with a 21-session record — value's in April 2010 —
to December 2022: each style's long/short return over the month, from the close of a formation's
session to the close of the next formation's session, regressed by ordinary least squares on a
constant for each style and an indicator that the style had earned at the formation (its 21-session
sum above zero), the standard errors clustered by month. The theory is refuted in this form if the
indicator's coefficient is zero or below: a style's month after it earned no better than after it
lost, as the bank's first refutation reads. Any other result short of passing gates 1 to 7 is not
proven, not refuted; a base that passes gates 1 to 7 while the clause refutes leaves the theory
refuted in this form. The second refutation is not graded: its stock half because the lab holds no
single stocks, its industry half reported by the clause over the equity markets alone, where no
industry enters. TM-024-01's panic group-months: if 90% or more of the momentum style's months in
which either group was in a panic state fall in one of the earned and not-earned sets, they leave
the graded sample and the clause with them is reported; otherwise they stay, disclosed, and the
clause without them is reported — the rule TM-038-01 set for published states.

**The test's power.** About 200 months of momentum and defensive and 155 of value, some 560
observations; the styles correlate within a month (momentum and value at about −0.28 in CA-024-01),
which the clustering takes in. With a monthly volatility of about 3.5% and styles that earn about
half their months, the coefficient is measured to about ±0.3% a month if the styles were
independent, and more widely if momentum's and value's indicators oppose each other as their
returns do: a correlation of +0.2 would show at about three and a half standard errors at best,
+0.1 at about two. With no persistence, the clause refutes half the time; at +0.1, about one time
in thirty.

**Measures stated before the run**, reported, not graded:

- the clause for each style apart, and for the variant, window 252, over its own months;
- the clause without value, whose months begin in 2010, over the months of 2006 to 2013 and of 2014
  to 2022, and over the holdout, 2023 to 2025;
- each style's lag-one to lag-three autocorrelations of its monthly long/short returns, their
  means, and the correlations between the styles;
- the share of months each style has earned, and the rule's average holding in each leg;
- the clause over each group apart, the sector funds and the equity markets: among the equity
  markets no industry enters, the second refutation's industry half as near as the lab's funds
  allow;
- the clause with, for each style, the month's return of its groups' ranked members held in equal
  parts as a further regressor, with a slope for each style, so that a style's persistence is read
  apart from its markets';
- each month, the next month's long/short return of the style with the highest last-21-session
  return less that of the lowest, the bank's cross-sectional reading;
- the correlations of the base's monthly returns hedged of its benchmark with the registry's hedged
  monthly series of CA-001-01 and TM-003-01, over their common months (`lab.compare` refuses cards
  whose universes differ);
- the alpha by year, and in 2009 and 2020, the two momentum crashes of the lab's years.

**Risks named before the run.**

- **The sign.** TM-038-01's published trend book reverted at one month; styles may too.
- **Defensive is short the market.** A least-volatile-minus-most-volatile third is dollar-neutral
  but of negative beta: "defensive has earned" partly means "the market fell last month", so that
  its clause partly reads the market's own monthly persistence, which TM-017-02 found near zero. The
  regression with the markets' return as a further regressor reports it apart.
- **Coarse styles.** A group of five funds gives legs of two; a style's long/short return is then
  the gap between two pairs of funds, as much the funds' own news as the style's.
- **Few independent styles.** Momentum and value correlate negatively; defensive and value may
  overlap in the equity markets. The clustering by month answers part of it.
- **Gate 5's first block.** The rule holds nothing before its first target, about April 2006, and
  value's third in equal parts until 2010: the first blocks judge two styles, not three.
- **Gate 6's neighbours.** Windows of 16 and 26, 10 and 32 sessions all read about the last month;
  they change the sign of a style's record only where its last weeks were near zero — for
  independent daily returns, a 16- or 26-session sum differs in sign from the 21-session sum in
  about 14 to 16% of style-months, some 80 of 555 — so that the targets change on some months, and
  `--try` warns once the card is locked if a neighbour's never do. Leaving a cluster out and one
  fund's share of the profit are low risks for a rule this close to the funds in equal parts.
- **Late funds.** XLRE is ranked by momentum and defensive from late 2017, by value from late 2021;
  XLC by momentum and defensive from mid-2019, by value never in-sample.

## Choices, and the options rejected

- **Factors built from single stocks** (Gupta and Kelly's, Ehsani and Linnainmaa's): the lab holds
  no single stocks and no accounting data.
- **Cross-sectional factor momentum**, holding the styles that earned most against those that earned
  least: with three styles, a ranking picks one; the time-series form keeps each style's own claim,
  and Ilmanen reports both. The cross-sectional reading is reported.
- **Lewellen's form**, relative momentum among size and book-to-market portfolios: among the lab's
  funds it is CA-001-01's rule among the equity markets, QQQ for growth and IWM for size, already
  published.
- **Timing by valuation**, the value spread: CA-022's and CA-015's claims.
- **A short leg**: the lab is long only; the timing moves a style's third between its long leg and
  the funds in equal parts, the only long-only form of a switch between a style and none.
- **The commodity group**: three funds, one a basket; the bank names equities and crypto for this
  theory, and the lab holds one crypto-asset.
- **The styles' parameters as parameters**: the momentum and value styles are the lab's published
  rules, fixed; moving them would test other styles, not the timing.

## Implementation

Long only, sixteen equity funds, each with a European (UCITS) fund that tracks it, monthly targets
filled at the close of the session on which they are set; gate 6 adds a day's delay.
