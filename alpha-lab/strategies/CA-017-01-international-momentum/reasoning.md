# CA-017-01 — International momentum: reasoning, not testable

## The theory, and the forms its sources give it

CA-017 holds that national and regional equity indices rank persistently: the markets with the
strongest recent relative performance keep outperforming the weakest, because macroeconomic news,
earnings revisions, international flows and global investors' preferences diffuse slowly across
borders. It refutes itself if national indices' past relative performance does not predict their
later relative performance; if the persistence is a currency effect, appearing or vanishing with the
currency the returns are measured in, or comes entirely from the geographic concentration of
sectors; or if rankings reverse at changes of global regime often enough to cancel the continuation.
Its references are Rouwenhorst (1998) and Bhojraj and Swaminathan (2006), neither held in the lab's
library; what the library says of them, and of the claim, gives four forms.

- **Momentum among individual stocks in many countries.** Rouwenhorst's paper, as the library
  reports it, ranks stocks, not indices: "individual stock return data from 12 European markets for
  the 1978-1995 period" (*The Handbook of Equity Market Anomalies*, 2011, chapter 8), winners over
  losers earning "about 1% per month". It shows that stock momentum is "not just a U.S. phenomenon";
  the lab holds no stock.
- **Momentum among national indices.** "Chan, Hameed, and Tong (2000), and Bhojraj and Swaminathan
  (2003) find that international stock market indexes also exhibit momentum" (*Advances in
  Behavioral Finance*, volume II, 2005, chapter 10); Huang (2006), on the broad indices of 17
  countries from 1969 to 1999, finds it "only in up markets" (the same handbook). Ilmanen (*Expected
  Returns*, 2011, chapter 14): "Momentum works for country allocation"; his remark that momentum
  gains are more obvious within a country than across countries concerns bonds. Asness, Moskowitz
  and Pedersen (2013, Table I), whose sizes CA-001-01 took, report the top third of 18 country
  indices, from 1978 to 2011, earning an alpha of 4.4% a year against the world index, an appraisal
  ratio of about 0.7.
- **Momentum in the news, not the prices.** The bank's data name earnings revisions; Ilmanen's later
  book (*Investing Amid Low Expected Returns*, 2022) describes a "macro momentum" that "would favor
  an equity market with a benign backdrop - recently strong growth surprises, soft inflation news,
  easy central bank policies, and improving terms of trade", applicable to "cross-country
  allocation".
- **Regime rotations.** The bank's mechanism names exporting markets against domestic ones and
  commodity producers against energy importers.

## Why the lab cannot test it

**The theory ranks national and regional markets, and the lab holds three, one fund each.** The
bank's CA-017 says "some national or regional markets become lasting leaders", and CA-001-01 left it
the ranking of "national and regional markets alone": the lab's regional funds are within its scope,
and the wall is how many there are. Its equity funds are SPY (the United States' large caps), QQQ
and IWM (two baskets inside the United States), EFA (the developed markets outside North America, in
one fund) and EEM (the emerging markets, in one fund). The sources rank eighteen countries or more;
the lab can rank the United States, the developed world outside it taken as one, and the emerging
world taken as one.

**Three markets cannot carry a card.** The four-asset rule bounds a card's universe, not a theory,
and a ranking of three inside a larger universe has been run (CA-024-01 ranked GLD, SLV and DBC as
one group). But a card holding SPY, EFA and EEM alone is refused, since with three assets one of
them carries more than 30% of the profit and gate 6 fails whatever the edge; and a larger universe
holds nothing more of CA-017's. The theory names no other market the lab holds to rank beside the
three but QQQ and IWM, and with them the group is CA-001-01's, published; any other fund added would
be held at a fixed weight or ranked by another theory's claim. Funds held at a fixed weight only to
dilute the shares would pass gate 6's measure, which reads each asset's share of the whole
portfolio's profit, while adding no decision of CA-017's; the ranking's own profit would still rest
on three funds, one of them carrying at least a third of it, which gate 6 would not see. The lab
does not draw a card whose extra assets exist only to get under the four-asset rule: that is a
judgement, and no code checks it. Such a card would also grade CA-001-01's equity group without QQQ
and IWM, a quantity close to one published at +0.19.

**The five equity funds' momentum is published.** CA-001-01 ranked SPY, QQQ, IWM, EFA and EEM as one
of its three groups by their past year, the last month left out, and held the top third, from 2006
to 2022: the equity markets' own appraisal ratio was +0.19, 2.1 standard errors below the sources'
country indices; its variant kept the last month. TM-040-01 ranked the same five by value and
momentum together, and TM-001-01 ranked asset classes among which the three regions stand. A CA-017
card on the five funds by past return would grade a quantity already published, and would set QQQ's
technology and IWM's small caps against two foreign aggregates: a gap of sector and style, which the
bank's second refutation names as the explanation that would refute the theory, not as its test.

**What the three regions' ranking is.** Counted on the lab's closes before this judgement, reading
no return, as the lab's decision of 2026-09-25 allows a count of a signal's changes when the pace is
in doubt — here it was not, so the count is a departure, recorded — and ranked each month by the
past year as CA-001-01 ranked them, the leader among SPY, EFA and EEM changes 28 times from February
2006 to December 2022 with the last month left out (29 decisions for gate 1, the first holding
counting as one), and 29 times with it kept (30 decisions). A count that depends on prices is not a
wall, and faster forms change more: over six months, 42 times with the last month kept and 49 with
it left out; Chan, Hameed and Tong find index momentum "especially when holding periods are one
month or less" (the Zacks handbook, chapter 8). SPY leads in 119 of the 203 months, EEM in 71 and
EFA in 13: the ranking is mostly the United States against the emerging markets, a contest in which
the dollar, commodities and technology's weight may weigh — the currency and sector effects the
second refutation names — as much as national news diffusing slowly; the counts do not say which.

**The currency refutation cannot be graded.** The lab holds two exchange rates, the dollar and the
euro in Swiss francs; EFA's and EEM's returns are in dollars over baskets of many currencies, the
yen, the pound and the emerging currencies among them. Measuring the regions in their own
currencies, which the refutation asks, needs rates the lab does not hold.

**The hand-offs.** Five earlier files name CA-017. CA-001-01 left it the ranking of "national and
regional markets alone"; TM-001-01, which held the two best of seven funds by their past year — SPY,
EFA and EEM among them, with IEF, TLT, GLD and DBC — and stopped at gate 2 (1.78% a year above the
seven before costs, 0.57 standard errors from zero), named "Rouwenhorst's twelve European markets"
as "the claim of CA-017, country-index momentum, still to be drawn"; TM-006-01 left it country-index
momentum across cultures, the library divided on Asia; TM-019-01 noted that CA-017 names earnings
revisions among its data but claims the persistence of country indices' price ranking; CA-016-01
left it momentum across countries. None hands a form the lab can hold beyond those above:
Rouwenhorst's twelve markets, as the library reports them, are the individual stocks of twelve
markets, not twelve indices.

**What was considered, and why it does not rescue the test:**

- **Dual momentum**, the region leading by its past year held only while it beats the bill, a bond
  fund otherwise — Antonacci's global equity form, the one rule on the three regions with a fourth,
  non-regional leg, which the four-asset rule would not refuse when that leg is a fund in the
  universe, SHY or IEF (with the bill alone the universe is three, and refused); a gate 6 failure
  would be likely, SPY leading 119 of 203 months, not certain. The library names Antonacci's book by
  its title alone (Gray and Vogel's *Quantitative Momentum*, its notes; the bibliography of *Finding
  Alphas*), with no rule, no sign and no size, and no form of the United States against the world;
  TM-001-01 set the mix aside because "the library gives the mix no sign"; its absolute half is
  **TM-017**'s, run on SPY, EFA and EEM with four others.
- **Directional trend in each region**, SPY, EFA and EEM each against the bill: **TM-017**'s, run on
  those three funds with four others (TM-017-01 stopped at gate 3 and is paper-traded as the test of
  gate 8's chain; TM-017-02 stopped at gate 2).
- **Faber's country rotation** (*Global Asset Allocation*, 2015, Figure 46, 1976 to 2013) counts the
  previous year's return among its factors; the library describes the figure without a legible sign
  for it.
- **Earnings revisions across countries**: the lab holds no earnings or analysts' forecasts; an
  index's revisions are **EF-034**'s.
- **Macro momentum across countries**: the lab holds no country's growth surprises, inflation news,
  policy rates or terms of trade.
- **Regime rotations**: exporters against domestic markets, producers against importers, need
  country funds the lab does not hold; commodities into equities are **CA-028**'s, and the dollar's
  rotation **CA-014-02**'s, run under CA-014.
- **Reversal across countries at long horizons**: **CA-019**'s.

## The bank's other theories this judgement touches

- **CA-001**, momentum within groups, tested: the five equity funds ranked by their past year, +0.19
  among them, published. **TM-040**, value and momentum within the same groups, and **TM-001**,
  momentum between asset classes, tested inconclusive.
- **TM-017**, trend following, in progress while TM-017-01, stopped at gate 3, is paper-traded as
  the test of gate 8's chain: each region against the bill.
- **CA-016**, equity country allocation, recorded not testable on the same three regions;
  **CA-018**, country value, **CA-019**, reversion across national markets, and **LL-017**,
  cross-country information diffusion, untouched.
- **EF-034**, earnings revisions, and **CA-028**, commodities into equities, untouched; **CA-014**,
  intermarket analysis, tested inconclusive, its dollar relations run by CA-014-02.

## Status

CA-017 is recorded `not-testable`: it ranks national and regional markets, and the lab holds three,
one fund each — too few to carry a card of their own; padded with funds held at a fixed weight, one
of them still bears at least a third of the ranking's profit, which gate 6, reading the whole
portfolio's profit, would not see; and the theory names no other market the lab holds but QQQ and
IWM, with which the group is CA-001-01's, published. That ranking sets American styles against two
foreign aggregates, the sector confound the theory's second refutation names; dual momentum on the
regions, the one form with a fourth leg, has no rule or sign in the library, and its absolute half
is TM-017's; the currency refutation needs the rates of the regions' own currencies. No card is
drawn, and no trial is spent. The theory would become testable on single-country equity funds, a
dozen or more, with the currencies to measure them in.
