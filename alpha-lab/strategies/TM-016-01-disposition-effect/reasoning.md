# TM-016-01 — Disposition effect: reasoning, not testable as a theory of its own

## The theory, and the forms its sources give it

TM-016 holds that investors sell their winners too early and hold their losers too long (Shefrin and
Statman, 1985), so that prices underreact to news around the holders' reference prices: stocks with
a large capital gains overhang, on which most holders sit on unrealised gains, outperform stocks on
which they sit on losses over the following weeks and months, and the overhang captures most of the
momentum effect (Grinblatt and Han). The effect plays out around earnings announcements and is
modulated at the year's end. Its refutations: investors who realise gains and losses at the same
rate; stocks with a large overhang earning no more than stocks with large unrealised losses once
past returns are controlled for; classic momentum surviving intact once the overhang is controlled
for; prices adjusting fully to news whether holders sit on gains or losses; no year-end modulation
of selling by investors holding losses. The bank names stocks and crypto-assets, and daily prices,
trading volumes and earnings announcement dates as its data.

**Its sources, as the lab read them in the library.** The library holds none of the four papers the
bank cites, and reads them through others' books.

- **Shefrin**, *A Behavioral Approach to Asset Pricing* (2008, chapter 29): Odean's brokerage
  accounts from 1987 to 1993, where a stock up in value was about 60% more likely to be sold than
  one down — 14.8% of gains realised against 9.8% of losses over the whole year, 15.2% and 9.4% from
  January to November — the pattern reversing in December, when losses are realised for tax; Barber
  and Odean's own account (Thaler, ed., 2005, chapter 15) finds the same out of sample, 14.4%
  against 8.6% in 78,000 households from 1991 to 1996: single stocks, from accounts; mutual fund
  managers showing the effect less than individuals (Frazzini). **Grinblatt and Han** (§29.4): the
  mean of the holders' reference prices, estimated as an average of past prices weighted by
  turnover, gives the capital gains overhang; in weekly regressions on NYSE and AMEX stocks from
  1962 to 1996, with past returns at one, twelve and thirty-six months, market capitalisation and
  turnover, the overhang is highly significant and the twelve-month return loses its significance;
  its coefficient is larger in December and changes sign in January. **Frazzini** (§29.4.3): the
  overhang of mutual funds' holdings, crossed with the earnings surprise, predicts the drift after
  the announcement, 2.4% a month long and short. Evidence from Taiwan does not support the link to
  momentum (Barber, Lee, Liu and Odean).
- **Barberis and Thaler**, in *Advances in Behavioral Finance, Volume II* (Thaler, ed., 2005,
  chapter 1): the effect is not confined to stocks — Genesove and Mayer find it among home sellers —
  and Grinblatt and Han may explain momentum through it.
- **Ilmanen**, *Expected Returns* (2011, chapter 6): the disposition effect can generate momentum
  and the drift after earnings announcements; the difference between the price and the holders'
  average buying price "needs to be estimated", from data on turnover or on holdings. **Campbell**,
  *Financial Decisions and Markets* (2018, §10.4 and problem 10.2), formalises Grinblatt and Han's
  model for "a market for an individual stock": the price a weighted mean of fundamental value and
  the holders' reference price, the reference moving toward the price at a speed the problem asks
  the reader to relate to trading volume, and left unset — a price rising faster the further it
  stands above the reference; and he notes "a reverse disposition effect for mutual funds", whose
  investors sell their losing funds. **Shleifer** (*Inefficient Markets*, 2000, chapter 5) gives the
  underreaction around earnings (Bernard) and six-month momentum, read through conservatism, not
  through this effect.
- **Pedersen** (*Efficiently Inefficient*, 2015, chapter 12) counts the disposition effect, after
  Shefrin and Statman and Frazzini, among the causes of the initial underreaction that trend
  following trades, beside anchoring, rebalancing against trends and slow-moving capital: a cause of
  time-series momentum, which he measures on liquid futures and forwards, with no measure of the
  effect's own share.

Every size the library measures on prices is on single stocks — Grinblatt and Han's coefficient of
0.004 a week, about 12.5% a year between the 90th and 10th percentiles of the overhang; Frazzini's
2.4% a month; Shumway and Wu's 7% a year in Shanghai — with the overhang estimated from turnover,
funds' holdings or accounts; among futures and currency traders (Locke and Mann, Coval and Shumway,
O'Connell and Teo) it measures the behaviour, not a sign on prices.

## What the lab can read of it, and where each form already stands

The lab holds the daily closes of funds and of bitcoin, and the Treasury bill's yield; it holds no
trades or accounts, no holdings, no earnings dates, and passes a strategy no volume. On those data,
each of TM-016's forms is either unreadable, already read by a verdict of the lab, or the claim of
another theory of the bank:

- **The rates at which gains and losses are realised**: investors' trades, which the lab does not
  hold.
- **The capital gains overhang itself**: the holders' average buying price, which Grinblatt and Han
  weight by turnover and Frazzini takes from funds' holdings; the lab passes no volume and holds no
  holdings. With the turnover held constant, the reference price becomes an average of past prices
  with weights decaying at a rate Grinblatt and Han tie to each stock's own turnover, and the
  overhang a distance of the price from that average: a reading of past prices whose window no
  source gives for funds, among the forms already read on the same sessions — the past year's return
  of each asset against cash (TM-017-01, stopped at gate 3) and within groups (CA-001-01, stopped at
  gate 2, refuted in its base form), six-month returns among sector funds (TM-003-01, stopped at
  gate 3, not proven), and the nearness to a reference price, the 52-week high, among sector funds
  (**TM-002-01**, stopped at gate 3, not proven, 0.21% a year below TM-003-01's ranking, standard
  error 1.01%).
- **Momentum absorbed once the overhang is controlled for**: a horse race between two measures
  across many stocks, which needs the overhang and a stock universe; on the funds, momentum is read
  (above).
- **The drift after earnings announcements**: announcement dates, which the lab does not hold, and
  in Frazzini's form the funds' holdings too; **EF-035**'s claim.
- **The year-end modulation**: Grinblatt and Han's December and January coefficients read on the
  funds as winners gaining before the year's end and losing in January. December strength in the
  year's winners, which **SC-006**'s bank file ties to the size of their unrealised gains, is read
  by **SC-006-01** (the three sector funds nearest their year's high over December's last sessions,
  −0.03% a year against the others, 0.14 of a standard error from zero, not refuted, not proven);
  tax-loss selling is **FP-026**'s claim and the turn of the year **SC-002**'s, read by SC-002-01;
  momentum's January reversal is **TM-023**'s, and TM-001-01 has published its January gap between
  asset classes (−1.84% a year in January against +2.09% in the other months).
- **Crypto-assets**, which the bank names: the holders' cost basis on-chain, which the lab does not
  hold and no other theory of the bank claims (FP-027 and AS-035 read other on-chain data, exchange
  reserves and supply concentration); bitcoin is one asset, and the library gives its trend no sign
  (TM-005-01); none of its books on crypto-assets gives the cost basis a sign.

**The bank's siblings.** **TM-002**, anchoring on the 52-week high, read by TM-002-01; **TM-001**,
**TM-003** and **TM-017**, the momentum the overhang would explain; **EF-035**, the drift after
earnings; **SC-006**, **SC-002**, **FP-026** and **TM-023**, the year's end; **TM-005**,
conservatism, recorded not testable; **TM-012**, mutual funds' momentum trading, recorded not
testable.

**What was considered, and why it does not rescue a card of TM-016's own:**

- **A price-only overhang among the sector funds or the equity markets**: the funds' distance from a
  decaying average of their past prices, ranked within a group. The library signs the overhang only
  on single stocks, with the reference weighted by each stock's turnover; on funds, with no volume,
  the decay rate would be chosen without a source, and the ranking sits between TM-003-01's past
  returns and TM-002-01's 52-week high on the same funds and sessions, both published, so a window
  chosen now would be chosen with their results in view; and the one statement the library makes
  about the effect among funds' investors runs the other way (Campbell's reverse disposition effect
  for mutual funds). A fund's price is tied to its holdings' prices, so the overhang that would
  matter is on the stocks it holds, which the lab does not hold.
- **The overhang in commodities** (Ilmanen, *Expected Returns*, 2011, chapter 14): among the
  refinements of commodity trend following, "momentum and underreaction effects are related to" the
  overhang, with no measure; the lab holds three commodity funds, fewer than a card's four, and the
  chapter's own rule, the price against its 12-month average, is trend following, TM-017's, with
  commodity momentum within groups read by CA-001-01.
- **The overhang's December strength and January reversal on the funds**: SC-006-01 read the year's
  winners in December; the January reversal is TM-023's, whose card must disclose TM-001-01's
  published gap.
- **The disposition effect in housing** (Genesove and Mayer): the lab holds one listed real-estate
  fund, and home sellers' reluctance gives no sign on its price.
- **Bitcoin's holders**: one asset, and its cost basis needs on-chain data.

What would make it testable as its own: a stock universe with turnover, or funds' holdings, from
which the overhang can be estimated, frozen in the snapshot.

## Status

TM-016 is recorded `not-testable` as a theory of its own: its realisation rates need trades, and its
overhang needs turnover or holdings, which the lab does not pass or hold; without them the overhang
is a reading of past prices without a sourced window, among forms read by TM-017-01, CA-001-01,
TM-003-01 and TM-002-01; its drift after earnings is EF-035's; its year-end modulation is read by
SC-006-01 and is SC-002's, FP-026's and TM-023's; its crypto form needs on-chain data. No card is
drawn, and no trial is spent.
