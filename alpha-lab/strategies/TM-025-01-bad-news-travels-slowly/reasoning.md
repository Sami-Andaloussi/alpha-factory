# TM-025-01 — Bad news travels slowly: reasoning, not testable as a theory of its own

## The theory, and the forms its sources give it

TM-025 is Hong, Lim and Stein's (2000) reading of momentum: firm-specific information, and bad news
above all, spreads slowly among investors where few analysts follow a firm and where the firm is
small, so that momentum is more profitable among small and little-covered stocks, and among them
comes mostly from losers continuing to lose; prices adjust to bad news more slowly than to good, in
several stages, over an intermediate horizon. Its refutations: momentum profits that do not vary
with firm size or analyst coverage; bad news incorporated as fast as good news; prices that adjust
in one step to negative disclosures, even for firms whose managers disclose little and whose
coverage is limited. The bank names stocks only, and daily prices, market capitalisations and
analyst coverage as its data.

**Its source, as the lab read it in the library.** The library holds the paper in others' reports,
its own tables among them.

- **Hong and Stein**, in *Advances in Behavioral Finance, Volume II* (Thaler, ed., 2005, chapter
  14), reproduce the paper's tables (14.1 to 14.3, figure 14.5), January 1980 to December 1996,
  six-month momentum on the best and worst 30%: 0.53% a month over all stocks (t 2.61); by NYSE and
  AMEX size decile, none in the smallest, the most in the small deciles past it, and falling to
  nothing in the largest — "once one moves past the very smallest-capitalization stocks" its
  profitability "declines sharply with market cap"; by residual analyst coverage, above the NYSE and
  AMEX 20th size percentile, markedly more momentum among the least covered, the gap coming from the
  losers, and returns positively correlated for up to about two years among the least covered
  against less than one among the most. **Jegadeesh and Titman** (chapter 10, section 5) reproduce
  the coverage table (10.7) and note that it fits overconfidence as well as slow diffusion.
- **Barberis and Thaler**, in *Advances in Behavioral Finance, Volume II* (Thaler, ed., 2005,
  chapter 1): Hong, Lim and Stein confirm that momentum is more prominent among small firms and
  firms with low analyst coverage, and find that among firms with low coverage "momentum is almost
  entirely driven by prior losers continuing to lose": a firm sitting on good news spreads it, while
  bad news is "swept under the carpet".
- **The Zacks *Handbook of Equity Market Anomalies*** (2011, chapter 8): their six-month momentum,
  the best and worst thirtieth percentiles, from January 1980 to December 1996, earns 0.53% a month;
  sorted by size it traces an inverted U, no momentum among the smallest and the largest stocks and
  significant momentum among the medium-sized, most of it from the losers; the bottom 30% by analyst
  coverage earns significantly more momentum than the top 30%.
- **Shefrin**, *Beyond Greed and Fear* (chapter 8): momentum smaller for larger firms more closely
  followed, and the coverage effect stronger among past losers; his *A Behavioral Approach to Asset
  Pricing* (2008, chapter 18) cites the paper only for size standing in for coverage in his own
  survey data. **Gray and Vogel** (*Quantitative Momentum*, 2016, chapter 6) cite it for limited
  attention.
- The paper is also cited, with no sign beyond the stock-level one, in *Short Selling* (Fabozzi,
  ed.) — Miller's disincentive to publicise bad news as one reason for momentum, and Diamond and
  Verrecchia's slower adjustment to bad news under short-sale constraints, read through short
  interest, which the lab does not hold — and in both editions of Chincarini and Kim's *Quantitative
  Equity Portfolio Management* (whose text on neglected firms stresses good news adjusting slowly),
  Jansen's *Machine Learning for Algorithmic Trading* and *Risk-Based and Factor Investing*.

Every sign the library gives for TM-025's asymmetry is on single stocks, sorted by each firm's size
and analyst coverage, and its losers' side is the short side of a long-short portfolio; at the level
of indices and industries the library's signs do not favour it: trend profits lie on the long side
and industry momentum on the winners' side (below).

## What the lab can read of it, and where each form already stands

The lab holds the daily closes of funds and of bitcoin, and the Treasury bill's yield; it holds no
market capitalisations of single firms, no analyst coverage and no disclosures, and trades no short
side. On those data, each of TM-025's forms is either unreadable, already read by a verdict of the
lab, or the claim of another theory of the bank:

- **Momentum varying with firm size and analyst coverage**: firms' sizes and coverage, which the lab
  does not hold; its one fund-level reading of size, IWM against the large caps, is **TM-041**'s
  claim and rests on one small-cap fund (TM-008-01), and the library's size result is on stocks, not
  on an index's own trend.
- **Bad news incorporated more slowly than good, and losers continuing to lose**: on firms with low
  coverage, and on the short side; the funds, IWM aside, hold baskets of the most covered firms,
  where the source predicts the least momentum. Price momentum without the coverage split is read on
  the lab's funds — TM-017-01, CA-001-01, TM-003-01 and TM-001-01.
- **Prices adjusting in one step to negative disclosures**: disclosure dates and news, which the lab
  does not hold; the drift after earnings announcements is **EF-035**'s claim.
- **The slow diffusion of information itself**: **TM-008**'s, Hong and Stein's model, recorded not
  testable, which handed its coverage form here.

**The bank's siblings.** **TM-008**, gradual information diffusion, recorded not testable;
**TM-041**, momentum and size; **LL-009**, industry information diffusion, untouched, the one other
theory of the bank that claims a bad-news asymmetry — small firms lagging large ones in the same
industry, after Hou (2007), whom the library does not hold, and whose pairs of small and large firms
within a sector the lab does not hold; **TM-007**, underreaction to public information, recorded not
testable; **EF-035**, the drift after announcements; **TM-001** and **TM-003**, momentum among
stocks and industries, read.

**What was considered, and why it does not rescue a card of TM-025's own:**

- **Underweighting each group's laggards, the long-only side of losers continuing to lose**: the
  library signs the losers' drift only among firms with low coverage, and the lab's funds hold the
  most covered firms, where it predicts the least momentum; at the level of industries the library
  signs momentum on the winners' side (Moskowitz and Grinblatt, in the Zacks handbook, chapter 8);
  the leaders' side of the same ranking is CA-001-01's, refuted in its base form, and the laggards'
  side would be a trial on the same ranking with no sign of its own for funds.
- **A fund's own downtrend lasting longer than its uptrend**: the library's signs at the level of
  indices and sectors run the other way — trend following on futures earns on the long side and
  loses on the short outside crises, equity indices +5.44% a year long against −1.33% short since
  2001 (Clenow, *Following the Trend*, chapter 5), and industry momentum comes from the winners, not
  the losers (Moskowitz and Grinblatt, in the Zacks handbook, chapter 8). The long-or-cash trend on
  the funds is read by TM-017-01, the trend after the market's falls by TM-018-01 (its crisis
  variant −1.18% a year against TM-017-01's rule held always), and momentum within groups in panic
  and down states by TM-024-01 and TM-006-01 (no weaker after two years of losses, +0.19% a year, t
  +0.04).
- **IWM against SPY and QQQ**: TM-041's, and one asset; the library's size result sorts stocks,
  momentum peaking among small stocks past the microcaps and absent among the largest, and gives no
  sign for a small-cap index's own trend.

What would make it testable as its own: a stock universe with market capitalisations and analyst
coverage, which the lab's scope provides for theories that need one.

## Status

TM-025 is recorded `not-testable` as a theory of its own: its size and coverage forms need firms'
sizes and coverage the lab does not hold, and its one fund-level size reading is TM-041's; its
losers' drift is signed on little-covered firms and on the short side, which the lab's funds and its
long-only rules cannot read; at the level of indices and industries the library signs momentum on
the winners' side and trend on the long side; its adjustment to disclosures is EF-035's; its
diffusion is TM-008's. No card is drawn, and no trial is spent.
