# TM-011-01 — Informational cascades: reasoning, not testable as a theory of its own

## The theory, and the forms its sources give it

TM-011 holds that under great uncertainty investors who observe the actions of others rationally
set aside their own information and follow them (Bikhchandani, Hirshleifer and Welch, 1992): an
initial move attracts imitators, the move continues over days to weeks, prices can stay away from
value for a long time, and phases of continuation end in breaks of regime; when uncertainty is high,
even rational arbitrageurs may follow the crowd (De Long, Shleifer, Summers and Waldmann, 1990). Its
refutations: investors' trades that follow their own signals rather than others' actions, even
under high uncertainty; no clustering of trades in the direction of earlier trades, and no stronger
continuation of price moves when uncertainty is high; prices that return quickly to value after a
burst of imitative buying.

**Its sources, as the lab read them in the library.** The bank cites Bikhchandani, Hirshleifer and
Welch, and De Long and co-authors; the library holds neither paper, and reads cascades through
others' books.

- **Hirshleifer and Teoh**, "Thought and behavior contagion in capital markets", chapter 1 of the
  *Handbook of Financial Markets: Dynamics and Evolution* (Hens and Schenk-Hoppé, eds., 2009),
  survey the theory and its evidence: cascades form only when actions are discrete, bounded or
  gapped, since a continuous action always reveals some of the private signal. In their section on
  prices (1.9), rational cascades in a frictionless market cannot explain return predictability; in
  a liquid securities market, price moves against imitative trades, so trading cascades need
  frictions — price limits, prices held continuous in crashes, transaction costs — and then show as
  blocked information and sudden avalanches (Lee, 1998), not as a signed continuation. They report
  institutions as a source of positive serial correlation in portfolio returns (Sias and Starks,
  1997), and the autocorrelation of emerging-market returns rising sharply while institutions
  expanded there (Aitken, 1998).
- **Aronson** (*Evidence-Based Technical Analysis*, 2007) argues that the uncertainties of investing
  make imitation likely, that imitation gives rise to cascades, that trends spawned by positive
  feedback can be seen as a cascade of trend-following signals, and that trend following is most
  effective when positive feedback dominates, a state he does not measure; his case study of 6,402
  rules on the S&P 500 from 1980 to 2005, which read no such state, found the best of them not
  significant after his correction for data mining.
- **Singal** (*Beyond the Random Walk*, 2003) names cascades of information — good news followed by
  more good news — not of imitation, among the reasons a drift after news persists; he finds no
  drift after large price moves alone, and a continuation over twenty days with high volume, and
  clearly only with public news too, among single stocks.
- **Ilmanen** (*Expected Returns*, 2011, chapter 6) names information cascades and groupthink,
  beside Shiller's word-of-mouth, among the social influences that support a bubble.

None of them gives a rule on prices alone of TM-011's sign a measured size: Singal's measured
continuation needs volume and news, which the lab does not pass a strategy.

## What the lab can read of it, and where each form already stands

The lab holds the daily closes of funds and of bitcoin, and the Treasury bill's yield; it holds no
trades, flows, positions or holdings, and passes a strategy no volume. On those data, each of
TM-011's forms is either already read by a verdict of the lab or is the claim of another theory of
the bank:

- **The clustering of trades in the direction of earlier trades, and imitation itself**: trades,
  flows and holdings, which the lab does not hold; **LL-020**, information cascades and rational
  observational learning, claims the same cascades among investors, **TM-031** behavioural herding,
  **TM-029** reputational herding and **FP-009** institutional herding.
- **The continuation of price moves over days to weeks**: at the index level, the positive
  autocorrelation of weekly and monthly returns over 2 to 16 weeks is **TM-043**'s claim, untouched;
  within the lab's groups, over a week, **MR-032-01** found a fund's past five sessions against its
  group predicting its next week with a rank correlation of +0.0106 (standard error 0.0114), a
  slight continuation that refuted its reversal; the trend over months is read by TM-017-01,
  CA-001-01, TM-003-01 and TM-001-01.
- **A continuation stronger when uncertainty is high**: the library signs it the other way. Ilmanen
  (*Expected Returns*, 2011, chapter 14) finds momentum and trend strategies doing better after
  periods of low volatility and reversal strategies after high volatility, momentum profits lower
  after "periods of heightened volatility", and short-term reversals stronger over a day or a week
  amid high volatility. At days to weeks the form is **MR-001**'s, short-term reversal as liquidity
  provision, which should strengthen with volatility and stress, and **FP-024**'s, whose positive
  feedback should be strongest in volatile markets: opposite signs, both untouched. Within the lab's
  groups, MR-032-01 reported its weekly rank correlation in 2008 and 2020, the years of high
  volatility, at +0.008 and +0.010 (standard error 0.056 each), not graded; a card of MR-001's that
  grades the weekly correlation by each group's volatility state leaves those years out and reports
  them apart. At months it is read: TM-047-01 (trend scaled down when volatility is high: a Sharpe
  ratio of 0.58 against 0.495, the gain from its tilt to bonds, not its timing), TM-018-01 (trend
  held only after crises or divergent months, about 1.2% a year of alpha below trend held always,
  TM-017-01's edge negative after crises, −1.75% a year), TM-024-01's panic states and TM-039-01's
  dispersed months, where the twelve-month momentum tilt lost; the crisis form is also **TM-038**'s
  and **TM-031**'s, and cascades in stress **FP-006**'s.
- **Prices kept away from value, and breaks of regime**: the lab holds no value measure for its
  funds; a bubble and its end are **MR-023**'s claim, positive feedback and its correction
  **FP-024**'s, and the momentum crash at a reversal of herding **TM-031**'s; the crash in panic
  states is **TM-024**'s, read by TM-024-01.
- **Crypto-assets**, where herding is often named: the bank does not name them for TM-011; herding
  in crypto-assets is named by **TM-031** (the 2017–18 boom) and positive feedback on crypto futures
  by **FP-024**; the library gives bitcoin's trend no sign (TM-005-01), and a card of one asset is
  not drawn.

**The bank's siblings.** **LL-020**, the same cascades in the lead-lag family; **TM-031**,
**TM-029** and **FP-009**, herding; **FP-024**, positive feedback; **FP-006**, liquidity spirals;
**MR-001**, short-term reversal; **MR-023**, bubbles; **TM-043**, weekly autocorrelation;
**TM-018**, **TM-047** and **TM-024**, read; **TM-038** and **TM-036**; **TM-012**, **TM-015**,
**FP-020** and **FP-025**, the flow and holding forms of imitation.

**What was considered, and why it does not rescue a card of TM-011's own:**

- **Weekly continuation within groups, stronger in high-volatility weeks**: the library signs it
  against TM-011; it belongs to MR-001 (the library's sign) and FP-024 (TM-011's sign), and drawn
  here it would spend a trial their cards must count for the same bet; MR-032-01's 2008 and 2020
  figures are published on the same sessions.
- **Continuation after large moves**: Singal finds none on prices alone; the volume forms are
  **MR-011**'s and **TM-027**'s, and the lab passes no volume; the reversal after extreme moves is
  **MR-002**'s; TM-007-01 set aside the funds' drift after their largest moves.
- **Herding in commodities**: Zaremba (*The Financialization of Commodity Markets*, 2015) gives a
  mechanism — Froot, Scharfstein and Stein's herding, feedback trading by index investors — and no
  measured sign; commodity trend is read by TM-017-01, TM-001-01 and CA-001-01, and MR-032-01
  published a weekly continuation among GLD, SLV and DBC of +0.046 (0.0243), so a form chosen now
  would be chosen from a published result on the same years.
- **Bitcoin**: named by TM-031 and FP-024, not by TM-011; no sign, and one asset.
- **Trends ending in abrupt breaks**: TM-031's, TM-024's, FP-024's and MR-023's; the library gives
  no rule that times the break.
- **Continuation in crises**: read by TM-018-01, refuted in its narrow form; also TM-038's.
- **Emerging-market autocorrelation with institutional entry** (Aitken): one emerging-market fund,
  and it needs flows.

What would make it testable as its own: investors' trades or flows, on which imitation and its
clustering could be read.

## Status

TM-011 is recorded `not-testable` as a theory of its own: its imitation and clustering of trades
need trades and flows the lab does not hold, and are LL-020's, TM-031's and FP-009's; its
continuation over days to weeks is TM-043's at the index level and was read within groups by
MR-032-01; its stronger continuation under uncertainty is signed the other way by the library and is
MR-001's and FP-024's at days to weeks, and read at months by TM-047-01, TM-018-01, TM-024-01 and
TM-039-01; its long deviations and breaks are MR-023's, FP-024's, TM-031's and TM-024's. No card is
drawn, and no trial is spent.
