# CA-014-01 — Sector rotation on the commodity/bond ratio: reasoning

## Why this theory now

CA-014, intermarket analysis, holds that the major markets absorb turns of growth and inflation at
different speeds and through different channels — bonds through rates, equities through earnings,
commodities through demand and supply, currencies through growth and yields — so that one market's
trend tells another's. Its source, Murphy's *Intermarket Analysis* (2004), is a practitioner's book
of charts. Its recap of the principles that ruled the last thirty years of the twentieth century
(chapter 6) lists the market relationships — the dollar and commodities trend in opposite
directions, bond prices and commodities in opposite directions, bonds and stocks normally together,
bonds peaking and troughing ahead of stocks — and the effects of the commodity/bond ratio: a rising
ratio favours inflation-type stocks — gold, energy and basic materials — and a falling ratio the
rate-sensitive ones — consumer staples, drugs, financials and utilities. Its chapters 3 and 4 build
the ratio — the CRB Index, or any commodity index, over Treasury bond or note prices — and use it
for sector rotation, consumer staples being tied to bonds and cyclicals to commodities; chapter 11
uses ratios between the asset classes to hold the stronger, a rising commodity/bond ratio favouring
commodities over bonds.

Murphy himself says that several of the relations changed after 2000, and records that in 1998,
when bonds rose while stocks fell, the link between bonds and stocks broke while the one between
commodities and bonds held. The commodity/bond link, and the rotation the ratio drove, are the ones he
records as holding through 1998.

The lab has tested one of the other principles already. LL-042-01 held equities while Treasuries
rose, Murphy's bonds turning before stocks, and found the lead and lag refuted in a narrow form. The
dollar relations cannot be tested: the lab holds no currency. This card tests the principle the lab
can hold whole, with its own instruments: the commodity/bond ratio and the rotation it is said to
drive.

The bank's neighbours: **LL-042**, cross-asset momentum between bonds and equities; **CA-012**,
sector rotation on monetary conditions, recorded not testable; **CA-001**, momentum within groups;
**TM-017**, trend following, each asset against cash, where this ratio's trend is the relative trend
of two assets.

## From the principle to a signal

- **The ratio.** DBC, a commodity index fund, over IEF, seven- to ten-year Treasury notes, as Murphy
  divides a commodity index by Treasury bond or note prices. Read from the market's signal prices,
  up to the session before, whether or not DBC and IEF trade that day, so that gate 6 can leave the
  commodity or bond cluster out and the rule still reads its signal. The lab's closes are adjusted
  for distributions, which adds IEF's coupon to the denominator: a small drift of the ratio towards
  the rate-sensitive side.
- **Its trend.** Above the mean of its last 200 sessions, the ratio is rising; at or below, falling.
  Murphy reads the major trend of markets from their 200-day average on daily charts and their
  40-week average on a Friday close, and of ratios too — the REIT/S&P 500 ratio crossing its 200-day
  average (chapter 7). He reads the commodity/bond ratio itself only with trendlines, some of them
  twelve years long; trendlines are drawn by eye and have no rule to lock, and the average is the
  device he uses for a trend's direction elsewhere.
- **The two sides.** Rising: energy (XLE), materials (XLB), gold (GLD) and commodities (DBC);
  falling: consumer staples (XLP), health care (XLV), the nearest fund, of which drug makers are a
  part — Murphy also files drugs under consumer staples — financials (XLF), utilities (XLU) and
  Treasury notes (IEF). Murphy's list for the ratio names stocks; his chapter 11 adds the classes
  themselves, commodities against bonds, and allows gold or gold-mining shares, so that the metal
  stands in for the gold stocks the lab does not hold (XLB also holds some miners). The base holds
  both; the variant, the sector funds alone, is the purer test of the intermarket claim, since a rule
  that holds DBC when DBC outruns IEF also earns the ratio's own trend. GLD, a commodity, is left out
  of the variant with DBC and IEF.
- **The pace.** Each week, on its first session, reading the close of the session before: Murphy
  reads the 200-day average on daily charts and the 40-week one on a Friday close; he gives no
  monthly reading. The count of the signal's switches was made before this card, which departs from
  the lab's practice, set by LL-042-01, of measuring no such figure before the card is committed:
  with a 200-session average, the signal changes side 64 times from 2006 to 2022 read weekly, and 30
  times read monthly. Monthly was never the source's reading, so the count did not decide the pace;
  it is written here so that it is seen, and the verdict will record the departure. Most of the
  weekly switches beyond the monthly ones are probably whipsaws around the same turning points: they
  count at gate 1, but they are not independent evidence, the warning CA-012's reasoning gave about
  a signal that changes more often.
- **Before the mean exists**, from the sample's start to November 2006 (DBC trades from February
  2006), nothing is held; the run starts at the first target.

Two variants, not three.

## What the battery judges, and what to expect

The benchmark holds the nine funds in equal parts, both sides at once: the alpha is what choosing
the side adds.

**The size predicted.** Murphy reports charts and episodes — 1993–94, 1997–98, 1999 — not
statistics, and no study the lab holds measures this rule. The card assumes a modest edge, an
appraisal ratio of about 0.2 to 0.3, and says so: the prediction is not the source's. On the
inflation side the base holds a quarter in each of four funds where the benchmark holds a ninth;
by a rough estimate of the spread between the two sides, its tracking error, the volatility of its
bets against the benchmark, is about 9 to 10% a year,
so that the assumed ratio is an alpha of about 2 to 3% a year.

**The regimes the theory excludes.** The bank's theory says the relations are strongly limited in
atypical deflationary regimes, in periods of overwhelmingly dominant monetary policy and at structural
breaks. The card dates one of the three, dominant monetary policy, by the bill rate: below 0.25% a
year, the top of the Federal Reserve's target range at its zero bound. On the lab's series the bill
was below it intermittently from September 2008, continuously from March 2009 to December 2015 with
returns below it until September 2016, and from March 2020 to February 2022: about three fifths of
the sessions from the first target. Deflation and structural breaks are not excluded, which makes
the test stricter on the theory; the ordinary sessions include most of 2008's deflationary shock.
The clause asks, before refuting, that the rule fail in the ordinary regimes too, as the bank's own
refutation asks; the split can turn a refutation into not proven, never a failure into a pass.

**The test's power.** Over about 16 years, an appraisal ratio is measured to about ±0.25; over the
ordinary regimes alone, about seven years, to about ±0.38. By a rough estimate, the clause refutes a
rule with no edge about one time in five, and a rule with the predicted 0.25 about one time in
twenty: when the whole-sample ratio is −0.1 or less, the ordinary-regime figure is zero or less
about 80% of the time, so the regime condition costs little power. Gate 4 will count about twelve
effective trials; an appraisal ratio of about 0.7 would be needed to pass it.

**Risks named before the run.**

- **A defensive tilt.** The ratio fell through much of 2011 to 2020, so the rule may sit on the
  rate-sensitive side — staples, health care, utilities, notes — for years; a defensive mix earned
  an appraisal ratio of 0.61 held fixed in TM-002-01's verdict. The verdict will report the share of
  time on each side and the alpha of the rule's average mix held fixed.
- **Financials.** Murphy files them as rate-sensitive, but in the lab's years banks often rose with
  yields, and XLF collapsed in 2008 while bonds rallied.
- **Gold.** Since about 2019 it has often moved with bonds, through real yields, rather than with
  commodities.
- **One fund above 30% of the profit.** Each side holds four or five funds; gold, which rose from
  2005 to 2011, or energy in 2021–22, may carry more than 30%.
- **The ratio's own trend.** In the base, DBC and IEF are held on the side their ratio favours:
  part of the alpha may be the ratio's momentum, which the variant removes.
- **2008.** The ratio rose into mid-2008 and collapsed in the second half, when the rule held the
  inflation side until the 200-session mean turned: a large loss is likely in that year.
- **Gate 6.** The clusters are the sectors, metals, commodities and bonds. Without the sectors, the
  base keeps three funds — GLD and DBC on the inflation side, IEF alone on the rate-sensitive one.
  Gate 6 moves the base only; the variant, which would hold nothing without the sectors, is not run
  there.

## Choices, and the options rejected

- **The bond/stock ratio of chapter 11**, holding bonds or stocks by their relative trend: an
  asset-class momentum rule, close to CA-001's and TM-017's, which tests the ratio's persistence
  rather than one market's trend moving another.
- **Industrials and consumer discretionary on the inflation side**: chapter 4's cyclical index
  includes industrials, but the recap's list for the ratio names gold, energy and basic materials;
  the card follows the recap, the principle's own statement.
- **The dollar relations**: no currency in the lab.
- **Bonds leading stocks**: LL-042-01's test.
- **Trendlines**: no rule to lock.
- **A continuous tilt** by the ratio's distance from its mean: Murphy reads a direction, not a size.
- **Long bonds (TLT) as the bond leg**: Murphy uses bonds or notes; IEF is the note, less exposed to
  duration than TLT, closer to the rate-sensitive stocks it stands with.

## Implementation

Long only, fully invested whenever a fund of the side held trades, nine funds, each with a European
(UCITS) fund or an exchange-traded commodity that tracks it, rebalanced weekly on closes with the
orders filled at the next close: the ratio reads the session before, so a day of delay is built in,
and gate 6 adds another.
