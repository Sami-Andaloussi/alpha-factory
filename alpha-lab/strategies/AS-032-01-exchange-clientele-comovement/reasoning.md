# AS-032-01 — Exchange clientele comovement: reasoning, not testable as a theory of its own

## The theory, and the forms its sources give it

AS-032 holds that tokens sharing the same exchanges, or the same investor clienteles, comove beyond
their individual fundamentals, even with no apparent fundamental link, the comovement recurring and
intensifying during speculative flows or news specific to a platform. Its refutations: tokens on the
same exchanges comoving no more than tokens on different ones, once fundamentals and the crypto
market are accounted for; a platform's flows and news moving only the tokens they concern; a token's
comovement with an exchange's other tokens unchanged when it is listed or delisted there. The bank
names crypto, over days and weeks, and exchange listings, daily prices and volumes by exchange; its
reference is Shams's working paper *Cryptocurrency Exchanges and Comovements of Cryptocurrency
Returns* (2022), which the library does not hold.

**Its sources, as the lab read them in the library.** The reference is not in the library, and the
lab does not read papers outside it. On shares, the library states the theory's mechanism directly:

- **Habitat and category** (Barberis and Thaler, chapter 1 in Thaler, ed., *Advances in Behavioral
  Finance, Volume II*, 2005, section 6.2): investors who trade only a subset of securities put a
  common factor in the returns of their main holdings, the "habitat" view; investors who allocate by
  category make stocks added to a category comove more with it, a stock's beta with the S&P 500
  rising on inclusion and its beta with stocks outside the index falling (Barberis and Shleifer,
  2003; Barberis, Shleifer and Wurgler, 2001) — the theory's third refutation on shares, an untimed
  claim and single stocks, **FP-011**'s form.
- **The location of trade**: twin stocks with fixed shares of one cash flow moving more like the
  market where each trades most, Royal Dutch with the American market and Shell with the British
  (Froot and Dabora, chapter 3 of the same book; Shleifer, *Inefficient Markets*, 2000, section
  2.1); securities fundamentally unrelated comoving because similar investors trade them (Shleifer,
  chapter 3), closed-end funds comoving with stocks held mostly by individuals, and closed-end
  country funds traded in the United States comoving as much with the American market as with the
  market of their assets (Shleifer, sections 3.4 and 3.5; Barberis and Thaler, section 6.2) — single
  stocks and funds, **MR-037**'s and **MR-026**'s forms; share prices reacting to inclusion in the
  S&P 500 (Shleifer, section 2.3, on Wurgler and Zhuravskaya), **FP-011**'s.

The library signs no comovement of tokens by exchange.

**Return signs near the theory.** On crypto, the library ties comovement to shared investors:
bitcoin's correlation with the capital markets near zero while few of their investors held it, and
expected to rise, positively or negatively, as their investors overlap (Burniske and Tatar,
*Cryptoassets*, 2017, chapters 7 and 9) — an untimed claim on bitcoin and the capital markets,
**CA-033**'s form; and, before the decision on the Winklevoss fund on 10 March 2017, bitcoin more
correlated with ether and monero, held with it in crypto portfolios, and negatively with litecoin,
which investors might have left for bitcoin (chapter 9) — a past episode on tokens the lab does not
hold; crypto's common factors are **CA-032**'s form. Smaller, less liquid exchanges seeing flash
crashes and spikes (Voshmgir, *Token Economy*, second edition, 2020, Part 3, "Challenges of
Centralized Exchanges") is an untimed claim on venues the lab does not see. None gives a timed rule
on exchanges.

## What the lab can read of it

The lab holds the daily bars and volumes of twenty-two funds and bitcoin — the funds from 2005 or
their later start, bitcoin from September 2014 — adjusted for dividends and splits, which encode the
funds' distributions, though no strategy reads them until a point-in-time reading with its own test
is built; the Treasury bill's yield from 2005 and two franc exchange rates; the funds' prices, never
their holdings' prices; and no other cryptoasset, no listing calendar and no volume by exchange; its
snapshot is closed, an asset or a series being added only before the first card. Nothing was
computed from the bars for this judgement. On those data:

- **Comovement of tokens sharing an exchange**, the claim and every refutation: a cross-section of
  tokens and their exchanges, of which the lab holds one token and no exchange's listings; bitcoin
  trades on every exchange, so it cannot be sorted by venue.
- **Platform shocks on bitcoin**, the theory's intensification during news specific to a platform:
  inside bitcoin's in-sample years the library dates about eight — hacks of exchanges in 2016, 2018
  and 2019 (Harvey, Ramachandran and Santoro, *DeFi and the Future of Finance*, 2021, chapter VII,
  "Custodial Risk"), China's investigation of its exchanges from 6 January 2017, after which trading
  in dollars and yen replaced trading in yuan (Burniske and Tatar, chapter 9), an exchange's client
  funds frozen at its payment processor by mid-2018 and its New York settlement in April 2019
  (Schianchi and Mantovi, *The Economics of Cryptocurrencies and Digital Money*, 2023, section 6.1),
  and the failures of FTX in November 2022 and of Celsius in 2022 (same book, section 1.1.2, which
  judges bitcoin's foundations seemingly not undermined by spring 2023; Huang and others, *Web3*,
  2024, chapter 7) — most dated to the year alone and none with bitcoin's return; in the holdout
  only the SEC's actions against two large exchanges in 2023, dated to no month (Huang and others,
  section 6.1.4). A rule that enters and leaves around each makes at most about seventeen decisions
  in-sample and three in the holdout, fewer if events fall within five sessions of one another,
  certain to fail gate 1's thirty whatever the prices (RUNBOOK step 9).
- **Bitcoin's comovement with the funds**: readable, but it is not a clientele of exchanges; bitcoin
  shares its holders with the funds through crowded ownership, **FP-010**'s form, through funds that
  hold it, none of them in the lab's universe, **FP-014**'s, and through shared investors across
  markets, **CA-033**'s.
- **A fixed stance on bitcoin** makes one decision, and a strategy on bitcoin alone breaks the
  RUNBOOK's rule of at least four assets.

**The bank's siblings, and how AS-032 differs.** **AS-031**, the exchange listing shock, recorded
not testable, the listing's own premium where AS-032 claims what listed tokens do together;
**AS-009**, market segmentation, recorded not testable, which read bitcoin's access events and the
location of trade; **AS-008**, clientele effects, recorded not testable, on shares; **LL-037**,
fragmented crypto venues' price discovery, venue clienteles read as lead and lag rather than
comovement; **MR-038**, crypto's segmentation across exchanges, price gaps between venues rather
than comovement; **MR-037**, twin shares' deviations from their parity, the location of trade on
shares; **MR-026**, closed-end fund discounts, comoving with individuals' stocks; **FP-010**, common
ownership and crowding, comovement from shared holders on shares and crypto; **FP-011**, index
inclusion, the category view's test; **FP-014**, flows into funds, crypto among them; **FP-021**,
common fund-flow beta, comovement from shared fund flows; **LL-019**, common institutional
ownership, on shares; **CA-032**, common risk factors in crypto, what all tokens share rather than
what a venue's tokens share; **CA-033**, crypto-equity connectedness; **EF-008**, the value premium,
whose value and glamour stocks comove by their standing as behaviourists read it (Ilmanen, *Expected
Returns*, 2011, section 12.4); **FP-006**, liquidity spirals, margin calls on centralised exchanges;
**FP-027**, exchange reserves and flows; all untouched but AS-008, AS-009 and AS-031.

What would make it testable: many tokens with their listings by exchange and volumes by exchange
over the lab's years — outside the lab's data — and a timed rule in the library.

## Status

AS-032 is recorded `not-testable`: its claim and every refutation compare tokens by the exchanges
they share, and the lab holds one token, traded everywhere, and no listing or venue data; its
reference is not in the library, whose habitat, category and location-of-trade evidence is on shares
and funds; platform shocks on bitcoin are about eight in-sample and one in the holdout, at most
about seventeen decisions. No card is drawn, and no trial is spent.
