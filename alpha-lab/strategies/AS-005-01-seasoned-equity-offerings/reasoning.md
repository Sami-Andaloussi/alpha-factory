# AS-005-01 — Seasoned equity offerings: reasoning, not testable as a theory of its own

## The theory, and the forms its sources give it

AS-005 holds that a listed firm's new issue of shares is read by the market either as a genuine need
for financing or as insiders exploiting an overvaluation — Tirole's lemons problem, adverse
selection and market timing — and that the price reacts accordingly, mostly at the announcement and
at the pricing, and in the regimes where equity again becomes the marginal source of financing. Its
refutations: no average price reaction at the announcements and pricings; a reaction unrelated to
the information asymmetry between insiders and the market, issues by firms hard to value received as
those by transparent firms; certification, collateral or the design of the securities removing the
reaction entirely. The bank names stocks, days, and offering announcement dates, offer prices and
daily prices.

**Its sources, as the lab read them in the library.**

- **Tirole** (*The Theory of Corporate Finance*, 2006, section 2.5.3): a permanent fall of about 3%
  in the share price after the announcement of a seasoned equity issue (Asquith and Mullins, 1986),
  much smaller for public utilities (−0.68% against −3.25% from 1963 to 1980, Masulis and Korwar,
  1986); in table 2.6, after Eckbo and Masulis (1995), two-day abnormal returns at the announcement
  of common stock offerings by NYSE and AMEX firms: firm-commitment offerings −3.1% for industrial
  firms (216) and −0.8% for utilities (424); for industrial firms, smaller for standby-rights
  (−1.5%, 32) and rights (−1.4%, 26) offerings; for utilities, −1.4% (84) on standby rights and
  −0.2% (27) on rights. The reaction is smaller in expansions (section 2.5.3, after Choe and others,
  1993); chapter 6 gives the adverse-selection model.
- **Singal** (*Beyond the Random Walk*, 2003): seasoned issues were underpriced by about 3% on
  average in the 1990s, the depressed price lasting only a few days after the pricing, with no clear
  profitable strategy but a gain for a holder who sells before the pricing and buys on its day
  ("Underpricing of Seasoned Issues"); issuers underperform the market by 20 to 35% over the next
  three years, robust in some specifications and not in others ("Long-Term Underperformance
  Following Seasoned Issues").
- **Cohen, Lys and Zach** (in Zacks, ed., *The Handbook of Equity Market Anomalies*, 2011, chapter
  6, "Net Stock Anomalies", section "Seasoned Equity Offerings"): issuing firms underperform for
  three to five years after the offering (Loughran and Ritter, 1995; Spiess and Affleck-Graves,
  1995); the bad-model problem makes long-horizon abnormal returns contested (Fama, 1998; Mitchell
  and Stafford, 2000).
- **Baker and Wurgler (2000)**, the aggregate form, reported by **Shleifer** (*Inefficient Markets*,
  2000, figure 2.2: the CRSP indexes' mean annual returns by quartile of the prior year's equity
  share in new issues, 1928 to 1997), **Barberis and Thaler** (in Thaler, ed., *Advances in
  Behavioral Finance II*, 2005, section 8.1: a high equity share "predicts low, and sometimes
  negative, stock returns") and Cohen, Lys and Zach, who give Schultz (2003) as a rival reading;
  **Campbell** (*Financial Decisions and Markets*, 2018, section 5.4.3) lists equity issuance among
  the predictors of aggregate returns, measured against all issuance (Baker and Wurgler) or against
  the market's value (Boudoukh, Michaely, Richardson and Roberts, 2007), with a negative sign.

## What the lab can read of it

The lab holds the daily closes and volumes of twenty-two funds and bitcoin — the funds from 2005 or
their later start, bitcoin from September 2014 — and no single stock, no offering date or offer
price, and no issuance series; its snapshot is closed, an asset or a series being added only before
the first card. On those data:

- **The reaction at announcements and pricings**, the claim and every one of its refutations: the
  issuing firms, their dates, their offer prices and how hard each is to value are not held; the lab
  cannot see a single event of the theory. The contrast between industrial firms and utilities, the
  refutation by information asymmetry, is read on single firms' events; a fund of utilities (XLU)
  holds each firm's offering diluted among the sector's other firms, on dates the lab does not hold.
  The regimes the prediction names are, in Tirole, a smaller reaction to single offerings in
  expansions: still an event the lab cannot see.
- **Long-run underperformance after the offering**: single stocks over three to five years; the
  cross-sectional form is **EF-028**'s, composite equity issuance, and **EF-027**'s, net share
  issuance. The drift after seasoned offerings that TM-005-01, TM-006-01 and TM-007-01 handed here
  goes with it, to EF-027 and EF-028, on single stocks and dates the lab does not hold.
- **The aggregate form**, signed in the library, needs issuance series the lab does not hold. The
  equity share in new issues, equity against debt financing, is a component of **TM-037**'s
  sentiment composite, whose card recorded issuance as not held; issuance relative to the market's
  value, the net payout side, is **EF-027**'s, which cites Ilmanen's net-buyback-adjusted yield.

**The bank's siblings.** **EF-027**, net share issuance; **EF-028**, composite equity issuance;
**EF-029**, share repurchases, the mirror; **AS-012**, dividend signalling, another reading of
insiders' information; all untouched; **AS-003**, IPO lock-up expiration, recorded not testable;
**TM-037**, investor sentiment, tested inconclusive.

**What was considered, and why it does not rescue a card:**

- **The utilities fund (XLU) against the other sectors, as the refutation by information
  asymmetry**: the contrast is at the announcements of single firms; the library gives no sign for a
  sector fund's returns, and the lab holds no calendar of the sector's offerings; its snapshot is
  closed to new series.
- **The equity share in new issues as a timing signal on SPY**: the sign is the library's, but the
  series is not held; an equity-share proxy built from prices would read valuation, since the equity
  share is higher when the market is highly valued (Barberis and Thaler), which is other theories'
  claim, not issuance.
- **Emerging markets' heavier dilution, EEM against the developed funds**: Ilmanen (*Expected
  Returns*, after Speidell and others, 2005; *Investing Amid Low Expected Returns*, after Mee, 2017)
  finds shareholder dilution much larger in emerging markets, the index divisor growing 3.3% a year
  against 0.5% in developed ones — a gap between economic growth and per-share returns, not a sign
  for EEM's returns against the others or a rule that varies over time; the dilution series is not
  held, and the form is EF-027's.
- **Bitcoin's issuance**, the mining reward as a tax on holders: **EF-044**'s, token issuance,
  untouched; not an offering read by the market as a signal.
- **ETF share creation**: fund flows, which no source signs as an issuance effect and the lab does
  not hold.

What would make it testable: the snapshot of a universe of single stocks with their offering
announcement and pricing dates and offer prices — outside the lab's funds.

## Status

AS-005 is recorded `not-testable`: its claim and every refutation are about single firms' offering
announcements and pricings, which the lab does not hold; the long-run underperformance after issues,
with the drift other judgements handed here, is EF-028's and EF-027's, on single stocks; the
aggregate forms the library signs need issuance series the lab does not hold, the equity share a
component of TM-037's composite, issuance relative to the market's value EF-027's. No card is drawn,
and no trial is spent.
