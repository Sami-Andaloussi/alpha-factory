# TM-031-01 — Behavioural herding: reasoning, not testable as a theory of its own

## The theory, and the forms its sources give it

TM-031 holds that investors, professional managers included, imitate one another under
uncertainty, and that this collective validation makes price moves correlated and persistent beyond
what fundamentals justify: a manager who sees an asset class fall and peers cut their exposures cuts
too, for fear of reputational risk, and the fall feeds the herding that feeds the fall, so that
crises produce very strong downtrends; the trends herding makes can end abruptly, momentum crashes
(Daniel and Moskowitz, 2016) often coming with a rapid reversal of herding. It is documented in
crises (2008, 2020) and manias (the technology bubble of 2000, the crypto-asset boom of 2017 to
2018), on stocks, bonds, commodities and currencies. Its refutations: investors whose decisions do
not depend on their peers' observed decisions under uncertainty; price falls that do not accelerate
as more investors cut their exposures; herding no more visible in crises and manias than at other
times; momentum crashes unrelated to reversals in collective positioning. The bank names stocks,
bonds, commodities, currencies and crypto-assets, and daily prices, fund flows and institutional
holdings.

**Its sources, as the lab read them in the library.**

- **Greyserman and Kaminski** (*Trend Following with Managed Futures*, 2014; the bank cites chapter
  3, the library's copy holds the passage in chapter 4, "Adaptive Markets and Trend Following",
  table 4.1): herding is one of the behavioural biases, with home bias, loss aversion and
  overreaction, that hinder market players' adaptation in a crisis, beside institutional
  restrictions (drawdown and risk limits, allocation constraints) and market functionality — the
  reasons they give for trend following finding its opportunity in crises, the crisis alpha. A
  mechanism, with no measure of herding.
- **Ilmanen** (*Expected Returns*, 2011): speculative bubbles tied to extrapolative expectations,
  overconfidence and herding (chapter 6), with herd behaviour and word-of-mouth perhaps more
  important than individual biases; the quant meltdown of 2007 as quant managers' herding into
  similar value indicators, unwound by crowded positions' synchronous liquidation (chapter 12,
  §12.2), single-stock long-short positions the lab cannot read. He gives herding a sign on prices:
  investors herding into successful asset classes and strategies after long uptrends that are then
  more likely to reverse (chapter 14, §14.1), and, across the asset classes and strategies of his
  book from 1990 to 2009, an average correlation of adjacent months' returns of +0.20 and of the
  latest six months' return with the six months' return 18 months earlier of −0.11, which he reads
  as market participants' collective actions (chapter 20, §§20.2–20.3, figure 20.1).
- **Hirshleifer and Teoh**, "Thought and behavior contagion in capital markets", chapter 1 of the
  *Handbook of Financial Markets: Dynamics and Evolution* (Hens and Schenk-Hoppé, eds., 2009), the
  library's survey of herding: they report some large correlations in returns but find them hard to
  attribute to herding.
- **Daniel and Moskowitz** (2016), read in Ang (*Asset Management*, 2014, chapter 7), the library
  holding no copy: momentum crashes after market declines, at the market's rebound; the crash is
  measured on prices, and no source ties it to herding.

## What the lab can read of it, and where each form already stands

The lab holds the daily closes and traded volumes of funds and of bitcoin, and the Treasury bill's
yield; a fund's volume is its own shares' trading, not its holders' (TM-028-01). It holds no
positions, flows or holdings of investors or managers. On those data:

- **Investors' decisions depending on their peers'**: trades and holdings, which the lab does not
  hold; institutional herding is **FP-009**'s claim, reputational herding **TM-029**'s.
- **Price falls accelerating as more investors cut their exposures**: the exposures are positions
  and flows, which the lab does not hold; the selling that feeds a fall is **FP-024**'s,
  **FP-006**'s (liquidity spirals and margin-driven deleveraging) and **TM-035**'s, all untouched.
  On prices alone the form is continuation after falls. Over days to weeks the library signs it the
  other way — short-term reversals stronger amid high volatility (Ilmanen, as TM-011-01 read him),
  **MR-001**'s claim — and a heavy-volume fall, the nearest the lab's volumes come to more investors
  selling, is signed as a reversal by Campbell, Grossman and Wang (in Campbell's *Financial
  Decisions and Markets*, 2018), **MR-011**'s claim, untouched. Over months the trend of a crisis is
  **TM-018**'s, read by TM-018-01, whose trend held only after divergent or crisis months lost about
  1.2% a year of alpha against TM-017-01's held always; the trend itself is read by TM-017-01, and
  the crisis form is also **TM-038**'s.
- **Herding into recently successful assets and strategies** (Ilmanen, chapters 14 and 20): on
  prices alone, short-term momentum and long-term reversal. The continuation over a month is
  **TM-017**'s, read by TM-017-01, and **TM-043**'s, untouched; the reversal 18 to 24 months on is
  **MR-021**'s, untouched; the momentum of strategies and styles **CA-003**'s, untouched — as
  TM-015-01 allocated the same figure. Herding adds a cause, not a rule.
- **Herding more visible in crises and manias than at other times**: a measure of herding, which the
  lab does not hold. The library holds Christie and Huang's cross-sectional dispersion only in
  Amihud, Mendelson and Pedersen's *Market Liquidity* (2012), in the chapter on the Tel Aviv
  exchange's transfers, as a descriptive measure of how prices move together over the business cycle
  and in extreme moves, with no herding reading and no sign on later returns; Chang, Cheng and
  Khorana and the cross-sectional absolute deviation are not in the library. Read as herding, low
  dispersion would condition momentum: that is **TM-039**'s variable. TM-039-01 held CA-001-01's
  tilt only after dispersed months and lost 1.72% a year of alpha; the tilt earned after calm months
  instead (in equity markets +1.21% a year, standard error 0.46%, over 126 months), and it recorded
  the complement as untested. The library signs that complement — momentum profits lower after wide
  dispersion across equities (Ilmanen, §14.5) — as volatility, not herding, and a card holding
  momentum after low-dispersion months would be chosen from TM-039-01's published result on the same
  sessions. Correlation rising in crises gives no rule either: Hirshleifer and Teoh report some
  large correlations in returns but find them hard to attribute to herding, and Ilmanen (§20.1) and
  Greyserman and Kaminski (chapter 4) read rising correlations as the trigger of risk-control
  selling, **FP-024**'s, **FP-006**'s and **TM-035**'s. Manias and their corrections are
  **MR-023**'s claim.
- **Momentum crashes and reversals of collective positioning**: the crash on prices is **TM-024**'s
  claim, read by TM-024-01, not proven and not refuted; the positioning is not held.
- **Crypto-assets**: bitcoin is one asset; a card of one asset is not drawn, and the library gives
  bitcoin's trend no sign (TM-005-01).

**The bank's siblings.** **FP-009**, institutional herding, untouched; **TM-029**, reputational
herding, and **TM-011**, informational cascades, recorded not testable — TM-029 and TM-031 hand
herding to each other, and the herding forms of both fall because the lab lacks the data, not
because the other owns them; **TM-015**, slow-moving capital and delegated management, not testable;
**FP-024**, positive feedback trading and stop-loss cascades; **FP-006**, liquidity spirals;
**TM-035**, procyclical leverage; **MR-023**, bubbles and their correction; **TM-018**, crisis
alpha; **TM-038**, adaptive markets; **TM-024**, momentum crashes; **TM-039**,
dispersion-conditioned momentum; **TM-043**, positive autocorrelation; **MR-021**, long-term
reversal; **CA-003**, factor momentum; **MR-011**, volume-conditioned reversals; **MR-001**,
short-term reversal; the others untouched, except TM-018, TM-024 and TM-039, tested-inconclusive.

**What was considered, and why it does not rescue a card of TM-031's own:**

- **Volume as herding**: a fund's volume is its own shares' trading, not the holders' positioning,
  and the library gives herding no volume sign; a heavy-volume fall is signed as a reversal
  (Campbell, Grossman and Wang), **MR-011**'s; the lab's one volume card, TM-028-01, read the quiet
  sessions' trend.
- **Trend in crises, or momentum switched off in crises**: read by TM-018-01 and TM-024-01; herding
  adds a cause, not a rule.
- **Dispersion falling in extreme moves**: in the library a description of how prices move together,
  with no herding reading and no trading sign; the dispersion-conditioned rule is TM-039's, and its
  complement's in-sample result is published by TM-039-01.

What would make it testable as its own: a measure of investors' positioning — holdings, flows or
trades — frozen in the snapshot, or a source signing a price-based herding measure for a rule.

## Status

TM-031 is recorded `not-testable` as a theory of its own: every one of its refutations reads
investors' decisions, positions or flows, or a measure of herding, which the lab does not hold; its
herding among investors is FP-009's, untouched, and TM-029's and TM-011's, not testable; its crisis
trends are TM-018's, read by TM-018-01, TM-017's and TM-038's; its crashes TM-024's, read by
TM-024-01; its manias MR-023's; its feedback selling FP-024's, FP-006's and TM-035's, and the
heavy-volume fall MR-011's; the momentum and reversal Ilmanen ties to herding into successful assets
are TM-017's, TM-043's, MR-021's and CA-003's; and dispersion, the one price measure the library
holds that could be read as herding, conditions momentum only as TM-039's variable, whose complement
TM-039-01 has already published on the same sessions. No card is drawn, and no trial is spent.
