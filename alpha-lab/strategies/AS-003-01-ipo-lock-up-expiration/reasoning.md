# AS-003-01 — IPO lock-up expiration: reasoning, not testable as a theory of its own

## The theory, and the forms its sources give it

AS-003 holds that a supply already created but temporarily blocked weighs on the price when it is
released: when the lock-up that keeps a newly listed firm's insiders, founders, employees and
venture capital investors from selling expires, the float may grow and better-informed holders may
sell, and arbitrage ahead of the date is imperfect. Its prediction: newly listed stocks fall around
the expiration of their lock-up, more so when the fraction released is large, when venture capital
or insider ownership is high, or when information on the firm is poor. Its refutations: no abnormal
decline around the expiration; a reaction unrelated to the fraction released, to venture capital and
insider ownership or to the quality of information; the whole adjustment at the offering or when the
terms become known. Its mechanism extends the logic to any asset whose supply, already created, is
temporarily illiquid. The bank names stocks and crypto, days, and lock-up expiration dates, daily
prices, shares outstanding and insider and venture capital ownership.

**Its sources, as the lab read them in the library.**

- **Singal** (*Beyond the Random Walk*, 2003, "Unlocking Value Around Expiration of IPO Lockups"),
  citing five papers, among them Aggarwal, Krigman and Womack (2002), Fields and Hanka (2001) and
  Ofek and Richardson (2001): the lock-up typically lasts 180 days; an abnormal return of about
  −1.5% immediately around the unlock day, about −3.0% for firms financed by venture capitalists and
  for firms not subject to SEC Rule 144; "the relatively small returns and the need to short-sell
  probably preclude" a trading strategy, though a holder who meant to sell anyway gains by selling a
  few days before.
- **Miller** (in Fabozzi, ed., *Short Selling: Strategies, Risks, and Rewards*, 2004, chapter 5,
  "Restrictions on Short Selling and Exploitable Opportunities for Investors"), after Ofek and
  Richardson: Internet stocks fell substantially as their lock-ups expired; some $300 billion of
  their shares became saleable within a short span up to the summer of 2000, which Ofek and
  Richardson link to the bubble's end. Miller reads the sign through a downward-sloping demand
  curve: opinions differ and short selling is restricted, so the newly saleable shares, held by
  insiders who are more pessimistic or merely undiversified, are absorbed only at a lower price.
- **Burniske and Tatar** (*Cryptoassets*, 2017, chapter 15, on the Bitcoin Investment Trust): new
  shares of Grayscale's trust, GBTC, reached public markets only after accredited investors who had
  bought into its private placement had held them for a year; GBTC's price could stand far above the
  bitcoin it held, a premium near 100% at its first trade — a lock-up on a fund's shares, with no
  claim about bitcoin's own price at the releases.
- **Huang, Yang, Zhang, Chen and Zhu** (*Web3*, 2024): a token's value falls as unlocked tokens
  reach the market faster than the project grows; bitcoin's issuance is by contrast a prefixed
  schedule, "eliminating any potential surprises", with no investor or team allocations to unlock.
- **Fabozzi, Füss and Kaiser**, eds., *The Handbook of Commodity Investing* (2008): Heidorn and
  Demidova-Menzel (chapter 32): central banks' sales from their gold reserves pushed gold down for
  many years before 2000, and the September 2004 agreement of fifteen European central banks capped
  their sales at 500 tons a year, which with mine closures left gold short of supply; Cai, Clacher,
  Faff and Hillier (chapter 31): the 1999 Central Bank Gold Agreement had capped European sales at
  2,000 tons over five years.

## What the lab can read of it

The lab holds the daily closes and volumes of twenty-two funds and bitcoin — the funds from 2005 or
their later start, bitcoin from September 2014 — and no single stock, no offering, no lock-up date,
no ownership and no issuance series; its snapshot is closed, an asset or a series being added only
before the first card. On those data:

- **Newly listed stocks around their lock-up expirations**, the claim and every one of its
  refutations: the stocks, their dates, the fraction released and the owners are not held; the lab
  cannot see a single event of the theory.
- **An aggregate form, a wave of unlocks weighing on the index that holds the stocks**: the one the
  library gives is Ofek and Richardson's Internet wave of 2000, as Miller reports it, a total for
  that year before the lab's data begin, with no lag, threshold or series a rule could read; the lab
  holds no count of unlocked shares for the years it covers, and no source signs an unlock effect on
  a fund's price or volume.
- **Aggregate equity issuance predicting the market**: a high share of equity in new issues is
  followed by low market returns (Baker and Wurgler, 2000, in *The Handbook of Equity Market
  Anomalies* and Thaler, ed., *Advances in Behavioral Finance II*; Campbell, *Financial Decisions
  and Markets*), a signed supply form on the index, but the lab holds no issuance series, and the
  form is **EF-027**'s, net share issuance, and a component of **TM-037**'s sentiment, whose card
  recorded issuance as not held.
- **The crypto analogy, tokens unlocked from vesting**: **AS-028**'s claim, untouched, which calls
  itself the crypto equivalent of the lock-up overhang; tokens are not held, and bitcoin, the lab's
  one crypto asset, has no vesting or unlock (Huang and others).
- **Bitcoin's own held-back supply released** — a bankrupt estate distributing coins, known sales by
  large holders: **AS-035**'s claim, supply concentration and the whale overhang, untouched, whose
  bank file names bankruptcies and known sales; the library dates no such release within the lab's
  bitcoin years and gives it no sign.
- **GBTC's lock-up**: the shares released were the trust's, not bitcoin, and the price the source
  ties to them is the trust's premium, which the lab does not hold.

**The bank's siblings.** **AS-028**, vesting and unlock overhang, the crypto form; **AS-035**,
supply concentration and whale overhang, bitcoin's held-back supply; **EF-027**, net share issuance,
the aggregate issuance form; **FP-008**, short-sale constraints and overvaluation, which cites Ofek
and Richardson and Miller's chapter; **MR-015**, downward-sloping demand curves, Miller's mechanism;
**EF-044**, token issuance, and **FP-029**, miners' selling, whose own bank files name the halvings;
**AS-005**, seasoned equity offerings, an issue read as a signal of overvaluation; **FP-011**, index
inclusion; all untouched.

**What was considered, and why it does not rescue a card:**

- **The technology sector fund (XLK) or the Nasdaq-100 (QQQ) in months of heavy unlocks**: the sign
  is the library's, negative, but no source measures unlocks after 2000 or gives a lag or a
  threshold, and the lab holds no unlock calendar; its snapshot is closed to new series.
- **Central bank gold sales, a held-back stock released on GLD**: the library's dated changes, the
  agreements of 1999 and September 2004, fall before the lab's in-sample years, and it dates none
  within them; a rule on one or two regime changes would make a handful of decisions, short of gate
  1's thirty, on one asset, where the lab requires at least four; and no bank theory claims it
  beyond this one's general mechanism.
- **Bitcoin around the halvings**, a known change in new supply: a change in the flow of new
  issuance, not a release of supply already created and held back; **EF-044**'s and **FP-029**'s.

What would make it testable: the snapshot of a universe of single stocks with their offering and
lock-up dates, shares released and venture capital backing — outside the lab's funds.

## Status

AS-003 is recorded `not-testable`: its claim and every refutation are about single newly listed
stocks and their lock-up dates, which the lab does not hold; the aggregate unlock wave the library
signs is the Internet's of 2000, before the lab's data, with no lag, threshold or series; aggregate
equity issuance is not held and is EF-027's; central bank gold sales changed regime before the lab's
years, on one asset; its crypto analogy is AS-028's, bitcoin's own released supply AS-035's, the
halvings EF-044's and FP-029's. No card is drawn, and no trial is spent.
