# TM-039-01 — Momentum held while returns are dispersed: reasoning

## Why this theory now

TM-039 holds that the dispersion of returns across a set of assets is a precondition of
cross-sectional momentum: a rule long the winners and short the losers earns what the assets'
returns differ by, nothing when they move alike, and more when asymmetric macroeconomic shocks,
crises confined to one asset class or strong sector rotations pull them apart. Its prediction is
that momentum's profit, cross-sectional and trend following alike, rises with that dispersion.

**The sources.** The bank attributes the claim to Greyserman and Kaminski (*Trend Following with
Managed Futures*, 2014, chapter 4, a section "Return Dispersion"). Their book does not hold it.
Chapter 4 has no such section. Their chapter on return dispersion (chapter 11) measures the
dispersion of returns *between trend following programmes* — how much systems that differ in their
lookbacks, position sizing or sector allocation earn apart in a given year — and finds it unrelated
to market volatility once 2008 is set aside. The book's measure of how much markets move apart is
its market divergence index, the average across markets of each one's trend over noise (chapter 5),
which TM-018-01 used; the book calls momentum the effect of divergence, not its cause, and does not
measure dispersion across assets. The lab records the misattribution; the theory stands as the bank
states it.

The relation has been measured on stocks and currencies. The lab could read only the abstracts:

- **Stivers and Sun** (*Journal of Financial and Quantitative Analysis*, 2010) find the market's
  recent cross-sectional dispersion of stock returns **negatively** related to the subsequent
  momentum premium and positively to the value premium, a relation that holds with macroeconomic
  controls; they read dispersion as a leading countercyclical state variable and momentum's premium
  as procyclical. A search engine's summary, not the abstract, describes their dispersion as the
  cross-sectional standard deviation of the returns of 100 or 25 portfolios formed on size and
  book-to-market.
- **Hurst and Docherty** (*Pacific-Basin Finance Journal*, 2018) report a strong relation between
  return dispersion and the momentum premium across international regions, and a momentum strategy
  scaled by the level of dispersion that beats the unscaled one in every region; the abstract does
  not give the relation's sign.
- **Eriksen** (*Journal of Empirical Finance*, 2019) finds, in currencies, that an unexpected rise
  in cross-sectional dispersion comes with positive returns to past winners and negative returns to
  past losers: a relation over the same period, the one the bank states.

The only *lagged* sign the lab could read is negative; the card predicts the bank's sign against it.
No source the lab holds gives a size or a parameter it could transfer, and the card adds no
parameter of its own to CA-001-01's rule.

**What a tilt without an edge can show.** CA-001-01 held the top third of each group by its past
year's return, the last month left out, and its base form was refuted: an alpha of 0.20% a year over
the nineteen funds held in equal parts, its choice of funds beating 47.6% of its placebos. Switching
such a tilt can show only whether its zero average hides a split by state. If dispersion decides the
sign of momentum's month, as the claim reads when momentum is to have a precondition, the tilt
earned after dispersed months and lost after the others, and switching it off after the others
gains. If dispersion only scales momentum's gains and losses alike, a tilt with no edge earns about
nothing in both states, and the difference is about zero.

**TM-039 and TM-018.** TM-018's bank file makes the dispersion claim for trend following too, from
the same book; TM-018-01 tested trend following switched by market divergence, each fund's own trend
over its noise. This card tests the part of TM-039 that TM-018 did not: the sort of assets against
each other, cross-sectional momentum, whose mechanism the bank states — long the winners, short the
losers, earning what the assets differ by. The trend following half of TM-039, a rule holding each
fund against cash switched by the dispersion across funds, is not tested: its bet is on each fund's
own direction, which dispersion across funds does not size. The campaign's decisions list it as an
untested form.

The bank's neighbours: **CA-001**, momentum within groups, whose rule this card switches;
**TM-018**, trend following switched by market divergence; **TM-024**, momentum switched off in
panic states; **TM-038**, the adaptive markets hypothesis, of which dispersion is one reading of a
changing environment.

## From the claim to a signal

- **The dispersion**: the claim is that momentum earns in a month by what the assets' returns differ
  by in that month. A rule cannot read the month it holds; the nearest reading is the month just
  past, and the card reads the standard deviation of the funds' returns over the last 21 sessions up
  to the session before the target — the month CA-001-01's ranking already leaves out, so the
  reading adds no window of its own. It stands in for the month held through how long dispersion
  lasts from one month to the next. Taken over the funds CA-001-01 ranks on that session.
- **Within each group**, the base: CA-001-01 sorts within groups, so the dispersion its bet can earn
  is its group's. **Across all nineteen**, the variant: the bank's own reading, dispersion across
  the assets of a universe, one state for every group. It is dominated by the spread between groups
  — the commodities against the sectors — which a rule that sorts within groups never bets on; the
  bank's sort is across the whole universe too, and the variant is only half its reading.
- **High**: above the average of the dispersion's own daily readings, from its first reading through
  the session before the target, the neutral line of the dispersion's own history, which TM-018-01
  used for its index; no source gives a level. Each group is set against its own history, so the
  commodities' wider spreads do not decide the sectors' state.
- **In the state**, CA-001-01's weights for that group. **Out of it**, the group's ranked funds in
  equal parts, its benchmark weights: the tilt is off and the group holds what the benchmark holds,
  as TM-024-01 held a group when its tilt was off. A group with fewer than two ranked funds has no
  dispersion and holds its funds in equal parts, which for one fund is CA-001-01's own weight.
- **The pace**: CA-001-01's, the first session of each month.
- **The first readings**: the dispersion reads its first ranked funds in January 2006, a few weeks
  before CA-001-01's first target on 2006-02-01, so that the first thresholds rest on a handful of
  readings. The commodities group has two ranked funds only from February 2007 (GLD and DBC), and
  three from June 2007 (SLV).

**A count made before the card.** The card's audit counted, on the lab's prices and signals only,
the states of a first draft that read the dispersion over the ranking's whole window: about 55% of
the group-months out of the state for the base and 52% of the months for the variant, in spells of
four to seven months; the readings skewed (the sectors' mean 0.111 against a median of 0.100, the
commodities' 0.157 against 0.131), the line raised after 2008–09, the sectors in the state in no
month of 2014 and nearly all of 2021–22. The count set no parameter. The audit's closing check
counted the card's reading, the month just past, in the same way: out of the state in about 60% of
the base's group-months (62% in the sectors and the equity markets, 60% in the commodities, counting
the 13 months in which they had one ranked fund) and 61% of the variant's months, switching about
five times a year in each group (4.6 in the sectors, 5.8 in the equity markets, 4.9 in the
commodities); at least one group is out of the state in 87% of the months. These counts set no
parameter either; they size the noise and the charge below.

Two variants, not three.

## What the battery judges, and what to expect

**Against CA-001-01.** Gates 2 to 4 judge the rule against the nineteen funds held in equal parts,
as they judged CA-001-01. The theory is judged by the difference between the base's alpha and
CA-001-01's, over the base's sessions and costs, by `python -m lab.compare`, as TM-024-01 and
TM-018-01 were judged against the rules they switch. Both first hold on 2006-02-01.

**The size predicted.** If the claim holds in the form that gives momentum a precondition,
CA-001-01's tilt earned after dispersed months and lost after the others; switching it off after the
others should cost nothing and may save losses. The card predicts a difference at or above zero and
no upper bound: the only lagged sign the lab could read is the other way, on stocks, and the lab
holds no size to transfer.

**The charge the switch pays.** A group out of the state holds its benchmark weights, where
CA-001-01's tilt ran at a whole-sample beta of about 0.95. `lab.compare` hedges each rule with its
own whole-sample beta, so it charges the switch for any market exposure it adds, even if its returns
were unchanged: TM-018-01's whole shortfall was that charge. By the lab's own record it is larger
than the average beta gap suggests: TM-024-01 turned the tilt off in only 7% of the group-months and
raised the whole-sample beta by 0.058, a charge of about 0.5% a year, since the tilt's beta was far
from its average in the states that switched it. Here the tilt is off about 60% of the time; if its
beta is the same in both states, the charge is about 0.03 times the benchmark's premium of about 8%
a year, about 0.24% a year, and if the states sort the tilt's beta as TM-024-01's did, 0.5% or more.
The switches cost too: about five a year in each group, each trading about 0.74 of the group's
weight in the sectors, 0.32 in the equity markets and 0.21 in the commodities, about 0.3% a year at
the lab's costs before, and perhaps 0.15 to 0.2% after, the leaders not traded while out of the
state. The charge is roughly 0.3 to 0.7% a year: a rough estimate. The measures reported below
separate it from the tilt's months.

**The noise of the difference.** CA-001-01's bets, hedged of its benchmark, moved by about 6 to 7% a
year (0.20% of alpha at an appraisal ratio of 0.03). The rule departs from CA-001-01 only in the
months out of the state, where its difference is minus CA-001-01's tilt in that group; with about
60% of the group-months out of the state, the difference moves by about 6.5 × √0.6 ≈ 5.0% a year, a
rough figure, a standard error of about 1.2% a year over 16.9 years. The clause refutes only below
−1% a year and more than one measured standard error below zero. At a standard error of 1.2%, a true
difference of zero with no charge would be refuted about 16% of the time; with a charge of 0.3%,
about 23%; with 0.6 to 0.7%, about 31 to 34% — the charge is then within a point of the band. A true
−2% would be refuted about 75% of the time. The clause can refute only a clear loss from the switch,
and cannot show the claim.

**The test's power.** Gate 4 will count about eighteen effective trials; an appraisal ratio of about
0.7 would be needed to pass it, where CA-001-01's was 0.03. The comparison with CA-001-01 is the
informative test.

**What the card cannot test.** The bank's claim is over the same period: momentum earns more while
returns are dispersed. A rule reads only the month just past; the card tests whether dispersion in
that month is followed by a better month for the sort. It holds no short side: the long-only tilt
earns the winners' excess over the group, about half of a long-short spread if the spread is
symmetric.

**Measures stated before the run**, reported, not graded:

- The base's and CA-001-01's alphas over the months in which the base's weights differ from
  CA-001-01's in any group, and over the other months, each rule given its own beta in each set:
  what the switch did where it acted, the charge set apart (TM-024-01's split). Only about 26 of the
  203 months have the two rules identical, so the beta fitted there is weak; the next measure gives
  the split group by group.
- Each group's tilt — CA-001-01's weights on its funds less its equal parts, a position that sums to
  zero, measured raw — over the months after that group's reading was above its average and the
  others: the claim's own split, group by group.
- CA-001-01's hedged edge in the months in and out of the variant's state, with a beta for each
  state; in the state the variant and CA-001-01 hold the same weights, out of it the variant holds
  the benchmark (TM-018-01's lesson on states read from the signal).

**Risks named before the run.**

- **Crises.** Dispersion across funds rises in crises — 2008, March 2020 — which are also when
  momentum crashes (TM-024's theory): the state may switch the tilt on just before a rebound in
  which the past losers lead.
- **A changing set.** The sectors whose dispersion is read change: XLRE is ranked from October 2017
  (it trades from September 2016 and needs a year), XLC from July 2019; the commodities go from two
  funds to three in 2007. The average mixes readings of different sets.
- **Skewed readings.** Dispersion jumps in crises; the average then stays raised for years after,
  and the state may be off through calm years that follow.
- **Small groups.** The commodities group's dispersion is the spread of two or three returns, which
  one fund's move can set.
- **The early thresholds**, above.
- **Gate 6's neighbours** move `skip`, which sets both the month the ranking leaves out and the
  month the dispersion reads: from 10 to 32 sessions.

## Choices, and the options rejected

- **The dispersion over the ranking's whole window**, the past year less its last month: the first
  draft's reading. The spread of the sorted returns sets no position — CA-001-01's weights depend on
  the rank alone — and a twelve-month reading is a slow stand-in for the dispersion of the month
  held; the month just past is the nearest reading at no cost in parameters.
- **Scaling the tilt by dispersion**, as Hurst and Docherty's conditional strategy does: the scale
  would be fitted to their data, not given by the theory.
- **A threshold at a fixed percentile** of the dispersion's history: no source gives one; the
  average is the neutral line.
- **Cash out of the state**: it would add a market-timing bet the theory does not make.
- **TM-017-01's time-series rule as the base**: the trend following half, above.

## Implementation

Long only, nineteen funds, each with a European (UCITS) fund or an exchange-traded commodity that
tracks it, rebalanced monthly on closes with the orders filled at the next close: the signals read
the session before, so a day of delay is built in, and gate 6 adds another.
