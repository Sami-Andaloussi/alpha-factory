# AS-010-01 — Separation of ownership and control: reasoning, not testable as a theory of its own

## The theory, and the forms its sources give it

AS-010 holds that stocks differ in their exposure to the risks of poor governance — entrenchment,
diversion of resources, insufficient monitoring — and that these features, agency conflicts, board
composition, ownership concentration, activism and takeover threats, part of each stock's internal
structure, go with recurring differences in its price behaviour, the link depending on the
institutional regime and on investors' power to discipline managers. Its refutations: stocks highly
exposed behaving as comparable stocks lowly exposed; differences in governance features bearing no
relation to price behaviour; a link the same in every institutional regime. The bank names stocks,
weeks and months, and board composition, ownership concentration, takeover and activism events and
daily prices.

**Its sources, as the lab read them in the library.**

- **Shleifer** (*Inefficient Markets*, 2000, chapter 7, section 7.3): insiders' expropriation of
  outside investors is mild in the UK and the US, "a more extreme form in Continental Europe", and
  central in some Asian countries and Russia; Johnson, Boone, Breach and Friedman (1999) find that
  in the crisis of 1997–98 the markets of countries with bad investor protection fell more than
  those with good protection — "countries with the best corporate governance experienced the mildest
  crises".
- **Tirole** (*The Theory of Corporate Finance*, 2006, chapter 1, "Corporate Governance"): the
  separation of ownership and control and the monitoring that limits it, with return signs only on
  single firms' events — an abnormal fall of 2% when a firm first discloses its CEO's aircraft perk,
  and underperformance of about 4% (Yermack, 2004); takeover targets gaining a premium of about 30%,
  bidders about breaking even.
- **Ang** (*Asset Management*, 2014, chapter 3, section 6.2, "Socially Responsible Investing"):
  Gompers, Ishii and Metrick (2003) rank firms from "dictatorships" to "republics" by their
  provisions entrenching managers and find republics earning more; Cremers and Ferrell (2012) find
  weak-rights stocks earning negative excess returns from 1978 to 2007, while Bebchuk, Cohen and
  Wang (2013) argue the result disappears in the 2000s; the same section reports high-KLD stocks
  earning more (Kempf and Osthoff), sin stocks earning more (Hong and Kacperczyk), hedge-fund
  activism adding value (Brav and others), and SRI mutual funds trailing their peers by about 30
  basis points a month (Geczy, Stambaugh and Levin).
- **Baker, ed.** (*Dividends and Dividend Policy*, 2009): chapter 25, "Dividend Policy and Corporate
  Governance", the conflicting evidence between the governance index and payout (Jiraporn and Ning,
  2006); chapter 27, "Dividend Policy in a Global Perspective", with its section on dividends,
  control and ownership, return signs on single firms' dividend announcements.
- **Chincarini and Kim** (*Quantitative Equity Portfolio Management*, 2006): a corporate governance
  factor, KLD's strengths less concerns, with an expected positive sign, on single stocks.
- **Ilmanen** (*Investing Amid Low Expected Returns*, 2022, chapter 14): governance signals and
  activism among the ESG features that have added to returns, overlapping quality, on single stocks.

## What the lab can read of it

The lab holds the daily bars and volumes of twenty-two funds and bitcoin — the funds from 2005 or
their later start, bitcoin from September 2014 — adjusted for dividends and splits, which encode the
funds' distributions; the Treasury bill's yield from 2005 and two franc exchange rates; the funds'
prices, never their holdings' prices; and no single stock, board, ownership, governance index,
takeover or activism event; its snapshot is closed, an asset or a series being added only before the
first card. On those data:

- **Stocks by their exposure to poor governance**, the claim and its first two refutations: every
  sign in the library — Gompers, Ishii and Metrick's and its disputes, KLD, perks, takeovers,
  activism — compares single stocks or their events; each fund holds governance of every kind in one
  line.
- **The regime contrast in crises, the third refutation**: Shleifer's sign is readable on the funds
  — the US funds (SPY, QQQ, IWM) the strong-protection market, EFA a mix of the UK, Continental
  Europe and Japan, EEM the weak-protection markets — as EEM falling more than the others in a
  crisis. A rule of it holds the five equity index funds and leaves EEM out while a crisis state is
  on. The library's crisis state that the lab has already read beforehand is the panic state of
  Daniel and Moskowitz, read monthly, that TM-024-01 applied to these five funds as a group — their
  market down over 504 sessions and its volatility over 126 sessions above its average — and
  TM-024-01's verdict published its months: October 2008 to September 2009, August and September
  2010, April and May 2020, and October 2022, four spells. A rule that changes its targets only when
  the state turns would make about nine decisions in-sample, the first holding and eight turns,
  short of gate 1's thirty; the count is published, so the failure is certain whatever else the
  prices hold, and by RUNBOOK step 9 no card is drawn for it, as for SC-020-01 and SC-011-01.
  TM-024-01 reports that a 252-session bear window changes 52 group-months across its three groups
  and doubles the panic states, but gate 1 counts the base's decisions, not its neighbours'. Reading
  the state daily would be a pace the lab chose, and the library's other states, a trend or a level
  of volatility, mark trends or risk, not crises. A crisis state wide enough to reach thirty
  decisions would be a width the lab chose, not the library's; the source's own crisis, 1997–98, is
  before the lab's years.
- **A static tilt, the strong-protection funds over EEM always**: about one decision, certain to
  fail gate 1, as AS-009-01's EEM against EFA; country allocation is **CA-016**'s and national
  markets' reversion **CA-019**'s.
- **ESG, SRI or governance funds**: signed by Geczy and others, not held. **Takeover and activism
  waves**: single firms' events; the waves Tirole dates are before the lab's years. **Governance of
  crypto protocols**: **AS-036**'s, governance tokens, not readable on bitcoin, which has no board
  or token vote; bitcoin's forks are a handful of events on one asset, where the lab requires four.

**The bank's siblings.** **AS-011**, free cash flow, the same agency conflict read through payout;
**EF-017**, asset growth, overinvestment where governance is weak; **EF-022**, earnings quality;
**MR-026**, closed-end funds, which activists open; **CA-016**, country allocation; **CA-019**,
national markets' reversion; **AS-036**, governance tokens; all untouched; **AS-007**, spin-offs,
recorded not testable; **TM-024**, momentum crashes, tested inconclusive, whose panic state is the
crisis state here.

What would make it testable: governance indices and ownership data for single stocks; or a crisis
state in the library, readable beforehand, with enough spells in the lab's years for gate 1.

## Status

AS-010 is recorded `not-testable`: its claim and its first two refutations compare single firms by
their governance, ownership and control events, which the lab does not hold; its third, the regime
contrast, is signed on the funds (Shleifer, after Johnson and others: the markets with weak investor
protection fell more in the crisis), but the library's crisis state readable beforehand, TM-024-01's
panic state on the same funds, has four published spells in-sample, about nine decisions, certain to
fail gate 1 (RUNBOOK step 9); a static tilt makes one; crypto governance is AS-036's. No card is
drawn, and no trial is spent.
