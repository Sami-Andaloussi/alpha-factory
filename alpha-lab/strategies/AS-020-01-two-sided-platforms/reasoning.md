# AS-020-01 — Two-sided platforms: reasoning, not testable as a theory of its own

## The theory, and the forms its sources give it

AS-020 holds that on a two-sided platform — cardholders and merchants, buyers and sellers,
developers and users, liquidity providers and traders — each side's value depends on the other
side's participation, so that one side can be subsidised while the other is monetised and
profitability can be read only for the whole ecosystem; the platform keeps this position by
controlling access, pricing and matching, in sub-universes of platform stocks and some crypto
infrastructures. Its refutations: one side's participation having no effect on the other's value;
platforms showing the same growth and profitability as ordinary firms with two independent customer
segments; platforms' profitability readable side by side, with no side subsidised. The bank names
stocks, sectors and crypto, over years, and participants on each side, platform revenues, margins
and daily prices; its references are Belleflamme and Peitz's *Industrial Organization*, whose first
edition (2010) is the one the library holds, not the second the bank names, and Porter's
*Competitive Strategy* (1980), held in the 1998 edition.

**Its sources, as the lab read them in the library.**

- **Belleflamme and Peitz** (*Industrial Organization*, 2010, chapter 22, "Markets with
  Intermediated Goods", section 22.3, "Intermediaries as Two-Sided Platforms"): two groups who each
  prefer a larger pool on the other side, indirect network effects running between them; the price
  structure of intermediation, one side charged below cost when its presence is worth more to the
  other; competing platforms under single-homing and multi-homing; implications for antitrust and
  regulation, with cases on credit cards' no-surcharge rule, satellite radio, video games, travel
  booking and mobile telephony's waterbed effect, and the warning that a side served below marginal
  cost does not mean the platform cross-subsidises it — the third refutation's own point; and
  chapter 20 on network goods — all on prices, participation and competition.
- **Porter** (*Competitive Strategy*, 1980): complementary products and their effect on an
  industry's growth and structure (chapter 8) — signed on industry structure, with no two-sided
  form.
- **Teo** (chapter 9, "Emergence, Growth, and Sustainability of Bitcoin: The Network Economics
  Perspective", in Chuen, ed., *Handbook of Digital Currency*, 2015): bitcoin's critical mass as a
  two-dimensional problem of users and complementary merchants, wallets and developers, after Evans
  and Schmalensee's two-sided platforms — a model of adoption that holds the coin's value constant,
  remarks that the value should rise with uptake, and leaves fitting it to coin prices to future
  research.
- **Others in the library**: winner-takes-all claims of digital platforms resting on network
  effects, few such businesses earning monopoly-size returns on capital (Koller and others,
  *Valuation*, eighth edition, chapter 33); riders and drivers drawn to the same ride-hailing
  platform, one network becoming dominant as winner-takes-all markets form, the market raising its
  hopes for the leader while cutting them for the rest, and Thompson's aggregators and platforms,
  Shopify between merchants and their customers, platforms creating switching costs for their users
  (Mauboussin and Rappaport, *Expectations Investing*, revised edition, 2021); payment processors
  such as Mastercard, Visa and American Express valued on the slice they take of the transactions on
  their platforms (Damodaran, *Investment Valuation*, fourth edition, 2025, chapter 21, "Transaction
  and Payment Processors"); exchanges' maker-taker pricing, rebating the traders who post limit
  orders and charging those who take them — the bank's liquidity providers and traders with one side
  subsidised, signed on spreads and order flow (Foucault, Pagano and Röell, *Market Liquidity*,
  chapter 7) — on single firms' returns on capital and value, or on market quality.

**Return signs near the theory.**

- **Ilmanen** (*Investing Amid Low Expected Returns*, 2022, chapter 2, section 2.4): the 2010s kind
  to US large-cap and technology stocks, beside the dominance of the five largest platform firms,
  which investors believed would concentrate monopoly profits — one decade described after the fact,
  with the warning that the next may differ; not a rule, and not a two-sided form.
- **Ilmanen** (same book, chapter 6, "Current Outlook"): disruptive technology platforms as the best
  broad story of value's losses to 2020 — **EF-008**'s form, recorded in AS-017-01.
- **Koller and others** (*Valuation*, eighth edition, chapters 1 and 33): the belief that the winner
  takes all driving the internet bubble, investors overpaying for digital platforms'
  winner-takes-all claims, and unicorns' values falling after their listings — told after the fact.
- **Burniske and Tatar** (*Cryptoassets*, 2017, chapter 7): the four FANG stocks outrun by
  bitcoin from Facebook's listing in May 2012 to January 2017 — past returns on assets near the
  lab's own, which a fixed stance on the Nasdaq-100 fund and bitcoin reads as at most two decisions.
- **Network forms recorded in AS-017-01**: Tvede's dot-com episode, Greenwald and others' technology
  returns since 2009, and the untimed claims on bitcoin's network (Glasner, Chuen) — past episodes
  or untimed, each unreadable as a rule.

The library's anomaly and factor books carry no rule that reads two-sided platforms on the funds.

## What the lab can read of it

The lab holds the daily bars and volumes of twenty-two funds and bitcoin — the funds from 2005 or
their later start, bitcoin from September 2014 — adjusted for dividends and splits, which encode the
funds' distributions, though no strategy reads them until a point-in-time reading with its own test
is built; the Treasury bill's yield from 2005 and two franc exchange rates; the funds' prices, never
their holdings' prices; and no single stock, participant count on either side, platform revenue,
margin or on-chain series; its snapshot is closed, an asset or a series being added only before the
first card. Nothing was computed from the bars for this judgement. On those data:

- **One side's participation raising the other's value**, the claim and first refutation:
  participant counts and prices on each side, not held.
- **Platforms against firms with two independent segments**, the second: firms' growth and
  profitability, not held; the sector funds mix platforms with ordinary firms.
- **Profitability by side and subsidies**, the third: revenues and costs by side, not held.
- **Bitcoin and crypto infrastructures**: bitcoin is the one crypto asset the lab holds; its
  two-sided adoption (Teo) needs counts of users and merchants the lab does not hold, and signs no
  timed return; the network-value forms on active addresses, user counts and transactions are
  **EF-037**'s, **EF-038**'s and **EF-046**'s, and protocols' liquidity providers and traders are
  not in the lab's universe.
- **Dated platform events**: the library dates about five inside the lab's years — Sirius and XM's
  merger announced in February 2007 and approved in June 2008, the European Commission's clearance
  of Travelport and Worldspan in August 2007 (Belleflamme and Peitz, chapter 22), the United States'
  Regulation NMS of 2005 and the NYSE's merger with Euronext in 2007 (Foucault, Pagano and Röell,
  chapter 1) — each a single firm's or venue's, none signed on returns, far fewer than gate 1's
  thirty decisions.

**The bank's siblings, and how AS-020 differs.** **AS-017**, network effects within one group of
users, where AS-020's run between two groups priced apart; **AS-014**, barriers to entry, the
family's umbrella; **AS-015**, switching costs; **AS-016**, economies of scale; **AS-018**, the
experience curve; **AS-019**, vertical integration; all recorded not testable, AS-014's
concentration form and the count for any fixed ranking of the sector funds holding here too;
**AS-021**, native network tokens; **AS-044**, decentralised exchanges' design, which holds the
protocols matching liquidity providers and traders; **EF-041**, adoption-driven token valuation,
**EF-042**, a token's claim on its platform's fee revenue, and **EF-043**, the loop of advertisers,
users and publishers around a token, which need on-chain data; **LL-014**, production
complementarity, the bank theory that signs returns on a platform's own ecosystem — a shock to a
platform reaching its complements' prices with a delay — and **LL-012**, horizontal industry
relationships; **EF-031**, intangible capital mismeasured, which counts platforms among the
intangible-heavy firms it reads on single stocks: AS-020 claims participation and pricing inside one
platform, not the lead-lag of news across complementary industries, which is LL-014's own form and
which this judgement does not draw on the sector funds; **EF-037**, **EF-038** and **EF-046**, the
crypto network-value forms; **CA-011** and **CA-013**, sector rotation, which read the sector funds
on time-varying signals; **EF-004**, franchise value, on single stocks; all untouched but AS-014 to
AS-019.

**What was considered, and why it does not rescue a card:** the funds heaviest in platforms — the
Nasdaq-100 fund, technology, communication — against the others: no source measures a fund's weight
in platforms or signs a return on it; Ilmanen's decade and Burniske and Tatar's comparison are past
returns read after the fact; a fixed tilt to the Nasdaq-100 fund makes one decision, and with
bitcoin two; and any fixed ranking of the sector funds makes at most three — the first holding and
the entries of the real-estate fund (September 2016) and the communication fund (June 2018) —
certain to fail gate 1's thirty whatever the prices, so no card is drawn for it (RUNBOOK step 9).

What would make it testable: participant counts, revenues and prices on each side of platforms over
the lab's years, or on-chain series for bitcoin — outside the lab's data — with a return sign for
them in the library.

## Status

AS-020 is recorded `not-testable`: its claim and every refutation read participation, prices and
profitability on each side of platforms, which the lab does not hold; its sources sign
participation, prices, competition and market quality; its nearby return signs are past episodes
read after the fact or EF-008's value form; the dated platform events are about five, single firms';
the platform-to-complements lead-lag is LL-014's; bitcoin's two-sided adoption needs counts the lab
does not hold; and a fixed tilt or ranking of the funds fails gate 1 by count. No card is drawn, and
no trial is spent.
