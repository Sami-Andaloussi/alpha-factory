# AS-036-01 — Governance tokens and DAO voting design: reasoning, not testable as a theory of its own

## The theory, and the forms its sources give it

AS-036 holds that a governance token is priced by the economic value of the control it confers and
by the quality of the voting design it supports — participation, concentration of voting power,
delegation, quorum, the weight of large holders, on-chain or off-chain governance, the rights
attached, the cadence of distribution — so that a vote without clear economic capture is worth
little and captured, plutocratic or apathetic governance durably discounts the token, repricing
around votes, conflicts and reforms of tokenomics. Its refutations: governance tokens' prices
unrelated to their rights or their protocol's voting design; tokens whose votes capture no value
trading at no discount to tokens whose votes direct fees, issuance or the treasury; captured,
plutocratic or apathetic governance carrying no lasting discount, and votes, conflicts and reforms
causing no repricing. The bank names crypto, over days, months and years, and governance votes,
voting-power distributions, token rights and daily prices; its references are Harvey, Ramachandran
and Santoro's *DeFi and the Future of Finance* (2021), Burniske and Tatar's *Cryptoassets* (2017),
Voshmgir's *Token Economy*, whose second edition (2020) is the one the library holds, not the third
the bank names, and two papers the library does not hold, Bellavitis and Momtaz (2025) and Laturnus
(2023).

**Its sources, as the lab read them in the library.**

- **Harvey, Ramachandran and Santoro** (*DeFi and the Future of Finance*, 2021):
  - Chapter IV, "DeFi Primitives": a governance token giving pro rata votes on the changes a
    platform's contracts allow, issued with a static, inflationary or deflationary supply —
    MakerDAO's MKR near static, Compound's COMP inflationary.
  - Chapter VI, "DeFi Deep Dive":
    - MKR's holders voting on collateral and parameters, a healthy platform raising the value of
      their share, global settlement diluting it, and a dividend voted to themselves to be weighed
      against a backlash that could lower MKR's value — the value a vote captures, untimed,
      **EF-042**'s form.
    - Compound Governance tuning parameters under a quorum; its seventh proposal, passed on 15 June
      2020, distributing COMP to users, and COMP's market value spiking above $2 billion once it
      traded — a dated vote followed by a governance token's repricing, the theory's own episode, on
      a token the lab does not hold.
    - UNI released in September 2020 by an airdrop of 400 tokens to each past user, 43% of its
      supply vesting to a treasury its governance controls, its price opening near three dollars,
      spiking above eight and settling at four to five.
  - Chapter VII, "Risks":
    - governance risk;
    - governance tokens likened to activist investors' shares without their fiduciary duty;
    - the attack on True Seigniorage Dollar on 13 March 2021, in which an attacker bought a third of
      the votes, voted himself new tokens and sold them;
    - these are past episodes and untimed claims on tokens the lab does not hold.
- **Voshmgir** (*Token Economy*, second edition, 2020):
  - Part 1, "Protocol Forks & Network Splits": a politicised hard fork as a black swan that can
    seriously affect the value of one's tokens, with Ethereum Classic and Bitcoin Cash as examples;
    Bitcoin Cash opening on 1 August 2017 near 240 dollars while bitcoin traded near 2,700 — the
    theory's governance-conflict repricing, untimed, and one past episode on bitcoin's split.
  - Part 2: on-chain governance as plutocratic, upgrades decided in proportion to uneven holdings,
    the top hundred holders of TheDAO's tokens holding over 46% in May 2016; TheDAO drained of 3.6
    million ether through a flaw in its minority-rights function, and the Ethereum hard fork that
    followed, as the failure of rules written in advance.
- **Burniske and Tatar** (*Cryptoassets*, 2017):
  - Chapter 8: governance among the four economic traits that set an asset class apart, cryptoassets
    governed by their developers, miners and service companies, held to account by holders whose
    leaving would lower demand and the price — untimed.
  - Chapter 5: TheDAO, whose holders voted on the projects it funded; its tokens trading from 28 May
    2016; its hack on 17 June 2016; a contentious hard fork often crashing the units on both chains,
    Ethereum paying dearly for its fork; and Poloniex listing Ethereum Classic on 23 July 2016, near
    5% of Ethereum's network value as the book was written — an untimed claim and a past episode on
    ether, which the lab does not hold.

**Return signs near the theory.**

- **Antonopoulos and Wood** (*Mastering Ethereum*, 2018, Appendix A, "Ethereum Fork History"): the
  DAO fork day by day, with a CarbonVote of 15 and 16 July 2016 in which 5.5% of the ether supply
  voted, 80% for the fork and a quarter of that from one address, the fork at block 1,920,000 on 20
  July, and Ethereum Classic listed on 24 July (Burniske and Tatar say 23 July) — a dated
  token-holders' vote showing apathy and concentration, with no price given, on a token the lab does
  not hold.
- **Huang and others** (*Web3*, 2024):
  - Section 6.3.3: governance tokens especially sought after, UNI, carrying several functions, more
    sought after than single-function tokens.
  - Section 7.3.1: whether governance tokens can hold value tied to their platform's growth not yet
    shown, and LUNA's death spiral in 2022 (**AS-039**'s episode).
  - Section 9.2.1: a MakerDAO proposal approved in October 2022 with only 15% of MKR voting, most of
    it the chief executive's.
  - Section 9.3.2: low turnout letting a few large holders decide.
  - Section 9.3.4: less than 1% of holders in major DAOs holding 90% of the voting power
    (Chainalysis, June 2022).
  - Section 9.3.5: flash-loan votes on MakerDAO by BProtocol on 26 October 2020 and on Beanstalk in
    April 2022.
  - Section 9.3.3: the CFTC's suit against Ooki DAO on 22 September 2022 and its ruling in 2023.
  - Section 11.1.3: MakerDAO's vote in March 2023 to invest $750 million more in Treasuries.
  - These are untimed claims, a static figure and past episodes with no price given, on tokens the
    lab does not hold.
- **On shares**:
  - the voting premium, the price gap between shares with the same cash flows and different votes,
    much larger in Italy than in the United States (Tirole, *The Theory of Corporate Finance*, 2006,
    section 1.7);
  - voting shares at an average premium of 5.44% over non-voting shares in 26 firms, votes being
    worth something only to the extent they can change how the firm is run (Lease, McConnell and
    Mikkelson, 1983, in Damodaran, *Investment Valuation*, fourth edition, 2025, chapter 16, "Voting
    Shares Versus Nonvoting Shares") — the theory's "a vote without economic capture is worth
    little" on shares;
  - a cross-listing in the United States lowering the voting premium (Doidge, 2004, in Foucault,
    Pagano and Röell, *Market Liquidity*, 2013, section 10.6);
  - these are untimed claims on single stocks the lab does not hold, **AS-010**'s form.

None gives a timed rule the lab could read.

## What the lab can read of it

The lab holds the daily bars and volumes of twenty-two funds and bitcoin — the funds from 2005 or
their later start, bitcoin from September 2014 — adjusted for dividends and splits, which encode the
funds' distributions, though no strategy reads them until a point-in-time reading with its own test
is built; the Treasury bill's yield from 2005 and two franc exchange rates; the funds' prices, never
their holdings' prices; and no other cryptoasset, no governance token, no vote and no voting-power
distribution; its snapshot is closed, an asset or a series being added only before the first card.
Nothing was computed from the bars for this judgement. On those data:

- **Governance tokens' prices against their rights and voting design**, the claim and every
  refutation: a cross-section of governance tokens, of which the lab holds none. Bitcoin carries no
  governance token: its holders hold no vote, and its upgrades are decided off-chain and activated
  by miners' signalling (Antonopoulos and Harding, *Mastering Bitcoin*, third edition, 2023, chapter
  12).
- **Other tokens' governance events**: inside bitcoin's in-sample years the library dates more than
  twenty, all on tokens the lab does not hold:
  - The DAO's crowdsale, drain, vote and fork, day by day from April to July 2016;
  - Steem's takeover and forks in 2020, recorded in AS-035-01;
  - Compound's vote of 15 June 2020 and UNI's release;
  - the flash-loan votes of October 2020 and April 2022;
  - True Seigniorage Dollar's attack;
  - the suit against Ooki DAO;
  - MakerDAO's vote of October 2022.
  
  In the holdout it dates MakerDAO's vote of March 2023 and the Ooki ruling, dated to 2023 alone.
  None gives a sign for bitcoin, so a rule trading bitcoin around them would be the lab's invention,
  not the theory's; and a strategy on bitcoin alone breaks the RUNBOOK's rule of at least four
  assets.
- **Bitcoin's own governance**: the library dates five changes inside its in-sample years:
  - the BIP65 soft fork in December 2015;
  - the CSV soft fork in 2016, dated to May in one chapter of *Mastering Bitcoin* and to July in
    another;
  - segwit in 2017, after the BIP148 standoff;
  - the Bitcoin Cash split, trading from 1 August 2017;
  - taproot in November 2021.
  
  None falls in the holdout. A rule entering and leaving around each makes at most eleven decisions,
  fewer if events fall within five sessions of one another, certain to fail gate 1's thirty whatever
  the prices (RUNBOOK step 9), as AS-026-01 counted. The one change carrying a sign, the contentious
  split, makes three.
- **Voting shares**: the lab holds no single stock, and no fund in its universe faces a contest for
  its own control.
- **A fixed stance on bitcoin** makes one decision.

**The bank's siblings, and how AS-036 differs.** AS-036 claims the price of the control a governance
token confers and of the voting design.
- **EF-042**, fee and revenue capture, the value a vote directs rather than the vote, and whose
  capture is fragile where governance can revise it.
- **EF-043**, purpose-driven token value capture.
- **AS-035**, supply and validator concentration, recorded not testable, the holders' weight rather
  than the voting rules.
- **AS-026**, token design, recorded not testable, of which governance rights are one attribute and
  which counted bitcoin's upgrades.
- **AS-021**, the native network token, recorded not testable.
- **AS-038**, crypto-collateralised stablecoins, MakerDAO's system that MKR governs.
- **AS-039**, algorithmic stablecoins.
- **AS-044**, decentralised exchanges' design, Uniswap's.
- **AS-042**, on-chain transparency, where participation can be seen.
- **AS-028**, vesting and unlocks, recorded not testable, UNI's treasury schedule.
- **MR-026**, closed-end fund discounts, where activists' votes on open-ending reprice a fund.
- **AS-010**, the separation of ownership and control, recorded not testable, votes on shares.

All are untouched but AS-010, AS-021, AS-026, AS-028 and AS-035.

What would make it testable: governance tokens with their votes, voting-power distributions and
rights over the lab's years — outside the lab's data — and a timed rule in the library.

## Status

AS-036 is recorded `not-testable`:
- its claim and every refutation read governance tokens, their rights and their votes, and the lab
  holds none, since bitcoin carries no governance token;
- its two papers are not in the library;
- the library's signs are untimed claims, a static figure and past episodes on tokens the lab does
  not hold, with the one exception of bitcoin's split;
- the other tokens' governance events give no sign for bitcoin;
- bitcoin's own five changes make at most eleven decisions.

No card is drawn, and no trial is spent.
