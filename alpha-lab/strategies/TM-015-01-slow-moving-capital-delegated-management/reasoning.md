# TM-015-01 — Slow-moving capital and delegated management: reasoning, not testable as a theory of its own

## The theory, and the forms its sources give it

TM-015 holds that institutions adjust their portfolios slowly after a shock — through committees,
split orders and mandates that limit deviations from a benchmark — so that their flows keep
supporting the move in the direction of the shock for weeks or months, typically 1 to 6 months, in
every major liquid asset class; in futures the slowness shows in the Commitments of Traders reports,
where the positions of large non-commercial traders change slowly and persistently. Its refutations:
institutional allocations that adjust within days; large-trader positions in futures that change
abruptly after shocks; no relation between institutional flows and the continuation of trends;
momentum absent from the major liquid asset classes, indices and styles; delegated investors who
tolerate prolonged deviations from the benchmark, and managers who do not limit them.

**Its sources, as the lab read them in the library.**

- **Vayanos and Woolley** (2013), which the library holds in others' reports: Ang (*Asset
  Management*, 2014, chapter 15, "Delegated Investing") sets out their model — a bad shock to
  fundamentals makes the funds that hold the assets lose, investors who now doubt the managers'
  skill withdraw slowly over several periods, the funds' sales push prices below value, and the
  flows come back once expected returns have risen: momentum, then long-run reversal. Ang reads
  momentum's presence in every large, liquid asset class — stocks, bonds, commodities, currencies,
  where institutions dominate — and its absence in over-the-counter stocks, where individuals
  dominate (Ang, Shtauber and Tetlock, 2013), as consistent with it; he adds that institutional flow
  predicts stock returns and that index inclusions move prices by 3% to 5%. Ilmanen (*Expected
  Returns*, 2011, §5.2) gives the same model, from its 2010 working paper: return-chasing flows push
  prices away from fair value, "inducing short-term momentum and long-term reversal patterns".
- **Ilmanen**, *Expected Returns* (2011, chapter 20), on feedback loops and fashion: across the
  asset classes and strategies of his book, from 1990 to 2009, the correlation of adjacent months'
  returns averages +0.20, and the correlation of the latest six months' return with the six months'
  return 18 months earlier −0.11, which he reads as fluctuating fortunes and fashionability; part of
  the short-term momentum may reflect illiquidity and smoothed returns. A strategy's life cycle —
  inflows after success, crowding, a liquidation that waits for bad times — gives no timing rule.
- **Gray and Vogel** (*Quantitative Momentum*, 2016, chapters 2 and 3): career risk — managers
  judged on relative performance over a year or five cannot hold a strategy that trails its
  benchmark for years, and momentum, like value, has trailed for stretches of five to seven years —
  keeps momentum from being arbitraged away. **Shleifer** (*Inefficient Markets*, 2000, chapter 1)
  names professional managers' herding and benchmark hugging. **Mitchell, Pedersen and Pulvino**
  (2007), which the library holds in full as chapter 8 of Amihud, Mendelson and Pedersen's *Market
  Liquidity* (2013), and **Duffie** (2010), held in others' reports, are slow-moving arbitrage
  capital, TM-014's sources, read with it: their sign is a drop and a rebound, which on liquid
  markets the library's examples resolve within days (TM-014-01). Pedersen (*Efficiently
  Inefficient*, 2015, chapter 12) counts them among the causes of a trend's initial underreaction,
  and Vayanos and Woolley's performance-chasing flows among those of its delayed overreaction, with
  moves extended over three to five years partly reversed: time-series momentum, read by TM-017-01,
  and the reversal, MR-021's. **Levy** (1967), relative strength, is stock momentum; **Bhattacharya
  and Pfleiderer** (1985), the contracting of delegated management, gives no price form.
- **Greyserman and Kaminski** (*Trend Following with Managed Futures*, 2014, "Adaptive Markets and
  Trend Following") read institutional restrictions — drawdown and risk limits, allocation
  constraints — as what makes market players act together in crises, creating the trends trend
  followers ride, their "crisis alpha": the trend over months in crises is **TM-018**'s claim, read
  by TM-018-01 (refuted in its narrow form), and trend following's cycles with crowding are
  **TM-038**'s, untouched.

None of them gives a rule on prices alone of TM-015's own: the one sign that reads prices only is
momentum itself, then reversal, which other theories claim and verdicts of the lab have read.

## What the lab can read of it, and where each form already stands

The lab holds the daily closes of funds and of bitcoin, and the Treasury bill's yield; it holds no
fund flows, holdings, institutional allocations or Commitments of Traders reports, and passes a
strategy no volume. On those data, each of TM-015's forms is either already read by a verdict of the
lab or is the claim of another theory of the bank:

- **Institutional flows that persist after a shock and support the trend, and their relation to the
  continuation**: flows and holdings, which the lab does not hold; Vayanos and Woolley's own
  mechanism, performance-chasing flows between funds and the momentum and reversal they cause, is
  **FP-020**'s claim, the price pressure of fund flows **FP-004**'s, the convex relation of flows to
  performance **FP-019**'s.
- **Large traders' positions in futures changing slowly**: the Commitments of Traders reports, which
  the lab does not hold; **TM-017** names the same reports and counts among its own refutations
  institutional allocations and hedgers' positions that adjust at once to shocks, TM-015's first
  two; **TM-037** names them for sentiment in futures.
- **Momentum in every major liquid asset class, indices and styles**: read on the lab's funds —
  **TM-017-01** (each asset's own trend, stopped at gate 3), **TM-001-01** (between asset classes,
  stopped at gate 2, 1.78% a year above the seven funds in equal parts before costs, 0.57 standard
  errors from zero), **CA-001-01** (within groups, stopped at gate 2, refuted in its base form),
  **TM-003-01** (sector funds, stopped at gate 3, not proven); the lab trades no currency.
  TM-017-01's card took the slow reallocation of institutional capital under mandates and committees
  as part of its own mechanism, and Levy's relative strength — a fund's past year against its group
  predicting its next months — is CA-001-01's, refuted in its base form. Momentum in styles, and
  Ilmanen's momentum of strategies, is **CA-003**'s claim, factor momentum. Ang's contrast with
  over-the-counter stocks needs a stock universe.
- **Continuation over one month**, Ilmanen's +0.20: at the index level over 2 to 16 weeks
  **TM-043**'s claim, untouched, and at one month **TM-017**'s, in progress; within the lab's groups
  over a week, **MR-032-01** found a slight continuation, a rank correlation of +0.0106 (standard
  error 0.0114).
- **The reversal that follows**, Ilmanen's −0.11 at 18 months and Vayanos and Woolley's return of
  flows: **MR-021**'s claim, read as value within groups by **CA-024-01** (0.63% a year below the
  groups from 2010 to 2022, stopped at gate 2, not refuted, not proven); the flows' own reversal is
  **FP-020**'s.
- **Benchmark hugging and managers who limit deviations**: holdings; **FP-013**'s claim,
  benchmarking and closet indexing, and the demand for index members **FP-011**'s and **MR-015**'s;
  the reputational herding of career concerns is **TM-029**'s, institutional herding **FP-009**'s.
- **Pressures that spread between markets**: the lead-lag family's; cross-asset spillovers are
  **LL-042**'s, read by **LL-042-01** (stopped at gate 2, its pure lead and lag refuted in its
  narrow form).
- **Slow arbitrage capital after losses**: **TM-014**'s, recorded not testable, and **FP-006**'s,
  **FP-017**'s and **MR-025**'s; the trend in stressed markets is read by TM-018-01, TM-024-01,
  TM-006-01 and TM-047-01.

**The bank's siblings.** **TM-014**, slow-moving arbitrage capital; **FP-020**, **FP-004** and
**FP-019**, flows; **FP-013**, **FP-011** and **MR-015**, benchmarks and index demand; **TM-029**
and **FP-009**, herding; **TM-012**, momentum trading by mutual funds, recorded not testable;
**CA-003**, factor momentum; **SC-012** and **SC-013**, rebalancing windows and infrequent
rebalancing, recorded not testable; **FP-023**, mandated rebalancing; **TM-023** and **FP-018**, the
calendar of funds' buying; **TM-017**, which carries the same inertia of institutional capital as
its second channel; **TM-018**, crisis alpha, and **TM-038**, adaptive markets; **TM-041**, momentum
and size.

**What was considered, and why it does not rescue a card of TM-015's own:**

- **Momentum stronger where institutions dominate than where individuals do**: on the lab's assets,
  the funds against bitcoin, one asset, whose trend the library gives no sign (TM-005-01). On the
  funds, the large caps against IWM is **TM-041**'s claim, whose source signs autocorrelation larger
  in small caps — against TM-015's reading — and rests on one small-cap fund (TM-008-01).
- **Institutional rebalancing at quarter-ends and the year-end**: a calendar of the same flows.
  Funds buying recent winners before their reporting dates — Sias's quarter-end momentum as Gray and
  Vogel report it — is **TM-023**'s and **FP-018**'s, both untouched, as TM-012-01 recorded; the
  mandated rebalancing of balanced and target-date funds, which trades against the preceding move,
  is **FP-023**'s, untouched; forced flows on rebalancing dates are **SC-012**'s and recurring
  pressure at monthly and quarterly frequencies **SC-013**'s, both recorded not testable (SC-012-01,
  SC-013-01). SC-009-01 found the months that end a quarter no weaker before their end.
- **Momentum's long stretches below the benchmark** (Gray and Vogel): a description of the
  strategy's risk that keeps arbitrageurs out, not a sign on returns the lab could grade.
- **Entering in the direction of a large move and holding it one to six months**: the plain
  continuation over six and twelve months is TM-017-01's (lookbacks of 126 and 252 sessions);
  conditioned on stress it is read by TM-018-01, TM-024-01, TM-006-01 and TM-047-01; after a large
  fall the library signs a rebound, not a continuation (TM-014-01).
- **The momentum of strategies and styles** (Ilmanen's fashion): CA-003's; a strategy's life cycle
  of inflows and crowding is TM-038's.
- **The one-month continuation of the lab's index funds**: TM-043's and TM-017's.

What would make it testable as its own: fund flows or holdings, or the Commitments of Traders
reports, frozen in the snapshot.

## Status

TM-015 is recorded `not-testable` as a theory of its own: its flows, positions and benchmark hugging
need data the lab does not hold and are FP-020's, FP-004's, FP-019's, FP-013's, TM-029's, TM-037's
and TM-017's, which carries the same inertia of institutional capital; the momentum in liquid asset
classes it relies on is read by TM-017-01, TM-001-01, CA-001-01 and TM-003-01, its styles are
CA-003's, its one-month continuation TM-043's and TM-017's, its reversal MR-021's; its quarter-end
calendar is TM-023's, FP-018's and FP-023's; its trends in crises are TM-018's, read by TM-018-01,
and its crowding cycles TM-038's; its slow arbitrage capital is TM-014's. No card is drawn, and no
trial is spent.
