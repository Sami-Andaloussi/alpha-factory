# TM-014-01 — Slow-moving arbitrage capital: reasoning, not testable as a theory of its own

## The theory, and the forms its sources give it

TM-014 holds that after arbitrageurs lose money and face redemptions, the prices of the securities
they held stay away from value for longer than expected, in a prolonged trend, until their capital
is rebuilt: a horizon of several weeks to a few months, seen in convertible bond and debt arbitrage,
and in equities when markets are stressed. Its refutations: price dislocations that close quickly
after the arbitrageurs' losses, without waiting for their capital to be rebuilt; fresh capital that
enters at once after forced liquidations; forced sales by arbitrage funds that leave no lasting
trace in the prices of what they held; no stronger continuation of dislocations in periods of stress
than in calm ones.

**Its sources, as the lab read them in the library.**

- **Mitchell, Pedersen and Pulvino** (2007), which the library holds in full as chapter 8, "Slow
  Moving Capital", of Amihud, Mendelson and Pedersen's *Market Liquidity: Asset Pricing, Risk, and
  Crises* (2013), and on which Pedersen's *Efficiently Inefficient* (2015, chapter 15, convertible
  bond arbitrage) draws. In the first quarter of 2005 more than 20% of the capital of convertible
  bond hedge funds was redeemed; their forced sales cheapened the bonds by about 3% against their
  theoretical full-liquidity value, most around redemption dates, and levered convertible arbitrage
  lost about 7% before making back 15% as the market recovered. In 1998, when Long Term Capital
  Management liquidated its convertibles, they cheapened by 4%, and "it took several months" for
  traders to rebuild their capital or for better-capitalised ones to enter. In the 1987 crash merger
  spreads widened several-fold and stayed wide for several months, although the market rebounded.
  Pedersen adds the spiral of the funds' funding problems and the drying of market liquidity, in
  1998, 2005 and most violently 2008 (after Brunnermeier and Pedersen, 2009, and Mitchell and
  Pulvino, 2012).
- **Duffie's "Asset price dynamics with slow-moving capital"** (2010) is cited by Campbell's
  *Financial Decisions and Markets* (2018), for which an asset's expected return rises with its
  supply relative to the wealth of its investors and, in Duffie's model, the short-term response to
  a supply shock is dampened by the expected return of capital; by Ang's *Asset Management* (2014),
  for the persistent returns of illiquid markets where capital cannot be deployed at once — real
  estate, private equity, timber, infrastructure — and for the limited integration of asset classes;
  and by Ilmanen's *Expected Returns* (2011), among the readings on liquidity droughts.

**The sign the sources give is a drop and a rebound; the drift Pedersen draws from it is time-series
momentum's.** The introduction to the third part of *Market Liquidity* reads the crises of 1987,
1998, 2005, 2007, 2008 and 2010 as "a drop and rebound of illiquid securities as capital becomes
tight and comes back over time", slower in more illiquid markets: the recovery is quick in liquid
markets — the flash crash of 2010 in the S&P 500 futures resolved within the day, and the quant
equity unwind of August 2007 within days — and slow in illiquid ones, the convertibles taking
months. Pedersen (chapter 12) counts slow-moving arbitrage capital among the frictions that "slow
price discovery and lead to a drop and rebound of prices", a cause of the underreaction that trend
following trades, and (chapter 15) has convertible arbitrage earning high returns "as the cheapness
diminished". On liquid markets the library's examples resolve within a day or days; it gives no
months-long horizon for liquid funds, which TM-014 would need.

## What the lab can read of it, and where each form already stands

The lab holds the daily closes of equity, sector, Treasury and commodity funds and of bitcoin, and
the Treasury bill's yield; it holds no convertible bonds, no corporate credit, no hedge fund returns
or flows, no theoretical values against which a dislocation is measured, and no record of
arbitrageurs' losses or redemptions. Every refutation TM-014 names reads a dislocation from value,
an arbitrageur's loss or a forced sale:

- **Dislocations from value after arbitrageurs' losses, and their slow closing**: a theoretical
  value and the arbitrage funds' capital, which the lab does not hold; the liquidity spirals of
  margin and deleveraging are **FP-006**'s claim, the supply shocks with slow capital **FP-017**'s,
  the limits of arbitrage **MR-025**'s, and slow capital in delegated management **TM-015**'s.
- **Forced sales leaving a trace in prices**: the positions sold, which the lab does not hold;
  forced sales by constrained intermediaries are **FP-006**'s and **FP-005**'s, and price pressure
  from fund flows **FP-004**'s.
- **A continuation stronger in stress than in calm**, on the lab's funds: the trend over months in
  stressed markets is read — TM-018-01 (trend held only after crises or divergent months, about 1.2%
  a year of alpha below trend held always, refuted in its narrow form; TM-017-01's own edge in the
  months after crises was −1.75% a year against +2.15% outside them, reported, not graded); in the
  panic months TM-024-01 read, the within-group momentum tilt lost 6.86% a year against the other
  months (t −0.96, reported in TM-006-01), and TM-006-01 found it no weaker in the other down states
  (+0.19% a year, t +0.04); TM-047-01's trend scaled down in high volatility gained from its tilt to
  bonds, not its timing. At days to weeks the library signs the form the other way (TM-011-01:
  short-term reversals stronger amid high volatility), and that form is **MR-001**'s.
- **The trend in time-series momentum**, which Pedersen ties in part to slow-moving capital through
  slower price discovery: read by TM-017-01, still TM-017's in progress.

**The bank's siblings.** **TM-015**, slow-moving capital and delegated management; **FP-006**,
liquidity spirals; **FP-005**, fire sales and their recovery; **FP-017**, search frictions and slow
capital; **MR-025**, limits to arbitrage; **MR-044**, countercyclical expected returns; **MR-012**,
market resilience after liquidity shocks; **MR-035** and **MR-037**, dislocations between related
assets; **TM-035**, procyclical leverage; **FP-024**, stop-loss cascades.

**What was considered, and why it does not rescue a card of TM-014's own:**

- **Buying the risky funds after a crisis and holding them for months, a recovery premium**: the
  sources' own sign, the rebound. Ilmanen (*Expected Returns*, 2011, box 18.1) advises buying
  illiquid traditional assets when liquidity is scarce and markets distressed, but (§5.2.3) finds it
  "not clear that high volatility predicts above-average near-term returns for the equity market":
  no signed monthly size for liquid funds. The rebound is **FP-005**'s claim (the selling phase
  followed by high returns as prices recover), **FP-006**'s (post-panic rebounds once funding
  stabilises), **MR-025**'s, **MR-044**'s and **FP-017**'s. And it is read on the same sessions:
  TM-018-01's base, holding the seven funds in equal parts out of the divergent state, caught the
  2009 rebound TM-017-01 missed, +13.0 points of hedged return from April to July; TM-024-01's base
  gained 9.3 points on CA-001-01's from March to November 2009, when the beaten funds led the
  rebound; TM-017-01 stayed out of equities from March 2009 into the autumn while SPY rose 50%, EFA
  66% and EEM 91%. A recovery card drawn now would be chosen from those published results.
- **The less liquid funds — IWM, EEM, the sectors — recovering against SPY after a crisis**: the
  library signs a slower recovery in less liquid markets, and Amihud, Mendelson and Wood find in
  1987 that the stocks whose liquidity recovered most recovered most in price; it is **FP-006**'s
  second prediction almost word for word (the assets sold far below value recovering relative to the
  liquid ones once the funding constraint eases), and TM-024-01's panic group-months read it in part
  among the equity markets.
- **Dislocations between related funds — GLD and SLV, IEF and TLT, SPY and QQQ — closing slowly
  after stress**: no parity links them and the library gives these pairs no sign; the lab holds no
  net asset values, futures, miners or off-the-run bonds; the forms are **MR-035**'s, cointegration,
  **MR-037**'s, parity deviations that widen under stress, and **MR-036**'s.
- **Volatility or the bill rate as the state that times liquidity provision**: at days to weeks
  MR-001's; at the market level Ilmanen gives no sign; the bill rate does not measure the funding
  tightness the sources read in margins, and it falls in the crises themselves.
- **The calendar of redemption deadlines**: Mitchell, Pedersen and Pulvino find the convertibles'
  discount deepest around the deadlines for investors' redemption notices, 45 days before the end of
  June and of December; the sign is on convertibles against their theoretical value, none on funds,
  and the lab holds no convertibles.
- **Persistent returns in illiquid private markets** (Ang): real estate, private equity and timber,
  none of which the lab holds; its real-estate sector fund is listed and liquid.
- **A continuation fed by deleveraging after losses**: **TM-035**'s, procyclical leverage, and
  **FP-024**'s, stop-loss cascades; at months read by TM-018-01, TM-047-01, TM-024-01 and TM-006-01.

What would make it testable as its own: prices and theoretical values of convertible bonds, or
credit spreads and the capital of the funds that arbitrage them.

## Status

TM-014 is recorded `not-testable`: every one of its refutations reads a dislocation from value, an
arbitrageur's loss or a forced sale, on securities the lab does not hold; its sources sign the
mechanism as a drop and a rebound, which on liquid markets the library's examples resolve within
days — MR-001's and FP-006's horizon; its continuation in stress on the lab's funds is read by
TM-018-01, TM-024-01, TM-006-01 and TM-047-01; its recovery forms are FP-005's, FP-006's, MR-025's
and MR-044's, read in part on the same sessions by TM-018-01 and TM-024-01; its spirals and slow
capital are FP-006's, FP-017's, TM-015's and MR-025's. No card is drawn, and no trial is spent.
