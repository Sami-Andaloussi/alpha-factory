# AS-013-01 — Firm life cycle theory of dividends: reasoning, not testable as a theory of its own

## The theory, and the forms its sources give it

AS-013 holds that payout rises with a firm's maturity — young firms with many growth opportunities
retain their cash, mature firms with few valuable projects distribute more — that each firm's payout
regime is stable because its life cycle moves slowly, and that its stock-market behaviour and
investors are tied to its stage. Its refutations: young, high-growth firms paying as much as mature
ones; payout regimes changing quickly with no change in opportunities or maturity; firms and sectors
moving into maturity, or finding new opportunities, keeping their payout unchanged. The bank names
stocks, years, and dividends, retained earnings, firm age, balance sheets and daily prices.

**Its sources, as the lab read them in the library.**

- **Baker, ed.** (*Dividends and Dividend Policy*, 2009), chapter 12, "The Firm Life Cycle Theory of
  Dividends": a simple model in which a value-maximising firm pays nothing while its investments
  earn more than its cost of capital and everything once mature, the intuition the chapter shares
  with Mueller (1972), whose managers' firm paying too little is AS-011's ground; initiators larger,
  more profitable and richer in cash, with fewer opportunities, than non-initiators at the same
  stage (Bulan, Subramanian and Tanlu, 2007), who also find the life cycle not explaining the
  reaction to initiations; the share of payers falling after 1978, partly because the new lists were
  young, high-growth firms, partly through a lower propensity to pay (Fama and French, 2001); the
  propensity to pay tied to retained earnings over total equity or assets (DeAngelo and others,
  2006); systematic risk falling around dividend increases and rising after cuts, tied to the
  positive announcement return (Grullon and others, 2002), evidence the chapter calls ambiguous.
- **Chapter 24**, "Why Firms Begin Paying Dividends: Value, Growth, and Life Cycle Effects":
  dividend initiators' announcement returns falling steadily as their market-to-book rises, their
  profitability rising before the initiation and falling back after, and the low market-to-book
  initiators' factor loadings falling after it — on single firms' initiations.
- **Chapter 13**, on catering: when initiations outnumber omissions, dividend payers' returns are
  relatively low over the next one to three years, and the reverse (Baker and Wurgler, 2004b) — the
  one market-level return sign on appearing and disappearing dividends.

## What the lab can read of it

The lab holds the daily bars and volumes of twenty-two funds and bitcoin — the funds from 2005 or
their later start, bitcoin from September 2014 — adjusted for dividends and splits, which encode the
funds' distributions, though no strategy reads them until a point-in-time reading with its own test
is built; the Treasury bill's yield from 2005 and two franc exchange rates; the funds' prices, never
their holdings' prices; and no single stock, firm age, retained earnings, balance sheet, earnings or
repurchase; its snapshot is closed, an asset or a series being added only before the first card.
Nothing was computed from the bars for this judgement. On those data:

- **Payout rising with maturity and stable regimes**, the claim and its first two refutations:
  firms' payouts against their age, opportunities and retained earnings, not held.
- **Sectors moving into maturity and their payout**, the third refutation: the funds' distributions
  give a dividend yield on price, not a payout ratio, which needs earnings the lab does not hold; a
  rising yield can be a falling price; and repurchases, the larger part of US payout (Baker, part
  III), are not in the distributions, so a sector maturing through buybacks would show nothing. Even
  the payout pattern cannot be read, and the library signs no sector fund's return tied to its
  stage.
- **Initiators' returns by market-to-book, and risk around dividend changes** (chapters 12 and 24):
  single firms' events and ratios, not held.
- **Payers against non-payers as initiations outnumber omissions** (Baker and Wurgler): the signal
  needs the market's counts of initiations and omissions, the target payer and non-payer portfolios;
  the funds' distributions stand in for neither, the number of payers having fallen while total
  dividends rose (Baker, chapter 2); no bank theory holds catering, whose dividend premium is one of
  the measures of **TM-037**'s sentiment composite, which its card recorded that the lab does not
  hold.
- **Payout and valuation**: the payout yield as a predictor is **EF-030**'s, repurchases
  **EF-029**'s, the index's dividend yield and news **MR-044**'s, the funds' yields as carry or
  value **CA-025**'s and **EF-008**'s, the agency cost of retention **AS-011**'s, signalling
  **AS-012**'s.
- **Other life-cycle readings in the library** — a maturing business's returns declining relative to
  its sector (Koller and others, *Valuation*), payout over the life cycle (Tirole, chapter 2), the
  life cycle as a valuation input (Damodaran), industry decline (Porter) — are on single businesses
  or carry no return sign; no crypto source ties a network's maturity to a payout, and bitcoin pays
  none.

The lab's decision that the bars encode the funds' distributions asked this theory's writer, when
picked, to build a point-in-time reading of them first. This judgement did not: its only form on the
funds is a yield pattern that is not the theory's payout and carries no return sign. The reading
falls to the first of MR-044, CA-025, EF-030 and EF-008 to be picked, as recorded beside that
decision.

**The bank's siblings.** **EF-029**, repurchases; **EF-030**, net payout yield; **EF-016**,
value-creating growth; **CA-013**, business-cycle sector rotation; **MR-044**, countercyclical
expected returns; **CA-025**, carry; **EF-008**, value; all untouched; **TM-037**, investor
sentiment, tested inconclusive; **AS-008**, **AS-011** and **AS-012**, recorded not testable.

What would make it testable: firms' payouts with their age, retained earnings and growth
opportunities, or the market's counts of initiations and omissions — outside the lab's data — or a
measure of a sector's stage readable on the funds with a return sign in the library.

## Status

AS-013 is recorded `not-testable`: its claim and refutations read firms' payouts against their
maturity and opportunities, which the lab does not hold; the funds' distributions give yields, not
payout ratios, and leave out repurchases; its price signs are all on single firms' dividend events,
and its one market-level sign, Baker and Wurgler's, needs counts of initiations and omissions the
lab does not hold. No card is drawn, and no trial is spent.
