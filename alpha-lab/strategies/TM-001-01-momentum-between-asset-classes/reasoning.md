# TM-001-01 — Momentum between asset classes: reasoning

## Why this theory now

TM-001 is the relative form of price continuation: the assets that did best over the past 3 to 12
months, the last month skipped, keep beating those that did worst over the next 3 to 12 months,
through underreaction, the slow diffusion of news and the flows that chase winners, and survive
because arbitrage is limited. Its refutations: past winners earning no more than past losers, in the
US or in other markets, or the spread vanishing once systematic risk is accounted for; momentum no
stronger among stocks with low analyst coverage; a continuation entirely explained by industries or
common factors, with no firm-specific part; firm news fully priced on its announcement; no weakening
of momentum in January, and no losses on the short side when markets rebound after a sharp fall.

**Its sources, as the lab read them in the library's books.**

- **Jegadeesh and Titman**, in their own chapter of *Advances in Behavioral Finance, Volume II*
  (Thaler, ed., 2005, chapter 10, "Momentum"): stocks traded on the NYSE and AMEX from 1965 to 1989,
  the decile of winners of the past 3 to 12 months beating the decile of losers over the next 3 to
  12 in every pairing; in their 2001 sample, to 1998 and with the Nasdaq, the six-month strategy
  loses in January, −1.55% a month, and earns in every other calendar month.
- **Ang** (*Asset Management*, 2014): momentum is "primarily a cross-sectional strategy within an
  asset class", and it "manifests across asset classes", citing Blitz and Van Vliet (2008), "Global
  tactical cross-asset allocation: applying value and momentum across asset classes" (chapter 7);
  in the same passage, rebalancing should be done at the level of asset classes, since they exist
  over the long run. Momentum is observed in equities, fixed income, currencies, real estate and
  commodities, "within and across asset classes" (chapter 14); a long-only investor still earns the
  value and momentum premiums, dented by 50 to 60%, as Israel and Moskowitz (2013) find (chapter
  14). Ang cites Asness, Moskowitz and Pedersen for momentum in equities, government bonds,
  currencies and commodities (chapter 7), though CA-001-01 read their table as showing none among
  government bonds. And in chapter 4 he judges the evidence for predictability weak, and recommends
  that tactical weights be small in practice.
- **Ilmanen** (*Expected Returns*, 2011, §14.5): momentum works in virtually every asset class
  studied, "as well as across asset classes"; recent winners among stocks beat recent losers by
  roughly 1% a month; fixed income country allocation fares least well, relative reversal dominating
  among long-dated bonds of different countries. In his box on equity momentum, the popular
  indicator skips the last month to avoid the short-term reversal; Novy-Marx finds the most
  information in the returns of 6 to 12 months ago. Across asset classes, volatility has been
  rewarded (chapter 2).
- **The Zacks *Handbook of Equity Market Anomalies*** (2011, chapter 10) reports Blitz and Van
  Vliet's design — twelve asset classes ranked monthly into four portfolios of three, from February
  1986 to September 2007 — and their value result, 7.9% a year between the top and bottom
  portfolios, not their momentum result; in chapter 11, a note says that tactical asset allocation,
  popular among institutions from 1985 to 1995, performed poorly. **Gray and Vogel** (*Quantitative
  Momentum*, 2016) say cross-sectional momentum is often used to select among asset classes, with no
  sign.

The library's sources give the between-class form a sign — positive — and no size. Dual momentum,
the practitioners' mix of the relative and the absolute rule, is named in the library by the title
of Antonacci's book alone, with no sign.

**Its forms, and what the lab has already read of each.**

- **Single stocks**, Jegadeesh and Titman's deciles, and with them the analyst coverage, the

  others.

  **TM-002-01**, the 52-week high among the sector funds, a neighbour of the claim rather than a

- **Industries**, Moskowitz and Grinblatt's: **TM-003-01**, the three sector funds with the best six

- **Countries**, Rouwenhorst's twelve European markets: the claim of **CA-017**, country-index

- **The crashes on rebounds**: **TM-024-01**, momentum managed for its crashes, stopped at gate 2,

- **With value**: **CA-024-01**, value alone within the groups, stopped at gate 2; **TM-040-01**,
  momentum with value, stopped at gate 2, not proven.
- **January's weakening**: the claim of **TM-023**, momentum seasonality, which includes January;

- **Between asset classes**, Blitz and Van Vliet's and Ang's: **no card grades it, and no untouched
  theory claims it.** CA-001, whose title names asset classes and which cites the same passage of
  Ang, ranks "within a group of comparable assets"; CA-001-01 ranked within groups only, and
  rejected one ranking across its nineteen funds because it "would compare a sector with gold by
  raw return, and hold whatever is most volatile after a rise; the theory compares comparable
  assets". That objection was CA-001's: its theory ranks comparable assets. The between-class claim
  is a different one, the global tactical allocation that Ang and Ilmanen name, which ranks unlike
  assets by design; the volatility the objection names is this card's first risk, and is reported
  (below). TM-017-01 held the same seven assets each by its own trend against cash, the absolute
  form, and stopped at gate 3. SC-023-01 reported, not graded, a residual form across five classes
  (below).

This card reads the one form of TM-001 on the lab's funds that no card grades and no other theory
claims.

## From the claim to a signal

- **The assets**: TM-017-01's seven — SPY, EFA and EEM, the US, developed and emerging equity
  markets; IEF and TLT, US Treasuries at two durations; GLD and DBC, gold and a commodity basket —
  ranked together. The same universe lets the between-class ranking be compared with the absolute
  trend on the same assets, and it spans the classes a global tactical allocation holds with the
  funds the lab has from 2005 and DBC from 2006. The sector funds, QQQ and IWM are left out: they
  would weight the ranking toward US equities, where CA-001-01 and TM-003-01 have read the
  within-class choice; SLV is left out as a second metal beside GLD; bitcoin, which the theory does
  not name, is left out. IEF and TLT are one market at two durations; Ilmanen's relative reversal
  among long-dated bonds is across countries, an analogy only here. When both lead, the rule holds
  duration; the share of months with both bonds held is reported.
- **The signal**: on the first session of each month, each asset's return over a window read up to
  the session before: it ends `skip` sessions before that session and starts `lookback` sessions
  before it — CA-001-01's window and names. The base is the sources' uniform measure, the past year
  with the last month skipped, `lookback` 252 and `skip` 21, which they call conservative outside
  single stocks, and which Novy-Marx's finding supports; the variant keeps the last month, `skip` 0,
  the form they find stronger outside single stocks, and the one that did a little better in
  CA-001-01. Neighbours: `lookback` 189 and 315, 126 and 378; `skip` 16 and 26, 10 and 32.
- **The rule**: the `top` assets ranked highest by that return, 2 of the seven, held in equal parts,
  the whole portfolio always invested, or all those ranked when fewer; the top third of seven,
  rounded, CA-001-01's share of each group. An asset is ranked when it trades on the target's
  session and traded on the window's first session, with a return over the window; an asset trading
  but not ranked is not held. DBC, which trades from February 2006, is ranked from about March 2007:
  until then, the rule chooses two among six, and the benchmark holds DBC from its first session.
  Ties, which daily closes make unlikely, go to the asset listed first. `top`'s neighbours are 1
  and 3.
- **The timing**: targets on the first session of each month from the first on which an asset is
  ranked, 2006-02-01, set then whether or not they change, as TM-017-01's were; traded at the
  session's close, as every target in the lab. About 203 months in-sample.

## What the battery judges, and what to expect

**The size predicted — a judgment.** The sources give the between-class ranking a positive sign and
no size. CA-001-01 predicted about 2% a year for the within-class form on these markets: half its
sources' long-only top third against its class, halved for a narrower universe and later years. The
card judges the top two's return above the seven's equal weight at about 0 to +2% a year before
costs, at or below that prediction, because it ranks unlike assets, where the gap to the seven
carries each class's volatility, and because Ang advises small tactical weights between classes and
the Zacks handbook reports tactical allocation's poor record; nothing places it higher in that
range. The lab's own within-class result, a tenth of CA-001-01's prediction, is disclosed above and
is not used to set the size. Costs: a change of one of the two holdings trades half the portfolio
each way, 5 basis points a side; if one holding changes in about two months of five — a judgment,
not a count of the signal — costs are about 0.24% a year, and 0.4% at about eight changes a year.
After the rule's costs, the gap is about −0.4 to +1.8% a year; the benchmark, rebalanced monthly,
pays costs of its own, which narrows the difference a little.

**The volatility the ranking chooses.** The seven funds' annual volatilities range from about 6.5%
(IEF) to about 22% (EEM) — measured in-sample before the card, see below — and a raw ranking by
past return holds, after a rise, whatever rose most — most often the most volatile. Across classes,
volatility has been rewarded (Ilmanen), so part of the rule's return may be its greater exposure,
not its choice; the battery's alpha is taken over the benchmark at the regression's beta, the
clause's own gap is the gap at a beta of one, and the card reports the gap of the same rule ranked
by the window's return divided by the window's daily volatility.

**Measured before the card.** Before this card, the writer computed on the in-sample closes, 2006 to
2022, the calendar-month volatility of each fund and of the gap between each of the 21 pairs and
the seven's equal weight: second moments only; no mean, no signal and no rule was computed. That
is a departure from the lab's practice of measuring nothing before the lock, and the verdict
records it. It was done after a first draft had judged TM-001 not testable, and before this card
was drawn.

**The gates.** The gap from a pair to the seven has moved by about 7.7% a year at the median of the
21 possible pairs in-sample, from 4.5 to 13.3% (measured before the card), and SC-023-01's top-two
rule across classes moved by 13.0%: an appraisal ratio of about 0 to 0.25 for the predicted range.
The card expects the base to fail gate 4 — the best of some fifty effective trials is expected to
reach an appraisal ratio of about 0.63 by luck, so that passing needs about 0.9 — and gate 5, whose
blend must pass gate 4; gate 3 unless the gap sits at the top of the range; gate 2 turns on the
Sharpe ratio of a portfolio of two funds, above the benchmark's only if the gap outweighs the added
volatility, and is near its threshold. Gate 6 is likely to fail: on one asset's share of the
profit, two assets being held at a time, each half the portfolio, and SPY having carried 33% of
TM-017-01's profit on the same seven, above the 30% any one asset may; or on the `top` 1 neighbour,
which holds one volatile fund at a time, the neighbour on which SC-023-01 failed. The `lookback`
neighbours at 315 and 378 sessions set their first targets about three and six months after the
base's and sit in cash until then.

**The clause.** Judged before costs, on the market's closes, over the in-sample months from the
first target, 2006-02-01, the last ending at the close of 2022-12-30: each month from the close of
its first session to the close of the next month's first session, the return of the top two by the
base's signal, in equal parts, less the equal-weighted return of those of the seven funds that
trade on the month's first session; the mean of those monthly gaps, its standard error from their
standard deviation over the months, and its t statistic, computed apart from the battery. The theory
is refuted in this form if that t statistic is −0.35 or below: the past year's leaders among asset
classes no better than the classes held in equal parts, the bank's first refutation for the lab's
markets. Above it, short of passing gates 1 to 7, it is not proven, not refuted; a base that passes
gates 1 to 7 while the clause refutes leaves the theory refuted in this form.

**The test's power.** About 203 months; a monthly gap moving by about 7.7 to 13% a year: a standard
error of about 1.9 to 3.2% a year on the mean. The clause refutes about 36% of the time with no
effect, and about 8 to 16% at +2% a year, the top of the range. It cannot tell an effect of the
predicted size from none.

**TM-001's refutations, and what the card can grade.**

1. *Past winners earning no more than past losers, or the spread vanishing once systematic risk is
   accounted for*: graded, on the long side, by the clause; reported, the winners less the losers,
   the top two less the bottom two by the same signal, with its t statistic; the battery's alpha
   reads the systematic risk of the benchmark, and the risk-ranked gap is reported.
2. *Momentum no stronger among stocks with low analyst coverage*, 3. *a continuation explained by
   industries or common factors*, 4. *firm news fully priced*: single stocks' refutations, which the
   lab's funds cannot read.
5. *No weakening in January, and no losses on the short side when markets rebound*: reported, not
   graded. January: the clause's gap in January and in the other months, TM-023's claim, which this
   card does not grade. The rebounds: a long-only rule has no short side, and reads a rebound only
   through leaders that trail it; the gap in the months whose target follows a fall of the seven's
   equal weight over the 252 sessions to the session before the target, and in the other months;
   from July to December 2008, and from March to December 2009.

**Measures stated before the run**, reported, not graded:

- the gap for the variant, `skip` 0; the top two less the bottom two, for the base;
- the clause's gap, the gap at a beta of one, set against the regression's alpha;
- the gap of the same rule ranked by the window's return divided by the standard deviation of the
  window's daily returns;
- how often each asset is held, how often both places go to one class — equities, Treasuries, or
  gold and commodities — and the months with IEF and TLT both held;
- the gap in January and in the other months; in the months after the seven fell over the past year
  and in the others; from July to December 2008; from March to December 2009;
- over 2006–2013 and 2014–2022, without the months whose target falls in 2008, and without those in
  2020; over the holdout, 2023 to 2025;
- set against TM-017-01 on the same months: the share of months in which the two hold the same
  assets, and the correlation of their monthly returns less the seven's;
- the alpha before and after costs, the neighbours' and the one-day-late rule's alphas and
  appraisal ratios.

## Choices, and the options rejected

- **Recording TM-001 not testable**: a first draft did, taking CA-001-01 to have read the
  continuation across the lab's markets; it had ranked within classes, not between them, and the
  between-class form has a sign in TM-001's own sources.
- **The within-class form, again**: CA-001-01's verdict; a repeat would read nothing new.
- **Volatility-scaled positions or a risk-adjusted ranking as the rule**: the sources' combined
  portfolios scale by volatility, but the plain ranking is the claim, and TM-047-01 has read scaled
  trend; the risk-ranked gap is reported.
- **Dual momentum** — the leaders held only while they beat cash: the absolute half is TM-017-01's,
  and the library gives the mix no sign.
- **Twelve classes**, as Blitz and Van Vliet's allocation: the lab's universe was fixed before its
  first card; the seven are the classes it holds from 2005, and DBC from 2006.
- **Three bonds**: SHY is cash at a short duration, and a third Treasury fund would give the bonds
  three places of eight in the ranking of one market.
- **Jegadeesh and Titman's 6/6 in overlapping tranches**: TM-003-01 read it on the sector funds; the
  126-session neighbour is a shorter window, not that rule.
- **Shorting the losers**: the lab does not short.

## Implementation

Long only, two of seven funds in equal halves, with European (UCITS) funds that track them; one
decision a month, the signal read up to the session before and traded at the close, a day's delay;
a change of one holding in about two months of five, a judgment.
