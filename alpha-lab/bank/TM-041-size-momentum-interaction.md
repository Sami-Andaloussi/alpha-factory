## Mechanism

Momentum is significantly stronger for small capitalisations than for large ones. Small firms are
covered by fewer analysts and traded less actively, and their information circulates more slowly
through the market: their prices take longer to complete their adjustment, which produces a
stronger and more durable momentum. Small capitalisations also have fewer sophisticated institutional
investors at the margin, which slows the arbitrage of the anomaly.

By analogy, in equity futures the mechanism can concern futures on small-capitalisation indices,
such as Russell 2000 futures; in commodity futures, the less liquid markets, such as small commodity
markets, tend to show stronger momentum.

## Prediction

Return autocorrelation is larger for small firms than for large ones: Lo and MacKinlay (1990) report
an autocorrelation of weekly returns of about 42% for small capitalisations against only 9% for large
ones. The effect plays out over weeks to months; its natural horizon is shorter for small
capitalisations than for large ones, but its amplitude is larger.

## What would refute it

Momentum and return autocorrelation no larger in small capitalisations than in large ones, or no
larger among small firms with little analyst coverage and trading.
