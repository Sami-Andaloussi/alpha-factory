## Mechanism

Earnings power value estimates the value of a company by capitalising its sustainable level of
current earnings, assuming no growth. The key point is that a large share of valuation errors comes
from over-optimistic assumptions about distant growth. Earnings power value starts again from a
normalised level of profits or of distributable cash, corrected for transitory items, for the
business cycle and for maintenance needs.

It works in two ways. First, it gives a conservative anchor for judging whether the market is paying
too much for speculative growth. Second, comparing earnings power value with asset value separates
several cases: under-profitability and value traps, a normal competitive business, and a genuine
franchise.

The mechanism bears mostly on mature, profitable companies, whose current profits already carry
information.

## Prediction

Where the price rests on speculative growth beyond the earnings power value, the market is paying too
much for that growth, since a large share of valuation errors comes from over-optimistic assumptions
about distant growth: such stocks are overvalued relative to the conservative anchor. The comparison
of earnings power value with asset value separates under-profitability and value traps, normal
competitive businesses and genuine franchises. The pattern concerns mature, profitable companies
above all.

## What would refute it

- Among mature, profitable companies, stocks priced well above their earnings power value, that is,
  paying for growth, that do not underperform stocks priced at or below it.
- Growth assumptions embedded in prices that turn out, on average, to be realised rather than
  over-optimistic.
- A comparison of earnings power value with asset value that does not separate value traps from
  competitive businesses and franchises in subsequent profitability.
