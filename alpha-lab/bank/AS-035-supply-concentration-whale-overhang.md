## Mechanism

An asset whose supply is highly concentrated among a few large holders does not have the same price
dynamics as a widely held asset. When a large fraction of the supply, or of the validation power, is
concentrated among a few addresses, funds, pools or validators, the asset acquires a structural
fragility of its own: the price becomes more sensitive to the liquidity needs of a small number of
actors, to explicit or tacit coordination, and to the market's perception of the real degree of
decentralisation. Concentration acts through several channels: a reduction of the float actually
available, the risk of discretionary sales of large blocks, the capacity to manipulate or influence
liquidity, and the disproportionate weight of some agents in governance or in the market narrative.
The mechanism is not only a matter of liquidity: it also bears on governance, on potential censorship,
on the risk of a fork and on the credibility of the protocol's monetary policy. The price then
incorporates a premium or a discount tied to the ownership structure itself.

The mechanism persists because large holders are not economically passive. They can have liquidity
needs, particular horizons, superior information or a political role in the protocol. The market does
not know exactly when or how the concentration will turn into flows. There is therefore a "latent
overhang", comparable in spirit to that of blockholders or non-floating holders in equities. In crypto
the phenomenon is especially salient when the initial distribution was concentrated, or when
treasuries and foundations hold large percentages of the supply. Concentration is not the same as poor
performance: a high concentration can also support the price through the scarcity of the float, until
the supply risk reappears.

## Prediction

Assets whose supply or validation power is concentrated trade at a premium or a discount tied to their
holding structure, and their prices are more sensitive to the liquidity needs of a few actors, to
explicit or tacit coordination and to the market's perception of the real degree of decentralisation.
The horizon can be durable, with accelerations around governance events, bankruptcies, vesting,
transfers to exchanges or known sales. The phenomenon has been documented in proof-of-work networks
concentrated in mining pools, in some proof-of-stake protocols, and in ecosystems such as Steem where
whales dominate the distribution. The setting is naturally crypto. By analogy, the logic extends
beyond crypto: it resembles the blockholder and reduced-float effects in equities.

## What would refute it

- Assets with highly concentrated supply or validation power show the same price dynamics as widely
  held assets.
- Governance events, bankruptcies, vesting, transfers to exchanges and known sales by large holders
  move the prices of concentrated assets no more than those of widely held ones.
- Once custodial addresses are separated from economic owners, the measured concentration bears no
  relation to price behaviour.
