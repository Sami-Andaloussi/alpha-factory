## Mechanism

Profitability measured high in the income statement, gross profits scaled by assets, is a cleaner
signal of true economic profitability than net income, EBIT, or even free cash flow taken alone. The
reasons are structural: the lower one goes in the income statement, the more the economic quality of
the business is mixed with discretionary management decisions, the capital structure, selling
expenses, research and development spending and accounting conventions. Expenses lower in the income
statement, notably selling, general and administrative costs, expansion spending, commercial policy
or some accounting choices, can mask the economic quality of the core business. A company can be
economically superior to its competitors while showing temporarily mediocre net income because it
invests aggressively in marketing, organisation or research and development.

Companies that are very profitable at the gross level can look expensive and so be ignored by
traditional value investors, even though their economic power is under-recognised. The market
underestimates the informative persistence of gross profitability relative to more "polluted"
measures, and the quality of that profitability is fully recognised in future flows or in multiples
only later.

## Prediction

Firms with high gross profits to assets earn higher subsequent returns than firms with low gross
profitability. The effect is documented mostly on equities, over long periods, with a very broad
cross-sectional reach that includes large, liquid stocks. The market thus undervalues not only cheap
companies but can also undervalue fundamentally excellent ones, when the relevant measure of economic
quality is not the most salient. The signal often complements value rather than substituting for it:
value works better when gross profitability is controlled for or combined with it.

## What would refute it

- No return premium for high gross profitability, in particular among large, liquid stocks.
- Gross profitability that predicts future profitability and returns no better than net income, EBIT
  or free cash flow.
- A value premium no larger once gross profitability is controlled for or combined with it.
- Companies with high gross profits and depressed net income, because of heavy marketing,
  organisation or research spending, that are already priced at the multiples of equally profitable
  peers.
