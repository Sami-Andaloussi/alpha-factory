## Mechanism

The economic value of an asset is the present value of the distributable flows it can generate for
its holders in the future. The mechanism is not discounted cash flow valuation as a spreadsheet
exercise, but the idea that the market price embeds imperfect expectations about three difficult
variables: the normalised level of the flows, the duration of the excess flows, and the appropriate
discount rate.

The gap between price and value can persist because investors often work on horizons that are too
short, rely on shortcuts such as the price-earnings ratio, extrapolate the latest results, or
underestimate the combined effects of competition, the business cycle, the reallocation of capital
and dilution. It is not fully arbitraged away because two investors can share the same financial
statements and still disagree sharply about how long competitive advantages will last or how quickly
margins will normalise.

The framework applies to equities in general, above all to mature companies. It is also the
conceptual matrix of several more specific mechanisms: residual income, earnings power value,
price-implied expectations, profitable growth, the value of share buybacks and, for crypto assets,
the discounting of a future economic utility.

## Prediction

The gap between the market price and the present value of the future distributable flows carries
information about subsequent returns: assets priced below that value earn higher subsequent returns
than assets priced above it. The gap can persist and is not fully arbitraged away. The pattern
concerns equities in general and mature companies above all; on the crypto side, the same framework
underlies the discounting of a future economic utility.

## What would refute it

- Gaps between a discounted cash flow estimate and the market price that carry no information about
  subsequent returns, including among mature companies.
- Prices that already reflect the normalised level of the flows, the duration of the excess flows and
  the appropriate discount rate, so that short horizons, price-earnings shortcuts and the
  extrapolation of recent results leave no measurable error.
- Gaps between price and value that do not close even when the flows turn out as estimated.
- Gaps that last no longer where the duration of competitive advantages and the normalisation of
  margins are disputed than where they are uncontroversial.
