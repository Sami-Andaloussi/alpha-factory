## Mechanism

Information diffuses slowly between technologically linked firms: firms that share technologies, depend
on the same technical building blocks, or are exposed to similar innovations and setbacks. News about
one firm can have implications for firms that are close to it in technology, even when there is no
immediate commercial relationship between them.

The mechanism is linked to knowledge capital. Investors often recognise the direct news faster than its
implications for the adjacent firms in the technology space, whether these concern productivity, the
substitutability of technologies, the risk of obsolescence, complementarity in innovation or the spread
of technical standards. Technological links are real but rarely fully salient in the flow of market
information, which slows their capitalisation into prices.

## Prediction

The literature shows that the returns of technologically linked firms predict the later returns of
the other firms in their technology network. The effect is most likely in R&D-intensive sectors, such
as software, semiconductors, biotech and digital infrastructure.

## What would refute it

- The returns of technologically linked firms do not predict the later returns of the other firms in
  their technology network.
- The predictability vanishes once thematic exposure is accounted for: the results can sometimes
  capture a shared theme rather than a causal diffusion of information.
