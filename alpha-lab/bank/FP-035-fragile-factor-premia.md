## Mechanism

Fragile factor premia are factor premia induced by investor flows. The theory explains the irregular
variation of some market anomalies, the style factors, by non-fundamental investor flows. Individual
investors often allocate their capital according to recent performance or to fund ratings such as
Morningstar's, and ignore the funds' exposure to systematic risks. Fund managers receive these
flows, which are uninformed as a whole, and buy or sell broadly because of them, which moves prices
temporarily. Huang, Song and Xiang (2019) show that aggregate flows into actively managed funds,
whose end investors are often naive about factor risks, lead to massive purchases, or matching
sales, of whole groups of stocks: small stocks, value stocks and others.

If many funds that hold small or value stocks attract inflows after a good performance, their
managers buy these securities en masse, which inflates the size or value premium artificially. When
the flows reverse or dry up, the premium falls back, with a strong reversion. The size and value
premia become fragile: they depend on flows and are not supported by fundamentals. The flows create
price deviations within portfolios sorted by size or by book-to-market, followed by a return towards
the mean once the flows stop.

The effect is not arbitraged away at once because positions and flows build up over periods, such as
quarterly flows, and because long-term managers confirm prices only later. The theory puts the
emphasis on noisy flows, and it implies that factor premia vary strongly over time. It relates to
the link between fund flows and factor returns already discussed in the literature, where fund flows
give rise to short-term momentum. It complements or extends the hedging-pressure hypothesis: instead
of predefined categories, it is the actual flows of equity funds that shift the premia.

## Prediction

Flows into funds that hold a factor raise the factor's return, and the premium then reverts markedly
when the flows reverse or stop. An increase in purchases of small capitalisations relative to large
ones raises the small-cap premium. Huang, Song and Xiang (2019) find that the positions of funds are
significantly correlated with factor returns, and that investor-driven flows into each factor
explain a large part of the factor premium and of its volatility. The mechanism appears above all in
equity portfolios over a medium horizon, from a quarter to a year. The effects have been observed
mainly on equities, from 1980 to 2017, above all in US equity mutual funds. By analogy, the concept
could extend to factors on futures if large participants follow these flows.

## What would refute it

- No relation between flow-induced trading of funds in factor portfolios and the returns of those
  factors.
- Factor returns that rise with fund inflows but show no reversion when the flows reverse or stop.
- Variation in the size and value premia unrelated to the flows of the funds that hold those stocks,
  flows explaining only a negligible part of the premia and of their volatility.
