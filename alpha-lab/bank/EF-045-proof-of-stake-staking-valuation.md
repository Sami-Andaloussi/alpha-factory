## Mechanism

In proof-of-stake networks, holding the token can give a right to quasi-conventional cash flows
through staking, but these flows can come partly from real fees and partly from new issuance. The
token is valued at the intersection of four elements: the economic activity of the network, fees and
burns, the staking yield, and the dilution of those who do not stake. A monetary policy that is too
generous increases staking, and hence security, but dilutes value; a policy that is too strict
protects scarcity but can weaken security.

Staking rights resemble cash flows and so allow classic valuation approaches. The crypto specificity
remains strong: price, security, money supply, burns and the staking ratio are jointly endogenous.
The framework concerns proof-of-stake networks.

## Prediction

The token is valued at the intersection of the network's economic activity, fees and burns, the
staking yield and the dilution of non-stakers: staking flows can come partly from real fees and partly
from new issuance, and issuance dilutes those who do not stake. A monetary policy that is too
generous raises staking, and hence security, but dilutes value; one that is too strict protects
scarcity but can weaken security. Price, security, money supply, burns and the staking ratio are
jointly endogenous. Design details matter a great deal, and liquid staking wrappers complicate the
economic transmission to the token.

## What would refute it

- Token values unrelated to fees, burns, staking yields and the dilution of non-stakers.
- Changes in issuance that leave the staking ratio and the security of the network unchanged.
- Staking yields financed by issuance that the market values like yields paid out of real fees.
