## Mechanism

The inelastic markets hypothesis holds that the quantities of assets demanded and supplied are
inelastic enough for an aggregate demand shock to move prices durably. In a Walrasian view,
investors quickly arbitrage away any price change unrelated to fundamentals. Here, clientele
preferences, mandates, institutional constraints, funding frictions and market segmentation make
the demand curve for assets slope downwards, so that flows into or out of an asset class become
determinants of its price.

The framework brings together several flow mechanisms: mutual fund flows, ETF flows, share buybacks,
issuance, rebalancing, central bank operations and shocks to ownership. It describes no single
participant but a property of equilibrium: the market lacks enough flexible, indifferent capital to
absorb at no cost the changes in position of constrained investors. Many public flows can therefore
move prices even when they carry no fundamental information.

The inelasticity persists because the capacity to arbitrage is scarce. The investors who could
supply the missing elasticity bear risk costs, have short funding horizons, face leverage
constraints or have allocation preferences of their own. The hypothesis has been applied mainly to
equities and to macro-finance; by analogy, its intuition carries over to crypto spot when a large
part of the circulating supply is "sticky", held by holders who rarely sell, and marginal liquidity
is limited.

## Prediction

Flows into or out of an asset class, or into or out of a security, move its price durably and by
more than a market with elastic demand would allow, whether the flows come from funds, ETFs,
buybacks, issuance, rebalancing, central bank operations or changes in ownership. Over short to
intermediate horizons, the inelasticity can be large.

## What would refute it

- Aggregate flows into or out of an asset class leaving no lasting trace on its price.
- Demand curves for assets that are flat: flows unrelated to fundamentals absorbed quickly by other
  investors at no price concession.
