## Mechanism

The "product" sold by a blockchain is not only its token but also access to scarce block space. When
the network is congested, fees rise, delays and the competition for inclusion change, and the economic
utility of the token can be reshaped. A layer-1 token therefore does not have the same value depending
on whether its block space is abundant, saturated, expensive, or in competition with layer-2
solutions. The price behaviour becomes a function of a technical constraint internal to the protocol.

The economic mechanism runs through several channels: transaction capacity, user experience, fee
revenue for validators and miners, the security budget of the network, and the arbitrage between
competing uses of the same block space. The phenomenon persists because the scarcity is built by the
protocol itself; it cannot be arbitraged away without changing the rules, increasing capacity or
moving the activity to other layers. Congestion can also have ambiguous effects: it signals at the same
time strong demand for the network and a deterioration of its marginal utility. The mechanism is
specific to the underlying and does not reduce to generic families; it is directly linked to network
adoption, security, fees and protocol design.

## Prediction

The value of a layer-1 token differs depending on whether its block space is abundant, saturated,
expensive or in competition with layer-2 solutions, both in short-term congestion peaks and in more
durable regimes of saturation or under-use. Congestion can have ambiguous effects: it signals at the
same time strong demand for the network and a deterioration of its marginal utility. The setting is
mainly crypto, particularly networks whose token is closely tied to the payment of fees.

## What would refute it

- Token prices bear no relation to congestion, fees or block fullness, in short peaks as in durable
  regimes of saturation or under-use.
- Among networks whose token is closely tied to the payment of fees, token prices bear no relation to
  the state of their block space.
- Changes of rules, increases of capacity or the migration of activity to other layers leave the
  relation between block space and token value unchanged.
