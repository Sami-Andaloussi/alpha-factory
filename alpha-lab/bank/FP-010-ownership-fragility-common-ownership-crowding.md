## Mechanism

Some securities are fragile because they are held by an investor base that is too homogeneous, too
correlated in its constraints, or too exposed to the same funding shocks. When the holders of an
asset sit in portfolios that resemble each other, capital outflows or a rise in risk in one segment
can trigger simultaneous sales. The price of the security then depends not only on its own
fundamentals but also on the financial health and the flows of its dominant holders.

The mechanism is particularly strong when the same funds own the same securities, when their end
investors subscribe and redeem in a correlated way, or when their mandates force them to react
alike. This creates a fragility of the ownership base: a shock to one fund or one asset class
spreads to individual assets through their common holders. It accounts for sales that look
excessive, for comovement without fundamental news, and for non-linear drawdowns.

The fragility persists because collective asset management tends structurally to concentrate
portfolios in liquid, benchmarked or popular names, and because end investors often react
procyclically. Arbitrageurs must not only absorb a flow but also judge whether it is the first leg
of a broader shock to the ownership base. By analogy, in crypto, the equivalent is the concentration
of tokens among particular categories of addresses, funds, treasuries or platforms.

## Prediction

Securities whose holders are concentrated, similar to one another and exposed to correlated flows
comove with the other securities held by the same investors without fundamental news, and they can
suffer simultaneous sales that look excessive, and non-linear drawdowns, when those holders face
outflows or rising risk. Shocks to a fund or to an asset class are followed by sales and price
declines in the individual securities it holds, without news about their fundamentals.

## What would refute it

- Securities with a homogeneous, flow-correlated ownership base comoving no more with their
  co-held securities, and suffering no larger drawdowns, than securities with a dispersed ownership
  base.
- Outflows from, or shocks to, the dominant holders having no effect on the prices of the
  securities they hold.
- Comovement among co-held securities fully explained by their shared fundamentals.
