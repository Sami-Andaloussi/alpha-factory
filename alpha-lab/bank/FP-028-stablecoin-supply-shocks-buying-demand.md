## Mechanism

The creation, expansion or reallocation of the supply of stablecoins can reflect an increase in the
purchasing capacity available inside the crypto ecosystem, and so act as a proxy for latent demand
for crypto assets. The mechanism is not monetary in the traditional sense: it is an increase in the
transaction power internal to the ecosystem, particularly on the platforms and protocols where
stablecoins serve as unit of account, collateral and settlement leg. It can be compared to an
observable shock of "dry powder".

A change in stablecoin supply is not automatically bullish: primary issuance differs from effective
circulation, idle holdings, use as collateral, arbitrage between exchanges, and defensive demand for
a cash equivalent. When new issuance is deployed on exchanges or trading protocols, however, the
theory holds that it increases the potential demand for the risky assets of the ecosystem. Part of
the literature discusses whether stablecoins drive prices or respond endogenously to market demand.
Public institutions have analysed stablecoins as a channel of liquidity and of stress transmission.

## Prediction

Stablecoin issuance that is deployed on exchanges or trading protocols increases the potential,
latent demand for the risky crypto assets of the ecosystem. The change in supply is not
automatically bullish: issuance that stays idle, serves as collateral, feeds arbitrage between
exchanges or answers a defensive demand for cash equivalents does not carry that demand.

## What would refute it

- New stablecoin supply deployed on exchanges followed by no increase in buying or in crypto prices.
- Stablecoin supply that only follows crypto prices, expanding after they rise, without ever
  leading them.
- No difference in subsequent price moves between issuance deployed on trading venues and issuance
  that stays idle or serves as collateral.
