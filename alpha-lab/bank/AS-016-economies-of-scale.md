## Mechanism

Economies of scale produce stock-market behaviour specific to the firms and sub-universes that can
spread large fixed costs over a larger volume base. If an entrant cannot quickly reach the efficient
size, the incumbent keeps a cost advantage, and often a price advantage.

The books stress that scale becomes a real barrier only if entrants do not have equal access to
customers; otherwise they too can converge towards the efficient size. The mechanism therefore
persists mainly when it is combined with distribution, with the capture of demand, or with
geographic segmentation. A technological change can lower the minimum efficient size or make large
assets less flexible.

## Prediction

Incumbents whose scale advantage is combined with distribution, captive demand or geographic
segmentation keep a cost advantage, and often a price advantage, over entrants who cannot quickly
reach the efficient size; where entrants have equal access to customers, they too can converge
towards that size. The mechanism is found in distribution, in some manufacturing industries, in
software, in infrastructure and in various sector sub-universes. A technological change can lower the
minimum efficient size or make large assets less flexible.

## What would refute it

- Large incumbents with high fixed costs have no cost or price advantage over smaller rivals.
- Scale advantages last just as long where entrants have equal access to customers as where scale is
  combined with distribution, captive demand or geographic segmentation.
- A technological change that lowers the minimum efficient size leaves the incumbents' advantage
  intact.
