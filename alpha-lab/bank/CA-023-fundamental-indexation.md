## Mechanism

Weighting by market capitalisation builds pricing errors mechanically into portfolio weights: the
more expensive a stock becomes relative to its fundamental value, the larger its weight. This
creates a structural inefficiency for every investor who takes the capitalisation-weighted benchmark
as an anchor. Fundamental indexation replaces market size with measures unrelated to price (sales,
cash flows, dividends or book value), and so turns benchmarked management into a long-only
relative-value strategy. The extra return comes from continuous contrarian rebalancing against price
excesses, against sector bubbles and against the mechanical buying of overvalued stocks by passive
flows.

The effect persists because the market remains centred on benchmarks, tracking error discourages
departures from capitalisation weights, and bubbles can last a long time.

## Prediction

Portfolios weighted by measures unrelated to price earn an extra return over capitalisation-weighted
portfolios of the same stocks; their performance has been studied in US stocks and in global
markets. A large part of the extra return comes close to the value effect, and market phases
dominated by mega-capitalisation momentum stocks can penalise fundamentally weighted portfolios.

## What would refute it

- Fundamentally weighted portfolios do not outperform capitalisation-weighted portfolios of the same
  universe.
- The whole of the extra return is explained by exposure to value, with nothing coming from the
  contrarian rebalancing against price excesses.
- The ground lost to capitalisation weighting during bubbles and phases of mega-capitalisation
  momentum is not recovered when prices correct.
