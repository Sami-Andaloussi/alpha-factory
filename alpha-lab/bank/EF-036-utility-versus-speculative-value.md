## Mechanism

In the crypto framework the library presents, the value of a cryptoasset breaks down into a present
use value and a speculative value tied to expected future use. Utility value comes from the actual
need to use the asset: to access a digital resource, transfer value, consume a service or take part
in the economy of the protocol. Speculative value corresponds to holding the asset in the
expectation that this utility value will be larger in the future. The split is a crypto analogue of
the equity distinction between current profits, future growth and market expectations.

Scarcity alone does not make a token worth something, and a useful network can still have a
speculative component. On many tokens the utility part is still small and the speculative part
dominant, which makes their valuation very sensitive to assumptions about adoption and its timing.

## Prediction

The part of a token's price that present use does not support moves with expectations of future use:
where the utility part is small and the speculative part dominant, as on many tokens, the valuation
is very sensitive to assumptions about adoption and its timing. Scarcity alone does not make a token
worth something, and a useful network can still carry a speculative component.

## What would refute it

- Token prices unrelated both to present use and to changes in expected adoption.
- Tokens valued for scarcity alone that keep their value durably without developing any use.
- Tokens whose value rests mostly on present use that are no less sensitive to revisions of adoption
  expectations than tokens whose value is mostly speculative.
