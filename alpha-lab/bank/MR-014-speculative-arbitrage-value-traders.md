## Mechanism

Harris (2002) and Campbell, Lo and MacKinlay (1997) formalise the role of speculative arbitrageurs, whether called value traders or informed traders, as the main restoring force in markets. They buy when the price is below their estimate of fundamental value and sell when it is above. Their collective activity is the force of reversion: the further prices move from fundamental value, the more arbitrageurs enter, and the faster the reversion.

The speed of reversion depends on the depth of arbitrageurs' capital, on how clearly the fundamental value can be established, and on the horizon of the arbitrage. Without a clear fundamental anchor, as for assets without cash flows, reversion can be slow or absent. The limits of arbitrage apply to this force too: Shleifer and Vishny (1997) document that arbitrageurs can run short of capital exactly when the deviation is largest, through forced selling during crises, which is the fundamental limit of arbitrage. Mean-reverting positions are therefore exposed to the risk that the deviation widens before it corrects. The theory is related to market resilience, regime risk and the limits to arbitrage.

## Prediction

Deviations of prices from fundamental value revert, and the reversion is faster for larger deviations, which draw in more arbitrageurs, and where arbitrage capital is deep and fundamental value is clear. It can be slow or absent for assets without a clear fundamental anchor, and in crises, when arbitrageurs are forced to sell, the deviation can widen before it corrects. Campbell, Lo and MacKinlay (1997) construct theoretical arbitrage portfolios to test the proposition that arbitrageurs' trading drives prices back.

## What would refute it

- Larger deviations from fundamental value reverting more slowly than small ones in normal markets.
- A reversion speed unrelated to the capital available to arbitrageurs.
- Reversion as fast for assets without a clear fundamental anchor as for assets with cash flows.
- Deviations that close without any arbitrage trading against them.
