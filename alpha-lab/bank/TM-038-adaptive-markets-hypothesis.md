## Mechanism

The adaptive markets hypothesis, developed by Andrew Lo (2004, 2017), proposes a reconciliation of
market efficiency and behavioural finance. Regularities of continuation can appear, disappear and
reappear. Markets are seen
not as a static system, perfectly efficient and instantly arbitraged, but as an evolving ecosystem in
which participants adapt to regimes, competition, regulation, constraints and behavioural errors.
Markets evolve with their environment: strategies that work today can stop working if too much
capital exploits them, and strategies that seemed dead can revive when conditions change.

The analogy is Darwinian: trading strategies are species that adapt to the market environment. Trend
followers are particularly well adapted to environments of high macroeconomic uncertainty and crisis,
and less well adapted to stable, low-volatility environments. Applied to trend following, the
hypothesis says that trends and reversals are natural products of an adaptive system: when the
environment changes faster than participants can adjust, when allocation rules, leverage
constraints, the structure of participants or economic policy evolve, pockets of predictability
persist. Markets adapt to the strategies exploited in them, but neither instantly nor completely,
which leaves residual opportunities. This makes it consistent that a style as public as trend
following can keep producing value in some regimes.

Greyserman and Kaminski use the framework to explain the variation of trend-following performance
over time and to give theoretical depth to managed-futures trend following, and Ilmanen presents it
as an attempt to reconcile efficiency and behavioural finance. It explains why momentum can
survive and change form.

## Prediction

Trend following works in some regimes, strong macroeconomic trends and crises, and not in others,
choppy markets and strong mean reversion. Its profitability varies in cycles: it tends to degrade when
too much capital follows it (crowding) and can return when capital leaves the strategy. Performance
cycles are linked to the evolution of the population of investors and to macroeconomic regimes, as
Greyserman and Kaminski document for trend following.

## What would refute it

Trend-following profitability that is constant across regimes, or unrelated to the amount of capital
following the strategy. Returns that do not recover after capital leaves the strategy, or that do not
degrade when capital crowds in. Regularities that, once exploited, disappear instantly and completely,
or never change at all.
