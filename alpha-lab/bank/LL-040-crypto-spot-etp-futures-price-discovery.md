## Mechanism

This is the crypto counterpart of the cash-futures and ETF-underlying relations. The price of a crypto
asset can be discovered first on the listed instruments that some investors find most accessible, such
as CME futures and crypto exchange-traded products (ETPs and ETFs), or on the large centralised spot
markets, and then spread to the other trading venues.

The mechanism depends on which instrument leads. Futures and ETPs can lead when they concentrate
institutional investors, macroeconomic information and risk management. The large spot markets can lead
when they concentrate liquidity and round-the-clock trading. Information then travels through arbitrage,
creation and redemption, cross-venue market making and inventory management, but frictions of capital,
trading hours, regulation and access keep the transmission from being instantaneous. The relation links
the crypto spot ecosystem to more institutional markets.

## Prediction

Price discovery follows distinct patterns between crypto spot markets, futures and listed products: the
futures or ETPs can lead when they concentrate institutional investors, macroeconomic information and
risk management, the large spot exchanges when they concentrate liquidity and round-the-clock trading.
Several studies find such distinct patterns, and several studies compare the price of bitcoin in futures
and spot markets. Price discovery is sensitive to episodes of stress, to the exact product and to the
trading venue, and it sometimes reflects microstructure more than the diffusion of fundamental
information.

## What would refute it

- Spot markets, futures and ETPs incorporate crypto news at the same time, with no instrument leading.
- Which instrument leads bears no relation to where institutional investors, macroeconomic information
  and risk management concentrate, or to where liquidity and round-the-clock trading concentrate.
- Price gaps between spot, futures and listed products close at once, despite the frictions of capital,
  trading hours, regulation and access.
