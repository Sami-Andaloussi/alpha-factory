## Mechanism

This explanation looks at the behaviour of investors, institutional and individual. Grinblatt,
Titman and Wermers (1995) show that investors tend to buy stocks after they have become winners,
chasing past winners, without selling past losers in the same proportion. The holdings of mutual
funds reveal this internal momentum trading: funds add to positions that are already rising and
leave the losers in place. Collective investors thus buy after rises, through performance chasing,
without systematically selling after falls.

This creates a demand effect that prolongs the rise of the winners more than that of the losers, and
the cumulative buying flow thus reinforces the upward trend. Flows into mutual funds that follow past
performance add to it. This mass behaviour is observable on equities, through US mutual funds, and,
informally, in crypto-assets through collective movements, such as mass buying after a rally.

## Prediction

Stocks that funds buy after their rise keep rising: the asymmetric buying of winners prolongs their
rise more than that of the losers. Studies find that performance-following flows of mutual funds
reinforce short-term momentum, over a few weeks, and the effect, being cumulative, runs from the
short term to the long term. Fund holdings show that funds tend to buy past winners.

## What would refute it

Funds that sell past losers as readily as they buy past winners, or whose purchases show no relation
to past returns. Stocks bought by performance-chasing funds showing no continuation of returns over
the following weeks. Inflows that follow past performance leaving no trace in the returns of the
funds' holdings.

## References

- Grinblatt, M., Titman, S. & Wermers, R. (1995). Momentum Investment Strategies, Portfolio Performance, and Herding: A Study of Mutual Fund Behavior. American Economic Review.
