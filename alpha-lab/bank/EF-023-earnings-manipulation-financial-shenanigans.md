## Mechanism

The theory goes beyond ordinary accruals and targets cases where financial statements are actively
"managed" to suggest a quality of profits, a growth or a stability that does not really exist.
Managers have incentives to beat the consensus, protect their compensation, ease a share issue,
support the share price or hide an operating deterioration. The tools are known: premature revenue
recognition, capitalisation of normal expenses, manipulation of reserves, artificial reduction of
provisions, one-off gains, reorganisation of accounting flows, and others. The price can then
temporarily disconnect from the true fundamental dynamics, until the balance sheet, the cash flows or
restatements force a re-rating.

The phenomenon persists because manipulation hides in the details of the accounts, because most
investors do not carry out a systematic forensic reading, and because signs of stress often appear
first in the balance sheet or the cash flow statement, before they reach the income statement.

## Prediction

The price of a company whose accounts are actively managed can disconnect temporarily from the true
fundamental dynamics, standing above what they support, until the balance sheet, the cash flows or
restatements force a re-rating. Signs of stress often appear first in the balance sheet or the cash
flow statement, before they reach the income statement.

## What would refute it

- Signs of manipulation in the balance sheet or the cash flow statement, or high scores on detection
  models such as PROBM, that are not followed by price declines.
- Prices that react as soon as those signs appear in the statements, leaving no delayed re-rating.
- Restatements and cash flow shortfalls that are not associated with a revaluation of the stock.
