## Mechanism

Beyond the best-known calendar anomalies, several books record average returns that vary with the calendar month. This is a broader and more diffuse family than the turn-of-the-month or holiday effects. It aggregates several possible causes: taxation, the calendar of flows, holidays, year-end balance sheets, cycles in risk, and sometimes climatic or psychological variables.

## Prediction

Average returns differ from one calendar month to another. Historically January was very strong, September and October weak, and the months from November to February solid, with large differences across countries, periods and asset classes.

Authors stress that these broad monthly regularities are less clean and less stable than the narrower calendar effects: several months that were historically strong become weak or noisy in modern samples. Monthly patterns have been examined in several countries, among them Japan over 1990 to 1994.

## What would refute it

- No difference in average returns across calendar months.
- A ranking of months that changes from one period to the next with no recurring pattern.
- Monthly differences unrelated to the calendars of taxes, flows, holidays and year-end balance sheets.
