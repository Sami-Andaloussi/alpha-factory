## Mechanism

Traditional accounting anchors undermeasure the intangible assets that companies create internally.
Internal intangible investments, which often generate real economic value, are frequently expensed
rather than capitalised, and the result is a double distortion. On one side, the assets and the
equity of intangible-intensive firms are understated. On the other, their current earnings can be
artificially depressed during the investment phase, after which some profitability ratios can look
artificially high. If the market compares the price with a book value that ignores a large share of
the real economic capital, intangible-intensive companies look artificially expensive. The market
can therefore misread the relative value of these companies. Part of the "death" of classic value
would thus be a mismeasurement of the denominator, not the disappearance of the fundamental
mechanism.

Capitalising certain intangible assets more fully, such as research and development, organisational
capital, brands and asset-creating selling and administrative spending, restores a more relevant
anchor for telling what is truly expensive from what is expensive only in accounting terms. The price
departs from a badly measured economic value, and the correction comes through a better
representation of productive capital. The mechanism extends the fundamentals family rather than
denying it: economic fundamentals sometimes have to be rebuilt where accounting distorts them. It
concerns above all modern equities: technology, software, services, pharmaceuticals and
biotechnology, consumer brands, platforms and knowledge businesses in general.

## Prediction

Intangible-intensive companies that look expensive on a reported book value ignoring their intangible
capital, but not once that capital is counted, are mispriced: the price departs from a badly measured
economic value, and the correction comes through a better representation of productive capital. Part
of the "death" of classic value would thus be a mismeasurement of the denominator.

## What would refute it

- Intangible-adjusted valuation anchors that predict returns no better than reported book value.
- Intangible-intensive companies that look expensive on reported book value and go on to earn the low
  returns that book-based value predicts.
- A weakness of classic value that persists unchanged once intangible capital is added to book value.
