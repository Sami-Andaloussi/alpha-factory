## Mechanism

The performance and the valuation of a token depend systematically on its own architecture: a
maximum-supply or an inflationary policy, transactional utility, governance rights, burn mechanisms,
consensus rules, privacy, the possibility of a fork, the integration of fees, and so on. Not all
tokens are interchangeable "weak equities" or "digital commodities"; some design features create
different structural profiles of demand, scarcity and value capture.

The economic mechanism comes from the way these design choices redistribute value among users,
validators, developers, speculators and long-term holders. A token that captures fees, imposes a burn
or confers an indispensable utility does not have the same sensitivity to market conditions as a
purely speculative or inflationary token. The phenomenon persists because design is not a mere
technical detail: it durably frames the incentives and the economic sustainability of the protocol.
Arbitrage does not erase it, because it is precisely a fundamental difference in internal structure.

## Prediction

In the cross-section of cryptoassets, design attributes explain differences in valuation and
performance, generally over the medium to long term, with sometimes abrupt repricing when a token
changes its tokenomics, its governance or its consensus. The literature documents that design
attributes matter for valuation.

## What would refute it

- Tokens that differ in supply policy, utility, governance rights, burn mechanisms, consensus rules,
  privacy, fork possibilities or fee integration show no systematic differences in valuation or
  performance.
- Tokens that capture fees, impose a burn or confer an indispensable utility are as sensitive to market
  conditions as purely speculative or inflationary tokens.
- Changes in tokenomics, governance or consensus cause no repricing.
