## Mechanism

The intermarket thesis is that the major asset classes do not incorporate macroeconomic information at
the same time or through the same channel. Bonds react early to turns in growth and inflation, through
interest rates; equities react next, through earnings and valuation multiples; commodities reflect the
pressure of demand and supply later; and currencies then redistribute the differences in growth, yields
and terms of trade. The effect concerns asset classes, zones and sector blocks.

The effect persists because investor bases are segmented, mandate constraints differ, the delays in
the transmission of macroeconomic shocks are real, and readings of the regime are open to dispute.

## Prediction

Turns in growth and inflation appear first in bond yields, then in equity prices, then in commodity
prices, and then in exchange rates. The relations have been observed in equities, bonds,
commodities, currencies and international markets. They are strongly limited in atypical
deflationary regimes, in periods of overwhelmingly dominant monetary policy and at structural
breaks.

## What would refute it

- Bonds, equities, commodities and currencies react to news about growth and inflation at the same
  time, or in no stable order.
- The ordering between markets breaks down in ordinary regimes too, and not only in the conditions
  that are its known limits: atypical deflationary regimes, periods of overwhelmingly dominant
  monetary policy, and structural breaks.
