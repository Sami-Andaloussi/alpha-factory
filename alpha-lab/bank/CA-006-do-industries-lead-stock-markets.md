## Mechanism

The theory extends lead-lag above the level of the sector: some industries react before the
aggregate market because they are closer to changes in real activity. Industries described as
informationally sensitive (retail, services, commercial real estate, materials, oil, transport and
others, depending on the period and the classification) absorb news about economic conditions
faster, and the overall market incorporates it later. At short horizons the aggregate market thus
becomes a relative laggard of some industrial sub-blocks.

The mechanism is twofold. On one side, some industries are structurally more closely tied to household
spending, business investment, credit conditions or production costs. On the other, although these
industries produce aggregate information about the cycle, investors do not immediately draw its full
implication for the broad market. This amounts to a gradual diffusion of macroeconomic information
through more specialised sub-markets. The effect concerns industrial or geographic blocks.

## Prediction

The lagged returns of informationally sensitive industries predict the subsequent return of the
aggregate stock market at short horizons.

## What would refute it

- Lagged industry returns carry no information about the subsequent return of the aggregate market,
  out of sample as well as in sample.
- The industries that lead the market are not those most closely tied to household spending,
  business investment, credit conditions or production costs.
- The broad market incorporates the information contained in industry returns as fast as the
  industries themselves.
