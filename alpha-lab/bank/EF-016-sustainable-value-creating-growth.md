## Mechanism

Growth has value only if it can be financed without degrading the economics of the business, and if
new investments earn more than their cost of capital. Many market errors come from confusing growth
in revenue, growth in earnings obtained through leverage, and genuine growth in economic value. The
library's texts show that a company can grow fast while destroying value if it reinvests at low
returns, if it finances its growth by dilution, or if it exceeds its sustainable growth rate without
a realistic change in financial policy. Conversely, a company with a high return on invested capital
and good payout discipline can turn the same growth into much more value.

## Prediction

Because many market errors come from confusing growth in revenue, growth in earnings obtained through
leverage and genuine growth in economic value, the market can overpay for companies that grow fast
while destroying value, through low-return reinvestment, dilution or growth beyond their sustainable
rate; a company with a high return on invested capital and good payout discipline can turn the same
growth into much more value. Growth stocks priced too expensively have been observed to underperform.

## What would refute it

- Growth priced by the market according to the return on capital it earns relative to the cost of
  capital, so that fast growers that reinvest below that cost or dilute their shareholders do not
  underperform.
- High-return companies with payout discipline that earn no more than low-return growers with the
  same growth.
- Expensive growth stocks that do not underperform.
