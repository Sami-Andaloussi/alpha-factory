## Mechanism

Payout policy follows the firm's stage of development. Young firms retain more cash because their
growth opportunities are numerous and external financing is more expensive for them; mature firms
distribute more because they have fewer projects with a high net present value and more free cash.

The mechanism ties together the firm's stock-market behaviour, the structure of its balance sheet, its
maturity and the profile of its investors. It persists because the economic life cycle of a firm
changes slowly, which keeps its payout regime relatively stable. Whole sectors can mature or find new
investment opportunities, and some firms remain structurally in an investment phase.

## Prediction

Payout rises with the firm's maturity: young firms with many growth opportunities retain their cash,
while mature firms with few high-value projects distribute more, and each firm's payout regime stays
relatively stable because its life cycle changes slowly. The firm's stock-market behaviour and the
profile of its investors are tied to its stage in the cycle.

## What would refute it

- Payout does not rise with maturity: young, high-growth firms distribute as much as mature firms with
  few investment opportunities.
- Payout regimes change quickly, without any change in the firm's growth opportunities or maturity.
- Firms and sectors that move into maturity, or that find new investment opportunities, keep their
  payout policy unchanged.
