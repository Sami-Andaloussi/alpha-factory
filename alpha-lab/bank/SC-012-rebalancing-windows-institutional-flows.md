## Mechanism

Several books show that a large part of calendar effects comes from "forced" flows rather than from beliefs. Salaries paid at the end of the month, pension contributions and employee savings, interest payments, rebalancing back to target weights, index reconstitutions, the resetting of risk budgets in January and the purchases that index funds are obliged to make all produce time windows in which supply and demand become predictable.

The mechanism is structural. It explains why some regularities survive even after academic publication: benchmarked or constrained investors have to trade on a fixed date even when the price is against them. It is not a single effect but a family of micro-mechanisms tied to the timing of flows. The books use it as an explanation of the turn-of-the-month effect, of the first-half-of-the-month effect, of part of the start-of-year effects, and of the distortions around index reconstitutions.

## Prediction

Supply and demand become predictable in the windows where constrained investors must trade: around the turn of the month, when salaries, contributions and interest are paid; at the start of the year, when risk budgets are reset; and around rebalancing dates and index reconstitutions, when index funds are obliged to buy and benchmarked investors must trade on fixed dates. A large part of calendar effects comes from these forced flows. Some of these regularities survive their academic publication, because the investors behind them must trade on a fixed date even when the price is against them. Price distortions around the reconstitution of the Russell indices have been studied.

## What would refute it

- No abnormal price pressure on the dates on which constrained investors must trade (the turn of the month, rebalancing dates, index reconstitutions), or pressure unrelated to the size of the forced flows.
- Calendar regularities that vanish after publication although the forced flows behind them continue, or that persist after these flows have stopped.
- Price moves around index reconstitutions and rebalancing dates no larger in stocks heavily held by index funds and benchmarked investors than in other stocks.
