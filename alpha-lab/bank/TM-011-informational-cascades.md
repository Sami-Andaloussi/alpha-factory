## Mechanism

Informational cascades describe how agents who observe the actions of others can rationally ignore
their own information and follow the herd (Bikhchandani, Hirshleifer and Welch, 1992). Under great
uncertainty, some investors imitate the majority instead of using their own signal: if many
investors start buying, perhaps wrongly, the others follow and the move continues. This can set off
self-reinforcing episodes, in which small shocks lead to large collective movements, and prices can
stay away from intrinsic value for a long time because nobody dares to act against the crowd.

The imitation produces a momentum pattern: an initial rise becomes visible and attracts still more
buyers, amplifying the trend. In financial markets the cascade can feed trends through sequential
imitation, through the amplification of weak signals, and through fragility near a decision boundary
("borderline"), which makes possible phases of continuation followed by breaks of regime. When
uncertainty is high, rational arbitrageurs may themselves follow the general movement rather than be
seen going against it, as De Long and co-authors point out, which strengthens the momentum. The
actors are typically retail investors or automated funds that, lacking clear signals, fall back on
the market consensus.

The mechanism does not require irrational agents. It rests on the structure of information and on
the observation of other people's actions (prices, flows, positions) as signals. It can operate
together with other mechanisms, its effects adding to those of positive feedback trading.

## Prediction

Under high uncertainty, price moves continue in the direction of the initial trades over short
horizons, from days to weeks, and prices can depart durably from intrinsic value; phases of
continuation can be followed by breaks of regime.

## What would refute it

Investors' trades that follow their own private signals rather than the observed actions of others,
even under high uncertainty. No clustering of trades in the direction of earlier trades, and no
stronger continuation of price moves when uncertainty is high. Prices that return quickly to
intrinsic value after a burst of imitative buying, without long deviations.
