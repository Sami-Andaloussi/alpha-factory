## Mechanism

For some sets of securities, the aggregate vehicle, in particular the exchange-traded fund, discovers
the price before the individual components. Common or macroeconomic information is incorporated first
into the ETF and then passed on to the underlying stocks with a delay.

The mechanism is structural. ETFs concentrate liquidity, attention, macroeconomic flows, basket
arbitrage and top-down views. When news hits a sector, a style or the whole market, it is often simpler
and cheaper to trade the ETF at once than to trade all its components, so the ETF becomes a place of
price discovery. Authorised participants and other arbitrageurs then carry the information over to the
underlying stocks, but not always instantly or completely.

## Prediction

For some sets of securities, ETF prices lead the prices of their components, and the aggregate signal
diffuses gradually to the individual names. The literature shows that index and ETF markets often play
a major role in intraday price formation. Other studies also find that ETF activity and ETF flows carry
information about the underlying securities, and that ETFs can improve, or sometimes disturb, the
informational efficiency of their components, depending on the context.

The effect depends strongly on the horizon, on the liquidity of the ETF relative to its components, on
whether arbitrage is possible, and on the regime of flows. In some episodes the ETF also transmits
non-fundamental pressure to its components.

## What would refute it

- The components lead the ETF, or ETF moves are not followed by moves in the components.
- ETF activity and flows carry no information about the later returns of the underlying securities.
- The lead of the ETF does not vary with its liquidity relative to the components or with the scope for
  arbitrage.
- What the ETF passes on to its components is non-fundamental pressure rather than common or
  macroeconomic information.
