## Mechanism

An asset is attractive if its price, or its enterprise value, is low relative to the economic
capacity it already generates. The enterprise-based versions partly neutralise the distortions from
leverage that affect purely equity-based ratios. The free cash flow yield measures the company's
capacity to finance itself and to distribute or reallocate cash after maintenance spending. The
enterprise yield, EBITDA or EBIT over total enterprise value, is a proxy of the operating return
earned by an acquirer of the whole company. The gross profits yield goes higher up the income
statement to limit the scope for manipulation.

The mispricing arises because the market often gives too much weight to growth narratives and too
little to the flows, or near-flows, that the company already generates and that are not yet fully
capitalised in the price. The effect is stronger among mature companies, or at least among companies
that already monetise their activity, and weaker among highly cyclical stocks, companies with very
high intangible intensity and companies in a phase of massive investment.

## Prediction

Stocks with high free cash flow, enterprise or gross profits yields earn higher subsequent returns
than stocks with low yields, because the flows they already generate are not yet fully capitalised
in the price while growth narratives receive too much weight. The effect is stronger among mature
and monetised companies, and weaker among highly cyclical, intangible-intensive and heavily
investing companies.

## What would refute it

- No return difference between high- and low-yield stocks among mature, monetised companies.
- A spread no larger among mature, monetised companies than among highly cyclical,
  intangible-intensive or heavily investing ones.
- Enterprise-based yields that carry no more information than equity-based ratios among highly
  levered companies.
- Low-yield stocks whose growth narratives are realised on average, so that the flows already
  generated by high-yield stocks confer no return advantage.
