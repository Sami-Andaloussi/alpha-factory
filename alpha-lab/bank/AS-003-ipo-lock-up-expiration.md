## Mechanism

A price can be affected by the existence of a future supply that is already known but temporarily
blocked. In initial public offerings, insiders, founders, employees and venture capital investors are
often prevented from selling for a given period. When this restriction expires, the market anticipates
a potential increase in the free float and, above all, the possibility that better-informed holders
will want to monetise their positions. The mechanism is therefore not only a mechanical supply shock;
it is also a signal about private information and about the incentives of the insiders.

The phenomenon persists because arbitrage ahead of the event is imperfect: the size of the future sales
is uncertain, not all insiders sell, and the expiration date does not remove the information
asymmetry. In addition, some investors cannot, or do not want to, take a large position against the
stock before the event. The lock-up is thus a structural mechanism of deferred supply overhang.

The mechanism differs from simple accounting dilution: what matters is the transition from locked-up
shares to saleable shares, and the informational reading of that transition. Its general logic is
wider than the IPO: any asset with a large supply that has already been created but is temporarily
illiquid can show a similar dynamic. By analogy, the mechanism is therefore often cited for
cryptoassets with vesting, foundations, teams, private investors and unlock schedules.

## Prediction

The prices of newly listed stocks fall around the expiration of the IPO lock-up. In the literature,
the declines are more marked when the fraction of shares released is large, when venture capital or
insider ownership is high, or when the quality of the information available on the firm is low. The
natural horizon is the event, around the expiration date. The most studied setting is equity IPOs.

## What would refute it

- Newly listed stocks show no abnormal price decline around the expiration of their lock-up.
- The size of the reaction bears no relation to the fraction of shares released, to the weight of
  venture capital and insider ownership, or to the quality of the information available on the firm.
- The whole adjustment takes place at the IPO or when the lock-up terms become known, with nothing left
  at the expiration date itself.
