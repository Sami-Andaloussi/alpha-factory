## Mechanism

A significant part of a token's price depends on the number, the quality and the type of venues where
it can be held and traded. A listing on a new exchange, especially a large centralised one,
mechanically widens the base of potential buyers, improves operational accessibility, reduces some
transaction costs, lends credibility to the asset and can increase the activity of its network. The
mechanism is therefore not only a "news reaction": it is a structural change in access to the market.

The actors are the exchanges, market makers, existing holders, new retail or institutional investors,
and cross-venue arbitrageurs. The phenomenon persists because the enlargement of the set of eligible
investors can have a lasting effect, especially if the token was previously hard to access. In some
cases the listing also creates a new layer of price formation and new possibilities of
collateralisation or derivatives. Arbitrage does not erase the listing premium if the change concerns
the effective size of the addressable market. The theory is closely linked to access segmentation,
applied to crypto markets and to exchanges as distribution institutions.

## Prediction

Tokens listed on a new exchange, especially a large centralised one, earn a listing premium around the
event, and the listing can increase the activity of their network; a medium-term component follows if
the listing durably changes the structure of liquidity and the composition of holders. The setting is
mainly crypto.

## What would refute it

- Tokens listed on new exchanges show no abnormal return around the listing, and the activity of their
  network does not increase afterwards.
- The effect is no larger for listings on large centralised exchanges than for other listings, and no
  more durable for tokens that were previously hard to access than for tokens already widely available.
- The listing premium is arbitraged away even where the listing changes the effective size of the
  addressable market, or durably changes the structure of liquidity and the composition of holders.
