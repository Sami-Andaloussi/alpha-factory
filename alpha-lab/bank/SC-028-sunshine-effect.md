## Mechanism

The sunshine effect belongs, with the winter-blues and lunar-cycle theories, to a more behavioural group of calendar effects, less structural than the effects of salary payments or taxation. The proposed mechanism is that investors' appetite for risk varies with their collective mood, itself influenced by light, by the season and possibly by some biological rhythms; the books theorise this group as a case in which exogenous changes in sentiment can produce different average returns at recurring dates or periods. In the sunshine effect, the light in question is sunshine, a weather seasonality.

## Prediction

Average stock returns differ with the amount of sunshine, through its effect on collective mood and appetite for risk. The authors stress that the effect seems stronger far from the equator.

## What would refute it

- No difference in returns between sunny and overcast days.
- An effect no stronger far from the equator than near it.
- A pattern that appears only in the samples in which it was first found.
