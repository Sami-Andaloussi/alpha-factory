## Mechanism

Dividend policy acts as a costly signal of the quality of future cash flows. Because raising or
maintaining a dividend commits the firm, and because backtracking is costly for its reputation and
its finances, not all firms can imitate the best ones. The signal can therefore carry information
about the robustness of profits, the confidence of management and the sustainability of the
distributions.

The mechanism persists because the information asymmetry between insiders and the market does not
disappear, and because dividends are harder to manipulate than mere statements. Signalling effects
mix with taxes, agency problems and investor clienteles, so the information content of a dividend
varies across regimes and sub-universes.

## Prediction

Changes in payout can carry information about the robustness of profits, the confidence of management
and the sustainability of distributions, and the market reads them as signals: raising or maintaining
a dividend is read as a sign of robust future cash flows, backtracking as the opposite. The effect has
been observed mainly in mature firms and around changes in payout, with an information content that
varies across regimes and sub-universes.

## What would refute it

- Dividend increases and cuts carry no information about the firm's subsequent profits and are met
  with no price reaction.
- Firms with weak cash flows raise or maintain their dividends as readily as firms with strong cash
  flows, and later cuts cost them nothing in reputation or financing.
- The price reactions to payout changes are fully explained by taxes, agency problems or investor
  clienteles, with nothing left that relates to future cash flows.
