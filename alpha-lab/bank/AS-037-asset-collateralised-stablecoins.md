## Mechanism

The token owes its price behaviour to the credibility of a stock of external reserves: cash,
commodities, securities or other assets held off-chain. The mechanism is structurally distinct because
the apparent stability of the price does not come from a mere convention but from a promise of
redemption and from a reserve-and-claim architecture.

The recurring behaviour then comes from confidence in the reserve, from the legal quality of the
claim, from the frequency of audits and from the capacity to redeem under stress. The inefficiency
persists because arbitrage is not frictionless: not everyone has the same access to redemption, and
information on the reserve can be opaque or delayed. If the reserve is opaque or politically
vulnerable, the asset behaves less like tokenised cash and more like a claim on an intermediary.

## Prediction

The recurring price behaviour of an asset-collateralised stablecoin comes from confidence in its
reserve, the legal quality of the claim, the frequency of audits and the capacity to redeem under
stress. The inefficiency persists because arbitrage is not frictionless: access to redemption is not
the same for everyone, and information on the reserve can be opaque or delayed. When the reserve is
opaque or politically vulnerable, the stablecoin behaves less like tokenised cash and more like a
claim on an intermediary. The theme is documented on Tether and other fiat-backed stablecoins.

## What would refute it

- The price of the stablecoin does not react to news about its reserves, its audits or its redemption
  conditions, in calm periods or under stress.
- Any inefficiency is closed at once by arbitrage, whatever the inequality of access to redemption and
  the opacity of information on the reserve.
- Stablecoins with opaque or politically vulnerable reserves trade exactly like those with transparent,
  frequently audited reserves.
