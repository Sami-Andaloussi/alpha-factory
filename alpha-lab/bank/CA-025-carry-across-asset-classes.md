## Mechanism

The theory of carry extends the classic notion of carry beyond currencies. The idea is that an asset
has an expected return tied to a component that is observable, ex ante and economic, and that is not
simply future price appreciation. In many assets a high carry signals a higher risk premium, the
slope of a curve, an implicit discount, or a reward for financing or holding the asset, so comparing
carry across assets becomes a direct form of cross-asset relative value. The key point is that carry
is not a purely empirical rule: it is an economic characteristic of the contract or of the asset.

The canonical case is currency carry, in which high-yielding currencies have historically
outperformed what uncovered interest parity implies, but the general logic covers assets that offer
a spread of yield, dividend, term or basis. By analogy, the logic extends to spot equities and
crypto-assets: a distribution yield, an implicit valuation slope, a structural discount, or the
yield "carried" by holding one asset relative to others.

The observed spread can pay for a risk, a funding constraint or a crash risk, or reflect a lasting
mispricing: its interpretation is debated between a risk premium and a mispricing. The effect
persists because carry assets often show a negative asymmetry, suffer abrupt unwinds when funding
tightens, and therefore cannot be arbitraged without a cost of capital. The books stress that this
logic runs across several asset classes.

## Prediction

In many assets, a high carry signals a higher risk premium, and so a higher expected return than a
low carry; high-yielding currencies have historically outperformed what uncovered interest parity
implies. If carry predicts returns across equities, bonds, credit, options, commodities and other
classes, markets systematically reward certain exposures to funding, liquidity, recession or
volatility risk. Carry strategies suffer synchronised drawdowns in certain macroeconomic episodes,
which reinforces the interpretation as a common risk premium. Carry returns show negative skewness,
depend on the macroeconomic regime, and suffer heavy losses in risk-off episodes.

## What would refute it

- High-carry assets do not outperform low-carry assets: high-yielding currencies depreciate by as
  much as uncovered interest parity implies, and the same holds for the carry of bonds, equities,
  credit, options and commodities.
- Carry strategies in different asset classes do not suffer their drawdowns together in
  macroeconomic stress, contrary to a common risk premium.
- Carry returns show no negative skewness and no losses concentrated in risk-off episodes and
  funding squeezes.
