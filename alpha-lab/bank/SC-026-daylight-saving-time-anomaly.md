## Mechanism

The daylight saving time anomaly links returns around the changes of clock time to the disruption of sleep and attention. The idea is that even a small shock of circadian desynchronisation can affect the mood, the cognitive performance and the risk aversion of investors, and so translate into abnormal returns immediately after the change of time.

The mechanism is behavioural and very short-term. It is more precise than the seasonal affective disorder theory: it depends not on a long season but on a single calendar event that is predictable, repeated and precisely dated. It implies that a very fine-grained institutional calendar can move prices temporarily through cognition.

## Prediction

Returns are abnormal immediately after each change of clock time.

Where and when it has been observed: studies find a negative effect after the changes of time, especially in spring or depending on the country; re-examinations covering more markets and more data do not always confirm these results.

## What would refute it

- Returns on the trading days just after the clock changes no different from those of comparable trading days.
- No effect in larger samples covering more markets and more years.
- No measurable change in the sleep, attention or risk-taking of investors around the changes of time.
