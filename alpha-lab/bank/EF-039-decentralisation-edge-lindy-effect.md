## Mechanism

The decentralisation edge asks first why a service needs to be decentralised to create value. A token
has no real fundamental thesis if the service it supports could be better provided by a centralised
database without any loss of functionality, neutrality or resistance to censorship: that is the first
economic filter. The Lindy effect adds a notion of durability: the longer a technology or a
cryptoasset survives, the higher its probability of surviving in the future, because an ecosystem of
developers, companies, hardware, liquidity and uses builds up around it.

Together, the two mechanisms explain why some cryptoassets acquire a more durable fundamental value
than others: they solve a real problem of decentralised coordination and, over time, accumulate an
installed base that is hard to displace.

## Prediction

Cryptoassets that solve a real problem of decentralised coordination, and that have already survived
for longer, are more likely to survive and to acquire a more durable fundamental value than younger
ones, or than tokens whose service could run on a centralised database, as ecosystems of developers,
companies, hardware, liquidity and uses build up around the survivors.

## What would refute it

- A probability of survival among cryptoassets that does not rise with the time they have already
  survived.
- Tokens supporting services that a centralised database could provide just as well that survive and
  keep their value as often as tokens solving a real problem of decentralised coordination.
- Long-lived cryptoassets whose installed base of developers, companies, hardware and liquidity is
  displaced as easily as that of young ones.
