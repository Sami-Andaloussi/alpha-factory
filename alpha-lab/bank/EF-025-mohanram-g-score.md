## Mechanism

The G-score is the growth counterpart of the Piotroski F-score. The market frequently overpays for the
growth story without discriminating the quality of that growth. Among low book-to-market stocks, some
companies have more durable, more stable, better financed and more credible growth; others are only
expensive, fragile or undisciplined stories. The GSCORE combines profitability, the stability of
profits and of growth, and the intensity of certain investments such as research and development,
capital expenditure and advertising.

The market values "growth" too crudely, as a single category: investors readily pay for the story but
do not assess the microeconomic quality of the growth process. When earnings surprises and analyst
revisions separate the good growth cases from the bad ones, prices converge.

## Prediction

Among low book-to-market (growth or glamour) stocks, companies with a high GSCORE earn higher
subsequent returns than companies with a low GSCORE, over a rather annual horizon, prices converging
when earnings surprises and analyst revisions separate the good growth cases from the bad ones. In
the original study (Mohanram, 2005), a large part of the spread comes from the short side, that is,
from the low-GSCORE growth stocks.

## What would refute it

- No return difference between high and low GSCORE stocks among low book-to-market stocks.
- Earnings surprises and analyst revisions that do not go, on average, in the direction indicated by
  the GSCORE.
- Growth stocks with weak profitability and unstable growth that are priced at a discount to
  high-quality growth stocks from the outset, leaving no later convergence.
