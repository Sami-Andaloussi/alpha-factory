## Mechanism

Investors learn not only from fundamentals and prices but also by watching what other investors do:
nearby institutions, opinion leaders, or geographically concentrated groups. An information cascade
occurs when the early decisions of a small number of agents change the beliefs of those who follow, who
then imitate them, rationally or for reasons of reputation.

Across many assets, this can produce a sequential diffusion: first the stocks held or discussed by the
leading group, then neighbouring stocks, by similarity, geographic proximity or imitation of portfolios.
The frictions that sustain the process are the cost of acquiring primary information, the externalities
of observation, reputation, and the fact that the actions of others serve as a signal when private
information is costly.

## Prediction

The trades of investors follow those of the agents they observe. Trading is correlated among individual
investors who are geographically close and among institutions, especially in small capitalisations.
Prices can then move first in the stocks held or discussed by the leading group and later in
neighbouring stocks.

## What would refute it

- The trades of geographically close individual investors, and of institutions, are no more correlated
  than common public information explains, including in small capitalisations.
- The trades of followers do not come after those of the leaders they could observe.
- Stocks held or discussed by a leading group and their neighbouring stocks move at the same time, with
  no sequence from the first to the second.
