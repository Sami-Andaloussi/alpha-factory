## Mechanism

Composite equity issuance generalises net share issuance by trying to measure all net creation of
equity, including forms less visible than a formal seasoned equity offering: compensation paid in
shares, acquisitions paid in shares, and changes in market capitalisation not explained by the
stock's own return. The market underestimates the negative information carried by net equity
creation in this broad sense. When a company "consumes" a high valuation to finance itself or to make
acquisitions, it often reveals that its stock is relatively expensive.

The theory treats external financing as a revealer of the gap between price and fundamental value.
The mechanism is linked to Daniel and Titman's distinction between tangible and intangible
information: part of the apparent glamour of a stock is in fact growth financed from outside, not
intrinsic value creation. The composite measure tries to capture the whole process by which
expensive companies convert their market valuation into real capital.

## Prediction

Companies with high composite equity issuance earn lower subsequent returns than companies that
create little or no net equity, over a medium-term horizon; the setting is equities.

## What would refute it

- No lower subsequent returns for companies with high composite equity issuance.
- Net equity creation whose negative information is fully priced when it occurs, leaving no
  subsequent underperformance.
- Glamour stocks whose growth is financed from outside that perform as well as those whose growth is
  financed internally.
