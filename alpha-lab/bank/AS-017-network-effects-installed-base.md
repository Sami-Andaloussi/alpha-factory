## Mechanism

In platform, software, payments and console industries, and more generally for network goods, the
value of a product for a user rises with the size of the installed base and with the density of
complementors. The result is a dynamic of tipping, of partial irreversibility and of path dependence.

The mechanism is specific to each firm because it depends on the technology, the compatibility, the
standards and the ecosystem of the underlying business. It persists because a large installed base
attracts more developers, retailers, content or counterparties, which reinforces the dominance of
the standard further. The books show that network effects often reinforce both switching costs and
economies of scale. Standards can change, and an inferior architecture can be overturned by a
technological or regulatory shock.

## Prediction

In network industries, the firm that holds the largest installed base attracts more developers,
retailers, content or counterparties, the market tips towards its standard, and its dominance becomes
partly irreversible and path-dependent. Standards can change, and an inferior architecture can be
overturned by a technological or regulatory shock.

## What would refute it

- In network industries, the value of the product for users does not rise with the installed base and
  the number of complementors.
- Markets do not tip: firms with the largest installed base lose share to rivals as easily as smaller
  firms do, without any technological or regulatory shock.
- The largest installed base attracts no more developers, retailers, content or counterparties than
  smaller ones.
