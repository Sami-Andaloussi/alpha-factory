## Mechanism

A basket instrument, such as an index future or an exchange-traded fund, concentrates information about
the common factor and passes it on faster than the individual components. Hasbrouck stresses that
diversification reduces the information asymmetry specific to each security and makes these
instruments cheaper to trade. Traders informed about a market factor, hedgers and arbitrageurs
therefore often prefer to trade the basket first and then transmit the information to the components
through arbitrage or hedging.

The gap is not fully arbitraged away because arbitrage between the basket and its components involves
many legs, is costly and depends on the microstructure of the cash market.

## Prediction

For information about the common factor, moves in basket instruments precede moves in their components.
Harris illustrates it: when an S&P 500 future rises faster than the cash index, purchases of the
components are likely to follow shortly. Lehalle and Laruelle add that the dominance of futures over
components can be real without being permanent: phases in which the basket leads probably alternate
with phases in which it lags, depending on the context.

## What would refute it

- Components lead the basket instrument as often as the reverse, whatever the context.
- A futures contract rising faster than its cash index is not followed by purchases of the components.
- Adverse selection costs are no lower in basket instruments than in the individual securities, or
  traders informed about the market factor do not trade the basket first.
