## Mechanism

The claim is not simply that a high return on invested capital (ROIC) is good: the speed at which a
high ROIC fades is one of the fundamental objects of valuation and of excess returns. The books show
that high returns on capital tend to revert towards the mean under the pressure of competition, but
not all at the same speed. Companies with genuine competitive advantages, pricing power or strong
operating discipline sustain an excess return on net operating assets (RNOA) or on invested capital
for longer. The mispricing lies in the error made in estimating that persistence: the market can
overestimate the durability of high profits or, on the contrary, normalise too quickly levels of
profitability that are defended by real barriers to entry. The theory ties the quality of the
business directly to the duration of excess cash flows, and links quality, profitability, franchise
value and valuation.

## Prediction

The market can overestimate the durability of high profits, which leaves those stocks overvalued, or
normalise too quickly levels of profitability defended by real barriers to entry, which leaves those
stocks undervalued. High returns on capital tend to revert towards the mean under competition, but not
all at the same speed: companies with genuine competitive advantages, pricing power or strong
operating discipline sustain an excess return on capital for longer.

## What would refute it

- High returns on capital that fade at the same speed whether or not the company has barriers to
  entry, pricing power or operating discipline.
- Prices that anticipate the speed of fade correctly, so that neither slow nor fast faders show
  predictable subsequent returns.
- Persistence of high returns on capital that disappears once the effects of the cycle, of leverage
  and of accounting are removed.
