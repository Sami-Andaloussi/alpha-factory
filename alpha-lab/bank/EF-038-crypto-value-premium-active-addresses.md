## Mechanism

The theory transposes value investing to crypto markets. Instead of comparing price with book value,
it compares the market value of a token with a measure of the real use of its network, the number of
active addresses. A token whose network is heavily used relative to its capitalisation can be seen as
"cheap on usage". The mechanism is close to classic value: the price departs from a fundamental anchor, here
the use of the protocol. The market can overpay for narratives, themes or speculative stories, and
underpay for networks whose real economic activity is stronger than their market capitalisation
suggests.

The anchor can be widened to the operating health of the protocol: tokens can be treated as economic
networks whose relative value depends on adoption, transaction activity, security, decentralisation,
technical resilience and the network's persistence over time. A network whose on-chain fundamentals
improve faster than its price becomes cheap relative to the others; one whose price runs far ahead
of its usage and robustness becomes expensive. The mispricing persists because the sector is new,
narratives dominate, markets are fragmented, and there is no lasting consensus on fair value.
Correlation regimes between crypto and traditional assets change over time, and one thesis holds
that crypto is being progressively integrated as an asset class.

## Prediction

Tokens whose networks have many active addresses relative to their market value earn higher returns,
on average, than tokens whose market value is high relative to their activity: empirical work finds
this outperformance. The setting is large cross-sectional samples of cryptoassets, at weekly
to monthly horizons. On the broader anchor of operating health, a network whose on-chain
fundamentals (adoption, transaction activity, security, decentralisation, resilience) improve faster
than its price becomes relatively cheap, and one whose price runs too far ahead of its usage and
robustness relatively expensive.

Crypto markets undergo rapid structural change.

## What would refute it

- No return difference between tokens with high and low activity relative to market value, at weekly
  or monthly horizons.
- A premium that comes from wash activity and automated addresses rather than from use, and vanishes
  once they are removed from the activity measure.
- Tokens with a high market value relative to their activity whose narratives are borne out, with no
  subsequent underperformance.
- Tokens whose on-chain fundamentals improve faster than their price that do not subsequently
  outperform, and tokens whose price runs ahead of their usage that do not subsequently
  underperform.
- Relative prices of crypto networks that bear no stable relation to adoption, transaction activity,
  security, decentralisation or resilience.
