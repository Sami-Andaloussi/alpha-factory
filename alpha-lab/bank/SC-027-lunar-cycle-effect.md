## Mechanism

The lunar cycle effect associates stock returns with the phases of the moon, generally with lower returns around the full moon than around the new moon. The proposed mechanism is behavioural: the phases of the moon would affect mood, sleep, attention or agitation, and these aggregated psychological variations would temporarily change risk-taking.

The mood the theory invokes is exogenous to the market and largely independent of the usual financial variables. It belongs to a more behavioural group of calendar effects, less structural than the effects of salary payments or taxation, in which appetite for risk varies with collective mood, influenced here by a natural cycle, a group the books theorise as a case in which exogenous changes in sentiment can produce different average returns on recurring dates.

## Prediction

Returns are generally lower around the full moon than around the new moon. The economic size of the difference is moderate, and the authors stress that the effect appears independent of the more classic calendar anomalies.

Where and when it has been observed: studies find the effect in many countries and over long periods, with no evident parallel variation in volatility or volume, which supports the idea of a mood channel rather than an information channel.

## What would refute it

- Returns around the full moon no lower than returns around the new moon.
- A lunar pattern that comes with changes in volatility or trading volume, which would point to an information channel rather than to mood.
- A lunar pattern that disappears once the turn-of-the-month and other monthly calendar effects are controlled for.
- An effect that does not replicate in new samples, markets and periods.
