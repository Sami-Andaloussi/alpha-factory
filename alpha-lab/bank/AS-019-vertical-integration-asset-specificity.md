## Mechanism

When assets, suppliers, distribution channels or skills are specific to an economic relationship, the
firm is exposed to risks of hold-up, of opportunistic renegotiation or of operational
desynchronisation. Vertical integration can then become a structural protection mechanism, not because
it is always efficient, but because it internalises assets and coordination that are hard to contract
for properly on the market.

This bears directly on the economic structure of the firm: its margins, its capital intensity, its
flexibility, its dependence on suppliers and its competitive resilience. The books also recall that
excessive integration can become a handicap when the industry matures and external suppliers become
more efficient. The phenomenon persists as long as the assets remain truly specific and the
modularity of the sector stays low.

## Prediction

In sectors where assets are relationship-specific and modularity is low, vertical integration can
protect firms against hold-up, opportunistic renegotiation and operational desynchronisation, which
bears on their margins, capital intensity, flexibility, supplier dependence and competitive resilience
compared with non-integrated rivals. Excessive integration can become a handicap when the industry
matures and external suppliers become more efficient.

## What would refute it

- Firms that depend on relationship-specific assets bear no hold-up or renegotiation costs, whether
  they are integrated or not.
- In sectors with specific assets and low modularity, integrated firms show no difference in margins,
  resilience or supplier dependence from non-integrated rivals.
- In mature, modular industries with efficient external suppliers, integrated firms do no worse than
  non-integrated ones.
