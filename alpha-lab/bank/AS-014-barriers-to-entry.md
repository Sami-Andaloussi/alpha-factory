## Mechanism

Barriers to entry are a sector mechanism for stocks. If an industry is structurally hard to enter, the
profit streams of the incumbents are more durable, their returns on capital more stable, and their
prices less sensitive to immediate competition.

The mechanism persists because barriers can rest on set-up costs, regulation, distribution channels,
standards, an installed base, vertical integration, or cost advantages that cannot be reproduced
quickly. It points to durable structural differences between firms and between sub-universes of the
market. Some barriers can vanish abruptly with a technological or regulatory change.

## Prediction

Incumbents in industries that are structurally hard to enter show more durable profits, more stable
returns on capital, and prices that are less sensitive to immediate competition than firms in open
industries, and these differences persist between firms and sub-universes for as long as the barriers
hold. Some barriers can vanish abruptly with a technological or regulatory change.

## What would refute it

- Firms protected by set-up costs, regulation, distribution channels, standards, an installed base,
  vertical integration or hard-to-copy cost advantages earn profits and returns on capital that are no
  more durable or stable than those of firms in open industries.
- The prices of protected incumbents are as sensitive to the arrival of new competitors as those of
  unprotected firms.
- The profitability of incumbents is unchanged after a technological or regulatory change has removed
  their barrier.
