## Mechanism

Some tokens capture, directly or nearly so, part of the economic value created by their protocol:
redistributed fees, buyback-and-burn programmes, exchange revenues, a share of validation fees, or
other claims that resemble cash flows. Such a token ceases to be only a speculative object or an
object of use; it looks more like a claim on the rents of a platform, and its valuation can approach
a discounted cash flow or the capitalisation of a rent.

The mechanism is very strong when there is a firm rule of value capture: the more fees and revenues
the protocol generates, and the more of that value is promised to holders, the higher the
sustainable fundamental value of the token. It is a crypto analogue of the equity theories based on
free cash flow, payout and capital discipline, and concerns exchange tokens, some revenue-sharing
governance tokens and burn-based architectures.

Many of these economic rights remain subject to governance, revisable or informal; capture can be
indirect and therefore fragile; and the gross revenue of a protocol is not always revenue for the
token holder.

## Prediction

The more fees and revenues a protocol generates, and the more of that value is promised to holders
under a firm capture rule, the higher the sustainable fundamental value of its token, which can then
be valued like a discounted cash flow or a capitalised rent. Where the economic rights remain subject
to governance, revisable or informal, or the capture is indirect, the capture is fragile, and the
gross revenue of the protocol is not always revenue for the token holder.

## What would refute it

- Token prices unrelated to the fees and revenues distributed to holders or burnt on their behalf.
- Tokens with explicit, firm capture rules valued no differently from tokens without them, for the
  same protocol activity.
- Governance changes that cut or redirect the value captured by holders without any effect on token
  prices.
