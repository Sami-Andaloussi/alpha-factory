## Mechanism

Seasoned equity offerings are a classic asset-specific structural mechanism in equities. When a
listed firm issues new shares, the market can read the issue either as a legitimate need for
financing or as the exploitation of an overvaluation by better-informed insiders. In Tirole's
account, this is tied to the lemons problem, to adverse selection, to dissipative signals and to
market timing.

The price behaviour around seasoned offerings therefore does not come from a generic factor but from
the information structure specific to the stock. The phenomenon persists because information
asymmetries are hard to eliminate, even with certification, collateral or the design of the securities
issued. The effects are mostly observable at the announcement, at the pricing, and in the regimes
where equity again becomes the marginal source of financing. An issue can also finance very good
projects.

## Prediction

The price behaviour around a seasoned equity offering depends on whether the market reads the issue
as a legitimate need for financing or as the exploitation of an overvaluation by better-informed
insiders; the average effect is mostly observable at the announcement and at the pricing of the
offering, and in the regimes where equity again becomes the marginal source of financing.

## What would refute it

- Announcements and pricings of seasoned equity offerings are met, on average, with no price reaction.
- The reaction bears no relation to the information asymmetry between insiders and the market: issues
  by firms that are hard to value are received no differently from issues by transparent firms.
- Certification, collateral or the design of the securities issued remove the reaction entirely.
