## Mechanism

This is the crypto equivalent of the IPO lock-up overhang, often with a greater intensity, because the
initial distribution of a token can be extremely concentrated among founders, teams, private
investors, foundations, treasuries and incentive programmes. When tokens already exist economically
but are not yet freely tradable, the market is exposed to a future increase of the float. The key
point is not only the "fully diluted" dilution but the actual path by which the supply becomes
saleable.

The initial and post-initial distribution of a token creates a mechanism distinct from spot supply:
the protocol treasury, the foundation, insider allocations, grants, vesting, cool-off periods and
future distributions governed by the DAO. A significant part of future supply depends on political and
contractual rules internal to the asset. This can generate a persistent overhang: the market knows
that part of the supply is not yet freely circulating but will become so on a known or semi-known
schedule. The overhang persists because protocols need reserves to fund development, incentives and
governance.

The mechanism has several layers: the supply pressure expected at the unlock date, the information
asymmetry of the early holders, the change in the composition of the holder base, and sometimes a
narrative reflexivity when the market anticipates that "everyone knows" a large unlock is coming. The
frictions that prevent complete arbitrage are large: not all unlocked tokens are sold, holders have
heterogeneous horizons, OTC transactions can absorb the flow, and the schedule information is not
always correctly incorporated. The market can therefore react repeatedly to a supply structure that is
known in theory.

## Prediction

Token prices can come under supply pressure around unlock dates, and more structurally with the slope
of the float's expansion: the natural horizon is the event around the unlocks, and also structural.
The market can react repeatedly to a supply structure that is known in theory. The link between
unlocks and price depends on the relative size of the unlocks, on the profile of the recipients and on
the depth of the market. The setting is mainly crypto: ICOs, venture-backed tokens and protocols with
long vesting.

## What would refute it

- Token prices show no abnormal decline around scheduled unlocks, and no relation to the pace at which
  the float expands.
- The reaction bears no relation to the relative size of the unlocks, to the profile of the
  recipients or to the depth of the market.
- The whole effect is priced when the schedule becomes known, with no reaction at the unlock dates
  themselves.
