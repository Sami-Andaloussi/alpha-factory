## Mechanism

The dispersion of returns across the assets of a universe is a precondition for the profitability of
cross-sectional momentum. If all the assets behave alike, being highly correlated, long-short
momentum strategies earn no differential return. The more the assets diverge, some rising strongly
and others falling strongly, the more strategies that are long the winners and short the losers
earn.

Dispersion tends to be high in three situations: asymmetric macroeconomic shocks, since a shock to US
interest rates does not affect all asset classes in the same way; crises specific to one asset class,
such as an oil shock that hits energy but may benefit consumers; and periods of strong sector
rotation.

## Prediction

Across a diversified universe of futures, the profitability of trend-following and cross-sectional
momentum strategies rises with the dispersion of returns across assets; Greyserman and Kaminski
(2014) document dispersion as one of the main determinants of trend-following profits. Periods of
asymmetric macroeconomic shocks, of crises confined to one asset class and of strong sector rotation
tend to show both high dispersion and high momentum profits; in periods of uniform, highly correlated
moves, long-short momentum strategies earn no differential return.

## What would refute it

Momentum profits that are unrelated to the dispersion of returns across assets, or that are as large
when assets move together as when they diverge. Asymmetric macroeconomic shocks and sector rotations
that do not raise dispersion.
