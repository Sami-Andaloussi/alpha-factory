## Mechanism

A utility token has a fundamental value because it must be held at the moment of service to access a
resource or a platform. Its value then depends not only on the demand for services but also on its
velocity of circulation: for a given demand for use, the longer the token has to be immobilised or
held in advance, the higher its equilibrium value can be. This is a micro-founded answer to simpler
approaches of the "MV = PQ" or network-value-to-transactions (NVT) type. The mechanism is a
liquidity constraint of use: the token is worth something because it provides a settlement or access
service and because its circulation is not infinitely fast, which ties its price directly to the
economic activity of the protocol: the price is supported by transactional utility and by the
scarcity of the liquidity needed. The same holds for monetary tokens: a token is worth not only the
size of the market it serves but also the speed at which its units turn over, so that a token that
must be held continuously to provide a service, or whose turnover is low, can support a higher unit
value than one that circulates fast. The value of the network is thus tied to the demand for its
use, to its supply and to its velocity, which links monetary design, use case and valuation, all of
them variables specific to the asset. The mechanism persists because the tokenomics and the
functional role of a token shape its structural velocity. Velocity is endogenous and often swamped
by speculative demand.

A related approach values a cryptoasset as the monetary base needed to serve certain economic uses,
adjusted for velocity, then discounted. If a token serves payments, remittances, collateral or a
store of value, the value it must carry depends on the economic volume targeted, its market share and
its velocity in each use. The use cases add up: the same asset can capture a share of remittances and
a share of the "digital gold" function. The valuation then joins the discounted cash flow and
residual growth frameworks used for equities: one chooses a horizon, an adoption path and a future
available supply, then discounts these future utilities at a high rate that reflects the risk. The
mispricing arises because the market often mixes adoption narratives, scarcity and real utility
without putting them through a coherent structure.

## Prediction

Token value rises with the economic volume the token serves and falls with its velocity: at a given
demand for use, tokens that have to be held longer can sustain a higher value. The value a token must
carry is the discounted monetary base that its use cases require, the sum of the values implied by
each use; the market often mixes adoption narratives, scarcity and real utility without putting them
through that structure. The relation is often swamped by speculative demand.

The mechanism applies mainly to tokens with a real internal utility; it weakens if use is
artificial, subsidised or purely speculative. It also depends on the design of the protocol, on
whether the service can be paid for otherwise, and on off-chain solutions.

## What would refute it

- Token values unrelated to the economic volume the tokens serve and to their velocity.
- High-velocity tokens valued as highly as low-velocity tokens at the same demand for use and the
  same supply.
- Designs that require holding the token to obtain a service that neither lower its velocity nor
  raise its value relative to designs that do not.
- Tokens whose service can be paid for in another asset, or settled off-chain, that hold the same
  value as tokens that must be held to access the service.
