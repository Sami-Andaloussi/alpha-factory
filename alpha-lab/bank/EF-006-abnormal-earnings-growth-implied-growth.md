## Mechanism

Abnormal earnings growth recasts fundamental valuation starting from earnings, and isolates the
growth in earnings that exceeds the "normal" growth required by the cost of capital. The no-growth
benchmark separates the value justified by current earnings from the additional, purely speculative
value that comes from implied growth. The market mechanism is that investors often pay for growth
without distinguishing profitable growth, growth financed by leverage, growth that comes from share
buybacks, and genuine operating growth. The framework turns a price-earnings or a price-to-book
ratio back into a growth assumption that can be challenged.

In some periods markets pay very high prices for growth stocks, or undervalue stocks whose implied
growth is too low relative to their fundamental base. The phenomenon persists because compact
multiples hide complex assumptions, because the market often reasons in slogans rather than in value
drivers, and because the boundary between normal growth and value-creating growth is often poorly
drawn.

## Prediction

Stocks whose price carries a purely speculative value from implied growth, beyond the value justified
by current earnings and normal growth, are overvalued, as when markets pay very high prices for
growth stocks; stocks whose implied growth is too low relative to their fundamental base are
undervalued. Investors often pay for growth without distinguishing profitable growth, growth financed
by leverage, growth from share buybacks and genuine operating growth.

## What would refute it

- Growth implied by prices above the no-growth benchmark that is realised on average, with no
  subsequent underperformance of the stocks that embed it.
- No return difference between stocks with high and low implied growth relative to their current
  earnings.
- Earnings growth from leverage or buybacks that the market prices at a discount to operating growth
  from the outset, leaving nothing to correct.
