## Mechanism

This theory stresses an asymmetry in the diffusion of information: bad news would be incorporated
into prices more slowly than good news, in particular when the structure of the market makes negative
information harder to produce and to relay (managers' incentives to disclose less, the need for
information intermediaries, limited analyst coverage).

The mechanism involves distinct actors, managers, analysts and investors with limited attention, and
a friction of information processing and disclosure. Prices adjust in several stages rather than at
once, which creates a continuation over an intermediate horizon. It has been documented on equities.

## Prediction

The literature associates the idea with cross-sectional variation in the profitability of momentum,
by size, analyst coverage and similar characteristics (Hong, Lim and Stein, 2000). Prices adjust to
bad news more slowly than to good news, in several stages, and the continuation this creates plays
out over an intermediate horizon.

## What would refute it

Momentum profits that do not vary with firm size or analyst coverage. Bad news incorporated as fast
as good news. Prices that adjust in a single step to negative disclosures, even for firms whose
managers disclose little and whose coverage is limited.
