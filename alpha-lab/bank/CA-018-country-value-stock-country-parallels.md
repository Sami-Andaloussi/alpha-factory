## Mechanism

The theory holds that the characteristics that predict the returns of individual stocks (value, size
and momentum) are found again at the level of national markets taken as "super-assets", which
carries the basic economic logic of stock selection over to the selection of countries. A national
market aggregates a sector composition, a macroeconomic regime, a level of political risk, a
sensitivity to monetary policy and an overall valuation. If investors under- or overreact to these
elements, national indices themselves can be temporarily under- or overvalued relative to one
another. Benchmarked flows, home bias, regional mandates and regulatory fragmentation help keep the
gaps open.

The effect concerns geographic relative-value rotation. Unlike pure country momentum, the theory
includes a logic of relative valuation at the level of whole markets. It also relates to the more
general theory of "value everywhere".

## Prediction

A country that is cheap relative to another, or whose past behaviour reveals a given regime, can
earn a relative premium afterwards: value, size and momentum, measured for whole national markets,
predict the relative returns of country indices as they predict those of individual stocks.

## What would refute it

- The relative valuation, size and past returns of national indices do not predict their subsequent
  relative returns.
- Valuation gaps between national markets close at once, leaving no relative premium, despite
  benchmarked flows, home bias, regional mandates and regulatory fragmentation.
- The country-level premium is entirely explained by the momentum of countries, with no separate
  role for their relative valuation.
