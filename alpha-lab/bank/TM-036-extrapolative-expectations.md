## Mechanism

Some investors form extrapolative beliefs: they project past returns into the future, expecting
prices to keep rising after they have risen. This generates a directional demand after rises, and
after falls in the opposite direction, and hence momentum-type dynamics (Barberis, Greenwood, Jin and
Shleifer, 2015).

In an equilibrium that combines rational and extrapolative investors, persistent trends can coexist
with incomplete arbitrage, because trading against extrapolation carries risk over an uncertain
horizon, and because extrapolative beliefs can be self-fulfilling over some windows. This class of
models links positive feedback trading, a trading behaviour, to belief formation, a psychological
process, and gives an explicit theory of trends as the product of an endogenous demand based on the
history of prices.

## Prediction

The directional demand that follows rises, and falls, produces momentum-type dynamics in prices,
and persistent trends can coexist with incomplete arbitrage by rational investors.

## What would refute it

Demand that does not follow past price moves: no directional buying after rises and no selling after
falls. Extrapolative demand that is fully offset by rational investors, leaving no continuation of
prices.
