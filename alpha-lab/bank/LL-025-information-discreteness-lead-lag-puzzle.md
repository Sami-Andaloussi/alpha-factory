## Mechanism

Information discreteness is not an extra source of lead-lag in the traditional sense but a structural
explanation of why some lead-lags exist and persist. Investors react more slowly to information that is
complex or not salient, and faster to highly visible signals. The discreteness of information creates a
lag in adjustment between the stocks that absorb information quickly and those that absorb it slowly.

The mechanism is both behavioural and informational. When a signal is hard to categorise, to aggregate
or to relate to other assets, the average investor underreacts. Firms that are "easy to read", or whose
signal is very salient, then become leaders, and firms with a less salient signal become followers. Many
forms of cross-predictability can thus survive even when the information is public.

A general cognitive friction slows diffusion across assets: it bears on the conditions under which
diffusion occurs more than on a specific economic channel.

## Prediction

Empirical work shows that the discreteness of the flow of information helps to explain the lead-lag
returns puzzle. Firms whose information is salient and easy to read lead, and firms whose
information is less salient follow: the returns of the first predict those of the second, and this
cross-predictability can survive even when the information is public.

## What would refute it

- Cross-firm return predictability bears no relation to the discreteness of the flow of information,
  nor to how complex or salient the information is.
- Firms with salient, easy-to-read information do not lead firms whose information is less salient.
- Cross-predictability between firms disappears once the information is public, whatever its
  salience.
