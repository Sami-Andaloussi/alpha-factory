## Mechanism

Information can diffuse between firms that share economic or style characteristics, even when they have
no obvious direct industrial relationship. Firms that are similar in valuation, growth, profitability,
investment, innovation profile or financial structure can react to one another because investors treat
them as informational comparables.

The mechanism is comparison and reasoning by analogy. When news about one firm reveals information about
an "economic category" of similar firms, the market can react first in the stock that carries the news
and then pass the information on gradually to the firms of the same style. Investors, analysts and fund
managers can organise their attention around groups of comparables rather than around strict contractual
links. Information can thus diffuse along latent graphs built on economic similarity.

## Prediction

Past returns of firms can predict the later returns of firms with similar characteristics ("styles"),
even when the firms have no obvious direct industrial relationship. Some studies suggest that part of
the predictability attributed to traditional economic links can in fact travel through similar styles
or exposures, so style links also stand as a competing explanation of the other channels of diffusion.

## What would refute it

- The cross-predictability between firms of similar style is fully accounted for by the factor structure
  of returns, that is, by common exposure to the same factors rather than diffusion.
- Firms of similar style show no lead-lag once industry and economic links are accounted for.
