## Mechanism

For cross-listed stocks, price discovery is not symmetric: the books show that the domestic market often
dominates the formation of the efficient price, even though the foreign market sometimes contributes
materially. The home market often concentrates the best environment for interpreting information about
the issuer: its natural investors, local coverage, knowledge of the regulation, and the depth of the
order flow tied to the company. The foreign market can still lead during its own trading hours, or when
it aggregates certain global news faster.

The result is a geographic lead-lag with a hierarchy of dominance that depends on relative liquidity,
trading hours, the structure of the investor base and the cost of arbitrage. It persists because two
listings of the same firm are not two perfectly frictionless mirrors: they differ in currency, time
zone, investor segmentation and microstructure, and there are sometimes asymmetries in transfer costs.

## Prediction

For cross-listed stocks, the books show that the home market often dominates the formation of the
efficient price and the foreign listing follows it, although the foreign market sometimes contributes
materially. The foreign market can lead during its trading hours, or when it aggregates certain global
news faster.

## What would refute it

- The foreign listing carries the larger share of price discovery for news specific to the issuer.
- The home market's share of price discovery bears no relation to where the natural investors, the local
  coverage and the depth of the company's order flow are.
- The hierarchy of dominance does not change with relative liquidity, trading hours, investor structure
  or the cost of arbitrage.
