## Mechanism

A governance token gives voting rights over the parameters of a protocol: upgrades, eligible
collateral, fees, issuance, the use of the treasury, even the liquidation rules. Its price then depends
on the economic value of control, and also on the quality of the protocol's constitution, that is, on
the decision architecture the token supports: effective participation, concentration of voting power,
delegation, quorum, the weight of large holders, the on-chain or off-chain nature of governance and
the articulation between on-chain and social governance, the rights actually attached to the token,
the cadence of distribution, and the protocol's capacity to implement decisions. The token is then not
only a speculative or utility object; it is a claim on a particular political and organisational
structure.

Governance determines the future allocation of value in the protocol: fee parameters, issuance,
treasury, incentives, security, upgrades and development priorities. Credible and effective governance
can support the value of the token; captured, apathetic or incoherent governance can destroy it. The
voting structure changes the way value is created, preserved or extracted. The phenomenon persists
because permissionless protocols need a decision layer suited to environments without formal
hierarchy, and because these characteristics are hard to arbitrage: they are specific to each
protocol, change slowly, and their impact depends on coalitions, social learning and coordination
between holders.

## Prediction

Governance tokens are valued according to the economic value of the control they confer and to the
quality of the voting design: a voting right without clear economic capture can be worth little, and
plutocratic or apathetic governance can durably discount the token. The horizon is medium to long
term, punctuated by votes, governance conflicts and reforms of tokenomics. The phenomenon has been
documented on Compound, Uniswap and MakerDAO. The setting is mainly DAOs and DeFi or infrastructure
protocols with tokenised governance.

## What would refute it

- The prices of governance tokens bear no relation to the rights they carry or to the voting design of
  their protocol: concentration of voting power, delegation, quorum, participation.
- Tokens whose votes capture no economic value trade at no discount to tokens whose votes direct fees,
  issuance or the treasury.
- Captured, plutocratic or apathetic governance carries no lasting discount, and votes, governance
  conflicts and tokenomics reforms cause no repricing.
