## Mechanism

The claim is not simply that low multiples outperform. When a structured estimate of intrinsic
value, built on future profits and on clean-surplus accounting, diverges from the market price, the
divergence carries information about future returns. The market incorporates imperfectly the
persistence of earnings, the quality of profits and the future path of invested capital, and a
residual income valuation can capture expectation errors that simple multiples miss. Among
low-multiple companies, some are truly undervalued and others have a low multiple for good reasons:
the price maps the fundamentals badly.

Residual income starts from the current accounting base and counts value creation only through
earnings in excess of a charge for the capital employed. In its unlevered variant, residual
operating income, operations are separated from financing: the analysis runs on net operating assets
and values the part of operating income that exceeds the cost of capital applied to those operating
assets. The associated mispricing is that the market often pays too much for profits boosted by
leverage, and too little for companies whose economic return on operating assets is misread.
Earnings per share and the price-earnings ratio can become misleading because of debt, share
buybacks or the financing structure.

## Prediction

Stocks with a high ratio of residual-income value to price (V/P) earn higher subsequent returns than
stocks with a low ratio. The horizon is longer than that of a drift after one-off news: the price
converges gradually towards a better-estimated fundamental value, often over one to five years. The
effect has been studied mainly in equities. Profits boosted by leverage are often priced too high,
and companies whose economic return on operating assets is misread too low.

## What would refute it

- No return difference between high and low V/P stocks over one to five years.
- A V/P ratio whose predictive power vanishes once the information already contained in analyst
  forecast revisions and in earnings quality is taken into account.
- High-V/P stocks that turn out to carry risks large enough to account for their higher returns.
- Profits boosted by leverage that the market prices from the outset at a discount to operating
  profits of the same size.
