## Mechanism

When a piece of information is interpreted differently by participants with unequal levels of information, the market can overreact to the signal at first. Less-informed traders (noise traders, traders who herd) amplify the initial move through panic buying or selling; better-informed traders (fundamental arbitrageurs, "smart money") then take the other side and bring the price back to a better-justified value. Market makers are also involved. The asymmetry of information thus creates an overshoot that reverses.

Narang (2024) identifies this as the second of the two basic drivers of short-term reversion, the first being the overshoot caused by the demand for liquidity alone, with no fundamental signal involved. Here a signal is involved, but it is misread by part of the market. It is related to the adverse-selection model of Glosten and Milgrom, to behavioural reversion, and to sequences of momentum followed by reversal.

The effect is stronger around ambiguous or only partly informative announcements, and it weakens as information spreads quickly, in transparent markets. In commodity futures it can appear around scheduled releases such as the USDA's World Agricultural Supply and Demand Estimates (WASDE), consumer price index (CPI) figures and US non-farm payrolls (NFP).

## Prediction

After a signal, the price can move further than the information justifies, then come back towards a better-justified value as better-informed traders take the other side. The horizon runs from a few hours to a few days. The overshoot is stronger around ambiguous or partly informative announcements and weakens as information spreads quickly, in transparent markets. In commodity futures, the overshoot can appear around macroeconomic and agricultural releases.

## What would refute it

- Moves after ambiguous announcements that persist or extend instead of reversing, i.e. moves that turn out to be durable informational adjustments.
- Overshoots no larger around ambiguous announcements than around clear ones.
- Overshoots no smaller where information spreads quickly and transparently.
- Reversals that occur without better-informed traders taking the other side, or that are fully explained by the demand for liquidity alone.
