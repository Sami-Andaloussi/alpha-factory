## Mechanism

Information about a firm's credit quality can be incorporated first in the bond or credit default
swap (CDS) market and then spread to the equity market, or the reverse, depending on the type of
news. Equity and credit are two different residual claims on the same economic balance sheet; when one
moves faster than the other, a temporary relative-value misalignment opens between the linked
instruments. A specialised market sometimes discovers the relevant component of the information before
the other.

Which market leads depends on the content of the news. Credit responds in a concentrated way to default
risk, leverage and the tails of the distribution, and credit markets can react faster to information
about distress, default, capital structure or the quality of cash flows; equity prices incorporate
growth, residual optionality and sentiment as well, and the equity market can react faster to news about
growth. In certain settings (negative surprises, rising uncertainty, private information, a specialised
over-the-counter market) credit can discover the price faster. This is not an unconditional dominance of
credit but a conditional asymmetry of informational leadership: a phenomenon of conditional price
discovery rather than a stable, universal premium. Specialised investors, regulatory constraints and
different tools of analysis segment the information between the markets, and the passage of information
from one market to the other can be delayed by the complexity of mapping the news into equity value.
This is a diffusion across markets of fundamental information about the same entity. The effect concerns
the stocks whose credit signal reveals a lag of the equity, and also sectors, through aggregate credit
spreads.

## Prediction

The literature on price discovery between stocks, bonds and CDS shows relations of precedence that vary
with credit quality, with the market regime and with the nature of the news. The effect has no single
universal direction: credit can lead for distress and default news, equity for growth news. Credit
markets (bonds, credit spreads, CDS) sometimes incorporate information about a deterioration or an
improvement of an issuer's fundamental quality faster than its shares, in settings such as negative
surprises, rising uncertainty, private information or a specialised over-the-counter market. The effect
often shows up more at high frequency or around credit events.

## What would refute it

- None of the three markets leads the others: stocks, bonds and CDS reflect news about a firm at the
  same time; or credit spreads and CDS premia do not lead share prices, equities moving first or both
  moving at the same time, including after negative surprises and when uncertainty rises.
- The direction of precedence bears no relation to the type of news (distress and default against
  growth) or to the firm's credit quality; where credit does lead, the lead is spread evenly across
  conditions rather than concentrated after negative news, in periods of uncertainty and where
  private information is present.
- Around credit events, equity prices do not follow credit prices, and credit prices do not follow
  equity prices.
