## Mechanism

Some cryptoassets are not mere speculative tickets but the internal currency of a protocol: they pay
the validators or miners, fund the security of the network, sometimes serve as the internal unit of
account, and coordinate the actors who build the infrastructure. Their price is therefore not only a
belief about a future cash flow but a function of the token's usefulness in maintaining the network
and in coordinating its participants.

This kind of value persists because a public network without a central authority needs a native asset
to pay for its security, to internalise incentives and to bring about a cooperative equilibrium. A
native token captures value only if its role is not cosmetic: if its use can be replaced at no cost by
ETH, by a stablecoin or by off-chain governance, its economic capture weakens.

## Prediction

Where the native token's role is not cosmetic, its price is a function of its usefulness in paying for
the security of its network and in coordinating its participants, not only a belief about a future
cash flow; where its use can be replaced at no cost by ETH, by a stablecoin or by off-chain
governance, its economic capture weakens. The phenomenon has been documented mainly on Bitcoin,
Ethereum and decentralised storage and computing networks.

## What would refute it

- The prices of native tokens bear no relation to the security they fund or to the activity of the
  networks they coordinate.
- Tokens whose role can be replaced at no cost by ETH, a stablecoin or off-chain governance capture as
  much value from their network as tokens whose role is indispensable.
