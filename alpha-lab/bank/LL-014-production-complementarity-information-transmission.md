## Mechanism

A finer version of economic links: information diffuses between industries or firms whose productions
are complementary. A shock to the demand for or the supply of one product can carry information about
the value of other firms whose activity depends on it in a complementary way, without that implication
being priced at once.

The mechanism is one of system complementarities. An expected rise in the sales of a component, a
platform or a standard can improve the prospects of complementary products; conversely, a negative shock
can spread to the ecosystem. The difficulty for the market is that these relations are less obvious
than direct customer-supplier links: they require an understanding of the economic architecture of the
production or consumption system. Information can thus travel through the structure of economic
complementarity, and not only along the linear value chain.

## Prediction

Studies show that information is transmitted measurably along relations of production complementarity
across industries: news priced in one industry or firm is reflected later in the prices of its
complements. The effect concerns in particular technology ecosystems and platforms.

## What would refute it

- No predictability runs along complementarity relations once declared supply-chain links are
  accounted for: information travels only along the linear value chain.
- A positive shock to a component, platform or standard is followed by no later rise in the prices of
  its complements, or they react at the same time.
