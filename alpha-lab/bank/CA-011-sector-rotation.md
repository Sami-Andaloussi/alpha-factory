## Mechanism

Sectors differ in their sensitivity to the growth cycle and in their exposure to inflation, interest
rates, credit and commodities. When the macroeconomic regime changes, flows and valuation multiples
move sequentially between defensive, cyclical, financial, energy, technology and other sectors. The
relative-value logic here is not an absolute forecast of the market but a directional comparison
between groups of stocks whose cash flows and valuations respond differently to the same
macroeconomic environment.

It persists because regimes are identified slowly, fund managers are benchmarked by sector,
institutional rebalancing is gradual, and the transitions from one phase of the cycle to the next
are ambiguous.

## Prediction

When the macroeconomic regime changes, flows and valuation multiples move sequentially between
sectors, and relative performance passes with them from one group of sectors to the next. The move
plays out over swings of several weeks to several months, and sometimes longer when the cycle is
clean. Its limits are non-standard shocks, central bank interventions, the temporary dominance of
mega-capitalisation stocks and changes in the composition of sectors.

## What would refute it

- Relative sector returns show no link with changes in growth, inflation, interest rates, credit or
  commodity prices, or leadership does not pass from sector to sector in sequence when the regime
  changes.
- Sector prices adjust to a change of regime at once, leaving no relative move over the following
  weeks and months.
- Sector leadership is driven by non-standard shocks, central bank interventions, the temporary
  dominance of mega-capitalisation stocks or changes in the composition of sectors, rather than by
  the macroeconomic regime.
