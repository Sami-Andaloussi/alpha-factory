## Mechanism

The issuance model is a crypto equivalent of dilution in equities. Too rapid a creation of new
tokens, an unbalanced initial distribution, a premine or a poorly calibrated rewards policy can
erode the value per unit of a token if its utility, fees or demand do not grow at the same pace. The
library widens the theme into a real "monetary policy" of networks: fixed supply or not, inflation
net of burns, staking, vesting, reserve pools, mining difficulty, and the role of fees as a
quasi-internal tax.

In many protocols the future supply is not discretionary but written into the code: issuance per
block, progressive slowdown, halving, a supply cap, or a predetermined inflation path. Two
cryptoassets that are similar in use can therefore have radically different dilution paths. The
persistence is strong because such a schedule is not a market opinion but a protocol rule, readable
by everyone and often credible ex ante. A scarce supply does not create demand; it changes the
sensitivity of the price to demand and to the financing of the network's security. The canonical
case is Bitcoin.

A cryptoasset can look fundamentally attractive through its use while remaining poor for the token
holder if value capture is continually diluted. Conversely, a sound issuance policy can support
scarcity and the alignment of participants. The effect depends heavily on the specific design of each
protocol.

## Prediction

Too rapid a creation of new tokens, an unbalanced initial distribution, a premine or poorly
calibrated rewards can erode the value per unit of a token whose utility, fees or demand do not grow
at the same pace: a cryptoasset can look fundamentally attractive through its use while remaining
poor for the holder if value capture is continually diluted. A sound issuance policy can support
scarcity and the alignment of participants. Cryptoassets similar in use can have radically different
dilution paths.

## What would refute it

- Value per unit unrelated to net issuance relative to the growth of utility, fees and demand.
- Tokens with premines, unbalanced initial distributions or poorly calibrated rewards whose value
  per unit keeps pace with that of comparable tokens without them, although their utility, fees or
  demand do not grow at the same pace.
- Networks whose use grows while heavy issuance dilutes holders, yet whose value per unit keeps pace
  with that use.
- Changes of issuance regime, such as halvings, that leave the price behaviour of the asset
  unchanged; a sensitivity of the price to demand that is the same whether the supply is capped,
  slowing down or inflationary.
