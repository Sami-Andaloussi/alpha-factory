## Mechanism

Franchise value is the part of a company's value that comes from returns on capital durably above
the cost of capital, earned thanks to defensible competitive advantages. If a company can repeat
excess returns for a long time, it holds an advantage that competitors cannot copy at the same cost
or at the same pace. The library's books point mainly to four families of drivers: customer
captivity and switching costs; economies of scale; brand and pricing power; de facto standards or
network effects.

The mechanism explains why some expensive valuations can remain rational and why not all growth is
worth the same. It also explains why a simple accounting discount is not enough: a mediocre asset
without a franchise can stay mediocre, while an asset with a franchise warrants a longer period of
excess profits. The phenomenon persists because the speed at which a competitive advantage (a moat)
erodes is very hard to measure in advance, and because the market alternates between excessive
extrapolation and underestimation of how durable the advantage really is.

## Prediction

Because the market alternates between excessive extrapolation and underestimation of how durable a
competitive advantage really is, franchise value is mispriced in both directions: where the market
underestimates the durability of a franchise, the stock is undervalued, even at an expensive
valuation; where it extrapolates excessively, the stock is overvalued. A cheap stock without a
franchise can stay mediocre, so an accounting discount alone does not mark an undervalued stock.
High returns on invested capital persist partially.

## What would refute it

- High returns on capital that fade at the same speed whether or not customer captivity, economies
  of scale, brand and pricing power, or network effects are present.
- No persistence at all of high returns on invested capital, or full persistence regardless of
  competitive position.
- Valuations that already anticipate the durability of excess returns correctly, so that neither
  expensive franchise stocks nor companies with eroding advantages show predictable subsequent
  returns.
- Cheap stocks without a franchise that re-rate as often as cheap stocks with one.
