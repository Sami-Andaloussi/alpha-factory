## Mechanism

This hypothesis holds that some intramonth effects do not come directly from the calendar as such, but from the fact that important macroeconomic announcements are themselves grouped at recurring points of the month, often in its first half. If prices react strongly to employment, inflation, production and other scheduled releases, a systematic concentration of information mechanically produces a systematic concentration of returns.

It replaces the reading of these patterns as an anomaly with a reading in terms of the timing of information. The calendar is then only a container for a monthly cycle of news production, which brings calendar effects closer to standard asset pricing: returns are high when important information arrives. In this reading, part of the turn-of-the-month and intramonth effects is not necessarily a mispricing once the risk borne over announcements is taken into account.

## Prediction

Returns are high when important scheduled macroeconomic releases arrive, and these releases fall at recurring points of the month, often in its first half. Controlling for these announcements removes part of the turn-of-the-month and intramonth effects.

What has been observed: the literature shows that, in some samples, the turn-of-the-month and intramonth effects fall sharply, or even disappear, once the major macroeconomic announcements are controlled for; the question has also been studied for European stock markets and US announcements. This does not mean that all seasonalities are explained, but that some of them are in fact shadows of scheduled information.

## What would refute it

- Turn-of-the-month and intramonth concentrations of returns that persist unchanged once the days of major scheduled macroeconomic announcements are controlled for, and once the risk borne over announcements is accounted for.
- No concentration of returns, or of price reactions, on the days of important macroeconomic releases.
- Intramonth return patterns that stay in place when the release calendar of the macroeconomic statistics moves.
