## Mechanism

The earnings yield, earnings over price, is the inverse of the price-earnings ratio and serves as a
bridge between simple valuation and fundamental anchoring. The mechanism is not only that a high
earnings yield is "cheaper": the market often embeds in the price an implicit combination of growth,
risk and capital structure, and the earnings yield allows that combination to be challenged more
directly. In the frameworks of Penman and of Graham, the earnings yield is a no-growth benchmark. If a
company only grows at the rate required by its cost of capital, a certain earnings yield is normal;
above it, the market may be too pessimistic or the capital structure too risky; below it, the market
is already paying for a strong franchise or for prolonged growth.

The theory ties together the earnings yield, the valuation anchor and the potential for a revision
of expectations.

## Prediction

Once leverage, aggressive accounting entries, cyclical profits and intangibles are neutralised, an
earnings yield above the no-growth benchmark marks a stock about which the market may be too
pessimistic, or whose capital structure is too risky; where the market is too pessimistic, the stock
is undervalued and exposed to a revision of expectations. An earnings yield below the benchmark marks
a stock for which the market is already paying for a strong franchise or for prolonged growth. The
earnings yield is one of the anchors with which the value premium has been documented.

## What would refute it

- High earnings-yield stocks, once leverage, accounting, cyclicality and intangibles are
  neutralised, that do not outperform low earnings-yield stocks.
- Earnings yields above the no-growth benchmark that are fully accounted for by a riskier capital
  structure, with no later revision of expectations.
