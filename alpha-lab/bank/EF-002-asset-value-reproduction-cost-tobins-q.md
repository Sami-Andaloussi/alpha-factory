## Mechanism

The market value of a company is compared with the economic value of its existing assets, measured
by their reproduction cost, by their replacement cost, or through Tobin's Q (market value over
replacement cost). If the market durably values a company well above the reproduction cost of its
assets while no barrier to entry protects it, the gap normally attracts entrants, who end up
compressing returns. Conversely, a market value below replacement cost may signal that the assets are
badly used, that the company is in distress, that its capital discipline is deficient, or that the
market's expectation of the future profitability of the assets in place is too pessimistic.

The comparison bears above all on mature, asset-heavy, cyclical or industrial companies, and on
those exposed to waves of overcapacity. The phenomenon persists because replacement cost is costly
to compute, because assets are heterogeneous, and because the market can take time to tell a
temporary under-use of assets from a structural destruction of value.

## Prediction

A company that the market values well above the reproduction cost of its assets, with no barrier to
entry, attracts entrants, who end up compressing its returns. A company valued below replacement
cost may be one whose assets are badly used, one in distress, one whose capital discipline is
deficient, or one about whose assets in place the market is too pessimistic; in that last case the
company is undervalued, and the gap persists because the market can take time to tell a temporary
under-use of the assets from a structural destruction of value. The pattern concerns above all
mature, asset-heavy, cyclical or industrial companies and those exposed to waves of overcapacity.

## What would refute it

- Companies valued far above the reproduction cost of their assets, with no barrier to entry, that
  keep their excess returns for long periods without attracting entry.
- Market values below replacement cost that always prove justified by misuse of the assets, distress,
  deficient capital discipline or a structural destruction of value, never by an expectation that was
  too pessimistic.
- Gaps between market value and replacement cost that close no faster where assets are homogeneous
  and their replacement cost easy to compute than where they are heterogeneous and costly to value.
