## Mechanism

The basic mechanism of listed stocks is the separation between the owners of the capital and the
managers who control the day-to-day allocation of resources. This separation creates recurring price
behaviour because not all stocks carry the same risk of poor governance, entrenchment, diversion of
resources or insufficient monitoring.

Agency conflicts, the composition of the board, the concentration of ownership, activism and the
threat of a takeover are therefore part of the internal structure of each stock, and not a universal
factor. The phenomenon persists because monitoring is costly and because shareholders are dispersed,
heterogeneous and often passive. The precise link with short- or medium-term returns depends on the
institutional regime and on the ability of investors to discipline managers.

## Prediction

Stocks show recurring differences in price behaviour according to their exposure to the risks of
poor governance, entrenchment, diversion of resources and insufficient monitoring, and the governance
features that bear on these risks (agency conflicts, board composition, ownership concentration,
activism, takeover threats) are part of the internal structure of each stock. The precise link with
short- or medium-term returns depends on the institutional regime and on the ability of investors to
discipline managers.

## What would refute it

- Stocks with a high exposure to the risks of poor governance, entrenchment, diversion of resources or
  insufficient monitoring show the same price behaviour as comparable stocks with a low exposure.
- Differences across stocks in agency conflicts, board composition, ownership concentration, activism
  or the threat of a takeover bear no relation to differences in their price behaviour.
- The link between governance and returns is the same in every institutional regime, whatever the
  ability of investors to discipline managers.
