## Mechanism

The information-cycle explanation reads cross-sectional return seasonalities not as a pure calendar mispricing but as the reflection of seasonal cycles in the disclosure of firm-specific information. If some firms systematically release important information (guidance, seasonal sales, budgets or signals about their cash flows) at certain times of the year, those months are structurally richer in valuation news for these firms.

The explanation ties a recurring empirical effect to a stable infrastructure of information, and it is consistent with the operating seasonality of firms: retail, tourism, energy, software, agriculture and activities tied to the fiscal year do not produce relevant information at the same pace. In this reading, the same-calendar-month effect would in part be a consequence of the repetition of the same windows of fundamental revaluation.

It does not remove frictions. Even when the calendar of information is known, the price impact is not trivially arbitraged, because the precise information contained in the window is not known in advance. The explanation also helps to understand why seasonalities can be stronger for some firms, industries or periods. It does not necessarily replace the explanations based on reallocation or on mood; it can coexist with them.

## Prediction

The same-calendar-month effect comes in part from the months in which a firm recurrently releases important information, months that are structurally richer in valuation news for that firm. Seasonalities can be stronger for some firms, industries or periods than for others, according to the pace at which they produce relevant information.

## What would refute it

- Same-calendar-month seasonality as strong in months without recurring disclosures as in months with them.
- Seasonality no stronger for firms and industries whose important disclosures recur in a few months of the year than for others.
- Seasonal returns that stay in the same calendar month when a firm changes its fiscal year or its disclosure dates.
