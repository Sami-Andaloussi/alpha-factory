## Mechanism

The mechanism here is more "real" than monetary: sectors have different cash-flow profiles depending on
the phase of the business cycle (recovery, expansion, slowdown and recession). Sectors tied to
investment, energy, materials or discretionary consumption respond differently from defensive sectors
such as utilities, health care or consumer staples. The economic principle is that the composition of
aggregate demand, investment, margins and bankruptcies changes over the cycle, which shifts the relative
hierarchy between blocks of stocks. The effect concerns sectors, countries, styles, growth against
value, and at times crypto against equity beta, according to the macroeconomic regime.

The theory rests on two propositions: that sectors have different sensitivities to the cycle, and
that the market adjusts to the phases of the cycle with a delay.

## Prediction

The relative performance of sectors tied to investment, energy, materials or discretionary
consumption against defensive sectors (utilities, health care, consumer staples) changes with the
phase of the business cycle. Studies generally find that sectors do have different sensitivities to
the cycle. On the delay, some studies find economic value in rotating across the cycle, while others
show that, even with a perfect dating of the cycle, the excess return is more limited than expected,
which signals a partial arbitrage already at work.

## What would refute it

- Sectors show no difference in sensitivity to the phases of the business cycle: the relative
  returns of these two groups of sectors do not vary between recovery, expansion, slowdown and
  recession.
- Sector prices anticipate the phases of the cycle fully, so that the phase, even when it is dated
  perfectly, carries no information about subsequent relative sector returns.
