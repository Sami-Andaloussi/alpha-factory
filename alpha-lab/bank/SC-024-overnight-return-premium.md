## Mechanism

The regularity holds that a large part of the return on equities is earned while the market is closed, overnight, whereas intraday returns are weak, or even of the opposite sign to the overnight move. It is narrower than the classic calendar effects, but it is a recurring pattern in time.

The books attribute it to the way market makers, specialists and opening mechanisms handle the imbalance of orders that accumulates overnight. The public does not have the same information as the participants at the centre of the opening process, which limits arbitrage. The regularity shows that part of the predictability of prices depends on how trading is segmented in time.

## Prediction

Close-to-open (overnight) returns account for a large share of equity returns, while open-to-close (intraday) returns are weak and can be of the opposite sign. The books report a strong negative autocorrelation between the overnight change and the intraday change that follows it.

## What would refute it

- Overnight and intraday returns of similar average size, with the overnight period earning no disproportionate share of the total return.
- No negative autocorrelation between the overnight change and the following intraday change.
- An overnight premium and an intraday reversal unrelated to the order imbalance accumulated overnight and handled at the open.
- The same pattern where the information on opening orders is available to all participants, which would contradict the information asymmetry that is said to limit arbitrage.
