## Mechanism

The media play a causal role in the diffusion of financial information. When the media's capacity to
spread news is disrupted, the incorporation of news into prices slows down. The lead-lag at stake is
therefore not only between assets but also between news, investors and prices, through a channel of
diffusion.

Even when information exists, it does not reach as far without the aggregation, selection and
broadcasting the media provide. The media lower the cost of attention, widen the audience of news and
speed up the synchronisation of beliefs. Without them, information stays more local and more segmented
and is integrated more slowly, especially for little-followed stocks. The speed at which prices
incorporate information depends on the channel of propagation itself, the infrastructure that spreads
it, and not only on the structure of the graph between assets.

## Prediction

When media diffusion is interrupted, prices adjust more slowly to news and information spreads less.
Studies of newspaper strikes find that the absence of the media slows the adjustment of prices and
reduces the diffusion of information. The slowdown is expected to be strongest for little-followed
stocks.

## What would refute it

- Interruptions of media diffusion leave the speed at which news enters prices unchanged.
- In today's digital media environment, the loss of a media outlet no longer slows the incorporation of
  news.
- Little-followed stocks are no more affected by the absence of media than widely followed ones.
