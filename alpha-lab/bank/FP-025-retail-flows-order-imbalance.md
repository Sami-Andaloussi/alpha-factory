## Mechanism

Individual investors produce aggregate order imbalances that can push prices, above all in small
capitalisations, in stocks that draw heavy media attention, and in stocks with a limited free float.
The generation of the flow can be behavioural (attention, extrapolation, social imitation), but the
market mechanism is structural: when these orders concentrate on particular securities, they create
price pressure because the available counterparties are limited.

The frictions that let the effect persist are limited attention, the segmentation of clienteles, the
constraints of market makers and, in some cases, limits on short selling or on securities lending.
The size and implicit coordination of the retail clientele can durably change the dynamics of a
market, particularly when short sellers are on the other side. The mechanism is closely linked to
meme stocks, squeezes and waves of flows into highly visible assets. In crypto spot, the phenomenon
is probably larger still, by construction.

## Prediction

Net buying or selling by individual investors concentrated in particular stocks can move their
prices in the same direction, above all for small capitalisations, high-attention stocks and stocks
with a limited float. The literature has documented short-term effects sometimes followed by a
reversal, and episodes in which coordinated retail buying changes price dynamics durably, especially
when short interest is high. These effects have been documented in equities.

## What would refute it

- Aggregate retail order imbalances with no effect on the prices of the stocks concerned.
- An effect no larger in small, high-attention or low-float stocks than in large, widely followed
  ones.
- Retail buying waves that change nothing in price dynamics even when short sellers hold large
  positions on the other side.
