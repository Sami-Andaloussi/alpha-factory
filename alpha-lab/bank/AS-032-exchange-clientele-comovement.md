## Mechanism

Cryptoassets listed on the same platforms, or held by similar clienteles of investors, can move
together for institutional reasons specific to the exchanges, beyond their individual fundamentals.
Flows, visibility, collateral constraints, leverage opportunities, trading interfaces and the habits
of each clientele create clusters of tokens that react together. The driver is therefore not only "the
crypto market" in the broad sense, but the structure of the trading venues and of the investor
communities attached to them.

The mechanism persists because exchanges are not neutral pipes. They determine which pairs are
available, which tokens are promoted, which derivative or margin products exist, which regional
clienteles have access to a token, and which liquidity shocks circulate within the ecosystem of the
platform. Arbitrage does not fully eliminate these comovements, because investors are not perfectly
substitutable across venues, and because some frictions of access, reputation or regulation really
segment the market. The theory is close to access segmentation, but it stresses the role of exchanges
as organisers of clientele and as generators of structural correlation.

## Prediction

Tokens that share the same exchanges, or the same investor clienteles, can comove beyond their
individual fundamentals, including tokens with no apparent fundamental link. The comovement is recurrent
and intensifies during episodes of speculative flows or of news specific to a platform. The setting is
crypto.

## What would refute it

- Tokens listed on the same exchanges comove no more than tokens listed on different exchanges, once
  their fundamentals and the overall crypto market are taken into account.
- Flows and news specific to a platform move only the tokens they directly concern, not the other
  tokens listed on that platform.
- The comovement of a token with the other tokens of an exchange does not change when it is listed on
  or delisted from that exchange.
