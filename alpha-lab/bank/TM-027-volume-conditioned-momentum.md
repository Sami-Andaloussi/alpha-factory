## Mechanism

The strength and persistence of momentum are linked to turnover and trading volume. Past volume would
carry information about the type of momentum, whether it is in an early or a late phase of its life
cycle, and about the kind of investors involved. Volume is read as a proxy for attention, trading
activity and disagreement: attention shocks and the entry of new participants can feed continuation,
while some configurations of volume can signal exhaustion and a transition of regime, without this
being a universal rule. The same past-return signal can be amplified or dampened depending on the
channel through which the market digests information and flows, a matter of microstructure and of
the composition of traders.

The volume that accompanies a price move is a signal of amplified momentum. The proposed mechanism
is twofold: high volume during a rise indicates strong conviction among buyers and a large
institutional flow, and high volume attracts more positive feedback traders, trend followers who
amplify the move. On an alternative reading, volume proxies for investor attention: information
about assets that draw heavy attention circulates faster and more widely but also creates more
short-term noise, and the persistence of high volume is therefore linked to the persistence of
attention and flows. By analogy, in futures markets the amplification signal comes from the volume
and open interest of the contracts, as momentum confirmed by volume.

## Prediction

Stocks, or futures contracts, whose price moves come with high volume show significantly stronger
momentum than those whose moves come with low volume: Lee and Swaminathan (2000) report that
high-momentum stocks with high volume outperform high-momentum stocks with low volume by a factor of
about three. The pattern is documented mainly on US stocks. The horizon is 6 to 12 months for
momentum, with volume used as a filter when the portfolio is formed. Past volume would also locate
momentum in its life cycle, in an early or a late phase, and some configurations of volume can signal
its exhaustion.

## What would refute it

Momentum no stronger among high-volume stocks, or futures contracts, than among low-volume ones with
the same past return. The strength and persistence of momentum unrelated to turnover and volume.
Past volume carrying no information about whether momentum is in an early or a late phase.
