## Mechanism

Over long periods, stocks priced low relative to their book value have outperformed stocks priced
high relative to it. The mechanism the library proposes combines two readings of the cause. In the
rational reading, part of the premium compensates for real difficulties, weak profits or a greater
sensitivity to recessions. In the behavioural reading, the market extrapolates the current weakness
of profitability too far, overestimates how permanent bad news is, and pays too much for glamour
stocks. The library's texts show that the value premium comes largely from the convergence of
valuation ratios as profits normalise, not from extraordinary growth among growth stocks.

The discount can also signal a value trap, especially when it reflects distress, disruption or poor
capital allocation.

## Prediction

High book-to-market stocks earn higher returns than low book-to-market stocks over long periods, the
premium coming largely from the convergence of the valuation ratios of cheap and expensive stocks as
profits normalise. Cheap stocks whose discount reflects distress, disruption or poor capital
allocation risk being value traps.

## What would refute it

- No long-run return difference between high and low book-to-market stocks.
- Profits of cheap companies that do not normalise and valuation ratios that do not converge, so
  that high book-to-market stocks stay cheap.
- Bad news about cheap companies that proves exactly as permanent as prices imply, together with a
  premium unrelated to weak profits or to sensitivity to recessions.
