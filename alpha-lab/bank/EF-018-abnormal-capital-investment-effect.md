## Mechanism

The theory targets surges in real investment, capital expenditure in particular, or investment that
is "abnormal" relative to the company's growth opportunities. Sharp increases in investment are often
read too positively by the market, whereas they also signal an expected decline in the marginal
return on capital, a managerial wish to grow for the sake of growing, or a moment of euphoria in which
the company can finance mediocre projects at attractive market prices. The market does not punish
aggressive investment quickly enough when future cash flows do not justify the spending.

The effect is close to the asset growth anomaly but focuses on the capital expenditure and investment
decision itself. It belongs with the mechanisms of capital discipline, linking a company's investment
policy to its future stock performance.

## Prediction

Firms whose capital investment rises abnormally earn lower subsequent returns than other firms. The
phenomenon is studied on equities, often over horizons of one to three years after the rise in
investment.

Some increases in investment reflect genuinely good growth opportunities, so the aggregate effect
can mask strong heterogeneity across sectors. The effect can be less clear among companies rich in
growth options or in innovation.

## What would refute it

- No underperformance in the one to three years after an abnormal rise in capital investment.
- Investment surges followed, on average, by cash flows that justify the spending.
- A market reaction at the time of the investment decision that already prices the lower marginal
  return in full, leaving no later drift.
