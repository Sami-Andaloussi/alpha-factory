## Mechanism

Two-sided platforms are a mechanism distinct from simple network effects. The value for each side of
the market depends on the participation of the other side: cardholders and merchants for payment
cards, buyers and sellers for a marketplace, developers and users for some software environments,
liquidity providers and traders for market protocols.

This structure produces very particular price and growth behaviour: one side can be subsidised while
the other is monetised, and profitability cannot be read side by side, only in the equilibrium of the
whole ecosystem. The phenomenon persists because the platform controls access, pricing and sometimes
the matching rules between the two sides. It concerns sub-universes of platform stocks and some crypto
infrastructures. A true two-sided market is distinct from a simple firm with two customer segments.

## Prediction

Platform firms and protocols show very particular price and growth behaviour driven by the balance
between their two sides: one side can be subsidised while the other is monetised, participation on
one side raises the value for the other, and their profitability is read in the equilibrium of the
ecosystem, not side by side.

## What would refute it

- The participation of one side of the platform has no effect on the value that the other side derives
  from it.
- Platforms that control access, pricing and matching show the same growth and profitability patterns
  as ordinary firms with two independent customer segments.
- The profitability of platforms can be read side by side, with no side subsidised by the other.
