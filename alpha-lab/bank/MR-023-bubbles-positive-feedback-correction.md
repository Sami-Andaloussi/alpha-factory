## Mechanism

Before prices revert, they can first move massively away from their fundamental anchor under the effect of feedback trading, herding, contagious narratives, psychological anchors and the extrapolation of past rises. Buyers buy because the price rises, which lifts the price further and draws in more capital. The deviation from value is therefore not an instantaneous error but a cumulative process of amplification. Shiller discusses such amplification mechanisms as naturally occurring Ponzi processes.

The return towards the mean comes when the flow dries up, when funding becomes harder to obtain, when the dominant narrative cracks, or when forced selling breaks the dynamic. The correction can be gradual, but it can also take the form of a crash, or of a violent rebound after a crash. The mechanism explains why contrarian strategies can be "right too early": the fundamental misalignment can widen for a long time before it closes. The theory concerns in particular markets prone to themes, panics and crowding into investment styles. The books show that it is not incompatible with local efficiency in some episodes: a market can seem to follow an almost trend-following dynamic for a while, then show an extreme reversal when the bubble bursts.

## Prediction

Prices driven by positive feedback and herding can move massively away from their fundamental anchors, then revert when flows dry up, funding tightens, the narrative cracks or forced selling breaks the dynamic; the correction can be gradual, or take the form of a crash or of a violent rebound after a crash.

## What would refute it

- Extended rallies driven by feedback buying that do not reverse, the new price level being justified afterwards by fundamentals.
- Turning points unrelated to the drying-up of flows, tighter funding, the cracking of narratives or forced selling.
- Deviations that close as soon as they appear, with no phase in which contrarian positions lose before the correction.
