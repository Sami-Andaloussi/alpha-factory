## Mechanism

The theory applies relative strength at the geographic level: some national or regional markets
become lasting leaders and others laggards, because macroeconomic news, international flows,
earnings revisions and the preferences of global investors diffuse slowly. The point is not only
that momentum exists within each country, but that national indices themselves display a persistent
ranking.

The mechanism can combine several forces: underreaction to macroeconomic and earnings news,
international segmentation, the domestic constraints of investors, the inertia of institutional
flows, benchmark effects, and the slowness with which global capital is reallocated. Performance
gaps between countries are therefore not arbitraged at once, even in very liquid markets, because
they reflect regimes of growth, currency, valuation and political risk that do not reduce to a
single risk-free opportunity. The effect concerns geographic rotation. The theory is also close to
rotation between regimes: exporting markets against domestic ones, commodity markets against energy
importers, and others.

## Prediction

National and regional equity indices with the strongest recent relative performance continue to
outperform those with the weakest, so that the ranking of countries by past performance persists,
including among very liquid index markets. Its limits are the geographic concentration of sectors
and a possible instability of the rankings when the global regime changes.

## What would refute it

- The past relative performance of national indices does not predict their subsequent relative
  performance.
- The persistence of country rankings is a currency effect, appearing or disappearing with the
  currency in which index returns are measured, or it comes entirely from the geographic
  concentration of sectors.
- Country rankings reverse at changes of global regime often enough to cancel the continuation.
