## Mechanism

The literature contains several more localised calendar anomalies, anchored in religious and cultural calendars: a fall or weakness around Rosh Hashanah followed by an improvement until Yom Kippur; outperformance during Ramadan in several Muslim markets; and the Golden Week effect in Japan.

What they have in common is that they are not only generic "holidays" but culturally charged sequences of the calendar, which change attention, participation, mood or flows. They show that calendar effects can be rooted in social and religious institutions, and not only in taxation or in the payment of salaries. Some of the explanations are largely behavioural.

## Prediction

Returns follow the local cultural calendar: prices fall or are weak around Rosh Hashanah and improve until Yom Kippur; the equity markets of several Muslim countries outperform during Ramadan; and Japanese equities show a Golden Week effect. These regularities are heterogeneous: they are not universal and are often geographically concentrated.

## What would refute it

- No fall or weakness around Rosh Hashanah followed by an improvement until Yom Kippur, no outperformance during Ramadan in Muslim markets, and no distinct pattern around Golden Week in Japan.
- Effects of these periods no different from those of ordinary market holidays, which would contradict the role of their cultural content.
- The same patterns in markets where the calendar in question carries no social weight.
- No change in attention, participation, mood or flows during these periods.
