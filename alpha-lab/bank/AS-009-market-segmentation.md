## Mechanism

Two economically similar assets can trade at durably different prices if their accessibility, their
tradability or their base of eligible investors differ. The classic cases are restrictions on foreign
ownership, share classes reserved for certain clienteles, and situations in which part of the capital
is non-tradable and then becomes tradable. The price then depends not only on the cash flows but also
on who has the right, or the possibility, to hold the asset.

The economic mechanism is simple: segmentation reduces the depth of potential demand, and it can make
some shares "scarce" for a given clientele or, on the contrary, "in excess" in a shallow segment. The
phenomenon persists because the law, regulation, mandates or access costs block arbitrage between
segments. Even when information circulates between share classes, prices can remain durably apart if
investors are not substitutable.

The theory generalises the idea of free float: the relevant supply is not the total supply but the
supply actually available to a given clientele. By analogy, beyond stocks, the logic extends to
cryptoassets that are listed selectively, geographically restricted, or held mostly outside the
float that can actually be mobilised.

## Prediction

Share classes or listings of the same, or of economically similar, firms that are open to different
sets of investors can trade at persistently different prices for as long as the segmentation lasts; when
the segmentation is suddenly relaxed, prices move at that event. This logic has been documented in
several emerging markets and in dual-class structures and access restrictions.

## What would refute it

- Share classes or listings of the same firm that differ only by who may hold them trade at the same
  price.
- Price gaps between segments close through arbitrage while the legal, regulatory, mandate or access
  barriers remain in place.
- The relaxation of a segmentation, such as an opening to foreign investors or a non-tradable block of
  capital becoming tradable, leaves prices unchanged.
