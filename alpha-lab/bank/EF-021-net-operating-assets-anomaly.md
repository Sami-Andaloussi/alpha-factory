## Mechanism

Net operating assets give a cumulative, balance-sheet-centred version of the accruals and investment
ideas. A high level of net operating assets means that, over time, the company has accumulated
operating assets and accounting earnings faster than it has converted them into free cash flow. A
"bloated" balance sheet therefore suggests economic performance less durable than investors focused
on accounting profitability or asset growth imagine. The measure turns an annual flow, accruals, into
a cumulative stock of capital misallocation, accounting optimism or low-return investment, and
belongs both to earnings quality and to capital discipline. The explanations put forward are limited
investor attention and agency problems.

## Prediction

Firms with high net operating assets earn lower subsequent returns than firms with low net operating
assets. The effect has been documented over long periods on equities, with a slower horizon than the
annual accrual anomaly: the return to economic reality can take several years.

The measure overlaps with the signals of investment, external issuance and accruals, but records an
accumulated state of the balance sheet rather than a single annual event.

## What would refute it

- No lower subsequent returns for firms with high net operating assets.
- An effect entirely absorbed by annual accruals, investment and external issuance, with no
  information left in the accumulated state of the balance sheet.
- High net operating assets followed, on average, by free cash flow that catches up with the
  accumulated assets and earnings.
