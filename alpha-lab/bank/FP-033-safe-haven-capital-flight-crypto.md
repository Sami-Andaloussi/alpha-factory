## Mechanism

Some market episodes show that crypto spot markets can receive buying flows driven by the search for
a refuge: outside the local banking system, beyond the reach of capital controls, or against the
devaluation of the domestic currency. This is not the same as a simple move towards risk. The buyer
wants to shift wealth into an asset that is portable, global and less dependent on national banking
infrastructure. Lee Kuo Chuen (2015) discusses the potential capacity of cryptocurrencies to get
around capital controls and to serve as a safe harbour during a crisis of a fiat currency.

It is an identifiable displacement of capital under macro-financial constraint. It persists because
what the buyer seeks is not fair value but a way out: transferability and independence from the
local system. It is not fully arbitraged because the demand comes from monetary or banking urgency,
not from a trade on valuation.

## Prediction

Banking crises, capital controls and devaluations of a domestic currency can be followed by buying
flows into crypto spot from holders seeking a refuge outside the local banking system. The episodes
last from a few days to several weeks, depending on the duration of the crisis. Lee Kuo Chuen (2015)
cites the Cypriot episode of 2013, and Burniske and Tatar (2017) document the link between the
devaluation of the yuan and demand for bitcoin.

## What would refute it

- No increase in crypto buying during banking crises, capital controls or currency devaluations.
- Crypto demand during such episodes that moves only with global appetite for risk, with no
  component specific to the country in crisis.
