## Mechanism

The December effect, also called the new December effect, is a phenomenon that the books distinguish from the January effect: close to it, but not identical to it. The year's big winners, above all among large capitalisations, often rise in December because their holders postpone selling them until January, which defers the payment of capital gains tax by almost a year. It is the mirror image of tax-loss selling on losers.

The mechanism does not require the whole market to rise in December. It only requires that some recent winners face temporarily less selling pressure, because rational investors wait a few days before realising a taxable gain.

The effect can be compressed if the market anticipates it and pushes prices up earlier towards the end of the year.

## Prediction

The year's large winners, above all among large capitalisations, often rise in December while the sale of their taxable gains is postponed into January; the effect does not require a rise of the whole market. The postponed sales of these winners fall in early January, once the gains belong to the new tax year. If the market anticipates the effect, it can be compressed, with prices pushed up earlier towards the end of the year.

## What would refute it

- No December outperformance of the year's large winners, in particular among large capitalisations, relative to other stocks.
- December strength in winners that is unrelated to the size of their unrealised gains, or that is equally present where capital gains are not taxed or where the tax year does not end in December.
- No increase in sales of the previous year's winners by taxable investors in early January.
- A December rise spread over the whole market rather than concentrated in recent winners.
