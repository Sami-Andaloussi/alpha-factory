## Mechanism

Among stocks with strong price momentum, those that trade with low volume, low turnover, do better
than those that trade with high volume. The result runs against the intuition that high volume
confirms momentum. High volume signals that the positive information is already widely known and
that the move may be close to its end. Low volume alongside positive momentum signals a phenomenon
that is still "under the radar" of most investors, with more potential for continuation because
fewer traders have already repositioned.

By analogy, in futures a momentum signal reinforced by still moderate volume can signal a trend at
the start of its formation, while a momentum signal accompanied by explosive volume may come near
the end of a trend.

## Prediction

Over 83 years of US data, from 1926 to 2009, stocks with strong price momentum and low volume
substantially outperformed stocks with strong momentum and high volume, with a compound annual growth
rate of 13.16% against 8.09% (O'Shaughnessy, 2011). The horizon is 6 to 12 months of momentum, with
the volume filter applied when the signal is formed.

## What would refute it

High-momentum stocks with low volume earning no more than high-momentum stocks with high volume over
the following months. High volume during a rise followed by continuation as strong as, or stronger
than, low volume.

## References

- O'Shaughnessy, J. P. (2011). What Works on Wall Street (4th edition), ch. 20, section "Trading Volume Refinement". McGraw-Hill.
