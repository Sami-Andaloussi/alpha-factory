## Mechanism

The economic logic here is that national equity markets are neither perfectly integrated nor valued
on a uniform basis. Home bias, local investor clienteles, regulatory constraints, accounting
differences, analyst coverage, sector structure and domestic flows create persistent gaps in
valuation and performance between countries or regions. In its relative-value version, the
phenomenon favours national markets that are relatively cheap, less crowded or improving on the
macroeconomic front over those that concentrate optimism, passive flows and familiarity premia.

The mechanism is not fully arbitraged because international capital moves slowly, political risk is
hard to diversify, differences in governance blur the notion of fair value, and many investors
remain tied to their domestic benchmark.

## Prediction

National markets that are relatively cheap, less crowded or improving on the macroeconomic front
outperform those that concentrate optimism, passive flows and familiarity premia. The phenomenon has
been observed in country allocation, in international comparisons of value and in global
diversification. Its main limits are the effect of sector composition, currency risk and
institutional breaks.

## What would refute it

- Relatively cheap, uncrowded or improving national markets do not outperform the expensive and
  crowded ones.
- The gaps in valuation and performance between countries are entirely explained by differences in
  sector composition, by currency movements or by institutional breaks, rather than by home bias and
  segmentation.
- Valuation gaps between national markets close as soon as they open, as they would if international
  capital moved freely.
