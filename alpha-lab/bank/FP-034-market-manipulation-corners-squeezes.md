## Mechanism

Corners and squeezes are exercises of market power over an asset, a contract maturity or a scarce
deliverable. The manipulator does not profit from knowing fundamental value better, but from
controlling or influencing the availability of the security or the commodity that others must buy,
deliver or buy back. In futures, the typical effect is an abnormal distortion of the relation
between maturities, especially between the nearby and the deferred contracts, because the nearby
maturity becomes artificially scarce.

The mechanism puts at its centre the structure of forced positions, delivery obligations and
logistical frictions. It persists because the deliverable supply is finite, because logistics are
costly, and because some participants must at all costs close their positions or deliver on a fixed
date.

## Prediction

Near critical expiries, the nearby maturity that has been cornered becomes artificially scarce, and
the relation between the nearby and the deferred contracts is abnormally distorted, away from the
normal behaviour of competitive spreads. In equities, corners and short squeezes bear on a security
that others must buy back while a dominant participant controls or influences its availability. Such
episodes have been observed in grains and other deliverable commodities, in markets exposed to
transportation costs or congestion of the deliverable supply, and in equity corners and short
squeezes; Pirrong (2011) discusses the Ferruzzi case and the distortion of the July/September spread
in 1989. They are rare and strongly dependent on the market regime.

## What would refute it

- Spreads between nearby and deferred contracts staying within their normal competitive range when
  one participant controls a large share of the deliverable supply ahead of expiry.
- Participants with delivery or buy-back obligations facing no price distortion when a dominant
  holder controls the available supply.
- Distortions between nearby and deferred contracts near expiry fully explained by legitimate
  scarcity, a genuine physical tension on the deliverable, rather than by a participant's control of
  its availability.
