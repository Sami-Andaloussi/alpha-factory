## Mechanism

The mechanism is no longer relative continuation but the slow reversion of relative prices between
national stock markets. When one country outperforms another too strongly over a long period, part
of the move reflects temporary changes in risk premia, sentiment, flows or over- and underreaction,
and not only a permanent divergence in fundamentals. National indices then move part of the way back
towards a more sustainable relation.

The economic logic can rest on the incomplete but real convergence of valuations, relative
profitability and risk premia across countries. International arbitrageurs do not close the gaps at
once because they carry genuine macroeconomic, political, currency and liquidity risk, and sometimes
governance risk. This mix of a real economic relation and a limit to arbitrage gives an anchor of
relative value between countries, but a noisy and slow one.

## Prediction

National markets that have been the largest relative losers over long horizons can offer higher
expected returns, as relative prices between countries partially revert: a geographic catch-up. This
holds especially when the past divergence reflected the revaluation of multiples and flows more than
an irreversible structural deterioration.

## What would refute it

- Relative prices of national markets do not revert after long periods of divergence: long-horizon
  relative losers do not subsequently outperform long-horizon relative winners.
- Divergences between countries prove permanent, matched by lasting divergences in fundamentals such
  as relative profitability, with no reversion of valuations or risk premia.
- Relative losers whose underperformance came from falling multiples and outflows revert no more
  than those whose fundamentals deteriorated structurally.
