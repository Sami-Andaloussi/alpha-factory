## Mechanism

Options markets can incorporate information before the underlying stocks. Option volumes, option order
flow, implied prices and the volatility surface contain information that is later transmitted to the
equity market.

Options offer leverage, convexity and sometimes a more discreet or more efficient way to express an
informed view, so some informed investors prefer the option to the stock itself. If the equity market
does not react at the same time, the signals of the options market become predictors of the future
returns of the underlying. Diffusion can be slowed by the complexity of interpreting option signals, by
the diversity of strikes and maturities, and by the fact that not all equity participants monitor the
information extracted from options in real time.

## Prediction

The literature shows that some segments of option volume and some option-derived indicators contain
information about future stock returns, and price-discovery studies measure the share of the options
market in the discovery of stock prices. The effect seems stronger when the options market is liquid and
when the information is asymmetric or tied to an event. It often plays out over short horizons. Part of
the options signal may reflect hedging, volatility or non-directional demand.

## What would refute it

- Option volumes and option-derived indicators do not predict the future returns of the underlying
  stocks, and the options market's share of price discovery is nil.
- The predictive power is no stronger when options are liquid or when the information is asymmetric or
  tied to an event.
- The predictive content of option activity comes from hedging, volatility or non-directional demand
  rather than from directional informed trading.
