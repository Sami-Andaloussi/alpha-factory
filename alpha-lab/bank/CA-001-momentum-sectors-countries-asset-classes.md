## Mechanism

Momentum rests on the idea that news, a change of regime or an imbalance in flows is not incorporated
into all prices at once. Underreaction, the gradual diffusion of information, constraints on
reallocation, institutional herding and trend-chasing create a continuation. The effect concerns
sectors, countries, baskets or themes whose leadership is already emerging.

The effect persists because capital adjusts slowly, because investors are sensitive to recency, and
because many investment processes update their positions with a delay.

## Prediction

Within a group of comparable assets (sectors, countries, baskets of stocks or themes), those with
the strongest recent relative performance go on to outperform those with the weakest. The
continuation is documented both in the cross-section and in the time series, for stocks, for factors
and across several asset classes; momentum and trend following in commodities are among the cases
studied. The continuation is vulnerable to violent market rebounds, to regime reversals and to
episodes of "momentum crash".

## What would refute it

- Among sectors, countries, baskets or themes, past relative winners do not outperform past relative
  losers afterwards, and an asset's own past trend does not predict its subsequent return.
- Prices incorporate news, regime changes and flow imbalances fully and at once, so that recent
  relative performance carries no information about the next period.
