## Mechanism

The theory holds that monetary policy and financing conditions systematically shift relative
leadership between sectors. The channels are discount rates, the supply of credit, the present value
of distant cash flows, refinancing, investment, inventories and the sensitivity of consumption. The
phenomenon is fundamentally one between assets: distinct blocks of sectors respond differently to
the same monetary state. It is not merely a macroeconomic signal but a theory of the structural
dispersion of sector exposures to monetary shocks. Part of the persistence may come from the market
not adjusting the whole hierarchy of sectors at once when a change of monetary regime becomes
credible.

The effect concerns sectors, styles or geographic zones, according to their sensitivity to financing.

## Prediction

When monetary conditions ease, cyclical sectors, sensitive to the cost of capital, to the duration
of their cash flows or to credit, tend to be favoured; when they tighten, defensive sectors, or
those less dependent on leverage and on discretionary demand, become relatively more attractive.

## What would refute it

- Relative sector returns do not differ between easing and tightening regimes: cyclical and
  rate-sensitive sectors do not outperform when conditions ease, nor defensive sectors when they
  tighten.
- The hierarchy of sectors adjusts fully as soon as a change of monetary regime becomes credible,
  leaving no relative move afterwards.
