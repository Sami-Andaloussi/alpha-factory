## Mechanism

Some securities are more exposed than others to the aggregate flows of the funds that hold them,
and this exposure can itself be priced or predictive. A security held by funds subject to heavy
subscriptions and redemptions, or by vehicles very sensitive to relative performance, can react
strongly to changes in the flows of that clientele. The key variable is no longer the level of
ownership alone, but the sensitivity of the security to the common flows of its owners.

The mechanism has two stages. First, investors allocate capital into or out of a family of funds.
Second, those funds adjust their portfolios, which weighs most on the securities over-represented in
that family. Securities with a high "flow beta" can therefore suffer recurring price pressure, or
command a risk premium if the exposure hurts in bad states of the world. The idea extends ownership
fragility into a more systematic measure of flow risk.

The effect persists because the composition of a security's shareholder base is not neutral. Its
owners are not frictionless intermediaries: they carry distinct flow behaviours, sometimes highly
correlated with one another. Arbitrageurs must deal not only with today's flow but also with the
probability that it recurs as long as the funds of that clientele remain under pressure. The theory
is centred on equities; by analogy, the logic could carry over to crypto-assets held heavily by
particular categories of vehicles or platforms.

## Prediction

Securities whose owners' funds have volatile, correlated flows can react strongly to changes in
those flows: they fall when the owner funds face outflows and rise when they receive inflows, and
the pressure can recur while the clientele remains under strain. Across securities, a higher
sensitivity to the common flows of the owner funds can go with recurring price pressure, or with a
higher expected return compensating for an exposure that hurts in bad states of the world.

## What would refute it

- Returns of securities unrelated to the aggregate flows of the funds that own them, once the level
  of ownership is accounted for.
- No difference in price pressure or in average returns between securities with high and low
  sensitivity to the common flows of their owners.
- Flow-driven pressure that does not recur while the owner funds remain under outflow pressure.
