## Mechanism

The Ramadan effect holds that the month of Ramadan changes sentiment, attention, the hours of activity, trading volume and possibly risk aversion, and that these changes can produce specific patterns of returns or of volatility. The mechanism is not purely religious in a descriptive sense: it is framed as a theory of sentiment and social routine. Ramadan changes economic life, waking hours and social synchronisation, and possibly the level of optimism. The higher returns often found during the month, and the lower volatility sometimes found, are interpreted as the result of a better collective state of mind, of greater sociability, or of calmer market behaviour.

Ramadan is a moving calendar cycle: it shifts through the solar year. The findings in crypto markets suggest that even a global market that trades around the clock can absorb cultural calendars if its investor base is exposed to them.

## Prediction

During Ramadan, the equity markets of majority-Muslim countries often earn more than in the rest of the year, sometimes with lower volatility, and trading activity changes. Because Ramadan moves through the solar year, the effect moves with it rather than staying in a fixed calendar month.

Where it has been observed: in the equity markets of several majority-Muslim countries, where studies often find higher returns and sometimes lower volatility during Ramadan, and have also examined trading volume; and in crypto markets, where Lopez-Martín (2022) finds Ramadan effects in several major assets, varying from coin to coin and with conditional volatility. The heterogeneity across countries, assets and periods is strong.

## What would refute it

- No difference in returns or volatility during Ramadan, compared with the rest of the year, in the markets of majority-Muslim countries.
- An effect that stays in a fixed solar month instead of moving with Ramadan, which would point to a seasonal or climatic cause.
- An effect as strong in markets whose investors are not exposed to the Ramadan calendar as in markets whose investors are.
- No change in trading activity, hours of activity or measured sentiment during Ramadan.
